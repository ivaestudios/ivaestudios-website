The Region

Why Los Cabos photographs unlike anywhere else in Mexico

The peninsula ends where the Pacific meets the Sea of Cortez, and the desert runs straight into both. That collision is what gives a Los Cabos wedding its light, and it behaves differently on each coast.

01

Two coasts, two kinds of light

The Pacific side faces west, so the sun sets into open water and the granite turns amber for the last forty minutes of the day. The Sea of Cortez side, toward San José del Cabo, is calmer and holds softer morning light. Where your ceremony sits decides which one you get.

02

Desert that reaches the sand

Cardón cactus, granite and dune sit within walking distance of the ceremony at most properties, which means the portrait set does not have to look like every other beach wedding.

03

Land's End and El Arco

The arch at the tip of the peninsula is reachable only by boat, and it is the one frame that says Los Cabos and nowhere else. It works best in the first hour of light.

04

A compact map

Cabo San Lucas, the twenty mile Tourist Corridor and San José del Cabo sit close enough that a single day can move between them without the drive eating the schedule.

Where We Shoot

The venues and corners that earn the frame

01

Pedregal and the cliffs

Portraits above Land's End with the Pacific behind you. The cliff terraces catch the last light directly, and the drop behind the frame does the work no backdrop can.

02

Esperanza, Punta Ballena

Boutique luxury on a private cove. Editorial coverage here tends to run quiet and close, which suits smaller guest counts.

03

One&Only Palmilla

Beach ceremonies on the Sea of Cortez side, with the chapel and the point giving two very different sets within a few minutes of each other.

04

Lover's Beach and El Arco

Boat access only, at the exact tip of the peninsula. Worth the crossing for couples who want the one location that cannot be mistaken for anywhere else.

05

San José del Cabo

The art district, the cobbled lanes and the mission church. This is where a Los Cabos wedding stops looking like a resort and starts looking like Mexico.

06

Along the Corridor

The properties between the two towns, including Viceroy Los Cabos, Chileno Bay and Fiesta Americana Grand. We work as an outside vendor and shoot at any of them, subject to the property's own vendor policy.

The Light

When your ceremony should start

The single decision that changes your photographs more than any other is the ceremony start time. In Los Cabos it is dictated by which coast you are on.

Pacific side, Cabo San Lucas

Aim to finish the ceremony about ninety minutes before sunset. That leaves the golden hour free for portraits and puts the ceremony itself in light that is warm but not yet flat.

Sea of Cortez side, San José

The sun sets behind the land here, so the beach cools earlier. A ceremony an hour before sunset gives even, shadowless light with no squinting.

Sunrise sessions

First light at El Arco or on an empty beach. The peninsula is quiet before eight and the water is at its clearest.

What the season changes

October to May is the long dry stretch with the most reliable light. Summer brings heat and humidity, so we move coverage to the edges of the day.

Before You Book

What couples ask about a Los Cabos wedding

Do you speak English?

Yes, the whole studio works in English and Spanish. On a Los Cabos wedding day that matters more than it sounds, because the couple and the guests are usually working in English while the venue staff, the officiant and the vendors are working in Spanish, and someone has to hold both.

Are you based in Los Cabos or do you fly in?

We are based in Cancún and travel to Los Cabos, with more than 80 sessions already photographed on the peninsula. Travel is arranged as part of the coverage and we arrive with time to scout before the day.

Can you shoot at our resort if it has its own photographer?

Almost always. Most properties in Los Cabos allow an outside photographer, and some charge a vendor fee for the privilege. We handle that arrangement with the property directly.

How much does a Los Cabos wedding photographer cost?

It depends on the hours, whether the celebration runs over more than one day, and the travel. Send the date and the venue and you get a real number the same business day, not a brochure.

How far in advance should we book?

For the dry season, October through May, six to twelve months is realistic for a Saturday. Shorter notice is often possible midweek and in summer.

Do you cover San José del Cabo as well as Cabo San Lucas?

Both, and everything along the Corridor between them. They are close enough that a single day can use more than one of them without the driving taking over the schedule.

Can we add a session earlier in the trip?

Yes, and it is the most common addition. A short couples or welcome session on an earlier day gives you images before the wedding-day nerves, and it lets us learn how you two are together before it counts.

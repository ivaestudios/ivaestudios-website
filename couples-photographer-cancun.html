The Session

Most of our couples are not engaged

Some are on honeymoon. Some are marking ten years, or twenty. Some booked a week in Cancún, realised the only photographs of the two of them together in five years are phone selfies at arm's length, and decided to fix it. All of those are the same session, and none of them need a reason.

An hour is enough. It is short enough that nobody gets tired of being photographed and long enough to move between two settings while the light is still good. We direct throughout, because almost nobody knows what to do with their hands, and the direction is the part that makes the photographs look like you rather than like a catalogue.

Where We Photograph

Settings that work for two people

01

Your resort beach

The easiest and the least disruptive. No transport, no schedule, and the walk back to your room takes two minutes. Most sessions start here.

02

The Hotel Zone shoreline

Long open stretches with the Caribbean behind you and no buildings in the frame. Best in the last ninety minutes of light.

03

A cenote

Freshwater, limestone and light falling through an opening in the rock. Cool, quiet, and the opposite of the beach in every way. Worth the drive inland.

04

Isla Mujeres

Twenty minutes by ferry. Playa Norte is the one beach nearby that faces west, so it is where a sunset session actually gets a sunset.

05

Puerto Morelos

A small fishing town half an hour south, with a leaning lighthouse and no resorts on the sand. Quieter than anywhere in Cancún.

06

The lagoon side

Behind the hotels, the Nichupté lagoon faces west. Mangrove, still water and the sunset the beach cannot give you.

Light & Timing

One hour, and which one

Cancún stays on Eastern Standard Time all year, so these hours hold in December and in July with barely any change.

Sunrise, the first hour

Empty beach, freshly cleaned sand, cool air and the sun coming out of the water in front of you. It costs you one early alarm on holiday and it is the best light of the day.

The last ninety minutes of afternoon

The sun has dropped behind the hotels and the beach sits in open shade with bright water in front. Comfortable, and the easiest hour to actually get out of the room for.

Twenty minutes after sunset

The sky over the lagoon holds colour after the beach goes dark. Short, and worth staying for.

Not midday

Overhead sun, hard shadow under the eyes and white sand throwing light straight up. We will say no and offer you a different hour rather than take the booking.

Straight Answers

What couples ask about this

Do we need a reason to book a session?

No. Honeymoon, anniversary, a holiday, or simply that no good photograph of the two of you exists. They are all the same hour to us.

We are terrible in front of a camera. Is that a problem?

It is the normal starting point, and it is the part of the job we are actually paid for. You are directed the whole way, so there is no moment where you are left standing there deciding what to do.

How long is a session?

One hour covers it, usually across two settings. Longer sessions exist but most couples find an hour is where it stops being fun.

Where do the photographs happen?

Most often your own resort beach, because it is simple. Cenotes, Isla Mujeres, Puerto Morelos and the lagoon side are all options and we will tell you which suits your dates.

When will we get them?

One to three days, in full resolution, with nothing written across them and no expiry date on the gallery.

Can you photograph a proposal?

Yes, and it is planned differently. See our surprise proposal page, where the whole approach is about not being seen beforehand.

Do you travel outside Cancún?

Yes. Isla Mujeres, Puerto Morelos, Playa del Carmen and the northern Riviera Maya, with no travel charge inside that stretch. Tulum has its own page.

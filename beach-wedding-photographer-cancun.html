The Coast

A beach that faces the wrong way for sunset

Cancún's Hotel Zone is a narrow strip with the Caribbean on one side and the Nichupté lagoon on the other. The sand faces east. The sun rises out of the water in front of your ceremony and sets behind you, over the lagoon, where no beach ceremony is ever set up.

Couples arrive expecting the sunset beach wedding they saw on Pinterest, which was almost always photographed on a west facing Pacific coast. Here, a four o'clock ceremony on the sand puts the sun directly overhead and behind the guests, and the light on your faces is the light bouncing off white sand, which is the harshest light on this coast. The fix is not a filter. It is the hour, and we will argue for it.

What the Beach Does

Four things the shoreline decides

01

The wind is constant

The trade wind comes off the water almost every afternoon. It moves veils, hair and anything unpinned. We plan for it rather than fight it, and a veil in the wind is usually the better photograph anyway.

02

Sargassum has a season

The seaweed arrives on this coast roughly from spring through early autumn and varies week to week. Resorts clean their stretch every morning, so an early ceremony sees the cleanest sand of the day.

03

The beach is public

Every beach in Mexico is federal land up to the tide line. Your resort controls its chairs and its access, not the sand itself, and that changes where a ceremony can legally be set and who else may be in the frame.

04

White sand is a reflector

Cancún's sand is limestone, not silica, and it stays cool and very bright. It throws light up under the chin, which is flattering at the right hour and merciless at noon.

Light & Timing

The hours that actually work here

Cancún stays on Eastern Standard Time all year with no daylight saving, so the usable hours barely move between winter and summer.

Sunrise, around six thirty

The sun comes out of the Caribbean in front of the ceremony. Empty beach, cleanest sand, coolest air. Almost nobody books it, which is exactly why it looks like nowhere else.

Late afternoon, the last ninety minutes

The sun is behind the hotels by then and the beach sits in open shade with the bright water still in front of you. This is where most beach ceremonies belong.

Noon, if it cannot be moved

Some venues only release the beach at midday. It is workable with shade, timing and the right angle to the water, but it is a compromise and we will say so before you book.

After sunset, twenty minutes

The sky over the lagoon behind you keeps colour after the beach goes dark. Worth stepping out of the reception for, and it is over quickly.

Straight Answers

What couples ask about the beach

What time should a beach ceremony in Cancún start?

The last ninety minutes before sunset, or sunrise. The beach faces east, so a late afternoon ceremony sits in open shade with bright water in front of it, which is the light everyone actually wants.

Will there be sargassum on our day?

It varies week to week from spring through early autumn and nobody can promise a clean beach months ahead. Resorts rake their stretch each morning, so an earlier ceremony sees it at its best.

Can we have a sunset ceremony on the beach?

Not with the sun over the water, because Cancún faces east. For a sun setting into the sea you want Playa Norte on Isla Mujeres, twenty minutes away by ferry, and we photograph there regularly.

Is the beach private?

No beach in Mexico is private. Resorts control their access and their furniture, but the sand to the tide line is federal. In practice a resort stretch is quiet, and we frame to keep strangers out of your photographs.

What about the wind?

It is almost always there in the afternoon. Pin what must stay in place, let the veil and the hair move, and it becomes the best part of the frame rather than the problem.

Do you photograph beach weddings outside Cancún?

Yes. Isla Mujeres, Puerto Morelos, Playa del Carmen and the northern Riviera Maya, with no travel charge inside that stretch.

How fast do we get the photographs?

One to three days, so most couples see the gallery before their trip is over.

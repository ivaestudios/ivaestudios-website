Why It Matters

The gap nobody warns you about

You booked a resort that answers in English. What you did not book is the officiant, the makeup artist who arrives at seven, the driver, the florist, the beach captain who decides where the arch can stand and the coordinator who runs the timeline in Spanish over the radio. On the day, those are the people who set the pace, and your photographer is the only vendor standing next to you from the first hour to the last.

Our whole team works in both languages. You hear the direction in English, in your own words, and everything that has to be negotiated on the spot happens in Spanish without you being pulled into it. There is no interpreter in the middle and no sentence that arrives to you already flattened.

Both languages, all day

This is not a translation service added on top. It is how the studio already works, written down so you know what you are getting.

01

Direction in English

Every instruction you receive, from where to put your hands to when to look away, comes in English from the person holding the camera, not relayed through anyone.

02

Coordination in Spanish

06

Same answer either way

Write to us in English or Spanish. The reply comes back the same business day, in whichever language you opened with.

Where This Shows Up

The moments where a language gap costs you

01

The morning, in the room

Getting ready is the hour with the most people and the least structure. Hair and makeup usually work in Spanish and run on their own clock, and somebody has to keep that clock honest without it landing on you.

02

First look and portraits

A quarter of an hour of real light, and one chance at it. Direction has to arrive fast and clean, in the language your body reacts to without translating first.

03

The ceremony

An officiant in Spanish, a bilingual script, or both. Knowing when the ring, the lazo, the arras and the kiss actually happen is the difference between the frame and the back of a head.

04

Group portraits

The list is read out loud twice, once in each language, so the aunt from Ohio and the uncle from Monterrey both step forward the first time.

05

The reception

The DJ, the coordinator and the kitchen run in Spanish. Cake, first dance and speeches move without warning, and we hear that change as it happens.

06

The vendor group chat

Most Cancún weddings live in one WhatsApp group, in Spanish, from the week before. We are in it, and we translate what actually concerns you.

Planning From Abroad

What changes when you are five hours away

Almost every couple we photograph plans the whole thing from the United States or Canada and lands three days before. That is normal here, and it shapes how we work.

Everything before the trip is remote

Dates, coverage, shot priorities and the timeline get settled over WhatsApp and email months ahead, in English, so nothing important waits for your arrival.

The venue walkthrough is ours, not yours

We know the Hotel Zone properties and we confirm access, permits and the ceremony spot with the resort directly. You do not spend a vacation day on that.

Time zones are on us

Cancún runs on Eastern Standard Time all year and does not change for daylight saving. In summer that puts us one hour behind New York, which is where scheduling calls usually go wrong.

Documents, if you are marrying legally

A civil wedding in Quintana Roo asks for apostilled papers, blood tests and witnesses with local identification. We are not lawyers and we will not pretend to be, but we will tell you the hour it takes so it does not eat your light.

Straight Answers

What couples ask about this

Do you actually speak English, or is it translated?

We speak it. Direction during the session, the written proposal and every message come from the studio directly in English. Nothing goes through a translator or an app.

Our families speak different languages. Is that a problem?

No, it is most of our weddings. Group portraits get called out in both languages and nobody is left standing there guessing what was asked.

Can you talk to our resort coordinator for us?

Yes, and we prefer it. The photo timeline, the ceremony location and any access rules get settled with them in Spanish before you land.

Do you charge extra for working in two languages?

No. It is not an add on, it is how the studio has always worked. The prices are published and they are the same either way.

We are having a bilingual ceremony. Will you know when things happen?

Yes. We follow the script in the language it is spoken and we know where the ring, the lazo and the arras fall in a Mexican ceremony, whether they are announced in Spanish or in English.

Do you cover legal civil weddings as well as symbolic ones?

Both. A civil ceremony in Quintana Roo has its own rhythm and its own rules about where a photographer can stand, and we work inside them.

What if we write to you in Spanish?

Then we answer in Spanish. You choose the language and we stay in it, all the way to the gallery.

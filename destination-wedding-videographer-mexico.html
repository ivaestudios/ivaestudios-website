The Studio

One studio, two crafts, filmed in the same register

A photograph holds the day still. A film gives it back its motion and its sound: the vows in your own voices, the catch in a father's speech, the way the wind moves a veil at golden hour. IVAE Studios films destination weddings across Mexico, and this page is the film service, the record that lives beside the photography rather than competing with it.

We are honest about where the studio stands. Our films are commissioned by inquiry and are in production for the couples who booked them, so this is a working film service and not a public archive. A private showreel is available on request when we talk, which is the most direct way to judge the cinematic eye before you commit.

Led by Director Vianey Díaz, the studio works in two languages and one visual register: warm restrained color, natural light, and a quiet presence that lets the day unfold without performance.

What We Film

The films a destination wedding in Mexico can carry

Nothing here is a fixed catalogue. Coverage is shaped around how your day actually runs, and the four pieces below are the ones couples combine most often.

01

Cinematic highlight film

Five to eight minutes that carry the arc of the day, cut to licensed music, with the vows and the speeches carrying the voice rather than a montage carrying it for them.

02

Full documentary edit

The long form. Ceremony and speeches in full, close to real time, for the people who could not travel and for the version you will want in twenty years.

03

Ceremony and vow film

A focused piece around the ceremony itself, useful when the celebration runs across several days and the ceremony is the part that has to be complete.

04

Same-day teaser

An optional short cut delivered while you are still at the reception, for couples who want something to share the same night.

Where We Film

The regions this studio films year round

01

Cancún and the Hotel Zone

The strip we film most. We know which terraces hold the sun longest and how the light behaves against the lagoon in each season.

02

The Riviera Maya

Playa del Carmen, Mayakoba and the properties along the corridor. Lagoon, jungle and beach can all sit inside one film without the day looking like three different weddings.

03

Tulum

Beach clubs, jungle and cenotes. The hardest light on the coast and the most cinematic once you know how it moves.

04

Costa Mujeres and Playa Mujeres

Newer resort campuses north of Cancún with the space and private beach a multi-day celebration needs.

05

Los Cabos

The Baja peninsula, where the desert meets the Pacific and the Sea of Cortez. Filmed by travel arrangement from the Cancún base.

06

Inland Mexico

San Miguel de Allende, Oaxaca, Mérida and Mexico City for couples whose celebration is colonial rather than coastal.

Film and Photo Together

Why one studio doing both changes the day

The most common problem couples describe is a photographer and a videographer who spend the day working around each other. When both come from the same studio, that negotiation never happens.

No competing direction

You are not being posed twice by two people with different ideas. One direction, two records of it.

The same visual register

The film and the gallery are graded to match, so the two do not look like they came from different weddings.

One timeline, agreed once

The schedule is planned once, against the light, with both crafts already accounted for instead of bolted together the week before.

One point of contact

Your planner and your venue deal with one studio, in whichever of the two languages suits them.

Before You Commission

What couples ask about the film

Can we see finished films?

A private showreel and reference work, yes, shared directly when we talk. We do not run a public film channel because the films in production belong to the couples who commissioned them.

Can we book film without photography?

Yes, though most couples book both. The reason to combine them is that a single studio plans one timeline and grades both to match, rather than two vendors negotiating on the day.

Do you film outside Cancún and the Riviera Maya?

Yes. Los Cabos, San Miguel de Allende, Oaxaca, Mérida and Mexico City are all filmed by travel arrangement from the Cancún base.

How long is the finished film?

The highlight film usually runs five to eight minutes. A full documentary edit runs as long as the ceremony and the speeches actually run, because cutting those down is the thing couples regret.

When do we get it?

The film takes longer than the photographs, which are delivered in 1 to 3 days. We give you a delivery window in writing when the coverage is agreed, and a same-day teaser is available if you want something to share that night.

Do you use drones?

Where it is legal and the venue permits it. Mexican airspace rules and resort policies both apply, and we confirm both before the day rather than turning up and hoping.

What about music rights?

Films are cut to properly licensed music. It matters more than it sounds, because an unlicensed track is what gets a wedding film muted or taken down when you share it.

The Island

It faces the other way

Everything on the Cancún coastline faces east. The sun comes out of the Caribbean in the morning and goes down behind you, over the lagoon. That is why every serious session over there is booked at sunrise or in the last hour of open shade.

Isla Mujeres breaks the rule. Playa Norte, at the top of the island, faces roughly west across the channel, and the sun sets into the water in front of it. If you came here wanting the photograph where the sea swallows the sun, this is the only place within half an hour of your hotel where it actually happens.

The island is about eight kilometres long and almost nobody drives a car. The northern end is walkable, the rest is a golf cart ride, and the whole thing empties out once the day boats leave.

Where We Photograph

The settings worth the ferry

01

Playa Norte

Wide, shallow and facing west. Palms, sandbars you can walk out onto, and the sunset that made the island famous. Busy from late morning, empty at both ends of the day.

02

The channel side

Looking back across the water toward Cancún, which at sunset turns into a distant gold line. A different frame from the same beach.

03

Punta Sur

The southern point: limestone cliffs, open sea, a sculpture path and the small Ixchel ruin. Wind, height and the most dramatic light on the island.

04

Centro's streets

Low painted buildings, narrow lanes and doorways. Early morning, before the shops open, it photographs like a town rather than a beach.

05

The eastern shore

Rock, surf and no swimming. Spectacular at sunrise and completely empty, because nobody comes here for it.

06

The lagoon at Makax

Sheltered water on the inland side, mangrove and boats. Quiet when the beaches are not.

Light & Timing

The ferry decides your hour

Quintana Roo stays on the same clock all year, so these windows barely move between December and July. What moves is the boat.

Sunset on Playa Norte

The reason to be here. The last hour before the sun touches the water, with the whole beach facing it. Check the last ferry back before you commit to this, because it leaves earlier than most visitors expect.

Sunrise, before the first boats

Playa Norte empty, sand freshly raked, and the light coming over the island from behind. Almost nobody books it and it is the calmest hour the island has.

Avoid eleven to four

The day trippers own the island in that window. It is not dangerous or unpleasant, it is simply full, and it will be in your frames.

Twenty minutes after sunset

The channel holds colour after the beach goes dark, with Cancún lit on the horizon. Short and worth staying for.

Straight Answers

What people ask about the island

Why photograph on Isla Mujeres instead of Cancún?

Because Playa Norte faces roughly west. The Cancún Hotel Zone faces east, so the sun there rises out of the sea and sets behind the buildings. On the island the sun goes down into the water in front of you, which is the photograph most people picture when they book a beach session.

How do we get there, and how long does it take?

About twenty minutes by ferry from the piers north of downtown Cancún, with departures through the day. There are also slower, more expensive crossings from a couple of Hotel Zone piers. We tell you which one fits your hour.

What about the last ferry?

It is the single thing that catches people out. The final return leaves earlier than most visitors assume, especially outside high season, so a sunset session has to be planned backwards from it. We do that when we set your hour.

Is there less sargassum on the island?

On Playa Norte, usually yes, because it sits on the sheltered northwestern side rather than facing the open Caribbean. In a heavy sargassum week on the mainland the island is often the cleaner beach.

Can we do a wedding or an elopement here?

Yes, and the island suits small ones particularly well. Two people and a witness, or a group of twenty. Anything bigger usually wants a mainland resort.

Do you charge extra to travel to the island?

No. Isla Mujeres, Puerto Morelos, Playa del Carmen and the northern Riviera Maya are all inside our normal coverage with no travel charge.

Is the island safe?

Yes for the visit almost everyone makes. The real risks here are golf carts on narrow roads and current on the eastern shore, not crime. We wrote a fuller guide to that.

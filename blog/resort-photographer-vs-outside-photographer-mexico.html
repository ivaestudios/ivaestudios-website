Resort Photographer or Outside Photographer: How to Choose in Mexico

In short

A resort's in-house photo team is convenient, easy to book on property and often quick. An outside studio is usually chosen for planning, direction and a gallery built around you. Neither is right for everyone. Ask your resort about its packages and its rules for outside vendors before you decide.

Many resorts in Cancun, the Riviera Maya, Tulum and Los Cabos offer in-house photography, and many couples and families also consider hiring their own photographer. We are an outside studio, so read this knowing where we stand. Both options have a place, and this guide explains what to compare.

What In-House Teams Do Well

Convenience. The team is already on property. You can usually book through the concierge or the photo desk, with no outside-vendor paperwork for you to handle.

Short notice. If you decide during your stay that you want photos, an on-site team is often the easiest way to get them.

Group and event moments. Resort photographers know the property and its events, which can suit welcome dinners, parties and quick group photos.

Getting photos before you leave. Many teams can show or deliver photos during your stay. Ask how yours works.

What an Outside Studio Adds

Planning before the day. An outside studio usually starts with a conversation about your trip, the light at your date and time, outfits and locations.

Direction. Editorial direction means thinking about where you stand relative to the light, how you move and what is behind you, so the session feels guided without looking posed. A Cancun photographer who works this way builds the session around you.

Locations beyond the property. Where you want more than the resort, an outside photographer can often plan a session at other locations in the Riviera Maya, subject to permits and the rules of each place.

One gallery with one look. A studio that edits your gallery individually can keep color and skin tones consistent across changing light.

What to Compare

Prices and quality vary on both sides, so compare the actual offers rather than the labels. Ask each option the same questions:

  • Who will photograph us? Can you see that person's work?
  • Can we see a full gallery? Not only the best images.
  • How many edited photos do we receive? At what resolution, and are they all included in the price?
  • What rights come with the photos? Can you print and share them?
  • When do we receive them? Get the date in writing.
  • What is the total? Include any fee your resort applies, travel and tax.

Outside-Vendor Rules

This is what most couples discover too late. Some resorts charge a fee for outside photographers, ask for paperwork, limit where they can work or do not allow them at all. These rules vary by property and event, and they change.

Before you book, ask your resort in writing:

  • Are outside photographers allowed for our session or event?
  • Is there a fee, how much is it and who pays it?
  • What paperwork does the photographer need, and how far in advance?
  • Which areas can the photographer work in, and at what times?

If you are still choosing a resort, ask before you sign, because it can change your photography budget. Our guide to all-inclusive resort photography in Mexico goes deeper. Always follow the resort's process, so your photographer can work without problems on the day.

When the Resort Team Is the Right Call

The in-house option can be the right answer when you want a few casual photos of your trip, when you decide at the last minute, when you need photos before you fly home, or when professional portraits are not a priority for your budget. If that is you, enjoy it.

Using Both

Some couples and families combine the two: an on-site team for a party or welcome dinner, and an outside studio for the portrait session or wedding day. If you do this, tell both sides in advance so nobody is surprised on the day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the resort photographer good enough?
It depends on what you want. An in-house team can be a great fit for convenience and casual photos. If you want planning, direction and an individually edited gallery, compare it with an outside studio by looking at a full gallery from each.
Can I bring my own photographer to an all-inclusive resort in Mexico?
Often yes, but it depends on the property. Some resorts charge a fee, ask for paperwork or limit where outside photographers can work, and a few may not allow them. Ask your resort in writing before you book.
How much is the outside-vendor fee?
It varies by property and event, and it changes. Some resorts charge nothing and others charge a fee, so ask your resort directly and get the amount in writing before you book.
Who pays the outside-vendor fee?
It depends on your agreement. Ask your resort how it is charged, and ask your photographer whether it is included in the quote, so the total is clear before you sign.
Can we use both the resort photographer and an outside photographer?
Yes, some couples and families do, for example an on-site team for a party and an outside studio for portraits. Tell both sides in advance.
How much does an outside photographer cost with IVAE Studios?
IVAE Studios sessions run from 295 to 895 USD. Weddings run from 1,800 to 2,800 USD for a half day, 3,500 to 5,500 USD for a full day, and 5,500 to 8,000 USD for a full day with a cinematic film.
How long does delivery take?
In-house timing depends on the team, so ask yours. IVAE Studios delivers a private 4K gallery in 1 to 3 business days, with a sneak peek in 24 to 48 hours.

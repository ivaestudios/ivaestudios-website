Isla Mujeres Couples Photo Session Guide

In short

Catch the first ferry from Puerto Juárez and be on Playa Norte fifteen minutes before sunrise, which falls between about 6:15 am in June and 7:10 am in December. At 6:30 am the beach belongs to two joggers and a pelican; by 10 am it holds hundreds, and the sheltered west side stays turquoise even in sargassum months.

Isla Mujeres sits twenty minutes off the Cancún coast, and it is the location we recommend most often to couples who want water that actually looks like the brochure. It is small, it is quiet at six in the morning, and because of the way it is oriented it holds clear turquoise on its western side even in the months when the mainland beaches are struggling. The whole session lives or dies on one thing though, and that is the ferry. Here is how we plan it.

Why the island water stays clearer

This is the practical reason to cross rather than shoot on the mainland, so it is worth understanding rather than taking on faith.

Sargassum reaches the Mexican Caribbean on currents that push against east-facing coastlines. The Riviera Maya from Puerto Morelos down through Playa del Carmen and Tulum takes the worst of it, and so does the ocean-facing side of Cancún's Hotel Zone. Isla Mujeres is a narrow strip roughly seven kilometres long running north to south, with the open Caribbean on its eastern flank and the sheltered channel toward Cancún on its western flank.

The east side of the island absorbs the impact. It is rocky, it is windy, the surf is real and nobody swims there. That is not a flaw, it is the island doing its job, because everything west of that spine, including Playa Norte and the whole town shoreline, sits in the wind shadow. During the heavy sargassum months from roughly April through August, we regularly photograph couples in clean, clear water on the west side of Isla Mujeres on days when the mainland beach in front of their resort is unusable. Our sargassum season guide covers the month by month picture in more detail.

It is a strong tendency, not a guarantee. A heavy week with the wrong wind direction can push material around the north tip. What it buys you is odds, and in a bad year odds are the whole game.

The single decision that matters

Where you catch the ferry decides whether a sunrise session is possible at all. Puerto Juárez has the early departures. The Hotel Zone piers do not. Everything else in this guide can be adjusted; that one cannot.

Ferry timing that makes a sunrise session possible

There are two ways to reach Isla Mujeres as a visitor. The main one is Puerto Juárez, just north of downtown Cancún, where the passenger ferries run frequently from very early in the morning until late at night and the crossing takes around twenty to twenty five minutes. The second is the set of Hotel Zone piers at Playa Tortugas, Playa Caracol and El Embarcadero, which are more convenient if you are staying in the Zone but start much later in the morning, run far fewer departures and take longer to cross.

For a golden hour session at first light, that leaves one answer. You go to Puerto Juárez. From a Hotel Zone hotel that is a taxi of roughly twenty five to thirty five minutes depending on where you are staying and how the boulevard is moving. Sunrise on this coast falls somewhere between about 6:15 AM in June and about 7:10 AM in December and January, and we want to be standing on the sand fifteen minutes before it.

So the arithmetic runs backwards from the light. Take the sunrise time for your date, subtract fifteen minutes to be in position, subtract ten minutes to walk from the Isla Mujeres pier to Playa Norte, subtract the crossing, subtract the queue at the ticket window, and subtract the taxi. In December that usually means leaving your hotel around 5:30 AM. In June it means around 4:45 AM. Nobody enjoys reading that, and every couple who has done it has told us afterwards it was worth the alarm.

Confirm the current first departure at the terminal the day before, or ask us and we will check. Schedules shift with the season and with weather, and the boat that leaves at a particular minute in a two year old blog post is not a plan.

Playa Norte: the shallow turquoise everyone comes for

Playa Norte occupies the northern tip of the island and it is the reason Isla Mujeres appears on every list of the best beaches in Mexico. The water is genuinely shallow a long way out, so a couple can walk thirty or forty metres from the sand and still be waist deep, standing in that flat pale turquoise with nothing behind them but water and sky.

Two things make it work for photographs. The first is the orientation: because it faces north and west, the beach gets soft indirect light at sunrise rather than a hard sun in your faces, and it is one of the only beaches in the region that also gets a real sunset over water. The second is the emptiness at dawn. Playa Norte at 6:30 AM belongs to two joggers and a pelican. By 10 AM it belongs to several hundred people with a beach club soundtrack, and the difference in a photograph is total.

It is a ten minute walk from the ferry terminal, which makes it the most logistically forgiving location on the island. Wardrobe wise, the shallow water is an invitation. Almost every couple ends up in it, so bring something you do not mind getting wet to the knee, and if you are wearing a long dress plan on it being soaked at the hem by the end. That is usually where the best frames come from.

Punta Sur: cliffs, wind and the east-side light

The southern tip is the island's other face and it could not be more different. Punta Sur is low limestone cliff dropping into open Caribbean, with the ruins of a small Maya temple to Ixchel, a sculpture garden along the path, and the easternmost point of Mexico at the very end. It is dramatic in a way Playa Norte is not, and it is where couples who want weather and scale in the frame should go.

There are two honest constraints. The first is opening hours: the Punta Sur park is a paid attraction and it opens in the morning, well after first light, so a sunrise session inside the park is not possible. What we do instead is photograph the southern coastline and the cliff edges from the public road and the viewpoints outside the gate at first light, then enter the park later in the morning if you want the temple and the sculptures.

The second constraint is the wind. The east side is exposed and the wind is real, most days, most of the year. That can be spectacular for photographs, and it can also be a problem for anyone in a veil or a light dress. We plan for it: hair up or deliberately loose, fabric that behaves in wind, and we shoot with the wind rather than fighting it. Couples who lean into the wind at Punta Sur get the strongest images of the whole morning.

Golf carts and moving between locations

The island runs on golf carts. Rental agencies cluster near the pier and along the main street in town, cars are rare and taxis are plentiful. From Playa Norte to Punta Sur is roughly fifteen to twenty minutes by cart along a single coastal road, and the road itself is one of the nicest drives in the state.

Whether you need one depends entirely on your plan. A session that stays at Playa Norte needs nothing but shoes. A session that covers both ends of the island needs a cart or two taxis, and a cart is usually the better answer because it turns the transit into part of the morning rather than dead time. We have made some lovely frames of couples in a cart on that coast road with the water on one side.

One practical note. Rental agencies do not all open at 6 AM. If your plan starts with a sunrise at Playa Norte and moves south afterwards, the cart is collected after the first location, not before it, and we build the walk back into town into the schedule.

"The island is seven kilometres long. That is small enough to see all of it in a morning and big enough that the two ends look like two different countries."

What a half-day on the island costs

In time, from a Cancún Hotel Zone hotel, plan on about six hours door to door for a session plus a proper island morning. That breaks down as roughly thirty minutes to Puerto Juárez, twenty five minutes crossing, ninety minutes of photography in the good light, a couple of hours to eat and wander, and the return. It is a half day. Couples who try to compress it into three hours end up rushing the part they came for.

In money, the components are the round-trip ferry ticket per person, the taxi to and from Puerto Juárez, and the golf cart if you want one. All three change with the season and with the operator, so we deliberately do not publish figures that will be wrong by next year. What we will say is that the ferry and the cart together are the cheapest part of the day by a wide margin, and that the taxi at 5 AM sometimes costs more than the crossing does.

If you are staying on the island already, most of this disappears. Isla Mujeres has genuinely good small hotels, and couples who stay a night or two get a sunrise session with no boat, no alarm at 4:45 AM and a much calmer morning. When a couple asks us how to make this easy, that is the answer we give.

A tested 6 AM to noon itinerary

This is the shape we use most often, written for a spring or autumn date with sunrise around 6:45 AM. Shift everything by the sunrise time for your own date.

Leave the Hotel Zone at 5:30 AM and reach Puerto Juárez by 6:00. Cross on the first available departure and land on the island by about 6:30. Walk to Playa Norte and be in position before the sun clears the horizon. The first ninety minutes are the session proper, and this is where the studio does its real work, because we photograph in golden hour only and this is the golden hour that matters here. Start on the sand while the light is still pink, move into the shallow water as it warms, and finish with the wide frames while the beach is still empty.

Around 8:15, walk back into town for breakfast. The town wakes up beautifully and the colour on the buildings at that hour is worth a handful of frames on the way through. Collect a golf cart afterwards, drive the coast road south, and stop at the viewpoints along the eastern cliffs for the wind and the drama. Reach Punta Sur when the park opens if you want the temple and the sculpture garden, understanding that the light by then is high and hard, so we shoot the architecture and the scale rather than close portraits.

Be back at the pier before noon and you are at your resort for lunch, with the whole afternoon intact. That last part matters more than it sounds, because a session that eats an entire day of a seven day trip is a session that couples quietly resent afterwards.

Questions couples ask

Why does Isla Mujeres stay clearer than the Cancún beaches?

Sargassum is carried onto this coast by currents that hit east-facing shorelines hardest. Isla Mujeres runs roughly north to south with the open Caribbean on its eastern side and the sheltered channel toward Cancún on its western side. Playa Norte and the whole west coast sit in the island's own wind shadow, so they usually stay far cleaner than the mainland during the heavy months. The east side takes the impact instead, which is exactly why nobody swims there.

Which ferry do we take for a sunrise session?

Puerto Juárez, just north of downtown Cancún. It is the only terminal with departures early enough for first light, the crossing takes around twenty to twenty five minutes, and boats leave frequently through the morning. The Hotel Zone piers at Playa Tortugas, Playa Caracol and El Embarcadero are convenient if you are staying nearby, but they start much later in the morning and run far fewer departures, so they do not work for sunrise.

Can we photograph at Punta Sur at sunrise?

Not inside the park. The sculpture garden and the cliff path at Punta Sur are a paid attraction that opens in the morning, well after first light, so a genuine sunrise session there is not possible. What we do instead is photograph the cliffs and the east-facing coastline from the public road and viewpoints along the southern end, then enter the park later in the morning if you want the temple and sculpture backdrops.

Do we need to rent a golf cart for the session?

Only if you want to move between the north end and the south end. Playa Norte is a short walk from the ferry terminal, so a session that stays there needs nothing. If you want both ends of the island, a golf cart is the practical answer and the drive between them takes roughly fifteen to twenty minutes. Rental agencies open early near the pier, and taxis are an alternative if you would rather not drive.

How long does a half day on Isla Mujeres actually take?

From a Cancún Hotel Zone hotel, budget about six hours door to door for a session with time to enjoy the island afterwards. That is roughly thirty minutes to Puerto Juárez, a twenty five minute crossing, ninety minutes of photography in the good light, a couple of hours on the island and the return crossing. It is a half day, not a two hour errand, and it is much better planned as a whole morning.

What months have the clearest water for photographs?

November through March is the most reliable window: sargassum is at its lowest, the water is clear and the mornings are cool. April through August is the heavy sargassum season on the mainland, and this is when the island's advantage matters most, because Playa Norte often stays photographable when the Hotel Zone beaches do not. September and October bring the wettest weather and the highest chance of a crossing being affected by wind.

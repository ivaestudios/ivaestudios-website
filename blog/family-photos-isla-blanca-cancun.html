In short

Yes, on a calm evening and with children who enjoy wind and open space. Isla Blanca is the wild strip of sand north of Costa Mujeres, with the Caribbean on one side and the Chacmochuch lagoon on the other. Plan for no shade, food or restrooms, expect kites, and arrive about an hour before sunset: the lagoon side faces west, so the sun sets over the water.

Families staying in Costa Mujeres hear about Isla Blanca from a driver, a kitesurfer or a map, and the natural question is whether it is worth the drive with the kids. We add it to sessions, so here is the honest version: where it is, what is and is not there, the wind, the best months, and when your resort beach wins. We checked every source on November 14, 2026.

Where Is Isla Blanca? The Map in Brief

Despite the name, Isla Blanca is not an island. On a map, follow the coast north from Cancun past Puerto Juarez and Punta Sam, where the car ferry leaves for Isla Mujeres, then through Playa Mujeres and the Costa Mujeres resorts. Around La Angostura the land narrows into a long strip with the open Caribbean to the east and the Chacmochuch lagoon, also spelled Chacmuchuch, to the west. That strip is Isla Blanca.

Nor is it in Cancun: the municipality of Isla Mujeres governs this mainland coast, and its finance law lists Isla Blanca among its mainland zones, next to Punta Sam, Costa Mujeres and La Angostura. Quintana Roo's tourism board calls it a public beach where the sea meets the lagoon, with pets allowed and camping permitted.

One naming tip: there is also an Isla Blanca Park in Texas, which Cameron County lists among its coastal parks. If a map pin or a review is not north of Cancun, it is the wrong Isla Blanca.

Getting There, and Is It Worth the Drive?

By land there is one way in: the coastal road through Costa Mujeres keeps going north, and Majestic Elegance Costa Mujeres, for one, gives its address on the Punta Sam to Isla Blanca road. Past La Angostura, OpenStreetMap maps the way along the strip as a dirt track, so allow time for a slow last stretch. The tourism board calls a rental car practical for moving between Costa Mujeres, Isla Blanca and Cancun. From the northern resorts it is a short drive; from the Hotel Zone, the drive is part of the outing. We found no entry fee for Isla Blanca in the municipality's finance law or its 2026 revenue law.

Is it worth it? On a calm evening, for children who love space and a little adventure, yes. With a baby, a toddler who hates the car seat, or a howling wind, your resort beach wins.

Restaurants, Shade and Restrooms

Plan as if there is no shade, no restroom, no lifeguard and nowhere to buy water. The tourism board describes the beach, the wind and the camping, not restaurants, and we would not plan a family meal around finding one open. Eat before you go, and pack:

  • More water than you think you need, and snacks.
  • Hats, sunscreen and a light long-sleeved layer; wind and beach umbrellas do not mix.
  • Insect repellent for dusk by the lagoon, and a bag for your trash.

For seafood afterwards, the same tourism board points to Puerto Juarez, a few minutes from Costa Mujeres, where fishing cooperatives supply the waterfront restaurants Cancun locals love: fried fish, or tikin xic, fillets marinated in achiote and orange and wrapped in banana leaf.

Wind, Kites and the Forecast

The tourism board calls Isla Blanca a well-known kitesurfing spot for its constant wind and gentle current, so plan around kites: long lines across the sand and fast boards near the water. We set up well away from wherever kites are launching, and small children stay within arm's reach at the water's edge.

Mexico's weather service, the SMN, follows cold fronts from September to May and says that after one passes, the wind turns west or northwest. At Isla Blanca that wind comes straight across the lagoon at you, the opposite of the still evening you came for. Check the forecast two days ahead and the day before: cancellation is free up to one day before, so moving the date for wind costs nothing.

Best Months, and How Long to Stay

The tourism board calls December to April the most pleasant months on this coast; they overlap with the cold-front season, so keep a spare evening in your week. Summer is hotter: in the SMN's 1991 to 2020 normals for its Cancun station, the average high is 35.1°C (95°F) in August, against 28.7°C (84°F) in January, and October is the wettest month.

With kids, treat it as an evening outing, not a beach day: arrive an hour or so before sunset, play, take the photos, and head back as the light fades. Without shade, two hours is plenty.

Family Photos: Sunset Over the Lagoon

Most beaches in Cancun and Costa Mujeres face east, so the sun sets behind the hotels. Isla Blanca has water on both sides, and its lagoon shore faces west: like the Nichupte lagoon in the Hotel Zone, it is one of the few places near Cancun where the sun goes down over water. Through the year the sunset swings from west-southwest in December to west-northwest in June, and from most of the lagoon shore it stays over the water. On a still evening, the lagoon can mirror the sky.

  • Babies and toddlers. Your resort beach, with no car seat and the room close by. Under-fives often do best at sunrise, which here comes up over the sea, and from the southern resorts often behind Isla Mujeres.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Isla Blanca worth visiting with kids?

On a calm evening, with children who enjoy wind and open space, yes. It is largely undeveloped, so plan for no shade, restrooms or food, expect kites, and stay about two hours around sunset. With a baby or toddler, or on a windy day, your resort beach is easier.

Where is Isla Blanca, and is it in Cancun?

It is the narrow strip north of Costa Mujeres, from around La Angostura, with the Caribbean to the east and the Chacmochuch lagoon to the west. It belongs to the municipality of Isla Mujeres, not Cancun, and the coastal road through Costa Mujeres leads to it.

Are there restaurants at Isla Blanca?

Do not count on one. The state tourism board describes a public beach for kitesurfing and camping, so eat before you go and pack water and snacks. For seafood, the board points to Puerto Juarez, where fishing cooperatives supply waterfront restaurants.

Is Isla Blanca good for kitesurfing?

Yes. Quintana Roo's tourism board calls it a well-known kitesurfing spot for its constant wind and gentle current. With children, set up well away from the kites, and watch the forecast: after a cold front passes, the SMN says, the wind turns west or northwest.

A baby will not pose, will not wait for the light and has no idea the sun is going down. That is fine with us. Families bring 11-month-olds, one-year-olds and babies in arms to their Cancun sessions, and the photos come out best when the session is built around the baby: the nap, the feeds and the mood of the day. Here is how we plan family photos with a baby or a young toddler on vacation, what parents have written about it, and straight answers to the questions we hear most.

Book Around the Nap, Not the Sunset

The most useful thing you can tell us when you book is your baby's nap and feeding rhythm, because we build the start time around it. A session that starts about an hour after your baby wakes beats any timetable on paper. A rested, recently fed baby gives us the good minutes. A tired one does not, however beautiful the light.

Online booking at ivaestudios.com/book offers five start times: 6:30, 7:00 or 7:30 in the morning, and 5:00 or 5:30 in the afternoon. If none of them fits your baby's day, message us on WhatsApp and we will look at other times.

The morning slots suit early risers. Little ones on vacation are usually awake at dawn anyway, the Hotel Zone beaches are at their quietest, the sand is still cool, and a sunrise session usually ends well before a late-morning nap. The afternoon slots suit a baby who naps after lunch and wakes up ready to play. Our guide to why sunrise often beats sunset with young kids compares the two.

Send the schedule with your booking

Tell us when your baby usually naps and eats. We would rather start inside your baby's good hour than at the prettiest minute of the evening.

There is no single right length, and our collections come in several sizes, each at a fixed published price. The Postcard, our shortest, is 35 minutes and 15 edited photos for 295 USD, and was made with couples in mind. The Keepsake is 45 minutes and 25 photos for 375 USD. The Chapter, 60 minutes and 35 photos for 475 USD, is the one we recommend for families, and the longer collections run to 90 minutes. All six are on our prices page.

A shorter session asks less of a baby. A longer one leaves room for a feed, a change and a reset without anyone watching the clock; as our guide to how long a family photoshoot takes with kids explains, the extra time goes to pauses, not to more posing. Feeds, Tears and Pauses Are Part of the Plan

A session with a baby usually has a pause or two, and we plan for them rather than fight them. If your baby is hungry, we stop and you feed. If your baby cries, we pause, a parent scoops them up, and we keep photographing gently. A baby curled into a parent's shoulder after the tears is often one of the most tender images a family takes home.

None of this is lost time. For us, the youngest child's break is the schedule, not an interruption to it. When your baby has reset, we start again, and when your baby is truly done, we stop. There is no pressure from us. The same approach, for children who are already running, is in our guide to family photos with toddlers in Cancun.

Stay Close to the Crib

With a baby, the best location is usually the nearest one. Your own resort beach or a garden path means no transfer, no car seat and no overtired arrival, and you stay minutes from the crib, the bottle warmer and a clean change. We plan around the youngest, with short walks and shade, and we photograph across the Hotel Zone, Playa Mujeres, Costa Mujeres and the Riviera Maya, on your resort beach or a quieter stretch of public beach nearby.

For the youngest babies, the session can stay out of the sun: first-year sessions are kept gentle and lifestyle-led, in the soft light of your suite or on a shaded terrace, at the baby's own pace. If your baby is only days or weeks old, read our guide to newborn photography in Cancun resorts. If this is your baby's first trip abroad, our guide to photographing a first family trip covers each age, from newborn to toddler.

One practical note: some hotels charge a fee for an outside photographer. You pay it to the hotel, and we confirm your resort's rule before the session.

Babies Don't Pose, and That Is Fine

We never ask a baby to perform. Babies who are not walking yet are usually content to be held, so we let you hold, play and walk, and we work around the edges, catching the glances between you. Expect your baby to ignore us at first. That is normal, and the first minutes are a warm-up for everyone.

Once your little one is on the move, the session becomes about following. We crouch, we chase, we let them dig, and we get the parents playing so we can photograph the reaction. A shell, the foam of a retreating wave or a swing up into a parent's arms earns more real laughter than any request to smile.

We take the group portrait first, while everyone is fresh, baby included. There is no limit on the number of people and no per-person charge, so grandparents, aunts and cousins are welcome. Huong's family was seven adults and one baby:

What to Bring and Wear

Protect the nap, then arrive fed: a baby who slept and ate within the hour before we meet is ready to enjoy it. Pack a backup outfit, the favorite pacifier or comfort toy, a muslin wrap, snacks if your little one is old enough, water, and sunscreen applied well before we meet so it has dried.

Dress the baby in soft, breathable fabrics they already know, nothing brand new, and skip the shoes: bare feet are perfect on the sand. Bring a sun hat in the bag rather than insisting on it. For the rest of the family, soft coordinated tones photograph better than exact matching, bright logos or busy prints, and linen and cotton survive being crawled on. Our guide on what to wear for a family photoshoot in Mexico has the full palette.

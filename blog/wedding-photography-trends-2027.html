In short

In 2027 couples want more than a wedding day album: coverage across the whole wedding weekend, vertical video for social media, a film or camcorder feel and editorial, heritage-inspired portraits. The data: nearly 1 in 3 weddings spans three days or more (The Knot), 40% of couples planning social-first content ask their photographers to capture it (Zola), and searches for wedding content creators rose 586% in 2025 (Hitch).

Photography trends move faster than dress trends, because phones and social media change what couples see every day. Here is what is actually changing for 2027, based on the largest wedding studies available, and what we think is worth adopting for a destination wedding in Cancún.

Coverage Across the Whole Weekend

The Knot's 2027 Wedding Trends Report, based on the activity of 1.7 million US couples, finds that nearly 1 in 3 weddings already spans three days or more, with each event treated as its own occasion with its own photography. The report also notes that photographers are covering couples from the engagement shoot through the whole wedding weekend. Zola's First Look Report 2026, a survey of more than 11,500 couples, adds that 37% host at least one extra event and 18% plan a full two- to three-day weekend.

For a destination wedding this is the natural format: the welcome party is often where the best candid photos of your guests happen. We plan multi-day coverage as one story with different moods, documentary at the welcome party, editorial on the wedding day and relaxed on the last morning. See how we approach a three-day wedding weekend.

Vertical Video and Social-First Content

Zola found that 50% of couples get wedding inspiration from TikTok, up from 41% a year earlier, that 21% plan to create their own social content on the day and that 40% of couples who plan social-first content ask their professional photographers and videographers to capture it. A trend report from the wedding app Hitch, reported by The Globe and Mail, found a 586% increase in searches for wedding content creators in 2025.

The practical answer is not to choose between a photographer and a phone, but to plan the formats from the start: vertical reels for social media and a horizontal film for the screen. We produce both: our reels are an Editorial Reel of 30 seconds for 295 USD, a Narrative Reel of 45 seconds for 445 USD and a Cinematic Short of 60 seconds for 695 USD. Our guide to photographer, videographer or content creator explains the differences.

Speed Matters More Than Ever

Content creators promise highlight reels by the next morning, and couples now expect to share something while the celebration is still fresh. For sessions, we deliver the full edited gallery in one to three business days. For weddings, ask any photographer you consider for two dates in writing: when you receive a first preview and when you receive the complete gallery.

The Film and Camcorder Revival

The Knot's 2027 report lists throwback camcorders, old-school cocktail glasses and vintage-style record players among the analog items couples are adding to their registries. The same nostalgia shows up in photos: grain, warm color and the slightly imperfect look of film. Some couples want real film; others want a digital camera with a film-like edit or a few minutes of a camcorder at the party. Our posts on real film at a destination wedding and the digicam film look show what each option gives you.

Heritage and Fine-Art Portraits

Antique lace and Victorian details are up 280% in The Knot's data, fine-art references such as Monet, Klimt and stained glass are up 195% and balletcore is up 106%. In portraits that means slower, more composed frames: soft window light, a veil caught in the breeze, a painterly sky at golden hour. It is the opposite of the fast, posed list and pairs well with a documentary approach for the rest of the day. Our post on editorial versus documentary style explains how we balance both.

Phone-Free Ceremonies

Bridebook's 2027 forecast quotes a planner predicting more phone-free moments and private weddings. For photography, an unplugged ceremony is the single easiest upgrade: guests look at you, not at a screen, and the aisle photos are clean. Our unplugged ceremony wording in English and Spanish makes it easy to ask nicely.

Flash, Motion and the Night

Direct flash at the reception and long-exposure motion blur on the dance floor remain popular, because they feel like the party looked. Used for part of the night rather than every frame, they add energy without losing the classic photos. See our take on direct flash at a beach reception.

Drones, With the Rules

Aerial photos of a beach ceremony are spectacular, but in Mexico they are regulated, and many resorts have their own policies. Our guide to drone wedding photos and the AFAC rules explains what is allowed and what to ask your venue.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the wedding photography trends for 2027?
Coverage across the whole wedding weekend, vertical video and social-first content, faster delivery, a film or camcorder feel, editorial heritage-inspired portraits and phone-free ceremonies. Nearly 1 in 3 weddings already spans three days or more, according to The Knot.
Do I need a content creator if I have a photographer?
Not necessarily. Zola found that 40% of couples planning social-first content ask their professional photographers and videographers to capture it. What matters is planning the formats, vertical reels and a horizontal film, from the start.
Is film photography coming back for weddings?
Yes, as part of a wider analog revival: The Knot's 2027 report lists throwback camcorders and vintage-style record players among popular registry items. Couples choose real film, a film-like edit or a few minutes of camcorder footage.
Should we have an unplugged ceremony for better photos?
Yes. Asking guests to put phones away keeps faces visible and the aisle clear, and it is one of the simplest ways to improve your ceremony photos.

Your child turns 5, 10 or 13 while you are in Cancun, and this year the birthday comes with a beach. A family session gives the day photos of its own, as long as it stays the birthday kid's session and not one more group portrait with a cake at the end. These beach birthday photoshoot ideas for kids are for a child old enough to have opinions about what to wear, where to stand and who gets in the picture. Here is how we plan a kid's birthday on vacation, age by age.

Make the Birthday Kid the Star

On a birthday, one person gets top billing, and the easiest way to show it is to hand them a few real decisions before the day. Here is the plan we suggest:

  1. Their outfit. Agree on a palette together, then let them choose what they wear inside it: the dress that twirls, the favorite shirt, the birthday T-shirt. The rest of the family dresses around them, not the other way around.
  2. Their spot. Offer two or three places near your hotel, such as the waterline, a palm or a garden path, and let them pick. Our guide to letting the kids pick the session location shows how to offer the choice without giving up good light.
  3. Their own portraits. The session opens with a short set of the birthday kid alone, at their spot, while they are fresh. Siblings and cousins wait behind us, ready to cheer.
  4. Then the family. Each parent with the birthday kid, then siblings, cousins and grandparents, and last of all everyone together, with the birthday kid in the middle.
Tell us at booking

Your child's age and birthday date, what they plan to wear, the spot they picked, who is joining and whether there will be a cake. We plan the order of the session around the answers.

Birthday Photo Ideas by Age

A five-year-old and a thirteen-year-old want very different photos. Pick two or three ideas for your child's age.

Turning 5: Keep It a Game

At five, the birthday is the whole personality. Keep their part short and playful, and let the energy do the work.

  • Five fingers up. Ask how old they are and photograph the answer, usually shouted.
  • The race. They run for the foam and the whole family chases.
  • The crown. A crown, a sash or a birthday badge from home, if they love it.
  • The wish. Whispering their birthday wish in a grandparent's ear.

Turning 10: Double Digits

Ten feels big, and 10th birthday trip photos should look like a ten-year-old, not a little kid. Give them ideas with some status.

  • The number in the sand. Write 10, or their name, in the wet sand and photograph them beside it before a wave takes it.
  • They direct one frame. They decide how the whole family poses for one photo, and everyone has to do it. Expect something silly, and keep it.
  • Their thing. Looking for birthday beach photoshoot ideas for a girl who loves a dress? A twirl at the waterline never gets old. For a kid who lives in a soccer jersey, bring the jersey and the ball.
  • The middle of the cousins. Everyone in a line by height, and the birthday kid in the center, whatever their height.

Turning 13: Hand Over Control

A thirteen-year-old may care more about who sees the photos than about the cake. The best birthday present is control.

  • Their outfit. Within the family palette, theirs to choose, with no commentary in front of the cousins.
  • Fewer props. Skip the crown and the number unless they ask for them.
  • Portraits that look like them. Walking, laughing or looking away at the spot they chose: photos they would post themselves.
  • First pick of the preview. Let them choose the first photo you share.

Cake, Balloons and the Hotel's Rules

Our collections cover the photography: the time, the edited photos and the private gallery. The cake, the candles and any decoration are yours to bring, and your hotel decides what can be set up on its lawns and beach area.

  • Ask the hotel before the trip. Can you bring a cake onto the beach or a lawn, light candles or put up a banner, and where? Rules vary from hotel to hotel, and it is worth asking whether the kitchen can make a small cake.
  • Keep it small. A small cake on a plate and something reusable from home, such as a fabric banner or a wooden number, rather than balloons, which can slip away and end up in the sea. Skip confetti and glitter too: they are almost impossible to get out of the sand.
  • Cake last. Frosting, sand and clean outfits do not mix, so the cake and candles close the session.
  • The outside photographer fee. Some hotels charge a fee for an outside photographer, which you pay to the hotel. We confirm your hotel's rule before the session.
What to bring

The outfit, or two if you book The Story or a larger collection; the crown or number if your child wants one; the cake, a plate, candles and a lighter; a towel, wipes and a change of clothes for after the cake.

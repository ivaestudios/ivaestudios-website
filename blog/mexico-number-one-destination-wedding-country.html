In short

Yes, by a clear margin: The Knot Worldwide's 2026 Real Weddings Study of 10,474 US couples found Mexico took 34 percent of international destination weddings, ahead of all of Europe at 26 percent and the Caribbean at 21 percent. Most are small: 58.7 percent of destination weddings had fewer than 50 guests.

When somebody asks where Americans get married abroad, there is now a clean answer. The Knot Worldwide's 2026 Real Weddings Study surveyed 10,474 couples married in the United States, and among those who married internationally, Mexico took 34 percent of destination weddings. Europe took 26 percent. The Caribbean took 21 percent. Beach resorts accounted for 41.3 percent of destination venue share, and 58.7 percent of destination weddings had fewer than 50 guests. We photograph on the receiving end of those numbers every week from Cancún, so this is not a data summary for its own sake. It is what each figure actually changes about how a day gets planned, what it costs, and how it has to be photographed.

The Headline Numbers, and Who Collected Them

Take the ordering seriously for a moment, because it is more striking than it first looks. Europe is an entire continent with dozens of wedding markets in it. The Caribbean is a region of dozens of countries and territories. Mexico is one country, and it still outdraws both of them individually for US couples marrying abroad.

Two more figures from the same study do most of the practical work. Beach resorts made up 41.3 percent of destination venue share, which means roughly four in ten destination couples are marrying on the same kind of property with the same set of physical conditions. And 58.7 percent of destination weddings had fewer than 50 guests, which is the single number that most changes how a day is built.

Mexico Versus Europe Versus the Caribbean

Why Mexico wins is not mysterious once you have watched a few hundred couples plan it. Flights: Cancún International is one of the busiest airports in Latin America with direct service from dozens of US and Canadian cities, so most guests can get here without a connection. Entry: no visa required for US and Canadian tourists. Currency: the peso against the dollar. Infrastructure: an entire coast of resorts built specifically for this, with in house wedding coordinators who work in English and run several weddings a week.

Europe's constraints are structural rather than aesthetic. Airfare is higher, jetlag costs guests a day at each end, venues price in euros, and, most importantly, a European venue rarely bundles accommodation, catering and ceremony the way a Mexican resort does. You are assembling a wedding from separate vendors in a country you do not live in, in a language you may not speak.

The Caribbean has comparable water and often worse flight connectivity, higher per night accommodation cost, and an extra leg for guests coming from inland US cities. Several islands are wonderful for a wedding of twenty. Very few are easy for a wedding of eighty.

The honest counterpoint, because there is one: Mexico's Caribbean coast gets sargassum roughly April through October, and Europe does not. If the pristine beach look matters more than anything else and your dates fall in June, the answer is not another country. It is Los Cabos on the Pacific side, or an inland Yucatán hacienda, or a property with a sheltered lee side beach such as Playa Mujeres or Isla Mujeres.

Why Destination Weddings Average 50 to 70 Guests

The 58.7 percent under fifty figure is the most consequential number in the study, and the mechanism behind it is simple. Asking somebody to buy a flight and three nights of accommodation is a filter. A two hundred name hometown list, full of colleagues and second cousins and your parents' neighbours, becomes sixty real names, and those sixty are the people who genuinely wanted to be there.

What that does to the day is significant and almost entirely good. Everyone can eat at one long table. The ceremony can be short because nobody needs a microphone for a crowd. The reception behaves like a dinner party rather than a production. And the couple actually speaks to every single guest, which at a two hundred person wedding is arithmetically impossible.

What it does to the photography is a change of emphasis. With forty guests we can photograph every person properly and the gallery ends up being about the people rather than about the setup. There are fewer set piece formal groupings and more of the room. It is a more documentary day by nature.

The counter case exists and deserves saying: a 150 guest destination wedding is a different animal with a longer timeline, a bigger team and a real logistics problem in getting everyone from ceremony to cocktails to dinner. Those weddings happen on this coast every week. They are just the minority of the market, and they should be staffed and timed accordingly.

Average Spend, Per-Guest Cost and Vendor Count

The first thing to say is that a destination wedding is not automatically more expensive than a wedding at home, and couples are routinely surprised by this. Guest count is the single largest driver of total wedding cost, and destination guest counts are smaller. The Knot's headline US average spend has sat in the low thirty thousands of dollars for several years running, and plenty of destination weddings land under it precisely because sixty people cost less to feed than two hundred.

The structure of the spend changes more than the total. At a hometown wedding you pay per plate and per head for almost everything. At an all inclusive resort, your guests' food and drink are already inside the room rate they are paying themselves, so the couple's budget shifts toward the ceremony setup, decor and flowers, the private venue fee for a reception space, and the vendors you choose to bring in from outside.

Vendor count drops sharply. A hometown wedding assembles a venue, a caterer, a bar service, rentals, a cake, a DJ, a florist and a coordinator as separate contracts. A resort bundles most of that into a package with an in house coordinator, and couples then hire from outside only for the things they care about most, which in practice is almost always photography, sometimes flowers, and sometimes music.

Now the trap, stated plainly because it costs couples real money: many resorts charge an outside vendor fee to bring in a photographer who is not on their in house list. The amount varies enormously between properties, some waive it above a certain number of room nights booked, and some restrict outside photographers to certain areas of the property. Ask about it before you sign the venue contract, not after you have booked a photographer. We tell couples the fee for their property when we know it, and there is a longer breakdown in our guide to hidden costs in all-inclusive wedding packages.

"Guest count is the budget. Everything else is a rounding error by comparison, which is why the smallest weddings on this coast are often the most beautifully produced ones."

Beach Resorts at 41.3%: What That Means for Your Photos

If four in ten destination weddings happen at a beach resort, four in ten couples inherit an identical set of physical conditions, and they are worth knowing before you pick a ceremony time.

Overhead sun. Between roughly eleven and three on this coast the sun is almost directly overhead. It flattens skin tones, blows out the sky and drops hard shadows into eye sockets. Every good beach wedding timeline here is built backwards from a late afternoon ceremony and a golden hour portrait block, and that is a scheduling decision you make months in advance with your coordinator.

Direction. Cancún's Hotel Zone beach faces east, which means the sun rises out of the sea and sets behind the hotels. Couples arrive expecting a sunset over the water and it is simply not geographically available on that beach. What you get instead is a spectacular sunrise and a soft, warm, even light on the water at the end of the day. Los Cabos faces south and west and does give you the sun sitting on the ocean.

Wind and sand. A beach resort is by definition exposed. Plan the hair, bring pins, and expect a veil to have opinions.

A covered plan. A beach ceremony needs a named indoor or covered alternative written down before the week arrives, not decided at four in the afternoon on the day.

That block sits exactly where the best light of the day is, so it has to be written into the timeline, timed, and staffed with a designated name caller from each family. If it is not, it will quietly eat your golden hour portraits, and that is the most common way a beautiful wedding ends up with a thin set of couple photographs.

If your priority is guests having an easy trip and everything being close together, Cancún. If you want visual range inside one property, from beach to jungle to water channels, the Riviera Maya, and Mayakoba in particular. If you want a wedding that looks nothing like a resort wedding and you accept the operational cost of that, Tulum. If you want dry season certainty and the sun setting on the ocean, Los Cabos.

Common Questions About Destination Weddings in Mexico

Which country hosts the most destination weddings for US couples?

Mexico. In The Knot Worldwide's 2026 Real Weddings Study, which surveyed 10,474 US couples, Mexico accounted for 34 percent of international destination weddings, ahead of Europe at 26 percent and the Caribbean at 21 percent. Mexico is a single country outdrawing an entire continent and an entire region.

How many guests does a destination wedding usually have?

Fewer than most people expect. The same study found 58.7 percent of destination weddings had under 50 guests, and in our own bookings on this coast the typical range is about 50 to 70. Asking guests to buy a flight and three nights filters a 200 name hometown list down to the people who will genuinely come.

Is a destination wedding cheaper than a wedding at home?

Often, and the reason is guest count rather than location. Guest count is the largest single driver of wedding cost, and destination guest lists are smaller. The structure also shifts: at an all inclusive resort your guests' food and drink sit inside their own room rate, so your spend moves toward the ceremony setup, decor, a private reception venue fee and the vendors you choose to bring in. Ask about the outside vendor fee before you sign anything.

Where in Mexico do most destination weddings happen?

The Caribbean coast of Quintana Roo takes the largest share: Cancún's Hotel Zone, Playa Mujeres, the Riviera Maya from Puerto Morelos down through Playa del Carmen and Mayakoba to Akumal, and Tulum. Los Cabos on the Pacific side is the second major hub, with a drier climate, sunset over water and a longer flight from the US east coast.

Do we need two photographers for a destination wedding?

It depends on guest count and on geography, not on prestige. Under about 30 guests one photographer covers the day comfortably. Between 30 and 60 a second is worth it only if the two getting-ready suites are far apart and happening at the same time. Above about 60 guests, or with a ceremony and reception in different parts of a large property, two is the honest answer.

When is the best time of year for a Mexico destination wedding?

On the Caribbean coast, November through April is the dry season with the most reliable weather and the least sargassum, and it is also the most expensive and the most booked. May and June are hot and can bring sargassum. September and October are the wettest months and the best value. Los Cabos runs on a different calendar, with its own hot summer and a beautiful autumn.

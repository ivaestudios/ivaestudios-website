In short

The industry standard for a full wedding gallery is four to eight weeks, ten to twelve in peak season. IVAE Studios delivers the complete edited 4K gallery in one to three business days, with a 15 to 30 frame sneak peek inside 24 hours, by capping the calendar at twelve weddings a year.

The question arrives in almost every consultation, usually right after the one about price: when do we actually get the photos? For most of the wedding industry the honest answer is four to eight weeks. Ours is one to three business days. That gap is not a slogan, it is a workflow decision the studio made when it opened in 2023, and it changes what the week after a destination wedding feels like. This is what the standard really is, why turnaround matters more when you flew in for the wedding, and how a gallery that can run past nine hundred images gets finished in the time most studios take to reply to an email.

What the Industry Standard Actually Is

Open ten wedding photography contracts and you will read some version of the same clause: full gallery delivered four to eight weeks after the wedding date. Plenty of studios write ten to twelve weeks for weddings booked in peak season, and a sneak peek of a handful of frames is usually promised somewhere inside the first week. Nobody is hiding anything. That window is simply what a traditional wedding workflow costs in time.

What has shifted is what couples expect. Imagen's 2026 delivery research found that 78 percent of clients expect a sneak peek within 24 hours of the wedding, and that studios delivering the full gallery in under two weeks see materially higher referral rates, on the order of 40 percent more, than studios sitting in the traditional window. The expectation moved because everything else in a couple's life moved. Guests posted their phone photos before the reception ended. Waiting six weeks for the real ones now feels like waiting for a fax.

It is worth being fair about why the standard exists. A photographer who books thirty five weddings a year in a temperate market has a genuine backlog in August. Culling four thousand frames by hand takes days. Studios that outsource editing add a round trip of a week or more to an overseas team, plus a review pass. None of that is laziness. It is the arithmetic of a volume business, and it is exactly the arithmetic we chose not to run.

The number behind the number

We cap the studio at twelve weddings a year. That single constraint is what makes a one to three business day turnaround possible, because there is never a queue in front of your wedding. Any studio can promise speed. Only a studio that limits its own calendar can keep the promise in April, when this coast is at its busiest.

Why Speed Matters More at a Destination Wedding

At a hometown wedding a six week wait is an inconvenience. At a destination wedding it is a different problem, because the people in the photographs have scattered across three countries by Tuesday. Your parents flew back to Ohio. Your closest friends spent real money and real vacation days to stand on a beach in Quintana Roo for you, and the window in which they are still living inside the weekend is about seven days long. Photos that land inside that window get looked at together. Photos that land in October get looked at alone.

There is a practical layer under the sentimental one. If a family formal got missed, if an aunt was blocked in the group shot, if the ring detail never happened because the ceremony started twenty minutes early, you want to know while everybody is still on the same peninsula. Twice in the last two years a couple has spotted a gap in the gallery on day two and we have simply arranged a short session before their flight. That recovery is impossible six weeks later.

Fast delivery also feeds the day-after session, which is common on this coast. Couples who see the wedding day gallery on Tuesday walk into a Wednesday sunrise session at a Riviera Maya beach knowing what they loved and what they want more of. That feedback loop is worth more than any shot list we could write in advance.

How a 900-Image Gallery Gets Delivered in Three Days

The speed is engineered at the front of the process, not the back. Four things do the work.

First, the calendar. Twelve weddings a year means your wedding is never third in a queue. Second, the shooting discipline. We plan every wedding around golden hour, roughly the 45 minutes before sunset on this coast, and we hold exposure and white balance consistent in camera across the ceremony and portraits. A consistent capture turns editing into a pass rather than a rescue operation, and that difference alone is worth days. Third, the cull happens the same night, on location, while we still remember which frame was the one. Fourth, editing happens in house. Nothing leaves the studio, so nothing waits on a time zone.

Then the gallery is built and uploaded to our own infrastructure. A full wedding at 4K is a large upload, which is the honest reason we quote business days and not hours. A Saturday wedding is typically live Tuesday or Wednesday. A Sunday wedding with a reception that ran past one in the morning may take the full three days. Mexican public holidays shift it. We tell couples the expected day in writing at the end of the wedding night, and if anything slips we say so before the deadline rather than after.

"Fast delivery is not a faster edit. It is a smaller calendar, a disciplined capture and a cull that happens the same night."

What Arrives First: the 24-Hour Sneak Peek

Before the full gallery, you get a short set, usually fifteen to thirty frames, inside 24 hours. It is not a random handful. We choose it as a compressed version of the day: the getting ready moment that set the tone, the walk down the aisle, the reaction on the other side, one wide frame that shows the water and the light, one detail, one portrait, one honest reception frame with everyone in it.

That set is what you send to the group chat, what your mother forwards to relatives who could not travel, and what you post if you want to post. It exists precisely because of the 78 percent figure above. The appetite for the wedding is highest the morning after, and a photographer who arrives to that moment empty handed has left something on the table for the couple, not just for themselves.

What the 4K Private Gallery Includes

The full delivery is a private, password protected gallery at 4K resolution. You get the complete edited set, not a curated slice of it. Every image is downloadable at full size, individually or in bulk. There is a favourites tool so you can flag album selections without emailing file numbers back and forth. There is a share link you can pass to family, and guests can view and download without creating an account, which matters when your grandmother is the one clicking.

It is built mobile first, because that is how nearly everyone opens it. The first thing most couples do is scroll the whole thing on a phone in an airport, and the gallery is designed for exactly that moment rather than for a desktop nobody is sitting at.

On longevity we will be plain. Your gallery stays live and we do not take one down without telling you first. Even so, we ask every couple to download the full resolution originals to their own drive or cloud storage within the first month. A gallery is a delivery tool. It is not an archive, and the only copy of your wedding that is genuinely safe is the one you hold yourself.

Speed Versus Care: What We Refuse to Rush

A turnaround promise is only worth something if the studio is willing to say what it will not compress, so here is our list.

We do not hand over an unculled take. Delivering everything the shutter recorded is fast and it is also a way of pushing the editing work onto the couple. We do not skip the hand finish on the frames that carry the day, the ones that end up printed and on a wall. We do not rush the reception edit just because it is the last folder, since low light coverage is where careless processing shows most.

Some things sit outside the three day window by design and we say so up front. Deep retouching requests, removing a stray guest from the background of a portrait or detailed skin work on a specific image, are handled after delivery and take longer. Albums and fine art prints run on their own production timeline through the lab, measured in weeks, not days. Video, when a couple hires a separate videographer, is a completely different craft with its own schedule and we never quote on their behalf.

And if a wedding runs to two in the morning with fourteen hours of coverage, we will tell you it is a three day gallery instead of a two day one. Quoting the same number regardless of the day would be marketing, not a promise.

Questions to Ask a Photographer Before You Book

Whether or not you work with us, these five questions separate a real delivery commitment from a comfortable one. Ask for the answers in writing, in the contract, not in a chat message.

One: what is the delivery window, and is it calendar days or business days? A studio quoting six weeks and a studio quoting thirty business days are quoting the same thing in different clothes. Two: how many weddings do you book in the month around my date? This is the question that predicts everything, because turnaround collapses under volume. Three: is editing done in house or outsourced? Outsourced work adds a round trip and a review pass. Four: what exactly is in the sneak peek and when does it land? Five: what happens if you fall behind? A studio with a real answer has thought about it. A studio that has never fallen behind enough to have a policy has usually never been tested in April.

One more, specific to Mexico. Ask who owns the gallery infrastructure. Some studios deliver through a third party platform that expires the link after ninety days and then charges to restore it. Knowing that before you sign is easier than discovering it the week you finally have time to download everything.

We photograph across Cancún, Playa Mujeres, Isla Mujeres, Playa del Carmen, Tulum, Mayakoba and Los Cabos, and the turnaround is the same at every one of them. Frequently Asked Questions

How long do wedding photos usually take to arrive?

Across the wedding industry the contracted standard is four to eight weeks, and in peak season many studios quote ten to twelve. A sneak peek of a handful of frames usually lands somewhere in the first week. IVAE Studios delivers the complete gallery in one to three business days, with the sneak peek inside 24 hours.

Does fast delivery mean fewer edits or lower quality?

No. Speed comes from the front of the process rather than the back: a hard cap of twelve weddings a year so there is no backlog, golden hour scheduling so exposure and white balance are consistent in camera, and a same-night cull. The colour grade and the hand finish on the frames that matter still happen. What we do not do is compress those steps to hit a number.

What is included in the private 4K gallery?

The full edited set at 4K resolution, downloadable at full size, viewable on phone, a favourites tool so you can flag album selections, and a password-protected share link you can send to family. Guests can view and download without an account of their own.

How long does the gallery stay online?

Your gallery stays live and we never take one down without telling you first. Even so, we ask every couple to download the full-resolution originals to their own drive or cloud storage in the first month. A gallery is a delivery tool, not an archive, and the only copy that is truly safe is the one you hold.

How many images come from a full wedding day?

It depends on coverage hours and guest count. A ceremony plus reception commonly lands in the several hundred range, and a full day with getting ready, first look, ceremony, portraits and a long reception can pass nine hundred edited frames. We deliver the full edited set rather than a curated fraction of it.

What should I ask a photographer about turnaround before booking?

Ask for the delivery window in writing, ask whether it is calendar days or business days, ask how many weddings they book in the month around your date, ask whether editing is done in house or outsourced, and ask what happens to your date if they fall behind. Vague answers to those five questions are the warning sign, not the number itself.

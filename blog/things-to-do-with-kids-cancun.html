Things to Do in Cancun With Kids, and the Photo Stops

In short

From the easiest outing for little ones to full days for older kids: resort beach mornings, Isla Mujeres by ferry, a lagoon sunset, the aquarium, El Rey and the Maya Museum, then cenotes, eco-parks and the big ruins. Take phone photos on the way, and book your family portrait on its own, at sunrise or in the last light.

Cancun is easy with children once you know which outings suit which ages. Here are the family activities in and near Cancun we recommend, ranked from babies to older kids, with the time to plan, the best hour and the cost. Third-party facts were checked on official or operator pages on November 14, 2026.

The Ranking at a Glance

  1. Your resort beach, early: every age.
  2. Isla Mujeres by ferry: every age.
  3. A Nichupté Lagoon sunset: every age.
  4. The aquarium at La Isla: toddlers and up.
  5. The El Rey ruins: about 4 and up.
  6. The Maya Museum and San Miguelito: about 5 and up.
  7. A cenote: matched to the child.
  8. An eco-park day: about 5 and up.
  9. Big ruins and the Tren Maya: kids who travel well.

Ages are our suggestion, unless an operator sets a rule.

For Every Age, Babies Included

1. Your Resort Beach, Early

Best for every age Plan on 2 to 3 hours Best hour first light to mid-morning Cost no ticket

With children under five, the best outing is usually the sand outside your room: no transfer, no car seat, shade and snacks close by. Go early, save midday for naps (see sun safety for kids in Cancun) and check the flag each morning with Cancun beach conditions today. If your hotel's water is rough, our guide to the safest Hotel Zone beach for kids compares calmer stretches.

2. Isla Mujeres by Ferry

Best for every age Plan on half a day or more Best hour an early ferry Cost 580 pesos round trip per adult

The ferry ride is half the fun. Ultramar sails from Puerto Juárez, Playa Tortugas and Playa Caracol, and its fares page lists the same standard fares from all three: 290 Mexican pesos one way or 580 round trip for adults, and 220 or 440 for children. Babies under one ride free with a boarding pass, children over 1.20 m pay the adult fare, Playa Caracol adds a dock fee, and prices can change without notice. For beaches and timing, see our Isla Mujeres family photo session guide or Cozumel or Isla Mujeres with kids.

Short Outings for Sunsets and Rain

3. A Sunset by Nichupté Lagoon

Best for every age Plan on the last half hour of daylight Best hour sunset Cost free to watch

The Caribbean side of the Hotel Zone faces east, so the sun sets over Nichupté Lagoon, across the boulevard. Half an hour there is a short, free outing that ends a beach day well and makes a lovely phone photo. Bring repellent for dusk. Our Nichupté Lagoon sunset guide has the spots.

4. The Aquarium at La Isla

Best for toddlers and up Plan on 1 to 2 hours Best hour midday or a rainy afternoon Cost 15.99 USD per person

When a shower rolls through, the Interactive Aquarium Cancun, at La Isla Shopping Village (km 12.5), makes a good short stop. Delphinus's Cancun page lists aquarium admission at 15.99 USD per person, with hours of 10:00 am to 5:00 pm, Monday to Sunday. For showers, see rain in Cancun by month.

Ages Four and Up: Maya History in the Hotel Zone

5. The El Rey Archaeological Zone

Best for about 4 and up Plan on 30 to 50 minutes Best hour the 8:00 am opening Cost 145 pesos per foreign visitor, under 13 free

Children can see real Maya history without leaving the Hotel Zone, and El Rey, at km 18, is small enough for short legs. INAH lists it as open daily from 8:00 am to 5:00 pm (last entry 4:30 pm), at 145 pesos for foreign visitors and 80 for Mexican nationals, with parking and toilets, and an INAH bulletin says a visit takes 30 to 50 minutes. Mexico's federal fee law (article 288) exempts children under 13 and adults over 60 from this fee and from the Maya Museum's. Go at opening, before the heat, and snack first, since food is not allowed in.

6. The Maya Museum and San Miguelito

Best for about 5 and up Plan on 2 hours or more Best hour the 9:00 am opening Cost 210 pesos per foreign visitor, under 13 free

A little further north, at km 16.5, INAH's visitor page lists the Museo Maya de Cancun as open Tuesday to Sunday from 9:00 am to 5:00 pm (last entry 4:30 pm), at 210 pesos for foreign visitors and 105 for Mexican nationals, with the San Miguelito archaeological site included. The same bulletin suggests about two hours for the museum. Food cannot come in, but INAH lists a snack bar. For bigger sites farther out, see our guide to Mayan ruins near Cancun with kids.

Older Kids: Cenotes, Parks and Big Ruins

The bigger Cancun excursions for families each deserve a day of their own.

7. A Cenote

Best for matched to the child Plan on half a day Best hour mid-morning Cost by cenote

Cenotes are freshwater sinkholes, and the right one depends on the child. As our guide to a cenote family photoshoot with kids explains, open-air cenotes with shallow ledges suit younger children, and cave cenotes suit confident swimmers of about eight and up. Go mid-morning, and check each cenote's rules and fees before you drive.

8. An Eco-Park Day

Best for about 5 and up Plan on a full day Best hour opening time Cost by park

The Xcaret group's parks on the Riviera Maya are full days. Xcaret's FAQ says children up to 4 enter free and children's tickets, for ages 5 to 11, cost 25% less than the adult ticket; Xplor's FAQ sets a minimum age of 5 for its activities, with height or weight limits on some. Our guide to Xcaret parks with kids matches each park to an age.

9. Big Ruins and the Tren Maya

Best for kids who travel well Plan on a full day

Sites farther out, from Tulum to Chichen Itza, mean long, hot days, so save them for children who travel well and check each site's status first. See our guide to Tren Maya day trips with kids and our 7-day Cancun family itinerary.

Photo Stops, and Where Cameras Have Rules

Most outings here make good phone photos, especially the morning beach, the ferry deck, the lagoon at sunset and the Cancun letters above Playa Delfines, where our Cancun letters guide helps you beat the queue. See also Cancun photo spots and phone photo tips for parents. Professional cameras are another matter:

  • INAH sites. Photography or filming with special or professional equipment at INAH zones, monuments and museums needs an authorization (procedure INAH 01-001), and the Maya Museum lists an extra fee for professional cameras and no flash (see INAH photo permits).
  • Eco-parks. Xplor's photo pass page says its activities need your hands free, so carrying a camera or phone will not be possible, and the parks sell their own photo pass, which we compare with a private family session.
  • Hotels. Some charge a fee for an outside photographer, which you pay to the hotel. We confirm your hotel's rule first.

So enjoy the outings with your phone and book the portrait on its own, on your resort beach or a quiet stretch nearby, at sunrise or in the last light. If your children have opinions, let them pick the location.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best things to do in Cancun with toddlers?

Short, close outings: your resort beach in the morning, the aquarium at La Isla on a rainy afternoon and a Nichupté Lagoon sunset. Isla Mujeres works too, and on Ultramar babies under one ride free with a boarding pass.

How much is the ferry to Isla Mujeres for a family?

Ultramar lists 290 pesos one way or 580 round trip per adult, and 220 or 440 per child, from Puerto Juárez, Playa Tortugas or Playa Caracol. Babies under one ride free with a boarding pass, children over 1.20 m pay the adult fare, and Playa Caracol adds a dock fee (checked November 14, 2026).

Are there Mayan ruins inside Cancun?

Yes, two in the Hotel Zone, according to INAH (checked November 14, 2026): San Miguelito, reached through the Maya Museum at km 16.5 (Tuesday to Sunday, 210 pesos for foreign visitors), and El Rey at km 18 (daily, 145 pesos for foreign visitors). Under Mexico's federal fee law, children under 13 do not pay.

Can a photographer take our family portrait at the ruins or the eco-parks?

It is harder there: photography with professional equipment at INAH sites needs an authorization (procedure INAH 01-001), and Xplor says carrying a camera or phone during its activities will not be possible. We suggest phone photos there and a portrait on your resort beach at sunrise or in the last light.

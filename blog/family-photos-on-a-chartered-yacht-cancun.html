A private catamaran, the whole family on deck, the grandchildren at the rail and turquoise water behind everyone. If the reunion's big day out is a yacht charter in Cancun, the family photos can happen on board, whether you are five people or three generations of sixteen.

Your Charter, Our Session

If you rent a yacht in Cancun for the reunion, the charter is yours: you choose the operator, the boat and the route, and the contract is between you and them. We bring the session, priced as one of our six collections.

Choose an operator with its paperwork in order. Under Mexico's Navigation and Maritime Commerce Law, offering nautical tourism on recreational boats requires a Port Captaincy permit (article 42), and the law's regulation requires operators to keep copies of that permit, the boat's safety certificate and the passenger insurance on public view (article 647). Ask about them before any private yacht charter in Cancun.

Planning a proposal instead? See our yacht proposal guide; for a slow boat through a resort's canals, photos on a boat at Rosewood Mayakoba.

Questions for the Operator

When you compare Cancun luxury yacht rentals, compare them on these points, not only on length and price, and get the answers in writing:

  1. Capacity, with us counted. The boat must display its maximum number of passengers and crew (article 650), and anyone aboard who is not the captain, the crew or someone working for the boat is a passenger (article 10). Ask us how many of the team will board, and make sure the number covers everyone.
  2. Life jackets in your children's sizes. Wearing them is mandatory, except on boats whose safety certificate sets them only as a precaution in case of risk (article 653). Either way, check each child's fit at the dock.
  3. Who may board. Article 653 also rules out pregnant women, people who report a history of heart, neurological or nervous conditions and anyone who has taken drugs or drunk alcohol, and for services other than towed rides its text allows minors only from 10 to 16, with an adult. Ask in writing how the operator applies this to your youngest children, a pregnant guest or a grandparent with a heart condition.
  4. Shade where grandparents and little ones can sit between frames.
  5. A calm stop: where the boat anchors or stops, and for how long.
  6. The marina and the times: the departure point, the boarding time and the return time.
  7. A photographer on board. Whether the operator allows an outside photographer and charges anything for one. That is the operator's rule, so confirm it before you book.
  8. The weather call. Who decides whether the trip goes ahead, when you will hear, and what happens to your payment if it is called off.

When to Shoot on the Water

The best yacht family photos come early: in the first calm stretch after the marina, before anyone swims, while hair is dry and outfits look fresh. The second window is the stop, at anchor or on the sand of a beach stop, where everyone can hold still for the big frame.

At midday the sun is high: faces fall into shadow under hats, eyes squint and a white deck throws glare. The first hours after sunrise and the last of the afternoon are softer. For an afternoon trip, ask when the boat must be back: the regulation allows nautical tourism in daylight, and tours after dark only with the Port Captaincy's prior authorization (article 653). Our sunset and golden hour times by month help you pick the departure.

Online booking has set start times; for a charter, arrange the time with us on WhatsApp.

Yacht Photoshoot Ideas That Work on Deck

A luxury yacht photoshoot depends less on the size of the boat than on a calm stop, good light and a family that is not rushed. Every idea below is photographed from the boat or the sand at the stop:

  • The whole group at the bow. At anchor or when the captain slows down, and only where the crew says people may stand. Grandparents sit in the middle, children in front.
  • Children at the rail, life jackets on. A child in a bright vest pointing at the water, a parent beside them, is the frame, not something to hide.
  • Grandparents in the shade. Seated under the awning, grandchildren leaning in. Open shade is kind to faces.
  • Each couple, a minute each. At the stern with open sea behind them, so every household leaves with its own portrait.
  • The jump, only if the crew allows it. At anchor, where the crew says swimming is safe, photographed from the deck after the dry-hair frames.

Between them come the moments nobody poses for: the walk down the dock, a hand on a shoulder. For arranging 10 to 16 people, see our family photo tips for large groups and our multigenerational family photo ideas.

Wind, Spray and Seasickness

Turn faces into the wind so hair blows back, not across the eyes, and bring a hair tie. Hats come off for the frames. Linen and cotton behave better than thin fabrics that lift and cling; our color ideas for a large family photoshoot work on a boat too. Ask the operator about shoes on deck.

Seasickness deserves a plan, especially with children. The CDC Yellow Book notes that children under 2 rarely get motion sickness and that susceptibility peaks between ages 7 and 12. Its advice: look at the horizon, keep away from spots of maximum motion such as the bow, avoid reading and alcohol, and arrive rested. Anyone who feels queasy can sit out a frame and join the next one. Before giving a child motion sickness medicine, talk to your pediatrician; the CDC advises caution with these medicines in children.

If the Sea Says No

The weather decides whether a charter leaves. The regulation allows nautical tourism only when the weather is suitable and the wind at sea level does not exceed 25 kilometers per hour (article 653), and the law fines a captain who puts to sea when the Port Captaincy has forbidden departures for bad weather or a forecast of it (article 327).

Our sessions have no deposit and free cancellation until one day before: follow the forecast with the operator, and if it looks doubtful by then, cancel at no cost or ask us to move the session to the beach. Your resort's beach at the same hour is the simplest plan B; some hotels charge a fee for outside photographers, paid to the hotel, and we confirm your hotel's rule beforehand. If the trip is called off on the day itself, message us on WhatsApp right away. Our family reunion photo ideas for a Cancun beach session work there too.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should we ask the charter operator before booking?

Ask how many passengers the boat may carry with the photographer counted, whether it has life jackets in your children's sizes and shade, how it applies Mexico's rules on minors, pregnancy and health, whether an outside photographer may board and at what fee, and who decides if the weather cancels the trip.

Does the photographer count toward the boat's capacity?

Yes. Mexico's navigation regulation counts as a passenger anyone aboard who is not the captain, the crew or someone working for the boat, and the boat must display its maximum number of passengers and crew. Ask us how many of the team will board.

When is the best time for family photos on a boat?

Early in the trip, in the first calm stretch after the marina and before anyone swims, and again at anchor or at a beach stop. The first hours after sunrise and the last of the afternoon give softer light than midday. Arrange the session time with us on WhatsApp, around the operator's schedule.

What if the charter is cancelled because of the weather?

Our sessions have no deposit and free cancellation until one day before, so follow the forecast with the operator as the date approaches; if it looks doubtful before then, cancel at no cost or ask us to move the session to the beach. If the trip is called off on the day itself, message us on WhatsApp right away.

Is there a per-person charge for a big family on a boat?

No. Our sessions have no limit on people and no per-person charge; the six collections run from 295 to 895 USD. For groups of up to 16, we can arrange a full van to the marina; ask for its price when you write.

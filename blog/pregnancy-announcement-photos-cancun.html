Some trips come with news. You are a few weeks along, the family back home doesn't know yet, and you are about to spend a few days by the sea with the people who will be happiest to hear it. Beach pregnancy announcement photos need less than you might think: one or two small things from home, an older sibling with a role to play, and the family session you were already planning.

Before There Is a Bump, Let the Details Speak

An early announcement has one challenge a maternity session doesn't: there is nothing to see yet. So the photograph says it another way, with an object that reads at a glance, even on a phone screen, and a face that does the rest. A big sister's grin. A grandmother's hand flying to her mouth.

That is why the announcement folds so easily into an ordinary family session. Most of the time goes to the family portraits you would want anyway, and the announcement is a handful of frames within it, best placed early if a young child has a part, while patience is still high.

If you are further along and want to reveal the baby's sex, that is a different session with its own planning, covered in our gender reveal photoshoot guide. Once there is a bump to photograph, our maternity photoshoot guide takes over, and if the trip is just the two of you, babymoon photography is the closer fit.

Announcement Ideas to Pack From Home

The details are personal, so they travel in your suitcase, and the pregnancy announcement ideas that work best on vacation are simple. Choose one or two: with more than that in the frame, it stops reading as news.

  • A printed sonogram. Paper rather than a phone screen, which catches glare in bright light. A print can rest in a small hand, in a parent's palm or on the sand.
  • Tiny shoes. One small pair lined up with everyone's bare feet at the waterline. It needs no words at all.
  • A sign or a letter board. Short enough to read in a second, such as the due month or the older child's new title. Bring it finished and ready to hold.
  • A big sibling shirt. “Big sister” or “Big brother” on a T-shirt, worn all session or hidden under an open shirt until the reveal.
  • Nothing at all. The due month written in the wet sand with a stick, photographed before the next wave reaches it.

Dress the family in soft, plain tones, so the sonogram, the sign or the shirt is the brightest thing in the picture.

The Big Sibling's Reveal

A pregnancy announcement with siblings gives an older brother or sister the best job of the day, and a big sister or big brother photo on the beach needs no caption. The role you give them depends on what they already know.

  • Already in on the secret. They hold the sonogram or the shoes, or open their shirt to show the new title. You get a proud face, and they get to feel part of the news.
  • Finding out on the beach. If your child doesn't know yet, the session can be the moment you tell them. Decide beforehand who says it and how, and tell us, so the camera is ready when the words land. A gasp, a giggle or a puzzled frown: all three are real, and all three belong in the album.
  • Too young to understand. A toddler can still wear the shirt or sit beside the tiny shoes. The sign says the words; the child only has to be themselves.

The Grandparents' Reaction

The grandparents-to-be may give you the photograph you end up treasuring most, and how you capture it depends on where they are.

If they are on the trip, you can announce the pregnancy to family in person, during the session itself. Hand them the sonogram, let the big sibling tell them, or simply turn the sign around. Tell us beforehand who should stand where, so we are watching the right faces when the news lands. A real reaction only happens once.

If they are back home, the photograph becomes the announcement, and when it arrives matters as much as the frame itself.

Traveling to Cancun While Pregnant

Whether this trip suits your pregnancy is a question for your doctor or midwife; we are photographers. Here is where the official guidance lives, as we read it on November 14, 2026:

  • The CDC's Mexico page lists Zika among the diseases in Mexico that are spread by bugs and cannot be prevented with a vaccine, notes that an infected pregnant woman can spread it to her unborn baby, and advises preventing bug bites.
  • The CDC's travel notices page listed no Zika notice for Mexico; its only Zika notice was for Bali, Indonesia.
  • The CDC's Zika travel page sets out its recommendations for pregnant travelers, and for people planning a pregnancy, by type of destination.
  • For Canadian families, the Government of Canada's Mexico travel advice says Zika virus is a risk in the country, and that anyone pregnant or planning a pregnancy should discuss the potential risks of the trip with a health care provider and may choose to avoid or postpone travel.

Read them with your doctor or midwife before you book. Sources, read on November 14, 2026:

Roughly 94 percent of couples now receive their wedding photographs as an online gallery and nothing else, which means the questions that used to be settled by handing over a box of negatives are settled by a paragraph in a contract most people read once, quickly, at midnight. Can we have the RAW files. Who owns these pictures. Can we print them wherever we like. Can the resort put us in a brochure. These come up in nearly every consultation we have, and the answers are less mysterious than they look. Here is the honest version, from a studio that works in Mexico and delivers a private 4K gallery on every job.

Why Photographers Do Not Hand Over RAW Files

Start with what a RAW file is, because the name misleads people. It is not a higher quality version of your photograph. It is the unprocessed sensor data, the digital equivalent of an undeveloped negative, and on its own it looks flat, cold and slightly underexposed. It only becomes the image you saw in the gallery once a colour profile, a white balance decision, a tone curve and a grade have been applied to it. Those decisions are the studio's craft, and they are the reason your gallery looks like one coherent day instead of four hundred unrelated frames.

Three practical consequences follow. A RAW file is not usable by most people, since it will not open on a phone, will not display correctly in a browser and cannot be sent to a print lab. An unfinished file circulating under our name misrepresents the work. And the files are enormous, tens of gigabytes for a full wedding, which creates a support problem for a couple who wanted a picture, not a project.

There is a version of this request that is completely reasonable, and it comes up often: you remember a moment you do not see in the gallery. Ask for that. If your uncle's toast is missing, or you want the frame just before the one we chose, say so. We can go back into the take, finish that frame and add it to your gallery. What is off the table is a hard drive of raw data. What is not off the table is the specific photograph you are actually asking for.

If a studio does offer RAWs

Some do, usually for a fee. If that matters to you, ask two follow-up questions before you assume it solves anything: are the files delivered with the studio colour profile so they can be reproduced, and does handing them over release the studio from any obligation to correct or reissue images later. Usually the answer to the first is no and to the second is yes, which is a worse deal than it first appears.

What You Actually Receive, and at What Resolution

Our delivery is the full edited set in a private, password protected gallery at 4K resolution, as high quality JPEG files in the sRGB colour space, with no watermark and no downsampling. Every image is downloadable at full size, individually or in bulk, and the gallery is live within one to three business days of the wedding with a sneak peek inside 24 hours.

Two things to check with any studio, not only with us. One: is the download the full resolution file or a web-sized version, because some galleries default to a reduced download and charge for originals. Two: are the files watermarked. A watermarked personal-use file is not a print-ready file. Both of these should be stated plainly in the contract rather than discovered afterward.

What you do not receive is the unselected take. We cull heavily, and that is a service rather than a withholding: three near-identical frames of the same moment, two of them with someone blinking, do not make your gallery better. What we deliver is the full edited set of the day, not a curated fraction of it.

Copyright Versus Usage Rights Under Mexican Practice

A short and necessary disclaimer first: we are a photography studio, not a law firm, and what follows is general information about how the industry works in Mexico rather than legal advice for your situation. If a lot of money or a commercial use is involved, talk to a Mexican lawyer.

Under Mexican federal copyright law the photographer is the author and holds the copyright from the moment the shutter closes. The couple who commissioned the work receive a licence to use the images, which in wedding photography is a broad personal-use licence. That structure is the same in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and most of Europe.

Where Mexico differs meaningfully is moral rights. Mexican law treats the author's moral rights, essentially the right to be recognised as the author and the right to the integrity of the work, as inalienable. They cannot be sold, assigned or waived, even by contract, even for a fee. The economic rights can be assigned in writing for a defined term, but the American idea of buying a photograph outright so that the photographer no longer has any relationship to it does not map cleanly onto Mexican law. If a photographer here tells you they are selling you the full copyright forever with no strings, that person is describing a document, not the law.

The second Mexican distinction matters even more to you, and it works in your favour. Mexican law protects the person portrayed: publishing someone's portrait generally requires that person's consent. So the photographer holds the copyright, but the photographer cannot simply publish your face wherever they like either. Both parties hold something, which is why the contract should describe portfolio use explicitly instead of leaving it to assumption.

"The photographer holds the copyright. You hold your own image. A good contract writes both of those down instead of hoping neither comes up."

Printing, Posting and Tagging: What Your Licence Covers

Here is our personal-use licence in plain language, and most reputable studios will look similar. You may print the images anywhere you like, at any size, in any quantity, with no per-print fee. You may post them on any social platform, send them to family, put them in a book, use them in a thank-you card, hang them at a party. You may keep them forever. None of that requires asking us.

Two limits. First, please do not apply heavy filters or recolour the images before publishing. Crop freely for a platform, that is normal and expected, but the grade is part of the authored work and its integrity is exactly what Mexican moral rights protect. In practice this almost never becomes an issue, and if a frame is not working as a vertical post, ask us for a different crop and we will send one the same day. Second, personal use is not commercial use. If you are a business owner and want your wedding portrait in your own company advertising, that is a separate and easy conversation, not a refusal.

On credit: we love a tag and we do not require one. If you post and mention the studio, other couples find work they might want and we are grateful. If you would rather post without any vendor tags because it is your wedding and not a campaign, that is completely fine and it changes nothing about your licence.

Can a Resort or Brand Use Your Photos? The Consent Question

This one surprises couples, and it happens on this coast more than anywhere. A resort sees a beautiful wedding on its own beach and would like it on the website, in a brochure or on Instagram. Sometimes the wedding coordinator asks the couple. Sometimes nobody asks anybody and the image simply appears.

Two permissions are needed and neither is optional. The studio holds the copyright, so any commercial use requires a licence from us. You are the person portrayed, so under Mexican law your consent is required before your portrait is published. A resort that has only asked one of the two has not finished the job. Our position is simple: we never license a client's wedding images for a third party's commercial use without asking the couple first, and if the couple says no, the answer is no.

If a resort or a brand approaches you directly, the useful reply is that nothing is agreed until it is in writing, and then ask four questions: which specific images, for how long, in which media, and is there any compensation. Plenty of couples are happy to say yes for free because they love the property and the exposure feels harmless. Say yes deliberately rather than by silence, and loop your photographer in so the licence side is handled properly.

The same logic runs the other way, which is why studio portfolio use belongs in your contract rather than in an assumption. Ours states that we may show selected images from your wedding in our portfolio, on our site and on social media, and that you may opt out entirely or ask us to exclude specific images at any time, for any reason, without explaining it. Couples in sensitive professions, couples with custody arrangements and couples who simply want a private day all use that clause, and it costs nothing to honour.

How Long the Gallery Stays Online, and How to Archive It

Your gallery stays live and we do not take one down without telling you first. That said, we ask every couple to treat delivery day as the start of their own archiving, not the end of the process, and to download the full resolution originals within the first month.

The reason is arithmetic about the next fifty years rather than pessimism about our own systems. Studios change platforms, close or are sold, cloud services deprecate products, and a link in an email from 2026 is not a plan for 2046. The most common failure we see is more mundane than any of that: a couple views the gallery on a phone, saves twenty favourites to the camera roll, never downloads the rest, and loses the phone.

The archiving rule photographers use themselves is three copies, two kinds of media, one of them somewhere else. In practice: download the originals to a computer, copy them to an external drive that lives in a drawer, and put a copy in cloud storage you pay for. Do the same with your video if you hired a separate videographer. Then, once, order prints. A print is the only format in this list that does not require a working device and a valid password to be readable, which is why the photographs that survive from a hundred years ago are the ones somebody printed.

The Clauses to Look For Before You Sign

You do not need a lawyer to read a wedding photography contract, you need a checklist. These are the lines that decide everything, and any studio worth hiring will answer all of them without hesitation.

Delivery: the exact turnaround window for the full gallery and for the sneak peek, and whether the number is calendar days or business days. Ours is one to three business days. Scope: how many hours, how many photographers, and which named person is shooting, because at some studios the person selling is not the person arriving. Coverage substitution: what happens if your photographer is ill on the day. Files: resolution, format, watermark policy, and an explicit statement of the RAW position rather than silence about it. Licence: exactly what you may do with the images, written in plain language. Portfolio and model release: whether the studio may publish your wedding, and whether you can opt out. Gallery duration and file retention: how long the gallery stays live and how long the studio keeps a backup. Money: deposit, balance schedule, currency, and what happens if the wedding is postponed rather than cancelled, which after the last few years should be in every contract on this coast. Travel: whether resort outside-vendor fees are included or billed to you.

If any of those answers arrives vague, ask again in writing. A contract is not an obstacle between you and a photographer, it is the document that means neither of you has to remember what was said on a call in March. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get the RAW files from my wedding photographer?

Almost never, and it is written into nearly every wedding contract. A RAW file is an unfinished negative that depends on the studio colour profile to look correct, it is not viewable without specialist software, and releasing it means releasing work under our name that we did not finish. If a specific frame is missing from your gallery, ask for that frame instead. That request we can usually solve.

Who owns wedding photos in Mexico?

Under Mexican federal copyright law the photographer is the author and holds the copyright from the moment of capture. The couple receives a licence to use the images. Moral rights, meaning authorship and the integrity of the work, stay with the author and cannot be transferred or waived in Mexico, which is a real difference from how a full buyout is sometimes described in the United States. This is general information and not legal advice.

Can I print my wedding photos anywhere I want?

Yes. Our personal-use licence covers unlimited printing at any lab you choose, at any size, with no per-print fee and no watermark on the files. We are glad to recommend a lab and to supply a file prepared for a specific paper, but you are never required to order prints through us.

Can I add a filter or crop the photos before posting?

Crop freely for a platform. We ask you not to apply heavy filters or recolour the images, because the edit is part of the authored work and moral rights over its integrity remain with the photographer under Mexican law. In practice this is rarely an issue, and if a frame is not working for a post, ask us for a different crop and we will send one.

Can our resort use our wedding photos in its advertising?

Only with permission from two parties. The photographer holds the copyright, so commercial use needs a licence from the studio, and Mexican law also requires the consent of the person portrayed before a portrait is published. If a resort or a brand asks, say nothing is agreed until it is in writing, and ask which specific images, for how long and in what media.

How should I archive my private gallery?

Download the full-resolution originals within the first month and keep three copies: one on your computer, one on an external drive, one in cloud storage. A delivery gallery is not an archive, phone camera rolls are not backups, and studios do not guarantee to hold your files forever. The safest copy of your wedding is the one under your own control.

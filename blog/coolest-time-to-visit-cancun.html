The Coolest Time to Visit Cancun, and Cold-Front Days

By Vianey Díaz · Published November 14, 2026 · 8 min read

In short

January is Cancun's coolest month: Mexico's weather service, the SMN, puts its normal high at 28.7°C (84°F) and its low at 20.0°C (68°F), against 35.1°C (95°F) for the August high. The catch: December and January are also the peak of the cold-front season, when a norte can bring a windy, cloudy day and a red flag. Pack a light layer and keep a day loose.

If summer heat keeps your family away from Cancun, winter is your window, with one catch: the cool months are cold-front months. Here is what the official averages say, what a norte looks like on the beach, and how to plan the kids, the clothes and the photos around one.

Mexico's National Meteorological Service (SMN) publishes 30-year averages, called climate normals, for its Cancun weather station. The current set covers 1991 to 2020:

January is the coolest month on both counts, and December and February come next. August is the hottest, with a normal high 6.4°C (11.5°F) above January's. If you want cool and dry together, look at February: by the same normals it has rain on 5.9 days, against 9.8 in January and 10.8 in December.

Is Cancun cold in winter? Not by US or Canadian standards. From December to February, a normal night bottoms out at 20 to 21°C (68 to 70°F). A strong front can push the mornings lower: in January 2025, the city's Civil Protection office forecast 15 to 17°C (59 to 63°F) around dawn, mostly in rural areas. The lowest reading in the station's 1991 to 2020 record is 9.5°C (49°F), on March 12, 1996.

The Catch: Cold Fronts and the Norte

The SMN describes a cold front as cold, dry air pushing under warm air. Before it passes, the wind usually blows from the south or southwest and the air is warm; after it passes, the wind swings to the west or northwest and the temperature tends to drop, and with enough moisture, clouds and thunderstorms can build. The north wind the cold air brings, often strong, is what the SMN and the city call a norte, and the SMN issues warnings for frontal systems and Norte events.

On the beach, a norte can mean wind, clouds, waves and a red flag. Strength varies from front to front, and a strong one is serious: on February 5, 2024, the city's traffic department reported gusts of 70 to 90 km/h (43 to 56 mph) in Cancun, with trees and billboards down, and Civil Protection asked residents to stay home where possible as the norte carried into the next day.

How many fronts this season? The SMN counts the fronts that enter Mexico each season, from September to May. Its long-term average is 50, with six or seven a month from November to April and the most, seven each, in December and January: the coolest months. For 2026 to 2027 it forecasts 56, with eight in November, nine in December, eight in January, seven in February and six in March. Those numbers cover the whole country; the SMN's forecast gives no separate count for Cancun.

The season is under way: by November 12, five fronts had entered Mexico, and in its warning of the night of November 13, the SMN placed front number 5 over the Yucatan Peninsula, with heavy rain in northern Quintana Roo. Red Flags, and Which Beach

On Cancun's public beaches, municipal lifeguards set the flags on the sand or on their towers. The city's Civil Protection office lists three colors:

  • Green: safe to swim.
  • Yellow: caution.
  • Red: entering the sea is prohibited.

At closing time, lifeguards take down the day's flag and leave a red one up, so a red flag at sunrise may be last night's. Before you go, check the city's public beach dashboard: according to a city release, it shows each public beach's flag, how busy it is and whether lifeguards are on duty, updated every two hours from 9 am to 7 pm. Outside those hours, every beach shows red.

Which shore? From km 2 to Punta Cancun, near km 9, Hotel Zone beaches face north onto the bay; from the point south, they face the open Caribbean. The bay side is usually the gentle one for small children, as our guide to the safest Cancun beaches for kids explains, but a norte blows from the north, straight onto it. On a front day, go by the flags, not a beach's reputation, and follow your resort's own flags if it posts them. Our calm bay or open sea guide shows how each coast looks on camera.

A Windy-Day Plan With Kids

Under a red flag nobody goes in the sea, so give the day a different shape:

  • Your resort first. A kids club, the pool if it is open, a covered terrace or a board game.
  • The Maya Museum. At km 16.5 of Kukulcan Boulevard, its galleries are covered spaces behind hurricane-proof glass, and the same ticket covers the San Miguelito ruins outside. INAH's visitor page gives Tuesday to Sunday, 9 am to 5 pm (last entry 4:30 pm), 210 pesos for foreign visitors and no entry with food, so have a snack before you go.
  • The aquarium at La Isla. For a shorter outing, the Interactive Aquarium Cancun is inside La Isla Shopping Village, at km 12.5. Delphinus lists admission at 15.99 USD per person, every day from 10 am to 5 pm.
  • Sand, not sea. On a bearable day, dig and build well back from the water, and follow the lifeguards.
  • Boats. If a boat or ferry day is planned, ask the operator how a norte affects it, and keep that day movable.

What to Wear, Evenings Included

By day, the cool months are still beach weather: swimsuits, shorts, sundresses, hats and sunscreen. With normal lows of 20 to 21°C (68 to 70°F) and a breeze off the water, evenings call for a light sweater, hoodie or long-sleeved linen shirt for each person, kids included after a late swim. If a strong front is due, add long pants and one warmer layer for the early mornings.

For photos on a breezy day, choose fabrics with some weight, bring hair ties and pins, and pick a light layer that suits your palette, since it may end up in the pictures. Our guide to coordinating family outfits by age has palettes for every age.

Photos on a Windy Day

A breezy session looks different, not worse. Hair and dresses move, the water turns a deeper, darker blue under cloud, and that light layer can become part of the outfit. When the wind is strong, shelter helps: a garden, a palm line or the side of a building out of the wind, where the hotel allows it. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the coolest month to visit Cancun?

January. In the SMN's 1991 to 2020 normals for Cancun, it has the lowest normal high, 28.7°C (84°F), and the lowest normal low, 20.0°C (68°F). December and February come next; August is the hottest, with a normal high of 35.1°C (95°F).

Is Cancun cold in winter?

Not by US or Canadian standards. From December to February, normal highs are 28.7 to 30.1°C (84 to 86°F) and normal lows 20.0 to 21.0°C (68 to 70°F). A strong front can bring cooler mornings; the station's lowest reading from 1991 to 2020 is 9.5°C (49°F), on March 12, 1996.

What is a norte in Cancun?

A north wind, often strong, that arrives with the cold air behind a cold front. Mexico's weather service, the SMN, follows cold fronts from September to May and issues warnings for frontal systems and Norte events. On the beach, a norte can mean wind, clouds, waves and a red flag.

How many cold fronts are forecast for the 2026 to 2027 season?

The SMN forecasts 56 for Mexico from September 2026 to May 2027, above its long-term average of 50, including eight in November, nine in December, eight in January, seven in February and six in March. The count covers the whole country, not Cancun alone.

What does a red flag mean on a Cancun beach?

On Cancun's public beaches, red means entering the sea is prohibited, yellow means caution and green means safe to swim. Lifeguards leave a red flag up when they close, so a red flag at sunrise may be last night's.

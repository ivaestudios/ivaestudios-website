In short

St. Regis Kanai sits roughly 35 to 45 minutes south of Cancún airport and offers three distinct light environments, mangrove, dune and beach, within a five-minute walk. Start the ceremony about 90 minutes before sunset, and file the resort's outside-vendor paperwork 30 to 45 days ahead, earlier in high season.

St. Regis Kanai is one of the few genuinely new marquee venues on the Riviera Maya, and it is built differently from the resorts around it. Instead of clearing the land flat to the sand, the property threads through protected mangrove and a low coastal dune, which means a photographer working here has three completely different light environments inside a five-minute walk. That is rare on this coast and it is the reason a wedding at Kanai can look like three locations without a single transfer. This is our working guide to the property: where the ceremony sites are, what each one does to your pictures, and how to time a day around them.

The Property: Mangrove, Dune and Beach in One Footprint

Kanai sits on the coast between Puerto Morelos and Playa del Carmen, roughly 35 to 45 minutes south of Cancún airport depending on how Federal 307 is behaving. The development it belongs to was planned around conservation, so the resort is laced with boardwalks over mangrove and low native vegetation rather than manicured lawn, and the buildings step back from the shoreline behind a dune ridge rather than sitting directly on the sand.

Three environments come out of that. The mangrove and boardwalk zones give you filtered green light, dense texture and long leading lines, and they are the coolest place on the property in the middle of the day. The dune ridge gives you sea grape, palms and low scrub with the sea just visible behind, and it is the most editorial surface on the resort because the vegetation breaks up the horizon instead of leaving a flat line. The beach itself is open Caribbean, east-facing, with hard clean light early and a wide horizon.

The practical consequence is that Kanai is a property where the portrait plan matters more than the venue does. Two couples marrying on the same beach at the same hour will get very different galleries depending on whether their photographer knows the boardwalk exists. Ceremony Locations and What Each One Does to Your Light

Kanai offers beach, garden and terrace ceremony settings, and the resort revises which specific spaces are available by season and guest count, so confirm your exact site with the wedding office rather than assuming. What does not change is how each type behaves.

A beach ceremony on an east-facing shoreline puts the sun behind your guests and in front of you in the morning, and behind the property in the afternoon. That afternoon geometry is the good one: at 4 to 5 PM the light comes over the buildings, lands soft and warm on the couple, and the sea behind them stays saturated blue rather than blowing out. Set the aisle so the couple faces roughly north or south rather than straight out to sea, and everyone gets side light instead of squinting.

A dune or garden ceremony trades the open horizon for texture. You get vegetation framing the aisle, dappled light through palms, and considerably more shade, which is a real gift for guests in June. The trade is that dappled light is technically harder, so the ceremony time matters more here than on open sand. Late afternoon, when the dapple softens into general shade, is the window.

A terrace or elevated ceremony gives you architecture, cleaner backgrounds and reliable footing for older guests, and it is the least weather-dependent of the three. It is also the one where flash discipline matters at the reception because you are working with built surfaces that bounce.

Golden Hour and Blue Hour Timing at Kanai by Season

This is the part of a venue guide that actually changes your pictures, so here are honest numbers for this latitude.

May to AugustApproximately 6:20 to 6:50 PM4:45 to 5:15 PM
March, April, September, OctoberApproximately 5:50 to 6:20 PM4:15 to 4:45 PM
November to FebruaryApproximately 5:10 to 5:40 PM3:30 to 4:00 PM

The rule underneath the table is simple: start the ceremony about ninety minutes before sunset. That puts the vows in warm light, leaves a full portrait window after the recessional, and gets you to blue hour with the couple still available. Blue hour on this coast is short, roughly twenty usable minutes after the sun goes, and it is when the sky over the sea turns deep and the resort lights come up. It is worth stealing the couple back for five of those minutes even in the middle of cocktail hour.

Between 11 AM and 3 PM the sun is nearly overhead and it is the worst light of the day on any beach in Quintana Roo. We do not schedule portraits then. The mangrove boardwalk is the exception, because the canopy turns overhead sun into usable filtered light, and a bridal party session there at noon can genuinely work when the same session on the sand would not.

Ask the coordinator one question

What time does the sun clear the buildings on the beach at my ceremony site, on my date? Large resorts cast long shadows late in the day, and a ceremony site that is in shade at 5 PM in December is a very different picture from one still in the light.

Reception Spaces and the After-Dark Flash Plan

Once the sun is down, the venue stops giving you light and the photographer has to make it. Kanai's reception options split between open-air terraces and beachfront setups on one side, and enclosed ballroom or interior spaces on the other, and they need different plans.

Open-air after dark is the harder of the two. There is nothing overhead to bounce off, so a bare on-camera flash produces flat faces and a black void behind. Our standard approach is two off-camera lights on stands placed behind and to the sides of the dance floor, aimed back toward the crowd, so the room reads with depth and the background separates. That requires two things from you: permission to place stands, which the coordinator has to approve in advance, and a conversation with the planner so the lights are not standing where the guest tables go. Both are easy if asked in March and awkward if asked at 8 PM on the night.

Enclosed spaces are more forgiving because ceilings and walls give you bounce, but they bring colour casts from decorative lighting. Warm uplighting on a white wall turns skin orange if it is not handled. We shoot these with gelled flash matched to the room's colour temperature so the ambience survives instead of being flattened.

One thing worth saying honestly: a beach reception is the most romantic setting and the most technically demanding one, and any photographer who tells you they will shoot it entirely on natural light after 8 PM is telling you they will deliver noisy pictures. Ambient-only night photography on a beach is a stylistic choice for a few frames, not a plan for four hours.

"Everything before sunset is a matter of choosing where to stand. Everything after it is a matter of what you brought and where you put it."

Outside-Vendor Policy and Approval Timeline

Kanai permits outside photographers subject to approval and an outside vendor fee, which is standard for luxury properties on this coast. The fee and its structure are revised by season and by wedding package, so ask your coordinator for the current figure in writing and ask specifically whether it is charged per person or per company, since a photographer plus a second shooter can be two charges.

The documentation set is consistent across the region: full legal name matching the photographer's identification, a copy of that identification, Mexican tax registration for the studio, liability insurance, requested arrival and departure times, and an equipment declaration if lighting stands or a drone are involved. Deadlines usually fall 30 to 45 days out, earlier in high season. Ask about drone use explicitly, because the answer on this coast varies by property and by month, and about whether the fee changes if the photographer is a registered guest, since several regional properties reduce or waive it in that case. We cover that mechanic in detail in our guide to the Hotel Xcaret vendor fee and room-block waiver.

We file all of this ourselves rather than leaving it with the couple, and we re-confirm access times a week before the date. It is unglamorous and it is the reason nothing gets billed to you on the day.

A Realistic 10-Hour Coverage Timeline

This is a summer schedule for a 5 PM ceremony. Shift everything earlier by roughly an hour for a December date.

10:00 AM. Arrival, room and detail coverage. Dress, rings, invitation suite, shoes, anything the florist delivered. This is quiet, unglamorous work and it is what makes an album feel complete.

11:30 AM. Getting ready with both parties, split between suites or covered by a second photographer. Mangrove boardwalk portraits with the wedding party if the group is ready early, since the canopy makes midday viable there.

2:30 PM. Dressing, the letter or gift exchange, and a first look if you want one. A first look at Kanai works best on the dune or a shaded boardwalk rather than the open beach at this hour.

3:30 PM. Couple and wedding party portraits in the dune and garden areas. Family groups done in shade with a set list, which we ask for in advance so nobody is hunted down later.

5:00 PM. Ceremony. Roughly thirty minutes, then congratulations and a champagne moment.

5:45 PM. Remaining family formals while everyone is still together, then guests to cocktails.

6:15 PM. Couple portraits on the beach and dune. This is the block the entire day was built around and it needs a protected thirty minutes.

7:00 PM. Blue hour, five to ten minutes, then reception detail coverage before guests are seated.

7:30 PM. Reception: entrances, dinner, speeches, first dance, party. Coverage through to 8:00 PM finish or later by arrangement.

Delivered in a private 4K gallery within one to three business days. That turnaround is not marketing language; it is why the studio caps itself at twelve weddings a year.

What to Book the Day After: Cenote or Beach

The best hour of a destination wedding is often the one nobody planned. A next-day session runs one to two hours, with no guests, no schedule and no makeup deadline, and it produces the frames the wedding day has no room for.

From Kanai there are two strong options. A sunrise return to the beach and dune costs you nothing in travel: the property at 6:30 AM is empty, the sea is glassy, and the light comes in low and horizontal across the sand. Dresses get wet, which is exactly the point, and you are back for breakfast. The alternative is a cenote. The Puerto Morelos cenote corridor is roughly thirty minutes north of the property and gives you limestone, cathedral light and turquoise water that looks nothing like the resort. Cenotes charge their own access fees and most limit tripod and lighting use, so this needs booking rather than deciding on the morning.

If your guests are still on property, a third option worth considering is a family session, which for multi-generational destination weddings is often the set of pictures the parents care most about. We do those at sunrise on the same beach, in about forty minutes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is St. Regis Kanai and how far is it from Cancún airport?
St. Regis Kanai Resort sits inside the Kanai development on the Riviera Maya coast between Puerto Morelos and Playa del Carmen, roughly 35 to 45 minutes south of Cancún International Airport depending on traffic on Federal Highway 307. For a photographer working from Cancún that is a comfortable same-day drive, though for a sunrise start we normally travel down the night before rather than risk the highway at 4 AM.
What time should our ceremony be at St. Regis Kanai?
Work backwards from sunset on your exact date. On this stretch of coast the last usable golden light lands around 6:20 to 6:50 PM between May and August, and closer to 5:10 to 5:40 PM in December and January. A ceremony starting 90 minutes before sunset lets the vows happen in warm side light and leaves a full portrait window afterwards, which for a summer wedding means about 5 PM and for a December wedding closer to 3:45 PM.
Does St. Regis Kanai allow outside wedding photographers?
Yes. Like most luxury properties on this coast, Kanai permits outside photographers subject to approval and an outside vendor fee, with documentation submitted through the wedding office. Expect to provide the photographer's legal name and identification, tax registration, liability insurance and requested access hours, typically 30 to 45 days before the date. Confirm the exact fee and the deadline in writing with your coordinator, because these are revised by season.
What makes the light at Kanai different from a standard beach resort?
Kanai is built into a protected mangrove and dune landscape rather than being cleared flat to the sand, so the property gives you three distinct light environments within a short walk: filtered green light in the mangrove and boardwalk areas, open reflective light on the dune, and hard open horizon light on the beach. That range means a portrait session can change character completely without a transfer, which is unusual on this coast.
Should we plan a session the day after the wedding?
It is one of the best decisions couples make. A next-day session runs one to two hours with no schedule pressure, no guests and no makeup deadline, and it gives you the frames the wedding day never has room for. From Kanai the two strong options are a sunrise return to the beach and dune, which needs no travel, or a cenote in the Puerto Morelos or Ruta de los Cenotes corridor about 30 minutes away for a completely different look.

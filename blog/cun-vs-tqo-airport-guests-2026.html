CUN or TQO: Which Airport Should Guests Use?

In short

Geography decides it: north of Playa del Carmen, Cancún (CUN) wins on every measure; south of Akumal, Tulum (TQO) wins clearly; between the two, price and flight availability break the tie. CUN runs high-frequency service from dozens of cities, while TQO's thinner schedule has had routes added and pulled through 2026, so re-check flights about four months out.

Somewhere between choosing a venue and sending save the dates, every couple marrying on this coast hits the same question from a relative: which airport should I fly into? Since Tulum opened its international airport in December 2023 the answer stopped being obvious, and 2026 has made it genuinely complicated. Tulum crossed a million annual passengers and US carriers kept adding routes, while traffic through the year has swung hard enough that a route your uncle finds in January may not exist in September. Cancún, meanwhile, has held steady and still runs the overwhelming majority of the region's international traffic. This guide is the answer we send couples: where each airport is, what the drive actually costs in time and money, and how the choice quietly reshapes your wedding timeline.

Where Each Airport Is and What Flies There in 2026

Cancún International, code CUN, sits about 15 kilometres south of the Hotel Zone and is the second busiest airport in Mexico. It runs multiple terminals with nonstop service from dozens of cities across the United States, Canada, Europe and Latin America, and it operates at high frequency, which is the part that matters. Frequency means a missed connection is recoverable. If your cousin misses the morning flight there is usually another one that afternoon.

Tulum Felipe Carrillo Puerto International, code TQO, opened in December 2023 about 25 kilometres southwest of Tulum town, inland, near the community of Chunyaxché. It is new, calm, quick to clear, and genuinely pleasant to arrive into. It also runs a much thinner schedule. A handful of US carriers serve it seasonally alongside Mexican domestic routes, and those routes have been added and pulled repeatedly since opening.

That volatility is the honest headline for 2026. Tulum passed the one million passenger mark, which is a real achievement for a two year old airport, but monthly traffic through this year has fallen sharply against its earlier peaks while Cancún has held. Anything specific we write about which airline flies where would be stale by the time you read it, so treat route lists as something to re-check the month you book, not something to trust from a blog post.

The rule we give every couple

Check the actual flight search for your guests' home cities before you recommend an airport on your wedding website. Do it again about four months out. Tulum routes have been added and withdrawn more than once, and a recommendation that was correct when you wrote it can strand somebody.

The line that decides most weddings sits around Playa del Carmen. North of it, CUN wins on every measure. South of Akumal, TQO wins clearly. Between the two, the drive times converge and the decision moves to flight availability and price rather than geography.

One detail people miss: TQO is inland and the Tulum hotel zone is a narrow beach road that gets congested, so the last twelve kilometres of that transfer can take as long as the first thirty. Forty minutes is the good version of that drive, not the guaranteed one.

Cost Differences: Transfers, Tolls and Private Versus Shared

Private van transfers from CUN to the Hotel Zone or Playa Mujeres generally run in the region of 70 to 120 US dollars each way for a vehicle rather than per person, and to Playa del Carmen or Mayakoba roughly 90 to 150. From TQO into Tulum, expect a similar band for a much shorter drive, because the airport is new, the operator pool is small and prices have not been competed down.

Shared shuttles cost a fraction of that and take considerably longer, since they stop at several resorts. The ADO bus is the genuinely cheap option and it is safe, comfortable and reliable, running from both airports to the main towns, though it drops you at a bus station rather than your resort door.

Tolls matter on the southern legs. The 305D between Playa del Carmen and Tulum carries a toll, and drivers price it into a private quote but not always into a rental car budget. If guests are renting, tell them to carry pesos in cash for tolls.

Ferry logistics apply to Isla Mujeres from either airport, since the crossing leaves from Puerto Juárez in Cancún regardless of where you landed. From TQO that means the full northbound drive plus a boat, which is why we steer Isla Mujeres weddings to CUN without hesitation.

What the 2026 Route Shifts Mean for Your Guests

Three practical consequences follow from Tulum's thinner, more volatile schedule.

First, fewer daily flights means less recovery room. On a route with one flight a day, a cancellation is a lost day, and on a wedding weekend a lost day can mean missing the rehearsal dinner or the ceremony itself. CUN's frequency is a form of insurance your guests do not know they are buying.

Second, seasonality bites harder at TQO. Several routes are seasonal, so a flight that exists in February may not exist in September, and a guest researching in one month and booking in another can find the option gone.

Third, price behaves differently. TQO fares are sometimes lower because carriers are stimulating a new route, and sometimes markedly higher because there is no competition on the pair. CUN pricing is more consistently competitive simply because so many airlines fight over it. Tell guests to price both and warn them the answer is not stable.

Arrival Day and Your Wedding Timeline

This is where an airport choice becomes a photography problem, which is why we care about it at all.

Our timelines are built around golden hour, roughly the last forty minutes before sunset, and in Cancún that lands between about 6:07 PM in mid November and about 7:33 PM in mid July. Anyone who is still in a van at that hour is not in your photographs.

So the rule we give couples is simple. Nobody who is in the wedding party should arrive on the wedding day, and ideally not on the rehearsal day either. A guest landing at CUN at 2 PM for a 5 PM ceremony in the Hotel Zone can technically make it, and will arrive stressed, unshowered and visibly so on camera. The same guest landing at TQO for a Tulum wedding has a shorter drive and a little more room, which is a genuine argument for TQO if your venue is south.

The wider point holds either way: build a full buffer day. It protects the ceremony, it protects the getting ready coverage, and it means the first frames of your wedding are of people who have slept.

When Splitting Airports Across Your Guest List Makes Sense

You do not have to pick one. For a Tulum or Akumal wedding, we routinely see guest lists split cleanly: anyone with a convenient nonstop to TQO takes it, everyone else flies to CUN and drives. That is a sensible outcome and you should say so on the wedding website rather than pretending there is one right answer.

Splitting works badly in two cases. If you are running a group shuttle, two airports means two shuttles or a very long combined route, and the cost usually exceeds any savings. And if your venue is north of Playa del Carmen, TQO adds so much drive time that offering it as an option mostly generates confused emails.

Our venues page lists the resorts we work regularly, and the Cancún wedding photographer and Riviera Maya wedding photographer pages show which stretch of coast each one sits on, which is the fastest way to work out which airport your guest list should default to.

What We Tell Couples to Put on the Wedding Website

Guests do not read paragraphs. They read a recommendation and a number, so give them exactly that.

Name one primary airport in bold and say why in a single line, for example fly into Cancún, CUN, about 55 minutes from the resort. Name the secondary option and its drive time so people with a convenient nonstop can self select. Give the approximate transfer cost and the name of the transfer company you have arranged, because an unknown price is the thing guests worry about most. State the arrival date you want everyone on the ground by, and say plainly that arriving on the wedding day is not recommended. Add one sentence about pesos for tolls and tips.

Six lines. That is the whole travel page, and it eliminates most of the questions that would otherwise land in your inbox during the month you can least afford them.

Common Questions About This

Should wedding guests fly into Cancun or Tulum airport?

It depends on where the venue sits. For Cancún, Playa Mujeres, Isla Mujeres and Puerto Morelos, recommend CUN without hesitation. For Tulum and Akumal, TQO is the shorter drive and a genuine advantage. Between Playa del Carmen and Akumal the drive times converge and the decision should be made on flight availability and price rather than geography.

How long is the drive from Tulum airport to the Tulum hotel zone?

Typically 40 to 60 minutes. TQO sits about 25 kilometres inland from Tulum town, and the last stretch along the narrow beach road is often the slowest part of the trip. Forty minutes is the good version of that drive rather than the guaranteed one, so tell guests to plan for an hour.

How long is the transfer from Cancun airport to Playa del Carmen?

Usually 50 to 75 minutes door to door in normal traffic, plus twenty to forty minutes inside the airport for immigration, baggage and finding your driver. Mayakoba is on the same stretch and runs to the same numbers. Saturday afternoons in high season are the slowest, because several wide body aircraft often land within the same hour.

Is Tulum airport reliable for a wedding weekend?

It is a good airport with a thin schedule, and that combination is the risk. Several routes are seasonal and some have been added and withdrawn since it opened in December 2023, so a route that exists when you write your wedding website may not exist when guests book. With fewer daily flights a cancellation can cost a full day, which on a wedding weekend is not recoverable.

Can guests arrive on the wedding day?

Technically yes, and we advise against it. Our timelines are built around golden hour, which in Cancún falls between roughly 6:07 PM in mid November and 7:33 PM in mid July, and anyone still in a transfer van at that hour is not in the photographs. Ask everyone in the wedding party to land at least a full day early, and ideally two.

How much does an airport transfer cost?

A private van from CUN to the Hotel Zone or Playa Mujeres generally runs around 70 to 120 US dollars each way for the vehicle rather than per person, and 90 to 150 to Playa del Carmen or Mayakoba. TQO into Tulum sits in a similar band for a shorter drive. Shared shuttles cost far less and take much longer, and the ADO bus is the genuinely cheap and reliable option if guests do not mind a bus station drop off.

If your venue is in Cancún, Playa Mujeres, Isla Mujeres or Puerto Morelos, recommend CUN and stop there. If it is in Tulum or Akumal, recommend TQO with CUN as the fallback and expect a real split. Between those two zones, let flight availability decide, and re-check it a few months before the invitations go out because this is the part of destination wedding planning that changes fastest right now.

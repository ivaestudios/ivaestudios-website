In short

An editorial photoshoot is planned like a magazine story: wardrobe, setting and direction are chosen before the day, so the photos read as one set. These 12 ideas for couples in Cancun rely on direction, not props, from the walk away in the wind to a backlit profile and a two-look story.

If your folder of editorial photoshoot inspo is full of fashion spreads, you know the look: clean lines, clothes that move, two people absorbed in each other rather than in the lens. Those pictures rarely need a prop. We photograph more than 200 couples a year, and the twelve ideas below rely on wind, walls, light and a few words of direction, at your resort, your villa or on the beach.

What Makes a Photoshoot Editorial

Editorial photos take their name from the editorial pages of magazines and newspapers, where pictures carry a story alongside the words rather than sell a product. In a portrait session, three things are decided before the day: what you wear, where you stand and how we direct you. Wardrobe is chosen for texture and movement, the setting for clean lines and light. Most editorial photoshoot themes on a trip are simple, such as a slow evening by the sea or day to night: pick one and let the outfits follow it.

Editorial does not mean stiff: the ideas below give you something to do rather than a pose to hold. Our guide to editorial vs documentary style explains how directing a couple differs from observing one. After a sunset session for a birthday and an anniversary, Claire L. wrote:

Four Frames in Motion

1. The walk away, fabric in the wind

Walk away from the camera slowly, hand in hand or a step apart, and let a long hem, a loose shirt or a scarf do the moving. With your faces hidden, there is no expression to manage. Slow steps keep fabric floating rather than flapping. When there is a breeze off the water, start here.

2. The over-the-shoulder look

One of you walks a few steps ahead, stops and turns only the head back, chin toward the shoulder, eyes on your partner or the lens. The body keeps facing away, drawing one line from heel to jaw that reads as fashion. It takes a few seconds. Try it once smiling, once not.

3. The slow dance

Hold each other as you would for the last song at a wedding and sway, with no steps. Swaying gives you something to do and brings your faces close without anyone deciding to hug. It works barefoot at the water's edge or on a terrace with the sea behind you. Hum if it helps.

4. The laugh we keep on purpose

Right after a pose breaks, someone laughs. We keep shooting, because that half second, hair across a face, is often more honest than the composed frame before it. An editorial gallery needs one or two of these: they keep the set from looking too perfect, and they look like you.

Four Frames That Use the Place

5. A lean against white architecture

A white wall, an arch or a column at your resort or villa works like a blank page. One of you leans back against it and the other leans in, or you each lean on your own wall or post and face each other, as the couple in our photo at the top of this page do against dark wood. A quiet background lets your lines carry the frame.

6. Seated on a palapa pier

If your resort has a wooden pier or a deck under a palapa roof, sit on the edge, close but not tangled, legs over the water or the sand. The boards and thatch give the frame structure, and sitting relaxes tense shoulders. Like every idea here, it uses what the place already has, not props.

7. Through the palm leaves

We stand behind a palm or a garden plant and shoot through its leaves, so a soft blur of green frames the two of you in sharp focus. It reads like a stolen glimpse of a couple on vacation. You just talk, walk or sit close; the framing comes from where we stand.

8. Small in a wide frame of sea and sky

We step far back until the two of you are a small shape low in a big frame of water and sky. The empty space works like a magazine page waiting for its headline; for you, it is the photo that says where you were, and it holds up printed large. Stand close and let the horizon work.

Four Frames of Light and Detail

9. A profile against the light

Face each other in profile with the light behind you: the edges of your hair and shoulders glow while your faces stay soft. Backlight needs a low sun, so this frame belongs to the start or the end of the day. Keep your faces a breath apart; that thin line of light is the picture.

10. The forehead touch, up close

Foreheads together, eyes closed, one hand at the jaw or the back of the neck, then breathe out. We crop tight, so the frame is about skin, hair and hands rather than the place. Nobody looks at the camera, which makes it one of the easiest frames for shy couples.

11. The hand and ring detail

Hands tell the story without faces: fingers laced against a linen shirt, a hand on a shoulder, a ring catching the light. In an engagement session it shares the news; at an anniversary, it can show two wedding bands side by side. Keep jewelry to one or two pieces so the hands lead.

12. A two-look story, from day linen to evening

Start in relaxed linen, barefoot, while the light is still bright, then change into your evening look for the last light: a long dress, a darker shirt with a sharp collar. Two looks give the set a beginning and an end. They come with The Story, The Editorial and The Heirloom, our 75- and 90-minute collections.

What to Wear for an Editorial Session

Editorial styling is mostly subtraction:

  • Solid colors with texture. Linen, crepe, silk or a fine knit in warm neutrals and soft earth tones look rich against white sand and turquoise water, even in black and white. Busy prints fight the background.
  • One statement piece each. A wide-brim hat, a sharply cut shirt or a long draped sleeve like the one in our photo above; one per person, so they do not compete.
  • No logos, no neon. Logos pull the eye and date a photo; neon fights the water.

Coordinate rather than match, plan to be barefoot on the sand and steam both looks the night before. Our guide on what to wear for a couples beach photoshoot in Mexico covers palettes and fabrics.

If You Feel Awkward in Front of a Camera

Most of these ideas are actions, not poses: walk, lean, turn, sway, laugh. You do something real for a few seconds and we photograph the middle of it. We tell you where to stand, where to look and what to do with your hands. If you feel stiff, start with the walking frames. Two clients put it this way:

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an editorial photoshoot?

A session planned like a magazine story: wardrobe, setting and direction are decided before the day, so the photos read as one set rather than separate poses. For a couple, that means coordinated outfits, clean lines or open sky, and frames built on movement.

What are good editorial photoshoot outfits for a couple?

Solid colors with texture, such as linen, crepe or silk, in warm neutrals or soft earth tones, plus one statement piece each, like a long sleeve that catches the wind or a wide-brim hat. Skip logos, neon and busy prints.

What are good editorial photoshoot poses if we feel awkward?

Actions rather than poses: walking away hand in hand, looking back over the shoulder, leaning against a wall, the slow dance and the forehead touch. We tell you where to stand, where to look and what to do with your hands.

Can we have an editorial engagement photoshoot?

Yes. Engagement sessions use the same six collections, and every idea on this page works for them, with the hand and ring detail as the frame that shares the news.

Do we need The Editorial collection for an editorial session?

No. The Editorial is our 90-minute collection, with 70 photos and 2 looks for 645 USD. One look in one place works from The Postcard, 35 minutes and 15 edited photos for 295 USD; two looks come with The Story, The Editorial and The Heirloom.

How soon do we get the photos, and is there a deposit?

The first preview arrives within 24 hours and the full private 4K gallery in 1 to 3 business days. There is no deposit: you pay at the end of the session, by card or in cash, and can cancel free of charge up to one day before.

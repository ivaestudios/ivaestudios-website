Two Pieces From Mexico, and Where They Come From

The guayabera. Mexico's Secretaría de Educación Pública (SEP) calls its origin uncertain, possibly Cuba or Mexico in the late 1700s or early 1800s. It spread across the Yucatan Peninsula, where it suited the hot climate, and it is a symbol of elegance and tradition in Yucatan, Campeche and Veracruz. Look for four front pockets and a light fabric, often embroidered. The name, the SEP adds, comes from the roomy pockets where farmworkers kept guavas.

The hipil. On the Yucatan Peninsula, the embroidered dress is the hipil. A committee report of Yucatan's congress calls the name a Maya form of the Nahua word huipil, and the hipil the state's traditional dress and its most embroidered textile. UNAM's Gaceta describes a garment with pre-Hispanic roots that Maya women of the region have kept wearing: traditionally white, to mid-leg, its lower part embroidered in many colors. A decree published in April 2024 made Maya-Yucatecan embroidery part of Yucatan's intangible cultural heritage.

How They Read on Camera Against White Sand

White on white is the classic, and it works when the white is warm. Optic white can glow and lose detail in strong sun; ivory, cream or natural linen keeps a guayabera's pleats and pockets visible against pale sand. Up close, embroidery is the detail everyone zooms in on; in a wide frame it reads as texture, so a family in white doesn't look flat. Our online start times, 6:30, 7:00 and 7:30 am and 5:00 and 5:30 pm, stay clear of the hard midday light that burns out thread colors. White cotton can turn see-through with the sun behind it, so pack a slip or choose a lined dress.

Colors that pair with turquoise water

A base of warm white, ivory, sand or natural linen, with accents taken from the embroidery: terracotta, rust, butter yellow, sage, blush or dusty blue. Skip turquoise and aqua, which melt into the sea, plus neon, big logos and head-to-toe black. More in what to wear for beach photos in Mexico.

Mexican Photoshoot Ideas for Families

  • Matching guayaberas: dad and son walking the waterline in the same shirt.
  • Embroidery up close: a daughter's hand on her mother's embroidered hem.
  • Three generations, seated: grandparents in a guayabera and an embroidered dress, the grandkids around them on the sand.
  • A walk under the palms: the whole family on a garden path, like the family at the top of this page.

Coordinating the Whole Family in Local Pieces

Not everyone needs a Mexican piece. For guayabera family photos, three approaches work: everyone in local pieces on a white or ivory base; guayaberas for the men and boys, solid linen dresses for the women and girls; or one hero piece per household, which suits big groups.

The rule that holds it together: the embroidery is the only pattern in the frame. If mom's dress has pink and green flowers, everyone else wears solids, one or two in a soft version of one thread color. Grandparents stay cool in a short-sleeve guayabera or a loose embroidered dress with flat sandals (see what grandparents should wear for beach photos); the full palette system is in what to wear for a family photoshoot in Mexico.

Sizing Guayaberas and Dresses for Kids

A guayabera is worn untucked, so fit shows. Measure a shirt that fits each child now, armpit to armpit and collar to hem, and shop with those numbers: the hem should land around the hip and the shoulder seam on the shoulder. For toddlers, soft cotton and short sleeves beat stiff linen.

For girls, a dress at the knee or mid-calf lets them run without stepping on the hem. Feel the back of the embroidery at the neckline; if it scratches, a thin cotton slip solves it. Buy early in the trip so every child can try their piece on at the hotel, and plan on bare feet on the sand.

Wrinkles, Humidity and the Walk to the Beach

Cotton and linen both crease, linen more. Hang everything when you arrive, then steam it the day before, with a travel steamer or in the bathroom while a hot shower runs. Iron embroidery from the back, face down on a towel, so the stitches keep their relief, and press a guayabera's pleats straight: they are the lines the camera follows. Dress last. Soft creases read as relaxed; suitcase folds are what the camera catches. Keep the Mexican look dry, since some colored threads run when wet. Our day-before checklist covers the rest.

Where to Buy, and How to Wear Them With Respect

Buy from the people who make these pieces, or from a shop that can tell you who did, and ask where each was made and whether it was embroidered by hand or by machine. Both can be good answers: Yucatan's 2024 decree defines Maya-Yucatecan embroidery as worked by hand or with simple machines and other artisanal tools. The 2023 proposal behind it warns that digital embroidery makes it hard for shoppers to tell the difference, and names the town of Tekit for its embroidered guayaberas. Bargain gently on handwork, if at all.

Wondering where to buy a guayabera in Cancun? Quintana Roo's official tourism board points visitors to craft markets such as Mercado 28, and describes Fifth Avenue in Playa del Carmen as a pedestrian shopping street lined with shops and art galleries. Neither page lists what each shop carries, so ask for guayaberas and embroidered dresses by name, and check hours before you go. Fifth Avenue's side streets, with murals, doors and shade, also make a backdrop for a Mexican look early in the morning, before the shops open; our Fifth Avenue photoshoot guide has the details.

Then wear the pieces as clothing, not costume: no sombreros, ponchos or fake mustaches, and no mix of regions into a generic Mexico outfit. Leave the terno to ceremonies and fiestas: UNAM's Gaceta describes it as three pieces, a lace-trimmed underskirt, the hipil and an embroidered, square-necked top over it. A simple hipil or an embroidered Mexican dress suits the beach.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a family wear for Mexican-themed photos?

Guayaberas for dads and boys and embroidered dresses for moms and girls, on warm white, ivory or natural linen. Keep the embroidery as the only pattern, dress everyone else in solids, and skip turquoise, neon and logos.

Is it respectful for visitors to wear a guayabera or a hipil?

Yes, when you wear it as clothing and buy it from the people who make it. Skip costume extras like sombreros, and leave the terno, the three-piece outfit for ceremonies and fiestas, to those occasions.

Where can we buy a guayabera or an embroidered dress near Cancun?

Quintana Roo's official tourism board points visitors to craft markets such as Mercado 28 in Cancun and to the shops of Fifth Avenue in Playa del Carmen (checked November 14, 2026). It does not list what each shop carries, so ask for guayaberas and embroidered dresses by name, ask where each piece was made, and check hours before you go.

How do we keep guayaberas and embroidered dresses from wrinkling in the humidity?

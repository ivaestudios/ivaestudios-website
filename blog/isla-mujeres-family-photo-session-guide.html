Isla Mujeres Family Photo Session Guide

In short

Twenty minutes by ferry from Cancun, Isla Mujeres is where the water stays clear from May to October: its famous beach faces the sheltered side and the sand shelves so gradually a child can walk out fifty metres at waist height. Take the early ferry and shoot right after sunrise, before the beach fills.

Isla Mujeres is twenty minutes by ferry from Cancun and it is the single best answer we have to the question every family asks between May and October: where is the water still clear. The island sits across the current from the mainland, its most famous beach faces the sheltered side, and the sand there shelves so gradually that a child can walk out fifty metres and still be at waist height. It is also a real place rather than a resort strip, which photographs better. This is how we run a half-day family session there, including the ferry timing that makes the whole thing possible.

Why the water on Isla Mujeres stays clearer

Two pieces of geography do the work. The island lies a few kilometres off the coast, running roughly north to south, and Playa Norte sits at the top of it facing north and west, which puts it on the lee side, away from the open Caribbean swell. Sargassum arrives on this coast carried by easterly currents and wind, so it hits east-facing sand hardest. Playa Norte, tucked behind the island, gets a fraction of what the Hotel Zone's ocean-facing beaches get.

The second factor is the shelf. Playa Norte is famously shallow, a wide flat sandbar rather than a slope, and shallow water over pale sand is what produces that specific luminous turquoise. For photography it means we can put a whole family standing in the water with the horizon behind them and nobody is out of their depth, which is not true on most mainland beaches.

Being honest about the limits: this is a tendency, not a guarantee. In a heavy sargassum year the east side of the island, Punta Sur and Playa Media Luna, can carry seaweed while Playa Norte stays clean, and occasionally the north gets a light band too. We check conditions the day before every session and will tell you plainly if the island is not the better option that week.

Ferry timing: making a morning session realistic

The ferry is the part that determines everything, so plan the day backwards from it.

The main crossing runs from Puerto Juárez, also signed as Gran Puerto, just north of downtown Cancun. It takes about twenty minutes, the boats are large and air conditioned, and departures run frequently from early morning until late at night. There is a second option from the Hotel Zone at Playa Tortugas and El Embarcadero, which is more convenient if you are staying on the strip but takes longer, costs more and runs far fewer departures. For a sunrise session, Puerto Juárez is the practical choice because it starts earlier.

Schedules change with season and we will not print times that might be wrong by the time you read this: check the current Ultramar timetable a week before, and again the day before. What is worth knowing is the shape of it. The first departures are early enough to put you on the island before the light gets hard, and the day-trip crowd arrives on the boats between roughly ten and eleven in the morning, which is the moment Playa Norte stops being empty. Everything we plan sits before that wave.

Buy a return ticket, keep it somewhere dry, and give yourself a buffer on the way back. A missed ferry on a travel day is a much worse problem than a missed photograph.

Playa Norte at sunrise versus mid-morning

Playa Norte faces north and west, which has a specific consequence: the sun does not rise out of the water in front of you the way it does on the Hotel Zone's east-facing beaches. What you get at sunrise instead is low, soft, sidelit light across the sandbar, no glare, and a beach with almost nobody on it. For family photography that is close to ideal, because side light gives faces shape without anyone squinting.

Mid-morning is a different session and not a worse one, just a busier and harder one. By nine the light has climbed, the water has gone from luminous to bright, and the loungers have filled. We can still work, particularly in the shallows where the reflected light is generous, but by ten we would rather be somewhere else.

Playa Norte's real showpiece is sunset, because west-facing beaches on this coast are rare and the whole sky goes over the water. If your family is staying on the island overnight rather than day-tripping, that is the session we would book. If you are coming over for the day from the mainland, sunrise is the one that works, and it is the one this guide is built around.

Punta Sur and the east side: rock, cliff and wind

Drive to the southern tip and the island changes completely. Punta Sur is limestone cliff, open Caribbean, real wind and a sculpture park, with the remains of a small Maya temple to Ixchel at the point. It is the easternmost land in Mexico, which is why it gets described as the first place the sun touches the country.

Photographically it is the opposite of Playa Norte: hard edges, drama, movement in hair and fabric, dark rock against bright water. It is a wonderful twenty minutes and a difficult hour. The wind is genuinely strong, there is little shade, the paths are uneven, and there is an admission fee to the park. With small children or less mobile grandparents we treat it as an optional add-on rather than the main event.

The east coast between the two ends, including Playa Media Luna, is worth a stop for the rougher water and the coastal road, but the surf there is not safe for swimming and the sargassum lands there first. We use it for standing frames, not for water frames.

Golf carts, strollers and moving a family around the island

The island is about seven kilometres long and a few hundred metres wide, and the way everyone gets around is a rented golf cart. From the ferry terminal at the north end to Punta Sur is roughly twenty to twenty-five minutes at cart speed, which is the correct speed for the road.

Three practical notes. Book the cart in advance in high season, because they genuinely run out by mid-morning in December, January and Easter week. Bring the driver's licence of whoever is driving. And accept that a stroller is not a great fit on a cart: for a session day, a carrier or a sling is easier, and the town centre is walkable enough that most families with a baby never leave the north end at all.

Taxis exist and are cheap for short hops if you would rather not drive. What does not work is trying to do the island on foot beyond the north end, which people attempt on hot days and regret.

What to pack for a half day off the mainland

Less than you think, but the right things. Cash in pesos for the cart, taxis, the Punta Sur entrance and breakfast, because small businesses on the island are not uniformly card friendly. Return ferry tickets. Reef-safe sunscreen and hats for after the session, since we would rather nobody is greasy in the first frames. A towel and one full change of clothes per child, because they will get into the water and you will want them dry for the ferry.

On wardrobe, Playa Norte's palette is pale sand and bright turquoise, so light warm neutrals sit into it beautifully: cream, oatmeal, soft white, sand, with a muted accent like sage or dusty blue if you want one. Long dresses and loose shirts move well in the island breeze. Skip hard brights, logos and optic white, which blows out against that much reflected light.

Leave behind: anything heavy, anything you would be upset to get sand in, and the plan to change outfits three times. Two looks is the practical maximum on a day that includes a ferry.

A tested 6am to noon itinerary with the session in it

This is the shape of the day we recommend to families staying in Cancun. Adjust the clock to the current ferry timetable and to sunrise on your date, which moves from about 6:05 in late June to about 7:20 in December because Quintana Roo keeps the same time all year.

  1. Around 6:00. Leave the hotel for Puerto Juárez. From the Hotel Zone it is roughly twenty to thirty minutes by taxi at that hour, and traffic is not yet a factor.
  2. First ferry of the morning. Twenty minutes across. Buy the return at the same window.
  3. On arrival. We meet you at the terminal. It is a five-minute walk to Playa Norte, so there is no cart needed for the session itself.
  4. The session, about an hour. Sidelit sandbar frames first while the beach is empty, then the shallows, then the loose material as the family warms up. Group portraits go in the first fifteen minutes.
  5. After the session. Breakfast in town, five minutes away, and this is the moment the children are promised. Then pick up a golf cart.
  6. Mid-morning. Punta Sur if the group can handle wind and uneven ground, or straight back to Playa Norte to swim, which by now has the day-trip crowd but also the warmest water.
  7. Before noon. Cart back, ferry back, and you are at the resort for lunch with the whole day still ahead of you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the water clearer on Isla Mujeres than in Cancun?

Playa Norte sits on the sheltered north and west side of the island, away from the easterly currents that carry sargassum onto the mainland's ocean-facing beaches, and it shelves as a wide shallow sandbar over pale sand. That combination is what produces the turquoise. The island's east side, including Punta Sur, still gets seaweed.

How long is the ferry from Cancun to Isla Mujeres?

About twenty minutes from Puerto Juárez, also signed as Gran Puerto, just north of downtown Cancun, with frequent departures from early morning. There is a slower and less frequent crossing from Playa Tortugas in the Hotel Zone. Check the current Ultramar timetable a week before and again the day before, since schedules change with season.

What is the best time for a family photo session at Playa Norte?

Sunrise, if you are coming over for the day. Playa Norte faces north and west, so early light arrives as soft side light with no glare, and the beach is empty until the day-trip boats land between about ten and eleven. If you are staying on the island overnight, sunset on that west-facing beach is spectacular.

Is Punta Sur good for family photos?

For about twenty minutes, yes. It is limestone cliff, open Caribbean, strong wind and a sculpture park with a small Maya temple at the point, and it photographs dramatically. It also has an admission fee, little shade and uneven paths, so with small children or less mobile grandparents we treat it as an optional add-on.

Do we need a golf cart for an Isla Mujeres photo session?

Not for the session itself, since Playa Norte is a five-minute walk from the ferry terminal. You need one to reach Punta Sur, about twenty to twenty-five minutes from the north end. Book the cart in advance in December, January and Easter week, bring a driver's licence, and use a carrier rather than a stroller.

Can you do an Isla Mujeres session and still have the rest of the day?

Yes, and that is how we run it. Leaving Cancun around six, you are photographed by mid-morning, have had breakfast in town, seen the island by cart and are back at the resort for lunch. Your private 4K gallery arrives within one to three business days.

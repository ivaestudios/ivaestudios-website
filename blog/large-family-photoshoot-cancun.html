Large Family Photoshoot in Cancun: How We Plan for 8, 14 or More

In short

No limit on group size and no per-person charge. We build the list of groupings with you before the trip, then photograph the full group first while everyone is fresh, followed by each household, the grandparents with the grandchildren and the cousins. Larger families usually take The Editorial (90 minutes, 70 photos, 645 USD) or The Heirloom (90 minutes, the complete gallery, 895 USD).

Eight people is a big family. Fourteen, with five children under ten, is a small crowd. Add a baby, grandparents who flew in from another country and two or three households sharing one resort, and a photoshoot becomes a little piece of choreography. It is very doable, with a plan made before anyone takes off their shoes. Here is how we organize a large family photoshoot in Cancun, and what families who have done it with us wrote afterward.

IVAE Studios is led by Vianey Díaz, who has more than ten years behind the camera. The studio has delivered more than 500 sessions on this coast and holds 5.0 across 75 Google reviews, 29 of which mention the children. Some of those reviews come from exactly the families this guide is for: a group of 14, a family of 8, seven adults with one baby. You can read more of them in our roundup of Cancun family photographer reviews.

The Plan Starts Before the Trip

With a big group, the most useful thing you can send us is not only your dates. It is the shape of your family: how many people are coming, the spread of ages, which resort, and the pairings you cannot go home without. Grandma with every grandchild. The siblings without their spouses. Each household on its own. We turn that into a written running order and build the combination list with you, because you know the relationships and we know the light.

That list is what keeps the day relaxed: with the groupings agreed beforehand, nothing gets skipped in the happy noise of a big group. Laura D.'s review names it among the things that went right: “capturing all the shots we had discussed beforehand.”

Send us four things

Your headcount, the ages (especially the youngest and the oldest), your resort and your must-have groupings. If someone has limited mobility, tell us privately: who it is, what they use and roughly how long they are comfortable being out. On the day, nothing has to be announced in front of anyone.

After booking we also send a style guide, which earns its keep with a dozen people in the frame: two or three coordinated tones that each person interprets in their own way, rather than identical outfits. Our guide to what to wear for a family photoshoot in Mexico covers palettes, fabrics and footwear for every age.

Everyone First, Then the Smaller Groups

On the day we work from the biggest frame down. The full group comes first, while everyone is fresh, dressed, still listening, and nobody has wandered off. We frame it twice: once formal, once with permission to laugh. Then each household gets its own portrait, so every branch of the family goes home with a picture of just them. Then the grandparents with the grandchildren, then the cousins together, then the smaller pairings from your list.

Two habits keep a big session calm. First, we release people in waves: once a young family's frames are done, their little ones can go and play at the water's edge with a parent instead of melting down in the back row of group after group. Second, we never keep the youngest children and the oldest grandparents waiting in position at the same time. Between their own frames, they rest in the shade or play off-camera while others are photographed.

Little Ones in a Big Group

Small children are the clock of a big family session. Their best frames usually come early, so after the full group we photograph the youngest while their patience holds, and treat anything later as a bonus. We work in play, not poses: we do not direct toddlers, we follow them. When a meltdown comes, we pause, let a parent scoop the child up and keep shooting gently. Some of the most tender images come from those minutes.

Not every child cooperates, and the reviews are honest about it. Haley W., whose family of 8 we photographed, wrote: “My kids were not cooperating and she made the best of it.” Making the best of it is the job. Jenn P. put it this way: “V made us feel completely comfortable and relaxed”, which she called “no small task with our crew.”

Babies fit into a big group too. Huong's family was seven adults and one baby:

Grandparents: Seated, Shaded, Photographed Early

The oldest generation sets the pace as much as the youngest. Grandparents are seated when they need to be seated and walked when they need to be walked. If anyone tires easily, we photograph the frames that include them right after the full group: the grandparents alone, then with the grandchildren, then with each of their adult children. Every picture involving them exists early, and they can rest in the shade or head back to the room while the rest of the family carries on. When mobility is a concern, we set a chair in the shade with cold water close to where we are working, and choose firm ground, a boardwalk or a pool deck over deep soft sand.

Two guides go further: family photos with less mobile grandparents, on boardwalks, seated portraits and beach wheelchairs, and what grandparents should wear for beach photos. Getting Everyone to the Same Stretch of Sand

With several households, logistics can quietly eat the best light. For the group portrait we usually work right on your resort's beach, which keeps a large group from loading into vans, and we coordinate the location and timing with your concierge before the date. Some hotels charge a fee for outside photographers; you pay it to the hotel, and we confirm your resort's rule before the session. If the family is split across two hotels, we choose a meeting point that is short and step-free for the oldest members.

Some families in our reviews also had the ride sorted. Haley W. wrote that booking was “extremely easy getting my family of 8 a ride to photos and back”, and Laura D. mentioned “our resort pickup and transportation to the photo location”. That was those two families' experience. If your group needs to get somewhere, say so in your first message.

Timing matters as much as place. We suggest booking the session early in the trip, so there are days in hand if the weather moves it, and in the soft light of early morning or late afternoon. Online booking offers 6:30, 7:00 and 7:30 am and 5:00 and 5:30 pm; for any other time, message us on WhatsApp. And because relatives do not always share a first language, we direct in English and Spanish. Laura D. called that “another huge plus.”

Which Collection Fits a Big Family

There is no limit on group size and no per-person charge: a collection costs the same for four people or fourteen. What a big family needs is time, because every extra household adds frames to the list. Larger families who want every combination usually take one of the two ninety-minute collections, which keeps the pace calm:

  • The Editorial: 90 minutes, 70 edited photos, 2 looks, 645 USD.
  • The Heirloom: 90 minutes, the complete edited gallery, 2 looks, 895 USD.

The Chapter, 60 minutes and 35 edited photos for 475 USD, is our usual recommendation for a family of four, and you can move up to a longer collection anytime, paying only the difference. Sessions need no deposit: you pay at the end, by card or in cash, and you can cancel free of charge up to one day before. The first previews arrive within 24 hours and the full private 4K gallery in 1 to 3 business days, with unlimited downloads and print rights, so every household can download and print its own copies. Full details are on our Cancun family photographer page.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a limit on group size or a per-person charge?

No. There is no limit on the number of people and no per-person charge, so a collection costs the same for a family of four or a group of fourteen. Some resorts charge their own fee for outside photographers, which you pay to the hotel; we confirm your resort's rule before the session.

Which collection fits a big family?

Larger families who want every combination usually take The Editorial, 90 minutes and 70 edited photos with 2 looks for 645 USD, or The Heirloom, 90 minutes with the complete edited gallery and 2 looks for 895 USD. You can move up to a longer collection anytime, paying only the difference.

How do you organize so many combinations?

We build the list of groupings with you before the trip. On the day, the full group comes first while everyone is fresh, then each household, then the grandparents with the grandchildren, then the cousins and the smaller pairings. The youngest children and any grandparents who tire easily are photographed early, so they can rest or play nearby while we finish.

Can you photograph a family of 14 with small kids?

Yes. Stephanie F.'s family was 14 people with five children under 10, and her Google review says: “They did the best job of photographing 14 people with 5 littles under 10 years old.” We photograph the little ones early, work in play rather than poses, and let them play nearby once their frames are done.

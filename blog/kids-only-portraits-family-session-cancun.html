Be honest: the photos you want most from this trip might not have you in them at all. You want the kids: all of them squeezed onto one towel, the little one cracking up at something a big sister whispered, a portrait of each child at exactly this age. That takes a little planning, because the same hour has to hold the family portrait too. Here is how we fit sibling photos on the beach and a portrait of each child into one Cancun family session, and keep the siblings friendly long enough to take them.

Right After the First Family Frames, While Outfits Are Clean

The best moment for kids-only photos comes early, right after the first full-family frames. By then everyone has warmed up and the hardest picture is done. Outfits are still crisp, hair is still where you put it, and nobody has been in the water yet. Later there may be sand in a braid and a wet hem: perfect for the end of the session, not for the portrait you plan to frame.

A running order that protects the kids' frames:

  • The whole family. A handful of close, posed frames, then a few with everyone moving.
  • The siblings together. Straight after, before anyone gets bored or sandy.
  • A portrait of each child. One child at a time, a few minutes each.
  • The pairings you care about. Each child with each parent, the kids with the grandparents, the cousins all together.
  • The messy finish. Sand, splashing and wet clothes come last, never first.

Not every kids-only frame has to be posed. One parent wrote about the candid side of their session:

Sibling Photos on the Beach: Ideas That Look Like Them

The best sibling photos look like your kids on an ordinary day, just in better light. A few photo shoot ideas for kids that work on the sand:

  • The secret. The oldest whispers something in the youngest's ear. It does not matter what; the reaction is the photo.
  • The squish. Cheeks pressed together, or a kiss on the cheek from both sides, and the child in the middle trying not to laugh.
  • The towel. Every child squeezed onto one beach towel, knees up and shoulders touching. It never quite fits, and that is the fun of it.
  • The carry. A big kid gives the little one a piggyback, or holds the baby while everyone else crowds in.
  • The walk away. Hand in hand down the beach, backs to the camera, the smallest in the middle. It is the one beach picture for kids that needs no smiles at all.
  • The race. On “go,” everyone sprints for the water. Kids who are trying to win forget there is a camera.
The family portrait needs everyone. The kids' portraits need the grown-ups out of the picture.

Kids photo shoot ideas work best when the kids have a say. If one of yours wants to pick the spot, our guide to letting the kids choose the session location lists six places kids enjoy and how the plan changes with their age.

A Portrait of Each Child

A sibling frame shows how your kids fit together. A portrait of each child, on their own, shows who each one is right now: the gap in a smile, the freckles from a week in the sun, the way one of them always squints.

We photograph one child at a time while a parent keeps the rest busy close by: digging, hunting for shells or keeping score of the waves. Each child gets the same few minutes in the spotlight, the youngest included. Aim for a small mix for each one: a close portrait, one in motion and one doing the thing they love.

Age changes the order. Little ones get their turn first, before their energy dips; older kids can hold out longer, especially with something to do while they wait. A teenager may actually enjoy a solo portrait, as long as it looks like them and not like a school picture. Keeping Siblings From Squabbling in the Frame

Siblings who adored each other at breakfast can be poking each other by the second pose. That is normal. A few things help:

  • Put the oldest in charge. Keeper of the bucket, judge of the race, the one who counts down to the next wave. A big kid who is running something is too busy to fight, and the little ones follow their lead.
  • Make touching the point, or take it away. If hands keep wandering, switch to a hug contest, or to a race where nobody can poke anybody.
  • Turn the fight into a game. A staring match: eyes locked, first to laugh loses. It rarely lasts ten seconds, and the giggle that ends it is the frame you want.
  • Keep each set short. A few minutes together, then a change: running, digging, a turn on their own. Boredom starts more squabbles than siblings do.
  • Don't compare out loud. “Look how nicely your sister is sitting” turns the frame into a contest one child is losing. Leave the directions to us.
  • Step back. Many siblings drop the act as soon as their parents are out of the shot. Stay close enough for a quick hug and far enough that they forget you are watching.

And if the little one shoves a big brother off the towel and both collapse laughing, let it happen; that may be the frame you print. New step-siblings? Ask each of them before the trip who is comfortable with a hug pose; our guide to blended family photos in Cancun shows how to build groupings so nobody ends up at the edge.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should we take the kids-only photos during a family session?

Right after the first full-family frames, while outfits are still clean and everyone is fresh. Take the siblings together first, then a portrait of each child, then the other pairings, and save sand and splashing for the end.

Can we get a portrait of each child in a one-hour session?

Yes, as long as the list stays focused. The Chapter is 60 minutes with 35 edited photos for 475 USD, and those 35 are shared by everyone in the session rather than counted per person. For a longer list or a second outfit, The Story gives 75 minutes, 50 edited photos and 2 looks for 545 USD.

How do you keep siblings from fighting in the photos?

Keep sets short and turn them into games: put the oldest in charge, race to the water, hold a staring match. Avoid comparing kids out loud, give each child a turn of their own and step back so they can just be siblings.

Is there an extra charge if cousins join the kids' photos?

No. There is no limit on people and no per-person charge, so cousins can join the kids-only set at the same price. A longer collection gives the extra groupings more room.

How soon will we see the kids' photos?

A preview arrives within 24 hours. The full private 4K gallery, with unlimited downloads and print rights, follows in 1 to 3 business days.

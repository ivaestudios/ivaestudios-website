There are two ways to hire a photographer for a trip to Cancún, and most couples only ever hear about one of them. The first is a marketplace: you pick a city and a date on a booking platform, pay a fixed price, and a local photographer is assigned to you. The second is booking a named studio directly, the way you would at home. Both are legitimate. They are not the same product, and the difference shows up in four places that nobody puts side by side for you: how many photographs you actually receive, how fast they arrive, whether you know who is holding the camera, and what happens to the files afterwards. This is our honest comparison, written by a studio that competes with marketplaces every week and would rather you booked with clear eyes than with a surprise.

How a Marketplace Booking Actually Works

A marketplace such as Flytographer or Localgrapher is a broker. You choose a destination, a date and a session length on their website, you pay the platform, and the platform then matches you with a photographer in its local roster. You usually get a confirmation with a first name and a small portfolio sample a few days later, sometimes closer to the trip. The platform holds the money, handles the messaging, and delivers the finished images through its own gallery system.

The value is real and worth naming. You are booking in one language, on one checkout page, with a company that has a support line if something goes wrong. For a traveller who has never hired a photographer abroad, that removes a lot of anxiety. The platform has also pre-screened its shooters to a baseline standard, so the floor is higher than picking a random name off Instagram.

The trade is control. The platform standardises the product so every city can deliver the same thing, which means the package is written to the average, not to your day. Session length, photo count, turnaround and file rights are set by the platform rather than negotiated with the person shooting.

Photo Counts: Why 15 Images Is the Standard Cap

The benchmark most marketplace shoots in Cancún land on is roughly 285 US dollars for a 30 minute session with about 15 edited photographs delivered. Those numbers move by city and season, so check the current listing rather than quoting this paragraph at anyone, but the shape holds: short session, small fixed count.

Fifteen images is not an accident and it is not stinginess. It is what a 30 minute session honestly produces once you subtract the walk to the spot, the first five minutes of a couple relaxing in front of a stranger, and the culling. A capped count also protects the platform, because it makes delivery predictable across hundreds of photographers with very different working speeds.

What it costs you is the middle of the story. Fifteen frames from thirty minutes gives you the greatest hits: the kiss, the walk, the two wide ones with the water behind you. It rarely gives you the in-between frames, the ones where somebody laughs at the wrong moment or a child runs into the shot. Those are usually the ones families keep. A direct studio booking has no cap, so the number you receive is whatever the session genuinely yielded, which for our sessions runs well past fifty.

Ask this before you pay anyone

How many edited images do we receive, is that a minimum or a maximum, and do we ever get access to the unselected frames? The answer separates a fixed product from a commissioned session faster than any portfolio comparison.

Turnaround Compared: Four to Five Days Versus One to Three

Marketplace delivery in this region is commonly quoted at up to five days, sometimes longer in peak season, because the file has to travel: the photographer culls and sends to the platform, the platform runs its own review and edit pipeline, and then the gallery is published to you.

IVAE Studios delivers in 1 to 3 business days into a private 4K gallery. We are not faster because we work harder. We are faster because there is no second organisation in the chain. Vianey shoots the session, the studio edits it, the gallery goes out.

Whether that matters depends entirely on your trip. If you are in Cancún for ten days, five days is invisible. If you are on a four night stay and you wanted a print at the front desk or a photo to send your parents before you fly home, the difference is the whole point. It matters most for proposals, where people want to announce the same week, and for family trips where grandparents are waiting.

Do You Know Which Photographer Will Show Up?

This is the single largest structural difference and it is the one couples are most surprised by.

On a marketplace you are buying a slot in a city, not a specific artist. Assignment happens after payment. You will normally see the assigned photographer's portfolio before the day and you can request a change, but you are choosing from availability rather than choosing first. If your photographer gets sick, the platform substitutes another and the session still happens, which is genuinely a strength.

Booking a studio inverts that. You looked at a body of work, you liked it, and that person is contractually the person who arrives. At IVAE the calendar is capped at twelve weddings a year specifically so that Vianey Díaz personally leads every one, and for portrait and family sessions the same rule applies.

The honest counterweight: a single named photographer is a single point of failure. Ask any studio you are considering what their contingency is, and be suspicious of anyone who says nothing can go wrong. Ours is a written associate arrangement plus a full refund if we cannot cover you, which is exactly the sort of question a marketplace answers structurally and a small studio has to answer explicitly.

Pricing: What the Marketplace Fee Pays For

Marketplace pricing is transparent and fixed, which is a real feature. What is less visible is that the platform typically keeps a substantial share of the fee, often cited in the range of a quarter to a third, for customer acquisition, support, insurance and the gallery infrastructure. The photographer on the sand receives the remainder.

That has a downstream effect worth understanding. A photographer earning a fraction of a 285 dollar session is running a volume business, which means back to back bookings, tight turnarounds and limited incentive to stay an extra twenty minutes because the light finally arrived. It is not a character flaw. It is arithmetic.

When you book a studio directly, the entire fee goes into the work, and the pricing is usually higher because the session is longer, the delivery is larger and the person is not amortising a platform cut. There is no universal winner here. Compare cost per delivered image and cost per hour rather than the sticker, and a fifteen photo half hour and a full session stop looking like the same purchase.

Reviews, Portfolios and Verifying a Named Studio

The reasonable fear behind marketplace bookings is fraud: sending money to a stranger in another country. That fear is solvable without a broker, and here is the checklist we hand people.

Look for a Google Business Profile with a real review count, not a testimonial wall on a website. IVAE holds a 5.0 rating from 62 Google reviews, and every one of those is a public, traceable account you can click. Look for a portfolio shot at the resorts you are actually visiting, because Caribbean light at Playa Mujeres does not look like Santorini and a generic portfolio often means a generic photographer. Look for a written contract, a real invoice and a business address. Look for a phone number a human answers, and check the studio replies in your language before you pay anything.

Then verify the work is theirs. Ask for one complete unedited gallery from a recent session at a comparable venue. Highlight reels are easy. Consistency across sixty frames is not, and no amount of platform branding substitutes for seeing it.

When a Marketplace Is Genuinely the Right Choice

We would rather say this plainly than pretend otherwise. Book the marketplace when you want a short, low stakes, low cost set of vacation photographs and the outcome is not emotionally load bearing. Book it when you are moving through several countries and want one account handling all of it. Book it when you are travelling somewhere we do not work and you have no way to research local studios. Book it when you genuinely prefer a corporate support line to a personal relationship, which is a perfectly rational preference.

Book a studio directly when the session carries weight. A proposal. A wedding. The one trip where three generations of a family are in the same place. A milestone anniversary. Anything where you would be upset to receive fifteen competent frames and nothing else, or where you want the person whose work you fell in love with to be the person who shows up. Common Questions About This

Is Flytographer worth it in Cancun?

It is worth it when you want a short, simple, fixed price set of vacation photographs and you value a corporate support line over choosing a specific artist. The publicly listed benchmark is around 285 US dollars for a 30 minute session with about 15 edited images and delivery of up to five days. If the session is emotionally load bearing, a proposal, a wedding or a rare family gathering, a direct studio booking gives you a named photographer, an uncapped image count and a faster gallery.

How many photos do you get from a marketplace session?

Typically about 15 edited images from a 30 minute session, and that number is a cap rather than a minimum. The count is set by the platform so delivery stays predictable across hundreds of photographers in different cities. A directly booked studio session has no cap, so you receive whatever the session actually produced, which for our sessions is comfortably more than fifty images.

How fast do photos come back?

Marketplace delivery in this region is commonly quoted at up to five days because the files pass through the platform's own review pipeline before reaching you. IVAE Studios delivers in 1 to 3 business days into a private 4K gallery, because the photographer, the editor and the gallery are the same studio. On a four night trip that difference decides whether you leave Mexico with your photographs.

Do I get the RAW files or the unselected frames?

Almost never on a marketplace, and rarely from any professional studio. RAW files are unfinished negatives and releasing them means your photographs circulate without the colour work that makes them ours. What you should ask instead is whether the delivered images are full resolution, whether you have printing rights, and how long the gallery stays online. Our galleries deliver full 4K files with personal printing rights included.

How do I know a Mexican studio is legitimate before I pay?

Check for a Google Business Profile with a real, clickable review count rather than a testimonial wall. IVAE holds a 5.0 rating from 62 Google reviews. Ask for one complete recent gallery from a comparable venue, not a highlight reel. Confirm there is a written contract, a proper invoice and a business address, and message the studio in your own language before sending anything so you can see how they reply.

Can I book a named photographer for less than a marketplace charges?

Sometimes, but compare like for like. A 285 dollar half hour with 15 images is roughly 19 dollars per delivered photograph. A longer studio session at a higher sticker price often lands at a lower cost per image and a much larger gallery. Send us your dates and what the session is for and we will quote it plainly so you can put the two numbers next to each other.

The comparison is not marketplace bad, studio good. It is a fixed product versus a commissioned one. If the fixed product covers what you need, it is the cheaper and simpler road and you should take it. If what you need is a specific photographer, an uncapped set of images and a gallery in your hands before you fly home, the broker is a layer between you and the thing you wanted.

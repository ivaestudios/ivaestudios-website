The first time you were expecting, it was just the two of you. This time there is a third person in the frame: small, sandy and completely unaware that their world is about to change. A beach maternity photoshoot with a toddler tells two stories at once, the bump and the big brother or big sister about to be, and it often happens on the last trip as a family of three. Here is how we plan these sessions in Cancun so both of them shine, without wearing anyone out.

The Last Trip as a Family of Three

If this trip is a babymoon with a toddler along, one more slow week before the house gets louder, these photos will mean more than you expect: within a few months, the toddler in them will be a big sibling.

So we plan the gallery like a short story in three parts:

  • The three of you. Walking the waterline with your toddler swinging between your hands, or riding high on shoulders.
  • The sibling-to-be. Your toddler with the bump: a kiss, a hug, an ear pressed to it, two small hands spread across it.
  • You, and the two of you. The maternity portraits that were the whole session the first time around.

If the babymoon is just for the two of you, our babymoon photography guide covers that session; if grandparents, aunts and cousins are joining the maternity photos too, see maternity photos with the whole family.

Your Third Trimester, Their Nap

Two clocks run this session: yours and your toddler's.

Yours comes first, and it belongs to your doctor or midwife: how late in the pregnancy you can travel, and whether you should fly at all, are their call. The CDC suggests a health appointment at least 4 to 6 weeks before you leave, and its page for pregnant travelers notes that some airlines let you fly until 36 weeks while others set an earlier cutoff. Its Mexico page lists Zika, which a pregnant woman can pass to her unborn baby, and dengue among the illnesses spread by mosquito bites, and advises avoiding bug bites. The CDC's current Zika recommendations ask pregnant travelers to avoid areas with an active Zika travel health notice (Mexico's page showed none when we checked) and, for countries with current or past Zika transmission, to discuss the trip and its possible risks with a healthcare provider. Make that the first conversation, before you book.

Your toddler's clock decides the hour. Our online calendar offers 6:30, 7:00 and 7:30 am and 5:00 and 5:30 pm starts, and other times can be arranged on WhatsApp. Both are the cooler ends of the day, which you will appreciate in the third trimester:

  • Early risers: a 6:30 to 7:30 am start catches them at their best, before a day of pool and sun.
  • Afternoon nappers: a 5:00 or 5:30 pm start works when they wake up about an hour before. A rested toddler beats the prettiest light on paper.
Send us their rhythm

When you book, tell us your toddler's nap and snack times, your hotel and the frames you would love most. We plan the start time and the order of the session around them.

Bump and Toddler First

The order matters more than any pose. We start with the frames that need your toddler, while their patience lasts, and save the portraits of you alone for the end, when your toddler can dig in the sand nearby with your partner. Traveling with a grandparent? They can mind the sand digging while we photograph you and your partner together.

These maternity photo shoot ideas with kids tend to work with a toddler:

  1. Say hello to the baby. Your toddler tells the baby a secret, kisses the bump or presses an ear to it. It is a game, not a pose, so the faces are real.
  2. Big sister, big brother. Your toddler on your hip or standing beside you, one small hand on the bump. Maternity photos with a big sister or big brother read best in soft, shared tones rather than matching outfits.
  3. The swing. You and your partner walk the waterline holding your toddler's hands and swing them over the foam. Toddlers usually ask for it again, which gives us more than one try.
  4. On shoulders. Your toddler on your partner's shoulders, you in profile beside them: a classic for big brother maternity pictures on the beach, and big sister ones too.
  5. The quiet one. Your toddler in the sand in front of you, or curled into your lap if tired. A sleepy cuddle beside the bump can be the frame you love most.

We guide you through every frame, and we don't ask a toddler to hold a pose: we set up the game and photograph what happens. Helping Your Toddler Through It

A few things that help before we start:

  • A real snack about thirty minutes before, with a small stash out of sight for later.
  • One small surprise in your bag, like bubbles or a favorite little toy. Novelty earns more laughter than any request to smile.
  • Clothes they already love: soft, breathable fabric in the lightest shade of your palette, nothing brand new, and bare feet on the sand.
  • Talk about the baby beforehand: tell them they are going to take pictures with the baby in your tummy.
  • Expect a wobble. If tears come, we pause, you scoop them up, and we keep photographing quietly. A toddler tucked into your neck, beside the bump, is a real moment too.

For your own dress, choose something long and light that follows the bump; see what to wear for beach maternity photos. For more on snacks, games and pacing, read our family photos with toddlers playbook.

Almost every couple we photograph in the Riviera Maya is getting married here. A growing number are not. They are marrying in Chicago, Toronto, Dallas or London, and they have flown down for four nights specifically to have their engagement photographs taken somewhere that does not look like the park near their apartment. Planning guides have started calling the trip an earlymoon. Whatever it is called, it is a real and sensible category, and almost nobody on this coast writes about how to plan it.

What an earlymoon is, and why the shoot gets added

An earlymoon is a trip taken after the engagement and well before the wedding, usually in the first half of the planning year. The logic is straightforward: the honeymoon comes at the end of a long, expensive, occasionally exhausting process, and by then you have spent a year talking about seating charts. An earlymoon puts a few days of nothing into the calendar while being engaged is still the best part.

The photo session gets added because the trip already solves every problem an engagement shoot normally has. You are somewhere beautiful, you have nowhere to be, you are not squeezing a session between work on a Thursday evening, and you are in the mood the pictures are supposed to capture. Adding two hours of photography to a trip you are taking anyway is a very different proposition from booking a shoot as a standalone errand at home.

There is also a practical timing argument. Engagement photographs feed save the dates, the wedding website and often a display at the reception. All of those want images early. A session in February for a November wedding is comfortable. A session in September for a November wedding is a scramble.

Marrying in Chicago, shooting in Tulum

The objection we hear is that the photographs will not match the wedding. They will not, and that turns out not to matter.

Nobody looking at a save the date is checking whether the palm trees are near the church. What people register is the two of you, warm light, and the fact that you look happy and relaxed. Engagement images are portraits of a relationship rather than documentation of a venue. If they were meant to match the wedding, everyone would shoot them in the parking lot of the reception hall.

Where it genuinely helps is season. A Midwest or Canadian couple marrying in autumn is choosing engagement photos in a February that offers bare trees, flat grey light and a coat over every outfit. The same week in the Riviera Maya offers warm evening light, colour, and skin. That contrast is the whole reason this category exists.

Where it does not help: if your wedding has a strong regional identity that you want echoed in the stationery, a vineyard, a mountain, a specific city. In that case a local session is the right call and we will say so. We would rather lose a booking than sell a couple a trip that fights their own wedding.

Cost: flights, hotel and session versus a local shoot

Here is the honest comparison, and it is more favourable than couples expect.

A local engagement session at home costs you the photographer's fee and an evening. A destination session costs you the photographer's fee plus flights, three or four nights of accommodation, ground transfers and any location entry fees. That is clearly more money. What makes it work is that Cancún is one of the best connected airports in the Americas, so the flight component is often the cheapest long haul you will book all year, particularly outside the December to March peak and outside spring break.

The line items to plan for are: flights for two, accommodation for three to four nights, an airport transfer each way, a car or driver for the session day if you are moving between locations, and cenote entry fees if a cenote is in the plan. Cenotes charge per person and some also charge a separate fee for professional photography, which we check in advance for the specific cenote rather than assuming.

The session itself is quoted separately and depends on how many hours and how many locations you want, so we do not publish a headline number that would be wrong within a season. Write to us with your dates and what you have in mind and we will send the current rate.

The comparison most couples end up making is not against a local engagement shoot at all. It is against a weekend away they were going to take anyway. Seen that way, the photography is the marginal cost, and it is a small one.

Book the light, not the week

Whatever your dates, pick the session day first and build the rest of the trip around it. We photograph in golden hour only, which means the session is anchored to a fixed ninety minute window at the end of a specific day. A trip planned around excursions with the shoot squeezed in at the end is the one reliable way to get tired photographs.

A three-day itinerary with the session built in

This is the shape we recommend most often for a four night trip, and it is deliberately unambitious.

Day one is arrival. Land at Cancún, transfer to wherever you are staying, and do nothing. Flights and transfers eat a day whatever the schedule says, and a session on an arrival day looks like a session on an arrival day. If you land early and want to be by water, be by water, but keep it flat.

Day two is the session. Sleep in, spend the day slowly, eat lunch early rather than late, and start getting ready about two hours before we meet. We photograph the last ninety minutes of light, which in this region means starting somewhere between roughly 4:45 and 5:30 PM depending on the month. Afterwards you go straight to dinner still dressed, which is the single best decision couples make on these trips.

Day three is yours: a cenote, the Tulum ruins, a boat, or a beach and nothing else. This is also the natural buffer day. If day two is rained out, and in the wet season it occasionally is, day three becomes the session and nothing is lost. That buffer is the main reason we ask couples to come for four nights rather than two.

Day four is departure, with the gallery arriving while you are still travelling or shortly after you land. The private 4K gallery is delivered within one to three business days, so a Tuesday session is usually in your hands before the weekend.

Beach, cenote and jungle in one day

Couples ask for all three constantly, and it is possible, but the light does not cooperate in the way people assume. This is the part worth reading twice.

Cenotes are the exception to everything else we do. An open or semi-open cenote photographs best when the sun is high, roughly late morning to early afternoon, because that is when light drops through the opening and hits the water. At six in the evening a cenote is a dark hole. So a cenote cannot share a time slot with a beach session; it has to happen in the middle of the day, hours before.

Beach and jungle, on the other hand, both want the last hour of daylight, and they can genuinely share it if they are close together. Much of this coast has jungle within a few minutes of the sand, which is why a session can produce two visibly different backdrops without a drive.

The practical day therefore looks like this: cenote late morning, back to the hotel to rest, dry off and change, then beach and jungle at golden hour. Add drive times honestly. The cenotes along the Ruta de los Cenotes near Puerto Morelos are roughly half an hour from Cancún. The better known cenotes around Tulum are closer to an hour and a half to two hours from Cancún, so a Tulum cenote plus a Cancún beach in one day is a lot of car for two people in nice clothes.

Our advice, offered as often as we can get couples to hear it: pick two locations, not three. Two done properly beats three done at a run, and the frames you will actually print come from the location where you had time to forget the camera was there. If you want the full cenote experience, our cenote couples session guide covers what that day involves on its own.

"A cenote and a beach are not two stops on one evening. They are two different times of day that happen to be in the same postcode."

What to wear across two or three looks

Two outfits each is the right answer for a standard session, three if there is a cenote in the day. More than that and you spend the golden hour changing in a car.

The first look should be relaxed and true to you: linen, a simple dress, something you would genuinely wear to dinner here. The second is the dressier one, saved for the last thirty minutes when the light is at its warmest, because that is where the framed print comes from. If a cenote is involved, the third look is functional: swimwear, or something you are entirely happy to soak, because you will get wet and pretending otherwise ruins the day.

Colour advice specific to this coast. Turquoise water and pale sand already carry the frame, so heavy patterns and bright saturated colours compete with the background and win. Creams, soft neutrals, warm earth tones, deep blues and black all sit beautifully against this palette. Avoid anything with a visible logo, and be careful with pure white in strong light, which can blow out before your skin does.

Two practical notes. Humidity here is genuine, so hair that needs to stay flat will not, and planning for movement is more productive than fighting it. And bring shoes you can carry, because every couple ends up barefoot within twenty minutes. Our what to wear guide for beach sessions goes deeper if you want fabric by fabric detail.

Save the dates, the website and the reception

The images have three jobs after the trip, and knowing them in advance changes what we shoot.

Save the dates need a specific kind of frame: room for text, a clean background, both faces visible, and usually a vertical or square crop. We shoot a deliberate set with that in mind, including verticals composed as verticals rather than cropped afterwards, because a horizontal beach photograph squeezed into a card format loses the sky that made it good.

Wedding websites want variety: a wide hero image with space at the top for a name, a couple of candid frames for the story page, and something quieter for the details. We shoot wider than the moment strictly needs precisely so a designer has room to work.

The reception display, if you are having one, wants large prints, which is why the gallery is delivered at full resolution with unrestricted printing. Files from these sessions are large enough for wall sized prints, and if you need an unusual crop for a frame you already own, write to us and we will prepare it properly rather than letting a lab guess.

Questions couples ask

What is an earlymoon?

An earlymoon is a trip taken between the engagement and the wedding, usually in the first half of the planning year, before the schedule gets heavy. Couples take it to have time together while they are still enjoying being engaged rather than exhausted by logistics. Adding a photo session to that trip is what turns it into a destination engagement session, and it is why the two ideas keep appearing together.

Does it make sense to marry in one country and photograph in another?

It does when the photographs are meant to look like the two of you rather than like the wedding venue. Engagement images are used for save the dates, the wedding website and the display at the reception, and none of those need to match the ceremony location. What matters is that the images are warm, that you look relaxed, and that they exist well before the invitations go out. A February session in the Riviera Maya delivers that when a February session at home would mean grey light and coats.

How much does flying here for engagement photos actually cost?

The travel side has four line items: flights to Cancún, three or four nights of accommodation, ground transfers, and any location entry fees such as cenotes. Cancún is one of the best connected airports in the Americas and off-peak fares from most of North America are modest, which is the main reason this works financially. The session itself is quoted separately and depends on hours and locations, so write to us with your dates and we will send the current rate rather than a number that will be wrong next season.

Can we do beach, cenote and jungle in one day?

Yes, but not in one continuous block, because the light windows fight each other. Open and semi-open cenotes photograph best when the sun is high, roughly late morning to early afternoon, when the light drops through the opening into the water. The beach and the jungle clearings photograph best in the last hour of the day. So the practical shape is a cenote in the middle of the day, a gap to travel and change, and the coast at golden hour. Trying to compress all three into a single evening produces three rushed locations instead of two good ones.

How many outfits should we bring?

Two for a standard session and three if you are doing a full day with a cenote. One relaxed daytime look, one dressier look for the last light, and swimwear or something you do not mind soaking if water is involved. Bring more than you plan to use and we will choose on the day based on the location and the light, but do not plan more than three changes, because every change costs ten to fifteen minutes of the best light of the evening.

When will we have the images for our save the dates?

Your private 4K gallery is delivered within one to three business days of the session, so most couples order save the dates before they have unpacked. That turnaround is one of the practical reasons to do this trip early in the planning year rather than three months before the wedding, when a delay in the images means a delay in everything downstream.

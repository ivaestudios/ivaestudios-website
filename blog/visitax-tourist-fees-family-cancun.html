In short

Quintana Roo's Visitax is 293 pesos per person on visitax.gob.mx (about 16 USD), babies included. Mexico's federal non-resident fee is already in your airfare: 983 pesos in 2026 and 1,158 from January 1, 2027. A foreign adult pays 265 pesos at the Tulum ruins, where a child under 12 pays 100, and 125 to 225 pesos a day in the federal marine parks near Cancun, where children under 12 do not pay. With one Tulum day and one snorkel day, a family of four pays 6,784 pesos (about 364 USD) and a family of six 10,466 pesos (about 562 USD), each with two children under 12, most of it inside the airfare.

With children, every fee charged per person multiplies fast. We checked the main ones a family meets, from the airport to a day trip, on official Mexican sources on November 14, 2026, and added them up for a family of four and a family of six.

The Visitax: Amount, Kids and How to Pay

The Visitax is Quintana Roo's fee for foreign visitors, and it covers the whole state: Cancun, Isla Mujeres, Cozumel, Playa del Carmen, Tulum and Holbox. Article 51-Octies of the state's fee law sets it at 2.5 UMA, a unit Mexican laws use to fix fees, worth 117.31 pesos since February 1, 2026. The official portal charges 293 pesos per person, about 16 USD.

For a 2027 trip. INEGI, Mexico's statistics institute, updates the UMA every January with the previous December's inflation, and the new value applies from February 1. Under the current law, the Visitax follows it from February 1, 2027, so check the portal before you pay.

Children pay too. The portal's FAQ is direct: there is no adult or child rate, and all foreign tourists must pay, infants included. That is 1,172 pesos for a family of four and 1,758 for six.

How and when to pay. On visitax.gob.mx you enter your country, your travel dates, an email and each traveler's name and passport number, up to ten travelers per payment, and pay by card in pesos. Each traveler gets a QR receipt, which you can download again with that traveler's passport number; keep them on your phone, as our luxury packing list suggests. The law ties the fee to entering the state, so pay before you fly or on your first day.

Look-alike sites. The state's tax office, SATQ, names visitax.gob.mx as its official portal. Check that the address ends in .gob.mx, and compare the price: until the new UMA takes effect on February 1, 2027, a page asking more than 293 pesos a person is not the state's portal.

Who does not pay it. Visitors who enter through the state's southern border, and cruise passengers, who pay a separate 5 USD state fee with their cruise ticket instead (articles 51-Octies and 51-Nonies).

The Federal Fee Already in Your Airfare

Mexico does charge an entry fee, but when you fly in, you do not pay it at a window. The non-resident fee covers the immigration document of a visitor who will not do paid work, and the Federal Law of Fees (Ley Federal de Derechos) requires airlines to collect it, so it is already in your ticket price.

Article 8 of that law sets it at 983 pesos per visitor in 2026. A decree published in the Diario Oficial de la Federación, Mexico's official gazette, on November 13, 2026 raises it to 1,158 pesos, about 62 USD, from January 1, 2027. Its exemptions in Article 11, such as land arrivals of up to seven days and certain crews, do not include children: at the 2027 rate, that is 4,632 pesos for a family of four and 6,948 for six. It is separate from the Visitax, so paying one never covers the other.

Passports and the FMM tourist card are in our entry guides for US travelers and for Canadian and European travelers.

Ruins, Reefs and Islands: Day-Trip Fees

Tulum ruins. INAH, the federal institute that runs Mexico's archaeological sites, lists one unified price for Tulum: 265 pesos for foreign visitors, including the charges of CONANP, the national parks agency, and of the Parque del Jaguar's operator (page updated August 10, 2026). The decree behind it, in force since July 20, 2026 and valid through 2027, splits the 265 pesos into a 100-peso park tariff and 165 pesos of federal fees. Children keep their federal exemptions on the 165, but the decree sets the park tariff for every foreign visitor and lists no exemption for children, so we count 100 pesos for a child under 12. Hours run from 8:00 am to 5:00 pm, last entry 3:30 pm. Plan the day with our Tulum family guide and Mayan ruins with kids near Cancun.

Other ruins. Elsewhere the federal fee follows the site's category. In 2026, Coba, San Gervasio on Cozumel and the Museo Maya de Cancun with San Miguelito are Category I, at 209.09 pesos, and El Rey in the Hotel Zone and El Meco are Category III, at 143.69, rounded to the nearest 5 pesos when you pay and updated every January 1. Children under 13 and adults over 60 do not pay it, and free Sundays are for Mexican nationals and foreign residents of Mexico, not for visitors.

Reefs and islands. Snorkel trips, boat tours and visits to protected islands pay CONANP's fee per person per day. In 2026 it is 125.32 pesos, paid as 125, in the national park off Isla Mujeres, Punta Cancun and Punta Nizuc and at the Puerto Morelos reef, and 225.56, paid as 225, at Cozumel's reefs, Isla Contoy, Holbox's Yum Balam protected area and the whale shark reserve. Children under 12 are exempt in 2026; from January 1, 2027, the exemption covers children under 13 and adults 60 and over. The law makes the tour operator responsible for paying it, so ask whether your price includes it. See our Isla Mujeres family guide and Cozumel or Isla Mujeres for a family day.

Isla Mujeres. The island also charges its own environmental sanitation fee to visitors who do not live in the municipality. On a day trip, the ferry company collects it with your ticket, and if you arrive on a tour boat it is paid with the dock fee. If you stay overnight anywhere in the municipality, which also covers the Playa Mujeres and Costa Mujeres resorts on the mainland, your hotel charges it per person per night. The municipal fee law, in the text the state Congress publishes (last amended July 3, 2025), sets it at 30% of the daily UMA, about 35 pesos a person in 2026. Its exemptions cover people who work in the municipality and visitors who travel with a local Isla Mujeres boat cooperative, not children.

Cenotes and cameras. Each cenote sets its own entry price and its own rules for cameras and drones, so they are not in our table; ask before you go. At federal archaeological sites, the fee law has no charge for your own photos, while commercial photo shoots pay 7,957.68 pesos per day in 2026 (Article 288-D).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Cancun Visitax in 2027?
On the official portal, visitax.gob.mx, it is 293 pesos per person, about 16 USD, as of November 14, 2026. Under the current law it is 2.5 UMA, so it stays at that amount until the new UMA takes effect on February 1, 2027, and then follows it.
Do kids pay the Visitax?
Yes. The portal's FAQ says there is no adult or child rate and that all foreign tourists must pay, infants included: 1,172 pesos for a family of four and 1,758 for a family of six.
Does Mexico charge an entry fee?
Yes, the federal non-resident fee: 983 pesos per visitor in 2026 and 1,158 pesos from January 1, 2027. When you fly in, the law requires your airline to collect it, so it is already in your ticket price. It is separate from the Visitax.
How do I avoid look-alike Visitax sites?
Pay on visitax.gob.mx, the portal Quintana Roo's tax office names as official, and check that the address ends in .gob.mx. Until the new UMA takes effect on February 1, 2027, a page asking more than 293 pesos per person is not the state's portal.
Do children pay at the ruins and marine parks?
Children under 12 do not pay the federal fees: archaeological sites exempt children under 13, and marine parks exempt children under 12 in 2026 and under 13 from January 1, 2027. At Tulum, the decree that sets the 100-peso Parque del Jaguar tariff for foreign visitors lists no exemption for children.
Is there a tourist fee for Isla Mujeres?
Yes. Under the municipal fee law, visitors who do not live there pay an environmental sanitation fee of 30% of the daily UMA, about 35 pesos per person in 2026, with no exemption for children: the ferry company collects it on a day trip, and hotels in the municipality, Playa Mujeres and Costa Mujeres included, charge it per person per night. The Visitax also applies, since Isla Mujeres is in Quintana Roo, and snorkeling or a boat tour in the national park off the island adds CONANP's fee: 125 pesos per person per day in 2026, with children under 12 exempt, or under 13 from January 1, 2027.

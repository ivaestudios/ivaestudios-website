Sixteen people, three generations, two hotels and one sunrise. When a big family wants its photos away from its own resort beach, the hard part is not the posing; it is getting everyone to the sand on time, dressed and still in a good mood. If you are looking for a photographer with transportation in Cancun, here is how it works with us, when the ride is worth it, and how to plan the morning backward from the light.

Your Resort Beach, or a Ride?

If all of you are staying at the same resort, start there: no ride, no car seat, no headcount in a lobby at dawn, and grandparents and little ones can go back to the room once their frames are done. Some hotels charge a fee for outside photographers; where yours does, you pay it to the hotel, and we confirm your hotel's rule with you before the session.

How we organize a big group once everyone is on the sand is in our guide to a large family photoshoot in Cancun. This page covers the other case: a large family beach photoshoot somewhere other than your own hotel.

When a Van Makes Sense

Three situations make the van worth it:

  • The family is split across two hotels. One household is in the Hotel Zone, another in Costa Mujeres or Puerto Morelos, and no beach is home for everyone. Rather than one branch making its own way to the other's resort, the van can take the whole group to neutral sand.
  • You want a public beach at first light. Mexico's sea beaches are public, and a 6:30 or 7:00 am start comes before the 9:00 am start the municipality has given for lifeguards and beach services. More on that below.
  • You want a location in another area. A stretch of coast your hotel cannot offer, such as Puerto Morelos or a beach farther down the Riviera Maya. The farther the location, the earlier the pickup.

It works the same for any group of up to 16, from one large family to several households, three generations or a reunion. In the reviews below, three families describe their rides to the photos.

Public Beaches in Cancun

In Mexico, the beach belongs to the nation. Under the General Law on National Assets, maritime beaches and the federal maritime land zone beside them are goods of common use (article 7), and since 2020 article 8 has said that access to them may not be inhibited, restricted, obstructed or conditioned, except in cases the regulation sets. PROFEPA, Mexico's Federal Attorney for Environmental Protection, describes the federal zone, known as ZOFEMAT, as the 20-meter strip of firm, passable land next to the beach.

In Cancun, two Benito Juárez municipality releases, of December 2025 and March 2026, count seven public beaches with Blue Flag certification for the 2025-2026 season, an international award covering water quality, safety, environmental management and services. The March release names them, all in the municipality's care: Playa del Niño, Las Perlas, Chac Mool, Marlín, Ballenas, Delfines and Coral. The December release lists restrooms, showers, lifeguards, palapas, universal access ramps and parking among the services at these beaches.

Two details matter for a sunrise session. The releases put the start of lifeguard service at 9:00 am for the year-end and Easter holidays, and the December one gives the same hour for restrooms, showers and the other services, so a session that begins at 6:30 or 7:00 am is over before they start: plan the restroom stop at the hotel, and leave swimming for later in the day. And the walk from the van to the water's edge belongs in the plan, especially for a grandparent with a cane or a wheelchair. Our guide to Cancun photo spots covers which stretch suits which hour, and family photos with less mobile grandparents covers seated portraits and firm footing.

Plan the Morning Backward From the Light

With 16 people, the clock runs backward from the first frame:

  1. The start time. Online booking offers 6:30, 7:00 and 7:30 am and 5:00 and 5:30 pm, Cancun time; other times are arranged on WhatsApp. Which slot sits closest to sunrise or golden hour depends on the month: see our sunrise and sunset times by month.
  2. The pickup time. Counted back from the start: the drive at that hour, plus the minutes it takes 16 people to board. Settle the pickup time and the meeting point with us before the day.
  3. One meeting point. One lobby, one time, and one person who holds the list and counts heads. In two hotels? Send both addresses when you ask.
  4. Dressed before boarding. Outfits, hair and shoes are done in the rooms, so nobody changes in the van or on the sand. For palettes that hold a big group together, see our color ideas for a large family photoshoot.
  5. Water, snacks and a seat. Pack water for everyone, a small snack for each child and hats for the walk, and plan where a grandparent can sit in the shade between frames.
  6. The ride back. Decide where everyone goes afterward, one hotel or two, and ask about the return in the same message as the pickup.

What to Send When You Ask

The van's price is not published; ask for it in your first message, with these details:

  • Headcount, and how many households.
  • Ages, especially the youngest and the oldest.
  • Pickup addresses: the hotel or villa where each household is staying.
  • Mobility needs: a cane, a walker, a wheelchair, or anyone who cannot stand for long. You can tell us privately.
  • Car seats: how many children ride in one at home, and their ages.
  • The session: your dates, a start time and the location you have in mind.

On car seats, ask rather than assume. Under Quintana Roo's mobility law (article 218), everyone on board must wear a seat belt, and drivers must use a child seat whenever a child under five is on board and seat minors in the back. Ask what the van can provide for each child; if a seat that fits is not confirmed, bring your own.

Excellence Playa Mujeres anchors the Costa Mujeres corridor north of Cancun, and it has become one of the most requested addresses on our calendar for reasons that are photographic rather than fashionable. The shoreline here bends, the water is calmer, the beach is wide and pale, there are almost no high-rises in the background, and the seaweed that plagues the coast further south generally arrives here in far smaller quantities. This is the studio's working guide to the property: where to put the ceremony, where the portraits actually live, and what to ask before you sign.

Why Couples Move North to Playa Mujeres

Costa Mujeres barely existed as a wedding destination ten years ago. It sits north of Puerto Juarez, past the ferry terminals, on a stretch that was empty until the resort building wave of the last decade. Excellence Playa Mujeres, adults-only and all-inclusive, opened in 2015 and is one of the properties that defined the corridor.

The draw for photography is the geography. The coastline here turns, so the beach faces roughly north and northeast rather than dead east. Water sits calmer and shallower, the sand is wide and pale, and the horizon holds Isla Mujeres in the distance rather than a wall of towers. Compared with the Cancun Hotel Zone, your wide ceremony frames simply have less clutter in them.

The honest cost is the drive. Excellence is forty to fifty minutes from Cancun International, longer than most Hotel Zone resorts, and there is nothing walkable nearby. Guests stay on property, which suits some weddings perfectly and frustrates couples who wanted their group wandering out to dinner.

Ceremony Sites and What Each Does to Your Background

Large properties like this one typically offer a beach setup, a shaded gazebo or terrace, and a garden or lawn. Site names and availability change with every renovation, so treat the current list from your wedding coordinator as the authority. What does not change is what each site does to your pictures.

The beach gives you the open horizon, which is why most couples come here, along with wind, chair legs that sink into soft sand, and light bouncing hard off pale sand into faces before four in the afternoon. The gazebo or terrace buys shade and weather insurance but creates a two or three stop gap between shaded faces and a bright background, which we manage with added light or by rotating the aisle. The garden or lawn is the underrated option: green, calm, sheltered from the breeze, and by far the best choice on a windy February afternoon.

Ask the coordinator which way the aisle faces and tell us. On a north-facing beach that answer changes everything about where the sun sits during your vows.

The Marina, the Dune and the Beach: Three Portrait Sets

Three distinct backgrounds sit within walking distance of each other here, which lets one twenty-five minute block produce three completely different looks.

The marina and waterway side gives hard lines: hulls, ropes, cleats, painted metal. It photographs best in the half hour before sunset when the light rakes low across the water and the boats throw long reflections. The dune and sea-grape scrub behind the beach gives green natural frames, backlight through the leaves and, crucially, no other guests in the picture. The beach is the wide open one and belongs to golden hour only. For the midday hours, the property's long shaded colonnades are the reliable fallback.

The one rule we never break

Nothing important gets scheduled between 11 AM and 3 PM. Overhead sun on pale Caribbean sand is the hardest light on this coast, and it flattens skin, blows the sky and drops shadows into every eye socket. Twenty-five minutes at golden hour outperforms two hours at noon, every single time.

Sargassum on This Shoreline vs Tulum

This is the practical reason we recommend Costa Mujeres to couples who are anxious about seaweed. Sargassum drifts in from the southeast, so it hits the east-facing coast first and hardest: Tulum, Akumal, Puerto Aventuras and much of Playa del Carmen take the worst of it. Playa Mujeres faces north and northeast and sits behind the shelter of Isla Mujeres and the bend in the coastline, so in a typical season it stays visibly clearer through the same weeks.

Two caveats, because honesty matters more than a good sales line. Clearer is not the same as clear. In the heaviest years, 2018 and 2022 among them, sargassum reached almost everywhere on this coast in some quantity. And conditions change week to week with wind direction, so a season forecast is a probability, not a promise. Check current-season reports close to your date and read our month-by-month Cancun sargassum guide while you are still choosing.

Vendor Fee, Day Pass and Approval Paperwork

Adults-only all-inclusive resorts almost always charge outside vendors, and Excellence-brand properties are no exception. It arrives as an outside-vendor fee, a per-person day pass, or a package that bundles the in-house photography team and prices any alternative separately.

Publishing a number here would be doing you a disservice, because it would be out of date within a season. Instead, ask this before you sign: is an outside photographer permitted at all, is the fee per event or per person, how many hours does a day pass cover, does a second shooter count separately, is proof of liability insurance required, and by what date must the studio be registered. Put the answers in the wedding contract. Our guide on whether resorts allow outside photographers walks through the conversation.

Sunset Direction and the Blue-Hour Step-Out

Here is the thing couples are surprised by. On this shoreline the sun rises out of the water and sets behind the property, over the mainland. There is no sun sinking into the sea in front of your ceremony. What the last half hour actually gives you is warm light reflecting off pale sand onto the couple, with a soft pastel sky opposite the sun, and that is a genuinely beautiful and slightly unusual look.

Then we use what comes next. Fifteen to twenty minutes after sunset the eastern sky over the water turns deep, saturated blue, and we bring the couple back outside for a short step-out: two or three frames lit with a warm flash against that blue. It takes four minutes, it usually happens between the first course and the speeches, and it is very often the picture that ends up on the wall.

Cancun-area sunset times run from roughly 6:06 PM in November to 7:32 PM in July, and Quintana Roo does not observe daylight saving, so we build the day around the sunset time for your specific date and work backwards from it.

Adding a Day-After Session at Isla Mujeres

Playa Mujeres sits close to the ferry terminals, and Isla Mujeres is a twenty to twenty-five minute crossing. A day-after session there is the best value add-on we offer on this side of the coast, because it gives you three backgrounds the resort cannot: the pale shallow water and sandbars of Playa Norte, the pastel painted streets of the town centre, and the low limestone cliffs at Punta Sur.

Practicalities: go in the morning, when the water is calmest and the streets are empty. Ferries run roughly every half hour. Budget four to five hours door to door, rent a golf cart on the island because taxis to Punta Sur add up, and note that Punta Sur charges an entry fee. Bring flat shoes for the limestone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there less sargassum at Playa Mujeres than in Tulum?
Usually yes. Sargassum drifts in from the southeast and hits the east-facing coast first, so Tulum, Akumal and much of Playa del Carmen take the worst of it. Playa Mujeres faces north and northeast behind the shelter of Isla Mujeres and typically stays clearer in the same weeks, though heavy years can reach everywhere.
How far is Excellence Playa Mujeres from Cancun airport?
Forty to fifty minutes by private transfer, longer than most Hotel Zone resorts. There is nothing walkable near the property, so guests who want to leave will need a taxi or an organised excursion. Most wedding groups here stay on site for the whole trip.
Which ceremony site photographs best at Excellence Playa Mujeres?
The beach for the open horizon, the garden or lawn when wind is a concern, and a shaded gazebo or terrace only if we can add light to close the gap between shaded faces and a bright background. Tell us which direction the aisle faces and we will tell you where the sun sits at your ceremony hour.
Do we see the sunset over the ocean from the beach?
No. On this shoreline the sun rises out of the water and sets behind the property over the mainland. The last half hour still gives you warm light bouncing off pale sand and a pastel sky opposite the sun, and about fifteen minutes after sunset we take the couple back out for a short blue-hour frame lit with warm flash.
Can we add an Isla Mujeres session the day after the wedding?
Yes, and it is our most requested add-on here. The ferry crossing is twenty to twenty-five minutes, mornings are calmest, and the island gives you Playa Norte's shallow water, the pastel streets of the town, and the cliffs at Punta Sur. Budget four to five hours door to door and rent a golf cart on arrival.

Three is a lovely number for a photograph. No rows to arrange, no sibling to coax back into the frame, nobody standing at the edge waiting for a turn: just the two of you and the person who made you a family, on a Cancun beach with the sea behind you. Which family of 3 poses work with one child, how long should the session be, and is there time for a photo of just the two of you? What Changes When There Is One Child

Three things change, and all of them work in your favor.

Every frame is a natural triangle

Three faces at three heights make a triangle, one of the steadiest shapes in a photograph. A parent standing, a parent kneeling, a child on a hip or a shoulder, and the frame composes itself. In a bigger family, the first minutes go into arranging heights; with three, they go into expressions.

Your child never waits for a sibling

With siblings, part of every session is waiting: one child is in the frame while another drifts toward the water. That is the quiet advantage of only child family photos: your child is in almost every frame, their good minutes go into the pictures instead of a queue, and the session moves at their pace.

There is time for every pairing

With three people, the list of groupings is short: the three of you, each parent with your child, your child alone, and the two of you. A 45-minute session covers most of them; an hour covers all of them without hurrying. As one father wrote:

“She captured the sweetest photos of our 11-month-old, my wife and me”

Ivan S., Google review

Family of 3 Photoshoot Ideas We Love

These are our favorite family of three beach photos. Pick the ones that suit your child's age and mood; we handle the direction, and you just play.

The three of you

  • The swing walk. Your child in the middle, holding both your hands, walking along the waterline. On “one, two, three,” you lift them over the next wave. The laugh on the lift is never posed.
  • The sandwich. Kneel facing each other with your child in between and squeeze in, cheek to cheek. Photographed close, the sea turns into a soft blur behind you.
  • Heads together. Lie on dry sand with your three heads touching, and we photograph from above: three faces, one small triangle. A little silliness goes a long way here.
  • Run to me. One parent holds your child a few steps back while the other kneels with open arms. The catch is the picture.
  • Three sets of footprints. Your feet at the waterline with your child's in between, or a small hand on top of yours. Details like these give a gallery its quiet pages.

Each parent with your child

Give each pairing its own energy: one big and active, like a child on a parent's shoulders with arms spread wide, as in the photo at the top of this page; the other quiet, like a cuddle while the two of them watch the waves. With one child, there is time for both. For a longer one-on-one session, see our guides to father and son photos and mother and daughter photos.

Your child, alone

A few portraits of your child on their own: running into the foam, filling a bucket, or looking up at you while you stand just behind us. A child looking at a parent, rather than at the lens, often gives the softest face.

The two of you

Since your child arrived, there may be few photos of just the two of you. Two ideas keep it easy. Make your child the director: they stand beside us and call out “kiss!” or “dance!”, and you follow orders. Or let them dig in the sand a few steps away, in the frame but out of focus, while you stand close. One parent remembered it this way:

“One cameraman was especially kind and continued capturing candid moments of my daughter while my partner and I were able to take a few photos together.”

Brennan W., Google review

Pacing Built for One Child, Not a Crowd

With one child, we plan the session around their energy rather than a long list of groupings. A simple order that works well with one child:

  1. Hello and play. A few easy minutes at the water, so your child gets used to us and to the sound of the camera.
  2. The three of you, while clothes are clean and everyone is fresh.
  3. Each parent with your child, one active set and one quiet one.
  4. Your child alone, often during a play break they choose.
  5. The two of you, with your child directing or digging nearby.
  6. A last walk, the three of you heading down the beach together.

If your child's mood dips, we change the order; a snack or a few minutes of sandcastle building usually resets things.

With an only child, every eye is on them. Some children love it; others feel watched, and a job helps: choosing the spot or directing the two of you. Our guide for shy or uncooperative kids goes further, and with a baby, plan around naps and feeds with our guide to family photos with a baby.

45 or 60 Minutes? An Honest Answer

How long should a family photoshoot be? For a family of three, usually 45 or 60 minutes, and these are the two collections that fit:

  • The Keepsake: 45 minutes, 25 edited photos, 1 look, 375 USD.
  • The Chapter: 60 minutes, 35 edited photos, 1 look, 475 USD, the collection we recommend for families.

Choose The Keepsake if what you want most is the three of you, each parent with your child and a few portraits of your child, and your child is happy in front of a camera. With nobody waiting for a sibling, 45 minutes covers a lot.

Choose The Chapter if you also want frames of just the two of you, if your child needs a slow warm-up or a break or two, or if you would like 35 photos instead of 25. With a baby or toddler, weigh it both ways: a shorter session asks less of your little one, and a longer one leaves room for feeds and resets.

What about The Postcard? At 35 minutes and 15 photos for 295 USD, it is made for couples. And if you book The Keepsake and decide you want The Chapter, you move up by paying only the difference: 100 USD. Frequently Asked Questions

What are good family of 3 photoshoot ideas?

Try the swing walk, lifting your child over a wave between you; a cheek-to-cheek sandwich hug; heads together on the sand, photographed from above; and a run into a parent's arms. Add each parent alone with your child, your child alone, and a few frames of the two of you.

How do you pose a family of three?

Think in triangles. Put the three faces at different heights, one parent standing, one kneeling, your child lifted or on a shoulder, and keep everyone close. Then add movement: walking, swinging, running. Three people never need to stand in a row.

How long should a family photoshoot be for a family of three?

Usually 45 or 60 minutes. The Keepsake is 45 minutes and 25 edited photos for 375 USD. The Chapter, our recommended family collection, is 60 minutes and 35 photos for 475 USD, with time for frames of just the two of you. The Postcard, at 35 minutes, is made for couples.

Can we get photos of just the two of us during a family session?

Yes. With one child there is time for a few frames of the two of you, especially in The Chapter. Your child can direct from beside us, calling out kiss or dance, or play in the sand nearby, in the frame but out of focus.

Do we pay a deposit to book?

No. There is no deposit: you pay at the end of the session, by card or in cash, and cancellation is free until one day before. A preview arrives within 24 hours, and the private 4K gallery in 1 to 3 business days.

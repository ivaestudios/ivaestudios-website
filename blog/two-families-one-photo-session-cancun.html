Two families on one trip make for a lively session: college roommates who now travel with kids, a brother's household and a sister's, or two couples who vacationed together long before anyone packed a car seat. Sooner or later someone says it: we should get photos while we are all here. Then come the fair questions. Does each family get enough time? Whose kids go first? Will one family go home with more pictures than the other? Here is how to share a family photo session between two households, so both leave with the frames they came for and neither feels like a guest at the other family's shoot.

One Session, Two Families

Yes, you can share. We have no limit on the number of people and no per-person charge, so a collection costs the same for one family of four as for two families of four together. Whether you call it a family photo group session or a friends and family group photoshoot in Cancun, a second household changes the list of frames and how much time you will want; it does not add a fee per head. One of you books, and the session covers everyone.

You could book one session per family instead. Each household would get its own session and its own gallery, at the price of two collections, but neither gallery would hold the frames only a shared session can make: both families in one picture, and all the kids together.

This guide is written for two households traveling together. If your group is closer to a reunion of 15 to 30 relatives, read how to book a reunion photographer in the Riviera Maya. If your group is adult friends celebrating at one resort, our guide to group photos at Devossion for birthdays and friends trips shows how we run that kind of session. And if grandparents are joining both families, our large family photoshoot guide covers bigger, multi-generation groups.

How We Share the Hour

Before the trip, we write the list of groupings with you. On the day, the order runs from the frame with the most people to the frame with the fewest, so the pictures that need everyone happen while everyone still has patience:

  1. Everyone together. Both families in one frame, first, while outfits are clean and the kids are fresh: once posed, once loose and laughing. This is the picture two separate bookings cannot make.
  2. All the kids together. They are already in place, so the parents step out and the children stay: cousins or best friends in stair steps, then one tangled hug. Some kids loosen up the moment the grown-ups leave the frame.
  3. Each family on its own. One household at a time, from the same list: the family together, the kids, each child alone, and the couple. The family with the youngest or sleepiest child usually goes first. That is not favoritism; it is nap time.
  4. The pairings that made the trip. The brother and sister who planned it, the two couples together, the friends who met in college. By now the children can play nearby while the adults finish.

While one family is on camera, the other can rest in the shade nearby, or help: a parent pulling faces behind the photographer earns more real laughs than any “say cheese.” If the kids are buzzing, we may swap steps. The list matters more than the order.

Keeping It Fair Between Households

Fair does not mean splitting the clock down the middle. A toddler who needs five minutes on a parent's lap should get them, and the other family can step in meanwhile, which makes two families more forgiving than one, not less. What we keep equal is the list: whatever one household gets, the other gets too. If one family has a family portrait, a frame of each child and a picture of the couple, so does the other, and each family adds its own two or three must-haves.

The edited photos in a collection cover the whole session, not each family, and there is no set number per person. The mirrored list is what keeps both families covered, and agreeing on it before the trip keeps the hour relaxed. In a Google review, Laura D. listed “capturing all the shots we had discussed beforehand” among the details coordinated for her family.

Send us both families' details

For each household: how many people, the children's ages, where you are staying, nap times, who tends to hang back, and the frames each family would hate to miss. We build the running order from your answers.

Which Collection Fits Two Families?

The Chapter, 60 minutes, 35 edited photos and one look for 475 USD, is the collection we recommend for a single family. Shared by two households, those 35 photos have to cover twice the list, so for two families we suggest starting one step up:

  • The Story: 75 minutes, 50 edited photos, 2 looks, 545 USD. A good fit for two smaller households with children close in age.
  • The Editorial: 90 minutes, 70 edited photos, 2 looks, 645 USD, and our best value. Our suggestion for two families with young children who need breaks, or with a wide spread of ages.
  • The Heirloom: 90 minutes, the complete edited gallery, 2 looks, 895 USD. With no fixed count, nobody has to wonder whether their family's favorites fit inside a number.

All three include two looks, and two families can use them well: one household can change while the other is on camera, so the second outfit costs the session very little time. You can move up to a larger collection by paying only the difference, and our guide to how many photos you get from a family session lays out all six collections side by side.

For outfits, agree on one palette across both families so the group frames read as one picture; the group at the top of this page chose all white. For one palette that works from toddlers to grandparents, see our guide to coordinating family outfits by age.

One Gallery, Both Households

A first preview arrives within 24 hours of the session, so both families have something to share, often before the trip is over. The full private 4K gallery follows in 1 to 3 business days, with unlimited downloads and print rights. Nobody has to ration: whoever receives the gallery can download every photo, the other family's frames included, and pass the files on, and each household can print its own copies at home. Decide before the trip who receives the gallery and shares it with the other family; it is one less message to chase once everyone has flown home.

Place, Time and Payment

For multi-family beach photos, the simplest plan is the resort where both families are staying: nobody has to buckle kids into a car. If you are at two different hotels, choose the one easiest for the family with the youngest children, and ask that hotel how the visiting family can come in for the session. Some hotels charge a fee for outside photographers; you pay it to the hotel, and we confirm that hotel's rule before the session. If you would rather go somewhere else together, we can arrange full-van transportation to another location for groups of up to 16 people, including several families traveling together.

Let the youngest child of either family set the start time. Online, you can book 6:30, 7:00 or 7:30 am, or 5:00 or 5:30 pm; other times can be arranged on WhatsApp. If one family is more at home in Spanish, we direct in English and Spanish.

There is no deposit, so neither family puts money down for the other before the trip. You pay at the end of the session, by card or in cash, and you can cancel free of charge up to one day before.

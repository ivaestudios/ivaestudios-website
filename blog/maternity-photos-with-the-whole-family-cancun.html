In short

First the two of you, for about 25 to 30 minutes while you are fresh. Then the family gathers around the bump: the cousins who will meet the baby, the grandparents-to-be, the siblings who will be aunts and uncles. Wear the one tone nobody else wears, start at 6:30 to 7:30 am or 5:00 to 5:30 pm, and choose The Story (75 minutes) or The Editorial (90 minutes) for a maternity look plus a family look. No limit on people, no per-person charge.

Sometimes the babymoon becomes a family trip: your parents fly in, your sister brings her kids, and the maternity photos you pictured for two suddenly have a guest list. Good news: the people who will hold this baby first belong in the pictures. Large family maternity photos just need their own plan, with the bump at the center of every frame and your comfort at the center of the schedule.

The Two of You First, Then the Family

In a big family session we usually photograph the full group first. At a maternity session with the whole family, we turn that around: your comfort sets the clock, so the two of you come first, while you are fresh.

Ask the family to arrive dressed about 25 to 30 minutes after we start. The little cousins don't wait through your portraits, the grandparents-to-be don't stand on the sand longer than needed, and you get the calm start a maternity portrait needs.

One exception: if this baby already has a big brother or sister who is still small, we start with the three of you while their patience lasts, and a grandparent who comes early can take over while we photograph the two of you.

Choose one organizer for the family side, ideally not you. Our family photo tips for large groups are written for that person, and how we organize a large family photoshoot covers the running order and the groupings.

Send us four things

The headcount, the ages of the youngest and the oldest, each household's hotel, and your must-have frames. Mobility needs can be shared privately.

Frames Around the Bump

These are the family maternity photoshoot ideas we suggest, roughly in the order we would take them. Cross out what doesn't fit and add the pairings only your family knows.

1. Everyone, with the bump at the center

The whole family in one frame, the two of you in the middle, everyone gathered close rather than spread along a row. Sit on a low wall or the sand if you like: seated, you stay comfortable and still at the center. We take it twice, once formal and once with a real laugh.

2. The cousins who will meet the baby

The cousins on the sand around you, one with an ear to the bump, the smallest whispering to the baby. Children get this game right away, and it works best early, while their patience lasts; then their parents can take them to play at the water's edge. An older brother or sister gets the spot closest to the bump.

3. Three generations, one on the way

The grandparents-to-be with the two of you, your hand on the bump. With both sets of grandparents, put one couple on each side: it reads like a family tree whose newest branch hasn't arrived yet.

4. Three hands on the bump

Your hand, your partner's and a grandmother's resting on the bump together, close up, with the sea soft behind. It takes a minute and needs no smile on command. Then a second version with a cousin's small hand.

5. The aunts and uncles to be

Your siblings and your partner's siblings around the two of you: the people this baby will call aunt and uncle. One frame close together, one walking at the waterline. On a big family trip, it may be the only time this year you all share a beach.

6. The grandparents-to-be, quietly

Last, when the beach has calmed down: each set of grandparents-to-be in turn with the two of you, and nobody else. An arm around a daughter's shoulders, a hand on the bump, a few words nobody else hears: the quietest frame of the session.

A Palette That Leads to You

With ten or sixteen people in a frame, the eye reads blocks of color before faces. Our large family color ideas put the softest tone on the grandparents at the center; at a maternity session, that place is yours. Family maternity photoshoot outfits that keep it that way:

  • One shared palette. Two or three soft tones that everyone wears in their own way, such as cream, sand and dusty blue. Coordinate, don't match.
  • Your tone is yours alone: one accent nobody else wears, such as blush beside cream, sand and dusty blue, or a deep rust beside neutrals. The eye goes there first.
  • Your dress is the one that moves: long, light and cut to follow the bump. Other women in the family can choose simpler cuts, shorter hems or linen trousers.
  • Your partner and the grandparents-to-be in the base tone, children in the lightest shades, in soft fabrics they can move in.
  • Leave at home logos, neon, small busy prints, turquoise, which disappears into the sea, and optical white head to toe.

An example for 14: the family in cream, sand and dusty blue, the cousins in cream, and you, the only one in blush, in a long dress. With two looks, only you and your partner change: your maternity look first, then a family look that sits with the palette, still in your own tone. A Short Rhythm for Heat and Comfort

Start early or late, when it is cooler and the light is softer: online booking offers 6:30, 7:00 and 7:30 am and 5:00 and 5:30 pm; for other times, message us on WhatsApp. A 75-minute session can divide like this:

  • Minutes 0 to 25, the two of you: the waterline, the profile of the bump, your partner's hands around it, portraits of you alone.
  • Minutes 25 to 35, a short pause: you change, ideally in a room close by, and rest in the shade while the family arrives dressed.
  • Minutes 35 to 75, the family: everyone first, then the cousins, the generations, the aunts and uncles and, last, the quiet frame with the grandparents-to-be.

With The Editorial's 90 minutes, give the two of you about 30 and the family about 50. Bring water and a folding fan: between groups you sit and rest while we arrange the next people around your spot, and if you need a break, we take one.

On a hot evening, shorter is fine, as one maternity client wrote:

“kept the session short and sweet while still managing to capture incredible pictures”

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the whole family join a maternity session in Cancun?

Yes. There is no limit on people and no per-person charge, so grandparents, siblings and cousins can all join. We suggest photographing the two of you first, while you are fresh, and then the family around the bump.

What should the family wear for maternity photos?

One shared palette of two or three soft tones, worn differently by each person, with the mother-to-be in the one tone nobody else wears and a long, light dress that follows the bump. Children wear the lightest shades.

How long does a family maternity session take?

We suggest The Story, 75 minutes with 50 edited photos and 2 looks for 545 USD, or The Editorial, 90 minutes with 70 edited photos and 2 looks for 645 USD. Plan about 25 to 30 minutes for the couple, a short break to change, and the rest for the family.

What time of day is best for family maternity photos in Cancun?

Early or late in the day, when it is cooler and the light is softer. Online booking offers 6:30, 7:00 and 7:30 am and 5:00 and 5:30 pm; for other times, message us on WhatsApp.

Our family is staying at different hotels. Can you help?

Yes. For groups of up to 16 people we can arrange transportation in a full van to the session location, priced on request. If everyone is at one resort, its beach is usually the simplest choice.

When in the pregnancy should we plan the session?

That is a question for your doctor or midwife, as is whether to fly. On the day, we pace the session around how you feel. There is no deposit, you pay at the end, and cancellation is free up to one day before.

Cancun With Kids for the First Time: What to Know

In short

In winter the sun sets in Cancun around 6 pm, and the clock never changes. Plan your airport ride before you land, pay in pesos, and pay the Visitax, 293 pesos per person with babies included, on the official portal, visitax.gob.mx. Install WhatsApp, read the beach flag before anyone swims, expect seaweed mostly in summer and cold fronts in winter, and book family photos for day 2 or 3.

A first family trip to Cancun comes with a few surprises the resort brochure leaves out, and each one is easier when you know it before you land. Here are ten, each with a short answer and a link to our longer guide; the official sources we checked are listed at the end.

Arriving: The Airport and the Map

1. The arrivals-hall sales pitch

Decide how you will reach your hotel before you fly, so there is nothing to negotiate between customs and the curb. If anyone offers a free ride or a cheap tour in exchange for your time, a polite no and a steady walk are enough. Canada's travel advice warns that timeshare sellers in Mexico can be very persistent and use pressure tactics, and notes that a timeshare contract can be cancelled without penalty within 5 working days. Our guide to getting around Cancun with kids covers taxis, Uber, private transfers with car seats and rental cars.

2. Distances are bigger than the map suggests

The famous day trips are real drives. INAH, the federal institute in charge of Mexico's archaeological sites, puts the Tulum ruins 128 km (about 80 miles) south of Cancun, and Xcaret places its park 74 km (45 miles) south of the airport, about an hour by road. With small children, we suggest one long outing per trip; check the drive from your hotel before you book anything. See Mayan ruins with kids and the Xcaret parks with kids.

Money, Fees and Phones

3. Pesos, cards and the dollar question

Mexico runs on pesos. When a card terminal or ATM offers to charge you in US or Canadian dollars instead, you can say no: Visa says that offer includes an exchange rate and additional fees, that merchants and ATMs should let you choose and must not choose for you, and that declining does not affect your ability to pay or withdraw cash. Ask your bank about foreign transaction fees before you fly, and follow Canada's advice to avoid withdrawing or exchanging money in the airport's public areas. Our guide to Canadian dollars in Cancun covers cards, cash and ATMs, and our Cancun family vacation cost guide prices a whole week.

4. The Visitax, babies included

Quintana Roo, the state Cancun is in, charges foreign visitors a fee called the Visitax. On the official portal, visitax.gob.mx, it is 293 pesos per person, about 16 USD, and the portal's FAQ says there is no child rate: all foreign tourists pay, infants included. For a family of four, that is 1,172 pesos, about 63 USD. Pay on the official .gob.mx portal and keep each traveler's QR receipt on your phone. Our Visitax and tourist fees guide adds the federal fee already in your airfare and the ruin and reef fees.

5. WhatsApp and your phone plan

Before you fly, check whether your phone plan covers Mexico or set up an eSIM, and install WhatsApp. We answer there in English or Spanish, and session times outside our online slots are arranged there. Our WhatsApp, eSIM and phone plan guide has the details.

Sun, Sea and Weather

6. Winter sunsets near 6 pm

From mid-November to mid-January, the sun sets in the Hotel Zone between 6:04 and 6:26 pm, according to US Naval Observatory data; in early July it sets at 7:33 pm. Mexico's time zone law keeps Quintana Roo on UTC-5 all year, so from the first Sunday of November to the second Sunday of March, Cancun matches New York's clock, and the rest of the year it runs an hour behind. In winter, a 60-minute session that starts at 5:00 or 5:30 pm, our two online evening times, ends around sunset, in time for an early dinner. See Cancun sunset times by month and Cancun's time zone and your kids' body clock.

7. Red flags and calm shores

Cancun's Civil Protection office says that on public beaches, municipal lifeguards keep watch from 9:00 am to 5:00 pm and put up three flags: green, safe to swim; yellow, caution; red, entering the sea is prohibited. A beach without a lifeguard may have no flag at all: Canada's travel advice warns that riptides are common in Mexico, that beaches are not always supervised and that many have no warning flags. If a current pulls you, the same office advises staying calm, not fighting it, swimming parallel to the shore, calling for help and floating until it weakens. Some beaches are much calmer than others; our guide to the safest Cancun beaches for kids shows where.

8. Seaweed months

Sargassum, the brown seaweed that drifts onto Caribbean beaches, has a season. In the Atlantic seaweed belt it typically peaks in June or July and is lowest in winter, according to the University of South Florida's Sargassum Watch, and the lab's October 31, 2026 outlook expected amounts to keep falling until possibly the end of November or December. It changes from beach to beach and day to day, so check close to your dates: our beach-by-beach sargassum report covers the months and the shores that stay clearest, and beach conditions today links the official maps and bulletins.

9. Nortes: winter cold fronts

Mexico's cold-front season typically runs from September to May. For 2026 to 2027, the national weather service, SMN, forecast 56 fronts, about 32 of them from November to February, and it sees more frequent so-called Norte events, which could bring stronger wind, higher waves and rain to the Gulf of Mexico coast. A front over the Yucatan Peninsula can mean rain, gusts and higher waves: in its forecast of November 13, 2026, for example, the SMN had front No. 5 stalling over the peninsula, with heavy rain expected in northern Quintana Roo, where Cancun is, on November 16 and 17. Check the forecast the day before a beach day or a session; Cancun in December explains how a norte feels on the coast.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do kids pay the Visitax in Cancun?

Yes. The official portal, visitax.gob.mx, charges 293 pesos per person, about 16 USD, and its FAQ says there is no child rate: all foreign tourists pay, infants included. For a family of four that is 1,172 pesos, about 63 USD at the November 13, 2026 exchange rate.

What time is sunset in Cancun in winter?

From mid-November to mid-January, between 6:04 and 6:26 pm in the Hotel Zone, according to US Naval Observatory data. Quintana Roo stays on UTC-5 all year, so Cancun matches New York's clock from the first Sunday of November to the second Sunday of March.

What do the beach flags mean in Cancun?

According to Cancun's municipal Civil Protection office, green means safe to swim, yellow means caution and red means entering the sea is prohibited. Lifeguards watch public beaches from 9:00 am to 5:00 pm and put up the flags, so a beach without a lifeguard may have no flag at all.

Is Cancun safe for a first family trip?

Read both governments' advice before you book. The US State Department's advisory for Mexico, issued May 29, 2026, is Level 2, exercise increased caution, the same advice it gives for Quintana Roo, where Cancun is. Canada's advice, updated October 19, 2026, is to exercise a high degree of caution in Mexico, and its regional advisories do not include Quintana Roo.

When should we book family photos on a first trip to Cancun?

On day 2 or 3, after the arrival day. The sneak peek arrives within 24 hours and the full gallery in 1 to 3 business days, and cancellation is free up to one day before if the weather turns. There is no deposit: you pay at the end of the session, by card or in cash.

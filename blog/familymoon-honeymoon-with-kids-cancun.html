What a Familymoon Is, and Why It Needs Two Kinds of Photos

A familymoon is a honeymoon the children come on. Sometimes they are kids you had together before you married; often they come from an earlier relationship, on one side or both, and the wedding made a step-parent official. Either way, the trip holds two stories: a marriage that just began and a family that has been taking shape for a while.

So we plan a familymoon session in two halves: first the new family, then the two of you, with the newlywed portraits you would have had on a honeymoon for two. One time slot, one stretch of beach, one gallery that tells the whole story in order. If the wedding itself is in Mexico, our guide to combining your wedding and honeymoon in Mexico covers the days in between.

Kids First, Then the Two of You

Kids are usually at their best early, so the family look goes first: everyone together while the outfits are still clean, then each parent with the kids, then the siblings. Once the kids have had their turn, the two of you change, and the second half slows down into newlywed portraits.

The practical question: who watches the kids while we photograph the two of you? If a grandparent or a friend came on the trip, that is your handoff. If it is just your family, the kids can stay a few steps away, building a sandcastle beside you or racing ahead while you walk behind them hand in hand; tell us when you book, so we can plan those frames. A New Family, Often a Blended One

Many familymoon families are blended: the wedding just made a step-parent official, and the family half of your session may hold the first professional portrait of all of you as one family. A honeymoon session raises two questions for a blended family that a regular vacation session does not, and both are easier to settle at home than on the sand:

  • Which family frame matters most? For some families it is everyone together, in the week the family became official; for others it is the step-parent with the kids. Tell us which one before the trip, and we make it early in the session, while everyone is fresh.
  • Who stays for the romantic half? Kisses, the wedding dress and the slow dance belong to the second half. If an older child would rather not watch that part, make it the moment of the handoff to a grandparent or a friend, before the change of clothes, so it happens quietly, as part of the plan.

Before you fly

If a child's other parent is not on the trip, plan the paperwork too. The Government of Canada recommends that children traveling abroad without one or both parents carry a consent letter, signed by each parent or guardian who is not on the trip. It is not legally required in Canada, but immigration authorities, airline agents and Canadian officials may ask for one, and a notary public as witness is strongly recommended. The same page notes that some countries have their own entry and exit rules for children and advises checking them with the destination country's embassy or consulate (travel.gc.ca, page modified September 2, 2026, checked November 14, 2026). Flying from the United States or elsewhere? Check Mexico's current requirements with a Mexican consulate and ask your airline before you fly.

Familymoon Photo Ideas for the Beach

Honeymoon photos with children work best when the kids have a job. Add the ideas that fit your family to the list you send before the trip:

  • The rings in small hands. Both wedding bands in a child's palm, a detail shot to open the gallery.
  • The date in the sand. The kids write the wedding date and everyone's initials at the waterline, and we photograph it before a wave takes it.
  • The first dance, again. The two of you dance barefoot at the water's edge, and the kids cut in.
  • The wedding outfits, one more time. If the kids were the flower girl or the ring bearer, bring those clothes for look one.
  • Just the two of you. Close, quiet portraits with nobody else in the frame, the kind a honeymoon is for.

Where to Stay, and How to Book

Choosing a resort for a familymoon in Mexico starts with one question: does it welcome children? Adults-only properties set a minimum guest age, so check it before you book; for a family honeymoon at an all-inclusive in Cancun, look for a resort that welcomes children and still gives the two of you some quiet corners. Some hotels charge their own fee for outside photographers, which you pay to the hotel; we confirm your resort's rule before the session.

When you write, tell us your wedding date, your resort, the kids' names and ages, who else is on the trip and any grouping we should skip. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a familymoon?

A familymoon is a honeymoon that includes the children, whether you had them together before the wedding or they come from an earlier relationship. For photos, it means two stories in one session: the two of you as newlyweds and the first portraits of your new family.

Can we get couple photos and family photos in the same session?

Yes. The Story (75 minutes, 50 edited photos, 545 USD) and The Editorial (90 minutes, 70 edited photos, 645 USD) both include two looks: one with the kids and one for the two of you. We photograph the family first, while the kids are fresh.

Do the kids add to the price?

No. There is no limit on the number of people and no per-person charge, so children and any relatives on the trip add no cost. You pay us only for the collection, with no deposit: payment is at the end of the session, by card or in cash.

How soon can we share the photos with our wedding guests?

A preview arrives within 24 hours of the session, so a first photo of the new family can reach your guests while the wedding is still fresh. The private 4K gallery follows in 1 to 3 business days, with unlimited downloads and print rights.

How do you photograph a blended family on a honeymoon?

In two halves. The family half comes first and starts with the frame that matters most to you, such as everyone together or the step-parent with the kids, and nobody is pushed into closeness they are not ready for. The newlywed portraits come second; if an older child would rather not watch, a grandparent or a friend can take over before the change of clothes.

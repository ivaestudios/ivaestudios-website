Riviera Maya vs Playa del Carmen: What People Actually Mean

In short

They are not two places to choose between. The Riviera Maya is the stretch of coast running south from Cancun, and Playa del Carmen is the town in the middle of it. So Playa is in the Riviera Maya, the way a city is in a region. What people are really asking is whether to stay in a walkable town with a main street, or in a resort on a private stretch of sand with nothing around it.

Why the comparison confuses everyone

If you search for the difference between the Riviera Maya and Playa del Carmen you will get a lot of confident answers, and most of them are comparing a region with a town inside it. That is why the question keeps getting asked.

Think of it the way you would think of a coastline and a city on it. The Riviera Maya is the coast. Playa del Carmen is the town. Asking which is better is a bit like asking whether you should visit the coast or the town on it.

What people mean, when you get down to it, is something much more useful: do I want to stay somewhere with a street, or somewhere with a beach and nothing else?

What the Riviera Maya actually is

The Riviera Maya is the run of Caribbean coast in the state of Quintana Roo that begins just south of Cancun and continues south for roughly a hundred and thirty kilometres, ending around Tulum. Puerto Morelos is on it. Playa del Carmen is on it. Akumal, Puerto Aventuras and the Mayakoba development are all on it.

Note what is not on it: Cancun. Cancun sits just north of where the Riviera Maya begins, which is why hotels there call themselves Cancun and not Riviera Maya, and why the airport serves both. Tulum is usually counted as the southern end, though people argue about that at the edges.

The character of the coast changes as you go south. The northern half is resort corridor: long private beaches, big all-inclusive properties, and a highway a few minutes inland with jungle on both sides. Further south it gets lower, quieter and more boutique.

What Playa del Carmen is actually like

Playa del Carmen is a real town of well over a hundred thousand people, about an hour south of Cancun airport, and it is the busiest place on the Riviera Maya.

Its centre of gravity is Quinta Avenida, Fifth Avenue, a long pedestrian street one block back from the beach, lined with restaurants, bars and shops for several kilometres. You can walk out of your hotel and be somewhere in two minutes, which is exactly what a resort on a private beach cannot offer.

The ferry to Cozumel leaves from here, which makes it a natural base if you want to dive. The beach in front of the town is public and busy. North of the centre, toward Playacar and beyond, it gets quieter and more resort-like.

The trade-off is honest in both directions. You get life, choice and the ability to leave your hotel on foot. You also get noise, vendors on the sand and a beach that belongs to everybody.

How to choose between them

Choose Playa del Carmen if you want to walk to dinner, you like having thirty restaurants rather than four, you are diving Cozumel, or the idea of eating every meal at the same resort makes you restless.

Choose a resort elsewhere on the Riviera Maya if you want a quiet stretch of sand, you are travelling with small children, you want the all-inclusive to be the whole plan, or you are getting married and need a property that can hold the whole group.

And a note on Cancun, since it is usually the third option in the same conversation: Cancun is closer to the airport, has the wider beaches and the bigger waves, and its Hotel Zone faces east, which changes the light entirely. The Riviera Maya coast faces roughly the same way, so the same rule about hours applies down the whole coast.

How each one photographs

We work both, most weeks, and they give you genuinely different pictures.

A resort stretch on the Riviera Maya gives you empty sand. At sunrise you will often have several hundred metres of beach to yourselves, with palms and nothing else in frame. That is the cleanest, quietest kind of session there is, and it is why so much of what you see from this coast is made on those beaches.

A session in Playa del Carmen is a different genre. The town itself is the backdrop: the pedestrian street, the doorways, the light between buildings early in the morning. It photographs like a place people live in, not like a postcard. For couples who want something with texture rather than sand and horizon, it is the better choice, and it has to be done early, before Quinta Avenida wakes up.

The one thing both share is the hour. This whole coast faces east, so the sun rises out of the water in front of you and sets behind you, inland. Sunrise or the last ninety minutes of afternoon. Never noon.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Playa del Carmen in the Riviera Maya?

Yes. The Riviera Maya is the stretch of coast running south from Cancun to roughly Tulum, and Playa del Carmen is the largest town on it, about halfway along. They are not alternatives to each other: one is a region, the other is a town inside it.

Is Cancun part of the Riviera Maya?

No. Cancun sits just north of where the Riviera Maya begins. The two share an airport and are often sold together, which is why the names get mixed, but hotels in Cancun call themselves Cancun for a reason.

How far is Playa del Carmen from Cancun airport?

Roughly an hour by road, depending on traffic and where exactly you are staying. Resorts in the northern Riviera Maya, around Puerto Morelos and Mayakoba, are closer, and Tulum at the southern end is considerably further.

Which is better for families, Playa del Carmen or a Riviera Maya resort?

For small children, a resort on a private stretch of the Riviera Maya usually wins: calmer beach, everything on site, and no road to cross. Playa del Carmen suits families with older children who want to walk out, eat in different places and take the ferry to Cozumel.

Does the beach face the same way in both?

Yes. The whole coast from Cancun south faces roughly east, so the sun rises out of the Caribbean in front of the beach and sets behind it over land. That is why photo sessions here are booked at sunrise or in the last ninety minutes of the afternoon, and never at midday.

Is Tulum part of the Riviera Maya?

Usually counted as its southern end, though people disagree at the edges. In practice Tulum is far enough south, and different enough in character, that most visitors treat it as its own destination.

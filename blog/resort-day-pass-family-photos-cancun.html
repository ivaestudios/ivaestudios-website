In short

A day pass covers your family, not a photographer. Before buying one for photos, ask the resort in writing whether an outside photographer may work there, whether they need their own pass and whether a fee applies. Some passes are adults-only, and RIU says its hotels admit only staying guests; when the answers do not fit, a public beach is simpler.

Staying in a rental, a condo or a downtown hotel, many families buy one resort day for the pools and the beach, then ask us: can the photographer come too? Sometimes. A day pass is the resort's product and so are the rules, so here is what the resorts' own pages, and one booking site, showed on November 14, 2026, what to ask before you pay, and when the public beach next door is easier.

Which Cancun Resorts Sell a Day Pass, and What It Includes

There is no standard day pass in Cancun. Each resort decides whether it sells one, to whom, for which hours and what it includes: an all-inclusive style day, a credit for one restaurant or a water park ticket. The best one for you depends on your children's ages and on whether the resort allows a photographer. These are the five resorts we checked:

None of these pages says whether you may bring a photographer; the closest is Aqua Nick's FAQ, which bans selfie sticks and drones in the water park. So a day pass alone does not tell you whether you can. Read the fine print, too: the banner on Oasis's day pass page says the pass is exclusive for local residents, with each adult showing an INE, the Mexican voter ID, while the list below it also sells passes without a residents' label. Prices move as well: SLS Cancun charges more from Friday to Sunday, so confirm yours with the resort for your date before you pay. And if a resort's own site lists no day pass, as with Club Med Cancun, ask the resort to confirm the one you found, in writing, before you buy it.

Does the Photographer Need a Pass Too?

The pass covers the people on it. A photographer working on the property is a separate question, and only the resort can answer it, so ask three things: whether an outside photographer may work there, whether they need a pass of their own and whether a fee applies. Some hotels charge a fee for an outside photographer; where yours does, you pay it to the hotel, not to us, and we confirm the hotel's rule with you before the session.

The pools, gardens, loungers and palapas belong to the hotel, so its rules apply there; the beach itself is public in Mexico, which makes the beach next door the usual backup plan.

Couples ask the same question; our answer for them is in Can You Bring an Outside Photographer for Couples Photos? The message below adds what a family needs: the children's ages.

How to Ask the Resort, in Writing

An email gives you an answer to show at the gate. Before you pay, write to the resort's guest services or day pass contact:

A message you can send

Hello, we would like a day pass for [date] for [number] adults and [number] children, ages [ages], and would like our photographer, IVAE Studios, to photograph us for about an hour at [time]. Could you confirm in writing whether an outside photographer is allowed, whether they need a day pass, whether a fee applies and how it is paid, which areas we may use, and the hours the pass covers? Thank you!

Send us the reply, or send us the resort's name and your date and we will ask the resort ourselves; either way, we confirm the hotel's rule with you before the session.

Match the hours to the light, too. Our online slots are 6:30, 7:00 and 7:30 am and 5:00 and 5:30 pm, with other times on WhatsApp. The Oasis passes open at 9:00 am and the Club Med day pass at 10:00 am, and both end at 6:00 pm, so a 7:00 am session falls outside them and an evening session has to finish by 6:00 pm. Each also sells a longer option: the Oasis extended schedule runs to 11:00 pm, and Club Med's full-day pass on ResortPass runs to midnight, from 3,837 MXN for ages 12 and up.

When the Public Beach Is Simpler

Mexico's General Law of National Property makes beaches, and the federal zone of 20 meters of land beside them, property for common use, and says access to them may not be inhibited, restricted, obstructed or conditioned except as the regulations provide (articles 7, 8 and 119).

In practice, you reach the sand through a public access, not through a hotel lobby, and the hotel's loungers and pools stay off limits. Our guide to photos on a public beach in Mexico explains where that line usually falls; it was written with weddings in mind. The public beach is usually the easier plan when:

  • the pass is adults-only, as at SLS Cancun, or the hotel admits only staying guests, as RIU's FAQ says of its hotels;
  • the resort does not allow outside photographers, or the pass and fee for the photographer cost more than the resort backdrop is worth to you;
  • the pass hours miss the early or late light;
  • you are renting on or near the beach, where the sand in front of your building may be all you need. Our guides for Cancun residences and guesthouses and Playa del Carmen guesthouses show the spots near rentals.

The sea, the sand and the light are the same on both sides of the property line; what a resort adds is gardens, pools and architecture.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can we bring a photographer on a resort day pass in Cancun?

Only if the resort allows it. A day pass covers the people on it, and none of the day pass pages we read on November 14, 2026 say anything about bringing a photographer. Ask the resort in writing whether an outside photographer may work there, whether they need a pass and whether a fee applies; we confirm the hotel's rule with you before the session.

Who pays if the resort charges for the photographer?

You do, directly to the hotel. Some hotels charge a fee for an outside photographer, and it is separate from our price: our collections run from 295 to 895 USD, with no deposit and no per-person charge. We confirm the hotel's rule with you before your date.

Does Club Med Cancun sell a day pass?

Not on its own website: we found none on Club Med's Cancun pages, US or Mexico site, on November 14, 2026. ResortPass listed one that day, an all-inclusive day pass from 10:00 am to 6:00 pm, from 2,169 MXN for ages 12 and up and 1,057 MXN for ages 4 to 11, with infants free. Confirm it with the resort before you pay, and ask about outside photographers in the same message.

Is there a Riu day pass in Cancun?

Not according to RIU. Its FAQ page, read on November 14, 2026, says only guests can access its hotels and enjoy their facilities, and the Riu Cancun page describes an adults-only hotel. For a family, a resort that sells a pass or a public beach is the better plan.

Is there a day pass for Nickelodeon Riviera Maya?

The official sites we read sell visitors a ticket to Aqua Nick, the resort's water park, not a resort day pass, and the tickets say admission only to the water park. On November 14, 2026, the Aqua Park ticket, with entry from 1:00 pm, was 2,572 MXN for ages 10 and up and 1,832 MXN for ages 3 to 9.

Can we do family photos on a public beach instead?

Yes, and it is often simpler. Under Mexico's General Law of National Property, beaches and the 20-meter federal zone beside them are for common use, and access to them may not be restricted except as the regulations provide. Reach the sand through a public access, not a hotel lobby.

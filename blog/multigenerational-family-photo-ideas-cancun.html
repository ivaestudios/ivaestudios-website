In short

Three generations means grandparents, their children and their grandchildren; a multigenerational family brings three or more together. The frames worth planning are the ones you can only take while everyone shares one beach: the generations line, three generations of hands, each grandchild with the grandparents, the original family, every household, the cousins and the eldest alone. Here are 14 ideas and a shot list for 10 to 16 people.

On most big family trips there is one evening when everyone is finally on the same stretch of sand: the grandparents who started it all, the grown children who flew in from different cities, the partners and the cousins. These are the frames we suggest when three generations book a session with us in Cancun, the ones hardest to recreate once everyone flies home.

Our multigenerational planning guide covers the order and timing of the day, and the three-generation portrait has a page of its own; this one is about what to photograph.

Three Generations, in Two Lines

What are three generations in a family? Grandparents, their children and their grandchildren. When a great-grandparent comes along it becomes four, and the generations line, the hands and the same-pose frame each gain one more person.

What is a multigenerational family? One that brings three or more of those generations together. The US Census Bureau draws the same line for households, “three or more generations under one roof”, in a June 13, 2023 article (checked November 14, 2026). On a trip, the roof is usually a resort.

Frames That Show the Whole Line

1. The generations line, oldest to youngest

Everyone in a single row, from the eldest to the youngest grandchild, the baby held at the end. Facing the camera, it is a portrait. Turned in profile, everyone looking the same way down the beach, it reads like a family tree you can hang on a wall. It works for six people or sixteen.

2. Three generations of hands

A grandparent's hand, a parent's hand and a child's hand, stacked or linked in a chain, with the sea soft behind them. A wedding ring, a watch, a small fist: the details tell the ages without a single face. It is quick, nobody has to smile on command, and it prints beautifully small.

3. The same pose at three ages

Grandfather, father and son side by side in the same stance, hands in pockets, looking out to sea. Then grandmother, mother and daughter, each with an arm around the next. Repeating the pose makes the likeness jump out: the same shoulders, the same tilt of the head, at three ages. Any line works, and with a toddler all three can simply sit on the sand.

4. A walk at the waterline, in generation order

The whole family walks along the wet sand at the water's edge: grandparents in front, then their children, then the grandchildren. Walking gives everyone something to do, so nobody has to hold a smile, and wet sand is firmer underfoot than dry sand. If a grandparent prefers to stand still, keep it to one short pass.

Frames Built Around the Grandparents

5. The couple who started it all

The grandparents alone, before anyone else joins them: walking, sitting close, or laughing at something a grandchild shouts from behind the camera. Put it near the top of your list: in a crowd of grandchildren, it is an easy one to forget.

6. The grandparents with each grandchild

One frame per grandchild with the grandparents: a baby on a lap, a six-year-old holding hands at the water's edge, a teenager with an arm around grandpa. Each one is quick, and each household goes home with a picture that belongs only to their child. Then all the grandchildren at once, gathered around the grandparents. More in grandparents and grandkids beach portraits.

7. Seated, with grandchildren on their laps

The grandparents sitting comfortably, on a bench, a low wall or the edge of a deck, with the youngest grandchildren on their laps and the older ones close behind. Sitting brings faces together and spares tired knees. If someone uses a cane, a walker or a wheelchair, see family photos with less mobile grandparents.

8. The original family

The grandparents with their grown children only, no partners and no grandchildren: the family as it looked before it grew. Adult siblings rarely stand together like this anymore, which is exactly why it belongs on the list. Then let the grandparents step out for one frame of the siblings alone.

9. A quiet portrait of the eldest

One portrait of the eldest member of the family alone, unhurried, looking at the sea or watching the grandchildren play at the water. No smile required. On a day full of noise and little ones, it is the quietest frame of the session, and it may become the most treasured.

Frames for Every Branch

10. Each household on its own

Every couple with their own children, one frame each, so every branch of the family goes home with a portrait of just them for their own wall. Add the grandparents to each household for a second version if you like: one family, two pictures.

11. The cousins together

All the grandchildren without the adults, arranged however they like: oldest to youngest, tallest at the back, the teenagers holding the babies. It is the frame that changes most from one trip to the next, so it is worth taking every time the cousins share a beach.

12. The run to the grandparents

The grandparents wait near the water, seated or standing, with their arms open, and the grandchildren run to them from down the beach. Small children understand the game immediately, and the hug at the end is real. Run it two or three times; no two runs look the same.

The Big Frame, and One Real Laugh

13. Everyone, with the grandparents at the center

The full family in one frame, the grandparents at the center and each household gathered around them rather than spread along a row. With 10 to 16 people, a mix of sitting, kneeling and standing keeps every face visible. Take it twice: once looking at the camera, once relaxed. More whole-group arrangements are in our family reunion photo ideas.

14. A laugh on cue

Every family has a trigger: grandpa's oldest joke, a grandchild counting down in Spanish, everyone shouting the dog's name at once. Write yours on the shot list and use it right after the formal version of the big frame. Same sixteen people, but suddenly they look like themselves.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are 3 generations in a family?

Grandparents, their children and their grandchildren. In a photo session that means the grandparents, their grown children and partners, and all the grandkids.

What is a multigenerational family?

A family that brings three or more generations together. The US Census Bureau uses the same measure for households: three or more generations under one roof (June 2023, checked November 14, 2026).

What should be on an extended family photo shot list?

The full group, the generations line, the grandparents as a couple and with each grandchild, the original family, each household, the cousins, three generations of hands and a quiet portrait of the eldest. Add names, then send us the list before the trip.

Is there a limit on people or a charge per person?

No. There is no limit on people and no per-person charge. For 14 to 16 people, the two 90-minute collections leave the most time: The Editorial (70 photos, 645 USD) or The Heirloom (the complete edited gallery, 895 USD). For groups of up to 16, we can arrange full-van transportation, priced on request.

What time of day is best for three generations on a Cancun beach?

Early morning or late afternoon, when it is cooler and the light is softer. Online booking offers 6:30, 7:00 and 7:30 am, and 5:00 and 5:30 pm; for other times, message us on WhatsApp.

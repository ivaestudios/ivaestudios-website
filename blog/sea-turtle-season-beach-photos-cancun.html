In short

Sea turtles nest on Cancun's beaches from May to October or November, and eggs hatch about two months later; in 2025 the city released its first hatchlings in mid-June and protected 10,438 nests in 53 corrals. Releases follow the nests, not a timetable: hands off, 2 meters back, no flash. For photos, we plan sunrise or the hour before sunset, clear of the nests.

If you visit between late spring and fall, three turtle questions come up: when they nest, whether the kids can see a release, and whether a beach session could disturb a nest. Here are the answers from the City of Cancun, Mexico's federal rules and NOAA, and how we plan photos on a beach that doubles as a nursery.

When Turtles Nest and Hatch in Cancun

The City of Cancun says the nesting season runs from May to October or November each year. Green turtles dominate: of the 10,438 nests the city protected in 2025, 10,350 were green, 73 loggerhead and 15 hawksbill.

NOAA Fisheries, a U.S. government agency, says green and loggerhead eggs hatch after about two months in the warm sand. The first hatchlings can come early: the city released its first 84 of 2025 on Saturday, June 14, at Playa Marlin. Hatching then builds through the summer, late nests keep hatching after nesting winds down, and the city's turtle program keeps a 24-hour watch from May to December.

Roped-Off Nests and Corrals on Resort Beaches

Mexico's federal standard for nesting beaches, NOM-162-SEMARNAT-2012, prefers that eggs incubate where they were laid, and treats moving them to a corral, a fenced area of beach where clutches incubate until the hatchlings emerge, as the exception. On Cancun's busy shore, corrals do much of the work: in 2025 the city's ecology department protected 10,438 nests using 53 corrals, with 930 day and night patrols, and released more than 1.1 million hatchlings from 1,238,306 eggs.

The standard asks for each corral to be fenced with mesh 2 meters high and moved to a new spot every year, so last summer's pen may be somewhere else. On the sand:

  • Give nests room. No digging, ball games or climbing beside a mesh pen or a staked nest.
  • Leave the beach flat. NOAA asks beachgoers to fill in holes, knock down sandcastles and take chairs and umbrellas off the sand, so turtles and hatchlings are not trapped or turned away.
  • Report what you see. If you spot a turtle nesting, stranded or in trouble, or hatchlings on the sand, the city asks you to call 911 or tell its ecology or Zofemat (beach zone) staff, or the hotel's staff.

Lights Out, No Flash, Keep Your Distance

NOAA explains that hatchlings find the sea by crawling toward the brightest horizon, and that artificial light can send them inland and keep nesting females from coming ashore. Mexico's standard asks nesting beaches to remove, redirect or modify lights that shine onto the sand at night, aiming them down and away from the beach and using shields, low-wattage bulbs, or yellow or red light. If your resort's beach is darker than expected on a summer night, this is why.

At an organized turtle walk or release, the standard sets the rules for visitors, kids included:

  • Hands off. Visitors may not handle hatchlings, not even to release them.
  • Two meters back. Stay at least 2 meters behind the hatchlings, never step on them or block their way, and let them cross the wet sand to the sea on their own.
  • No flash, no lights. No flash photos at any point during the walk, and no lights for visitors; only the guide carries one, with a red filter.
  • Small and quiet. Groups of up to 10 walk in a compact line, together and quiet. Near a nesting female, wait at least 10 meters away until she starts laying.

Will You See a Release?

Maybe, and planning for a maybe works best. The standard says hatchlings go to the sea with minimal handling as soon as they have surfaced and are active, which takes about an hour on average, so a release happens when a nest is ready, not on a schedule. Organized turtle watching is run by holders of a federal wildlife authorization, with their own guides.

To raise your odds, travel from July into the fall, stay on a beach with a corral, and ask guest services early whether guests are told when a nest hatches. If it happens, it is a gift; if not, the kids can still spot the corrals and learn why the beach goes dark at night.

Swimming With Turtles in Akumal Bay

The other way to meet turtles is snorkeling with them in Akumal Bay, about 37 kilometers south of Playa del Carmen. Under a federal decree published on March 7, 2016, 1,653 hectares of sea off Akumal and nearby towns form a refuge for green, loggerhead and hawksbill turtles, which nest, feed and breed there, and for its corals, mangroves and seagrass. The decree leaves the conditions for activities in the bay to a protection program from CONANP, Mexico's protected areas commission.

Quintana Roo's tourism board says snorkeling in the turtle area takes an authorized guide, in small groups, and NOAA's advice holds in the water too: admire turtles from a respectful distance and never touch or feed them. Ask the guide for the current rules, the youngest age they take and what children wear in the water.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is turtle nesting season in Cancun?

May to October or November each year, says the City of Cancun. Almost all nests are green turtles: 10,350 of the 10,438 nests the city protected in 2025.

When do baby turtles hatch in Cancun?

About two months after each nest is laid, NOAA Fisheries says for green and loggerhead turtles. In 2025 the city released its first hatchlings in mid-June, and hatching continues into the fall.

Can my kids release a baby turtle?

Not with their hands. Under NOM-162-SEMARNAT-2012, visitors may not handle hatchlings, even to release them. Kids can watch from at least 2 meters behind, with no flash and no lights, as the hatchlings crawl to the sea on their own.

Will our resort hold a turtle release?

Ask guest services whether there is a corral on your beach and whether guests are told when a nest hatches. Hatchlings go to the sea as soon as they have surfaced and are active, so a release is a maybe, never a booking.

What are the rules for swimming with turtles in Akumal?

Akumal Bay became a federal species refuge by a decree published on March 7, 2016. Quintana Roo's tourism board says snorkeling in the turtle area takes an authorized guide, in small groups. Never touch or feed a turtle, and ask the guide about ages.

What time should we book family photos in turtle season?

Sunrise or the hour before sunset, both in daylight. From May to August, book our 6:30 am online slot, or message us on WhatsApp for an evening start, since sunsets fall after 7 pm. In October, 5:00 and 5:30 pm work.

Why Cancun Light Fools Your Phone

Your phone sets brightness by reading the whole frame, and on a beach here most of that frame is bright. Four things go wrong:

  • Small faces go dark. Against a glittering sea, the phone protects the bright parts and faces sink into shadow. Apple says the iPhone camera uses face detection to balance exposure across faces, but a child running away from you or bent over a sandcastle gives it no face to find.
  • Noon is harsh. From about 11 am to 3 pm the sun is high: shadows under eyes and hat brims, squinting, glare off the sand.
  • The afternoon shade comes early. Cancun's Caribbean beaches face east, so the sun drops behind the hotels and the sand falls into shade before sunset, earlier in winter. A child in that shade, in front of a sunlit sea, is the classic dark-face photo; our sunset and golden hour times by month show when the light goes.
  • The lens is wet or smudged. Spray, a sunscreen thumbprint or fog on a phone fresh from the air conditioning puts a milky haze over everything bright.

Five Simple Setups That Work

None of these needs an app or a new phone.

1. Sun behind them, tap the face

Turn the kids so the sun is behind them or to one side, never in their eyes: no squinting, and a bright edge of light in their hair. Then tell the phone what matters:

  • iPhone: tap your child's face, then drag up beside the focus box to brighten, or down if the sea turns white. Touch and hold until AE/AF Lock appears to keep the setting; tap the screen to unlock it.
  • Pixel: tap your child, then tap Lock to hold focus and exposure on a moving subject. Brightness has its own slider; where it sits depends on your model.

With professional cameras we do the same when the light is hard: the sun behind the family, exposure set for their faces, as our guide to the best time of day for family beach photos in Cancun explains. If the sun hits the lens, shade it with your hand. For silhouettes, go at sunrise, when the sun comes up out of the sea, and tap the bright sky instead: the kids become clean outlines.

2. The shade line

In the afternoon, use the hotels' shadow. Stand the kids just inside the shade, facing the open sea, with sunny sand in front of them to bounce soft light into their faces. Tap a face and brighten until skin looks like skin; a paler sea is fine. At noon, find the same edge under a palapa or a white umbrella, because a colored one tints skin.

3. Down at their height, toward the sea

Crouch so the phone is level with their eyes instead of looking down at the tops of heads, then face the water so loungers and other families end up behind you. Turn on the grid (iPhone: Settings, Camera, then Grid and Level; Pixel: the camera's Settings, More settings, Grid type) to keep the horizon straight and clear of their necks. For close portraits stay on 1x: the 0.5x ultra-wide makes a nose or a hand near the lens look bigger. On a busy stretch, our guide to empty-beach photos in the Hotel Zone has more angles.

4. The wave chase

The best phone photos of kids on this coast are rarely posed. Stand on the firm, wet sand with the kids between you and the water and wait for a wave: the run back up the beach, the shriek, the jump. Hold the phone low and shoot a burst, then keep the best frame:

  • iPhone: drag the shutter button to the left and lift your finger to stop, or turn on Use Volume Up for Burst in Settings, Camera, and hold the volume-up button.
  • Pixel: set Top Shot to Auto in the camera's settings; it takes a burst of photos and picks the best one.

When the kids head into deeper water, put the phone away and watch them.

5. Get yourself into the picture

Scroll through most families' beach photos and you can tell who held the phone: they are missing, and it is usually the same parent all week. Fix it at least once:

  • Prop and run. Set the phone low and level on a beach bag or a cooler and use the 10-second timer, which iPhone and Pixel both have.
  • Use your wrist. The Camera Remote app on an Apple Watch works as a viewfinder for your iPhone within normal Bluetooth range, about 33 feet (10 meters), and you can tap to adjust exposure. It shoots with a 3-second timer by default.
  • Trade or switch. Swap photos with another family, or make the other parent the photographer for an afternoon.

A 30-Second Check Before You Shoot

  • Wipe the lens with a soft, lint-free cloth, not a sandy towel. Apple says to clean an iPhone right away after contact with sand or lotions, and to minimize its exposure to salt water, pool water and sunscreen.
  • Let a cold phone warm up. Fresh from the air conditioning, the lens can fog in humid air until the phone warms up. Apple also advises avoiding dramatic changes in temperature or humidity.
  • Keep it in the shade. Apple designs iPhone for ambient temperatures of 32 to 95 °F (0 to 35 °C) and lists long stretches in direct sunlight among the things to avoid; if it gets too warm, the flash or other camera features may be temporarily disabled.
  • Skip the flash. On mineral sunscreen it can turn faces chalky white; see why sunscreen makes faces glow white in photos.
  • Go easy on filters. The water really is that color; here is why we do not fade the Caribbean in editing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you take good beach photos of kids with a phone?

Put the sun behind them or to one side and tap a child's face, so the phone exposes for skin, not the bright sand. Crouch to their eye level, face the water and use burst for running and splashing.

Why do my beach photos come out with dark faces?

The phone sets brightness from the whole frame, and on a Cancun beach most of it is bright sand, water and sky, so a small or turned-away face can go dark. Tap the face, then drag the exposure up on an iPhone or raise the brightness slider on a Pixel.

What is the best time of day for beach photos of kids in Cancun?

The first hour after sunrise and the last hour before sunset. From about 11 am to 3 pm the sun is harsh, so use open shade. In the afternoon the hotels shade the beach, earlier in winter.

How can the parent who always takes the photos get in them too?

Prop the phone on a beach bag with the 10-second timer, use the Camera Remote app on an Apple Watch as a viewfinder for your iPhone, or trade photos with another family. Or plan a session.

Is a photo session worth it if we already take lots of phone photos?

Phone photos are perfect for everyday moments; a session is for the frame with all of you in it. For families we recommend The Chapter: 60 minutes, 35 edited photos, 475 USD, no deposit, and a preview within 24 hours.

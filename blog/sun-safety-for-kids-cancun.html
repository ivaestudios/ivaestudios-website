A breeze off the Caribbean makes the sun feel gentler than it is. Here is how strong it really is, what pediatricians and health agencies advise for children, which sunscreens the parks allow, and why photos belong early in the trip.

How Strong Is the Sun in Cancun? The UV Index by Month

The UV index measures the strength of the sun's ultraviolet radiation: the higher it is, the greater the potential for damage to skin and eyes. The World Health Organization sorts it into three bands: 0 to 2, you can safely enjoy being outside; 3 to 7, seek shade during midday hours; 8 and above, avoid being outside during midday hours.

NASA's POWER project publishes satellite averages of the UV index, hour by hour, for any point on the map. For the Cancun area from 2001 to 2025, the peak midday hour looks like this:

From March to September the midday average sits above 9, in the WHO's top band. From November to January it is about 6, which still calls for midday shade. These averages include cloudy days, and clouds are no shield: the AAP notes that up to 80% of the sun's UV rays can get through them, and that UV bounces back from water and sand. The CDC advises sunscreen and protective clothing even in the shade.

Shade Hours and the Shadow Rule

The AAP's advice is to limit sun exposure between 10 am and 4 pm, when UV rays are strongest. NASA's hourly averages for Cancun show the same pattern: the index passes 3 by mid-morning in every month, peaks around midday and is down to about 1 or less by late afternoon.

On the sand, use the EPA's shadow rule. If your shadow is shorter than you are, UV is high, so head for shade and cover up; if it is taller, early or late in the day, your exposure is likely lower. For a day with kids, that means the beach early, shade, lunch and naps through the middle of the day, and the water again late. Babies Under 6 Months: Shade First

The AAP says to keep babies younger than 6 months out of direct sunlight, in the shade of a tree, an umbrella or the stroller canopy. The FDA page on infants adds lightweight long pants and long-sleeved shirts and a hat with a brim that shades the face, neck and ears; a baseball cap leaves the neck and ears uncovered. It stresses the hours from 10 am to 2 pm.

Sunscreen is the last resort at this age. The CDC says it is not recommended for babies 6 months or younger, the FDA says to talk to your pediatrician before using any, and the AAP allows it on small areas, such as the face, when protective clothing and shade are not available. Kids 6 Months and Up: Hats, UPF and Reapplying

From 6 months on, the AAP's everyday advice applies:

  • Sunscreen. Broad-spectrum, with an SPF of at least 15; the AAP says SPF 15 or 30 should be fine for most people.
  • Reapply. Every 2 hours, and after swimming, sweating or drying off with a towel. On a pool day, a phone timer helps.
  • Hat. A 3-inch brim all the way around, to shade the face, ears and back of the neck.
  • Sunglasses. At least 99% UV protection, in youth sizes for children.
  • Clothing. Tightly woven fabric, or clothing labeled with a UPF rating (Ultraviolet Protection Factor). Our tip for the water: a long-sleeved rash guard that carries a UPF label.

Pack it all at home: our packing list for Cancun with kids goes age by age, and our Cancun packing guide has the grown-ups' list.

Park and Cenote Rules: Mineral Sunscreen

The Xcaret group's parks on the Riviera Maya spell out their sunscreen rule:

  • Xcaret: the park's FAQ asks you to use only sunscreen and sunblock containing titanium oxide and zinc oxide.
  • Xel-Há: the same rule, and some activities inside add their own.
  • Xenotes, the group's cenote tour, for ages 6 and up: the same two minerals only and a shower before the first cenote, and it asks you to skip makeup, perfume and repellents with chemicals that could affect the cenotes.

In practice, that means a mineral sunscreen: check the active ingredients for zinc oxide and titanium dioxide rather than trusting a “reef-safe” claim on the front, because the park rules name the minerals. Other cenotes and tours set their own rules; ask each operator before you go. One catch for photos: mineral filters can glow white on camera, and our guide to mineral sunscreen in photos shows a five-minute test to do at home.

If Someone Burns

A long pool afternoon can still end pink. For a mild sunburn, the AAP suggests water or milk to replace lost fluids, cool water to help the skin feel better, pain medicine for a painful burn (by age, as its page explains), medicated lotions only if your child's doctor says it is OK, and no more sun until the burn has fully healed.

Call a doctor right away if a baby younger than 1 gets sunburned, and for an older child if there is blistering, pain or fever. Our guide for when your child gets sick in Cancun has more.

The Photo-Day Rule

Sunburn, fresh tan lines and peeling skin all show in a close portrait, so our rule is simple: book the session in the first days of the trip, before the first long pool day. Day 2 or 3 works well: everyone has caught up on sleep, and there has been little time to burn.

If the session falls later in the trip, look at where the tan lines sit: on kids who live in rash guards, they follow the sleeves and hems. Choose outfits that cover the same places: sleeves at least as long, a similar neckline, shorts or a dress at least as long. On the day, put sunscreen on at the hotel 15 to 30 minutes before you head out, the time the AAP says it needs to absorb, and rub it in until none shows on the skin.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the UV index in Cancun?

Strong all year. NASA's satellite averages for 2001 to 2025 put the UV index in the peak midday hour at about 6 from November to January and 9 to 10 from March to September, cloudy days included. The WHO advises shade at midday from 3 and staying out of the midday sun from 8.

Can I put sunscreen on my baby in Cancun?

Under 6 months, shade and clothing come first: the FDA says to talk to your pediatrician before using any sunscreen, and the AAP allows it on small areas, such as the face, when shade and protective clothing are not available. For older children, the AAP advises broad-spectrum SPF 15 or higher.

What sunscreen is allowed at Xcaret and Xel-Há?

Only sunblock and sunscreen containing titanium oxide and zinc oxide, the mineral filters, at both parks. Xenotes, the group's cenote tour, has the same rule and asks everyone to shower before the first cenote. Other cenotes set their own rules, so ask before you go.

How often should kids reapply sunscreen at the beach?

Every 2 hours, and after swimming, sweating or drying off with a towel, according to both the AAP and the CDC. Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen with an SPF of 15 or higher.

What if my child gets sunburned before our family photo session?

Follow the AAP's steps: water or milk, cool water on the skin and no more sun until it heals. Call a doctor right away for a baby under 1, or for an older child with blistering, pain or fever. When should we book family photos on a beach vacation?

In the first days of the trip, before sunburn, tan lines or peeling show in close portraits. Book a soft-light start: online we offer 6:30, 7:00 and 7:30 am and 5:00 and 5:30 pm, and other times on WhatsApp.

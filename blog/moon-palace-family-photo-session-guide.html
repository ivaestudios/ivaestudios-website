In short

Moon Palace is a campus of three sections, Sunrise, Nizuc and the Grand, each with its own beach, so shoot on the shore nearest most of your group; internal shuttles can eat a golden hour that lasts about sixty minutes. Families report an outside vendor fee near 1,000 USD, so request your exact number in writing first.

Moon Palace is one of the largest family resorts in Cancun, and every year a steady stream of families book it, land, and then discover the outside vendor fee about a week before the trip. That is a bad moment to learn about it. This guide is the one we send families who write to us with "we are staying at Moon Palace" in the first line: how the property is actually laid out, what the fee is and what your real alternatives are, which spots photograph well at which hour, and how long a session with twelve relatives genuinely takes when half of them are under seven.

Three sections, three different beaches

Moon Palace Cancun sits on the mainland south of the Hotel Zone, on the highway toward Puerto Morelos, roughly twenty to thirty minutes from Cancun airport depending on traffic. It is not one hotel. It is a campus, and families routinely underestimate how long it takes to cross it. The property is divided into Sunrise, Nizuc and the Grand, each with its own lobby, its own pools and its own stretch of shoreline, connected by internal shuttles that run on their own rhythm.

This matters for photography in a very practical way. If half your group is in Sunrise and the grandparents booked the Grand, the meeting point is a decision, not a detail. We always ask which section each room block is in before we propose a spot, because a fifteen minute shuttle ride inside the resort eats a meaningful slice of a golden hour that only lasts about sixty minutes. Our default is to shoot on the beach nearest the section where most of the group is staying, and to have everyone gather in one lobby ten minutes before the session start.

The outside vendor fee and the alternatives

Here is the part nobody puts in the brochure. Palace Resorts charges an outside vendor fee when a photographer who is not part of their in-house team works on the property. Families consistently report a figure around 1,000 USD for a professional photographer, and around 500 USD even when the person shooting is a family member using professional gear. That second number surprises people the most, because it means "my brother-in-law will just bring his camera" is not automatically free.

Get the number in writing

Vendor fees are set internally, vary by section and package, and change without announcement. The figures above are what families report, not a quote from us. Before you plan anything, email your booking agent or the resort's wedding and events desk and ask for the outside vendor fee for your exact dates and room category in writing. Twenty minutes of email saves a very unpleasant conversation at the front desk.

If the fee is confirmed and you want the session on property, pay it and shoot with the photographer you chose. It is your trip and your album. But there are three honest alternatives, and we recommend them without any awkwardness because we would rather you get pictures you love than nothing at all.

The first is the in-house studio. It is included in some packages, it is convenient, and for many families it is genuinely fine. What you are trading is style and the ability to choose your photographer. The second is an off-property session nearby: Puerto Morelos is fifteen minutes south and has a quiet, low-rise beach with fishing boats and a small town character that photographs beautifully, and the whole session usually costs less than the vendor fee alone. The third is combining the session with something you were going to do anyway, like a cenote morning or a day in the Cancun Hotel Zone, so the photography rides along with an excursion rather than adding a separate outing.

Best beach and garden spots by hour

At sunrise, roughly 6:15 to 7:15 AM depending on the month, the beach is the whole show. The sand is freshly groomed, there are no loungers out, the light comes in low and horizontal off the water, and the resort is silent. If your family can physically get out of bed, sunrise at Moon Palace is the single best hour on the property and it is not close. We have photographed groups of sixteen at 6:30 AM with the beach completely to ourselves.

In the evening, roughly the last hour before sunset, the beach still works but the light is coming over the buildings behind you rather than off the water. That is when the gardens earn their keep. The landscaped walkways, the palm alleys and the shaded areas between the towers hold a soft, filtered light that flatters everyone and gives you a different look in the same session. We usually plan an evening session as two stops: twenty five minutes in the gardens while the light is still high, then down to the sand for the last warm pass.

"The single biggest quality difference in a resort family session is not the camera. It is whether the family agreed to get up early."

Naps, kids' clubs and the resort's schedule

The photography schedule and the toddler schedule are the same schedule. A two year old photographed at the end of a nap window will not smile, and no amount of professional patience fixes tiredness. So when we plan a Moon Palace session we ask three questions before we ask anything about outfits: when do the youngest ones nap, when do they eat, and what is the family already committed to that day.

For families with children under four, sunrise usually wins. Small children are at their best in the first hours of the day, they have not spent eight hours in the sun and the pool, and the session is done before breakfast so the whole day stays free. The cost is one early alarm. For families whose youngest is five or older, the evening window is easier and the kids' club schedule matters more: pick a session slot that ends before the club's evening activity or the show you already booked, not one that collides with it.

Sargassum on this stretch, and the backups

Moon Palace sits on the open Caribbean coast, which means it is exposed to sargassum in the same seasonal pattern as the rest of the region. The heavy months run roughly from April through August, with the worst weeks typically in late spring and early summer, and it varies year to year and week to week in ways nobody can forecast reliably more than a few days out.

Our approach is simple. We check the shoreline on the morning of your session, and we always plan a session with a beach spot and a non-beach spot. The gardens, the palm walkways, the pool deck architecture and the lobby spaces all photograph well and none of them care about seaweed. If the beach is unusable we shift the weight of the session inland and you still get a gallery that looks like a Caribbean holiday. Our full Cancun sargassum season guide has the month by month detail if you are still choosing dates.

Session length for eight to twenty people

A group of six to eight, one or two families, fits comfortably in sixty minutes: the full group, each nuclear family, the kids together, the grandparents alone, and a few loose candid frames. A group of eight to twelve needs seventy five to ninety minutes, because every additional combination costs three or four minutes and the list of combinations grows faster than the headcount. Fifteen to twenty people is a two hour session if you want individual family units photographed properly, and it needs one person from the family designated as the organizer who knows everyone's name and can call the next group forward while we work.

Distance is the other thing that stretches a session here. Moving a group of fifteen from a garden down to the sand costs five to eight minutes of walking, twice, so we build it into the plan.

The public beach question, answered honestly

Every family eventually asks whether they can simply be photographed on the sand and skip the fee, since all beaches in Mexico are federal property up to the high tide line. What is not public is the resort itself, its walkways, its gardens, its lobbies and the access that gets you to that sand. So the answer is: yes, a photographer can legally photograph you on a public beach, and no, that does not give a photographer the right to walk through Moon Palace to reach it. Resort security controls access and they are not unreasonable, but they are also not going to escort an outside vendor through the property for free after a fee exists. A public beach session near this stretch of coast means meeting at a public access point, which in practice means a short drive.

Whatever you decide, tell us your resort and your dates and we will be straight with you about what the session will look like. Questions families ask

How much is the outside photographer fee at Moon Palace?

Families report a figure around 1,000 USD for an outside photographer, and around 500 USD even when a relative is the one shooting with professional gear. Palace Resorts sets these amounts internally and they change, so the only reliable number is the one your own booking agent or the wedding and events desk gives you in writing for your dates.

Can we avoid the vendor fee by shooting off property?

Yes. The two common routes are a public beach session away from the resort, or a session at a nearby location such as Puerto Morelos, a cenote or the Hotel Zone, which usually costs less than the fee itself and gives you a different backdrop. The trade off is transport time and leaving the resort with the group.

What time of day is best for family photos at Moon Palace?

The beach faces east, so sunrise light lands directly on the sand and the water. For families with small children we usually prefer the last hour before sunset in the gardens and on the shoreline, roughly 5:05 to 6:05 PM in December and 6:30 to 7:30 PM in June, because nobody has to be dressed at 6 AM. Midday between 11 AM and 3 PM is the one window we avoid.

How long does a family session take with a group of twelve?

Plan on 75 to 90 minutes for a group of eight to twelve, and closer to two hours for fifteen to twenty people if you want individual family units photographed as well as the whole group. The photography is fast. Gathering people, walking between two spots and small breaks are what fill the time.

Is the beach at Moon Palace affected by sargassum?

It can be. This stretch of open Caribbean coast south of Cancun gets sargassum in the same seasonal pattern as the rest of the region, heaviest roughly from April through August. The resort clears the main swimming areas daily, and we keep garden, pool deck and architectural backups ready so the session still happens on a heavy week.

Can you photograph us on the public beach next to the resort?

All beaches in Mexico are federal and public up to the high tide line, but that is not a loophole that makes a resort session free. Resort security controls access through the property, so a public beach session means meeting outside the resort at a legal access point. We are happy to do that and we will tell you honestly which access points near Moon Palace are worth the drive.

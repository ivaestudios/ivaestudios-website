You booked Moon Palace Cancún, you found a photographer whose work you love, and then the coordinator mentioned an outside vendor fee. Nobody on the internet gives you a straight number, because the resorts do not publish one and most photographers would rather you not know. So here is the honest version from a studio that works inside these properties every week: what Moon Palace charges for an outside photographer, what that money actually buys, the three levers that genuinely move it, and the exact words to put in an email so nothing gets billed to you on the wedding day.

What Moon Palace Charges an Outside Photographer in 2026

The figure couples are quoted most often at Moon Palace Cancún is around 1,000 USD to bring in an outside wedding photographer. What surprises people is the second number: couples on the Cancún travel forums consistently report being charged in the region of 500 USD even when the person taking pictures is a guest or a family member with a camera, not a contracted professional. In other words, the resort is not charging for photography. It is charging for a person on the property who is not part of your room count.

Two things to hold onto. First, Palace Resorts revises these amounts by season, by wedding package tier and by how much your group is already spending, so the number in a 2024 forum thread is a reference point, not a quote. Second, the only number that binds you is the one written into your own wedding contract. If your coordinator gives you a figure verbally, ask for it in the contract or in an email you can save. We have seen the same property quote three different amounts to three couples in the same month, and in every case the couple with it in writing paid what they were told.

The one thing to do today

If your Moon Palace wedding contract is still unsigned, this is the moment the vendor fee is negotiable. Once your package is signed, the coordinator has almost no room to move it. Ask now, in writing, before deposits.

What the Fee Actually Buys: Day Pass, Meals, Access, Insurance

An outside vendor fee is an access charge paid to the hotel. It is completely separate from what you pay your photographer, and it includes zero images, files or edits. Broken into its parts, this is what a resort is covering with it.

The largest piece is the day pass. An all-inclusive resort sells occupancy, and every person walking the property is consuming food, drink, water, air conditioning and staff attention. A photographer working a full wedding day is on site eight to ten hours and will eat at least one vendor meal. The second piece is administrative: the resort collects your vendor's identification, insurance details and often proof of a Mexican tax ID, and someone in the wedding office processes that. The third piece is liability. If a non-employee falls off a beach platform at your reception, the property wants a documented relationship with that person. The fourth piece, which no resort will say out loud, is competitive: the hotel operates its own photography studio, and the vendor fee narrows the price gap between them and an independent.

What the fee does not buy is worth naming too. It does not buy your photographer better access, a reserved location, an earlier entry time or any priority with the banquet team. It buys the right to be there. Everything else still has to be arranged with the coordinator.

The Room-Night Route: Booking Your Photographer Into Your Block

The single most effective lever couples have is occupancy. Resort vendor fees exist because an outside supplier is a body on the property who has not paid for a room. When that supplier does have a room, the logic collapses, and coordinators know it.

Several Riviera Maya properties publish this openly. Hotel Xcaret, for example, removes its vendor fee entirely when the vendor sleeps three or more nights inside your room block, which we cover in detail in our guide to the Hotel Xcaret vendor fee and room-block waiver. Moon Palace does not publish an equivalent rule, and we want to be clear about that rather than promise something the resort has not committed to. What we do see repeatedly is that when a photographer is booked into the group block for the wedding night, the fee is either reduced or reclassified as a day pass at a lower rate. It is a conversation, not a guarantee.

Run the arithmetic before you propose it. If a night in your block costs 300 to 400 USD and the vendor fee is 1,000 USD, buying the room can be the cheaper path, and it has a real operational benefit: a photographer sleeping on property is on site before sunrise, is not fighting Hotel Zone traffic on the morning of your wedding, and can shoot the quiet detail work at dawn when the beach is empty. Some couples also apply group-block credits or comped nights earned by their guest count to this room rather than to an upgrade.

"The vendor fee is not a photography charge. It is a hotel occupancy charge wearing a different name, which is exactly why occupancy is the thing that moves it."

Neither column is automatically right. If your photography priority is modest and your budget is tight, a bundled in-house package with a small photo credit already inside your wedding plan is a rational choice, and we tell couples so. If the images are the part of the day you expect to still care about in twenty years, pay the fee and pick the person. What we would not do is choose in-house purely to dodge a fee and then discover the package includes forty images with the rest sold back to you at a per-file rate. Ask the resort studio for the exact deliverable count and the price per additional file, in writing, and put it next to your independent quote. Email Template to Get Your Photographer Approved in Writing

Send this to your Moon Palace wedding coordinator as early as possible, ideally before you sign. Copy your photographer. The goal is a single email thread you can point at if anything is questioned on the wedding day.

Subject: Outside photographer approval and fee confirmation, [your names], [wedding date]

Hello [coordinator name],

We are working with an outside photography studio for our wedding on [date]. Their details are below. Could you please confirm the following in writing so we can budget accurately and avoid anything being charged on the day?

1. The exact outside vendor fee for one photographer, and whether a second shooter or assistant is charged separately or at a different rate.
2. Whether the fee changes if we book the photographer a room inside our group block, and for how many nights.
3. What the fee includes: arrival time, departure time, meals, and access to the ceremony, reception and beach areas.
4. The documents you need from the vendor (identification, insurance, tax ID) and your deadline for receiving them.
5. Whether the fee is payable in advance to the resort or added to our final wedding invoice.

Vendor details: [studio name], [contact name], [email], [phone].

Thank you,
[your names]

Two notes on using it. Send it from the email address on the reservation, not a partner's, so it attaches to your file. And if the reply arrives verbally over a call, write back summarizing what you were told and ask them to confirm. A one-line confirmation is all you need.

Shooting Outside the Property: Legal Alternatives 10 Minutes Away

The vendor fee applies to the resort's land. It does not apply to the coast around it, and Moon Palace sits at the quiet southern end of the Cancún corridor with real options nearby.

Playa Delfines, the large public beach on the Hotel Zone's southern stretch, is roughly ten to fifteen minutes north depending on traffic. It gives you an open Caribbean horizon, high dunes and no vendor fee. It is public, so it is busiest in the afternoon and quietest at sunrise. Puerto Morelos, about twenty minutes south, is a small fishing town with weathered piers, pastel walls and a calmer reef-protected sea, and it photographs far more editorially than any resort lawn. The Nichupté lagoon side of the Hotel Zone gives you west-facing sunset over water instead of the east-facing beach, which matters if your ceremony already used your golden hour.

Be realistic about what this shifts. Your ceremony, your guests, your dinner and your dancing are all still inside Moon Palace, so the photographer still needs approved access for those hours. Going off property only moves the couples portrait block, typically thirty to fifty minutes. If you do it, plan the transfer into the schedule rather than into the golden hour itself, and remember that a wedding dress plus a taxi plus sand costs you time you did not budget. We plan these blocks routinely and can tell you honestly whether your date and light make it worth the drive. There is more on this in our guide to a destination wedding photographer in Mexico.

Questions to Ask Your Wedding Coordinator Before You Sign

Every one of these has cost a couple money at some point because it was assumed rather than asked.

Is the fee per vendor or per person? A photographer plus a second shooter is two people, and some properties charge both. Does it change by wedding package tier? At Palace Resorts the higher packages sometimes absorb it. When is it payable, and to whom? Advance payment to the resort behaves differently from a line added to your final invoice. What time can the vendor enter and when must they leave? A ten-hour coverage day that runs past a vendor curfew is a problem you want to find in January, not in November. Does the resort limit where an outside photographer can work, particularly restaurants, spa areas and other guests' pool zones? Is drone use permitted, and by whom? Many properties allow it only through the in-house team, or not at all.

Finally, ask what happens if the resort's own studio is contractually the exclusive supplier for any part of the day. A handful of properties reserve the ceremony itself. It is rare, but it is the kind of clause you want to read before the deposit, not after.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Moon Palace outside photographer fee?
Couples planning at Moon Palace Cancún are most commonly quoted around 1,000 USD to bring in an outside wedding photographer, with a reduced figure near 500 USD reported when the person shooting is a guest or family member rather than a contracted professional. Palace Resorts revises these amounts by season and by wedding package, and the number that binds you is the one written in your own wedding contract, not the one on a forum.
Can the Moon Palace photographer fee be waived?
There is no published blanket waiver at Moon Palace the way there is at some Riviera Maya properties. What does move the number is booking your photographer a room inside your group block for the wedding night, negotiating the fee into your package while the contract is still open, or having the coordinator apply it as a day pass rather than a vendor fee. Ask before you sign, because after signature the coordinator has far less room.
What does the outside vendor fee actually pay for?
In practice it covers property access for the hours of your wedding, meals and drinks under the all-inclusive plan while the vendor is on site, and the resort's administrative and liability handling of a non-contracted supplier. It is not a photography charge and it does not include any images, files or edits. It is an access charge paid to the hotel, separate from what you pay your photographer.
Is the in-house Moon Palace studio cheaper than an outside photographer?
On the invoice line it often looks cheaper because there is no vendor fee, and a small photo credit is usually bundled into the wedding package. The comparison changes once you count what you receive: in-house resort packages typically deliver a limited number of edited images with extras priced per file, while an independent studio delivers the full edited take. Compare deliverables and delivery time, not just the headline price.
Can we just shoot the portraits outside the resort instead?
Yes, and many couples do. Playa Delfines is a public beach roughly ten to fifteen minutes from Moon Palace with an open Caribbean horizon and no vendor fee, and Puerto Morelos is about twenty minutes south for a quieter, more editorial look. The ceremony still happens inside the resort, so this only shifts the portrait block, and you should build travel time into the golden-hour window rather than into it.

Is Isla Mujeres Safe for Tourists?

By Vianey Díaz · September 8, 2026 · 8 min read

In short

Isla Mujeres is one of the calmest places on this coast. It is about eight kilometres long, the north end is walkable, almost nobody drives a car, and the whole island runs on day visitors from Cancun. The realistic risks are not crime but the ordinary ones: golf carts on narrow roads, strong current at the wrong beach, and sunburn. Bring a card and some pesos, take the last ferry seriously, and it is a very easy day.

The short answer

Yes, for the visit almost everyone actually makes. Isla Mujeres is small, densely visited and entirely dependent on tourism, and it feels it. The northern end, where the ferry lands and where most people spend the day, is a grid of a few walkable streets with restaurants, shops and a public beach at the end of it.

We photograph on the island through most of the year, often at sunrise before the first ferries arrive, and the atmosphere is closer to a small coastal town than to a resort strip. That said, honest is better than reassuring: the things that actually go wrong for visitors here are not crime. They are golf carts, current and sun.

Getting there, and the ferry you should not miss

The island sits about thirteen kilometres off the coast and the crossing takes roughly twenty minutes. Ferries run frequently through the day from the mainland north of downtown Cancun, and there are also departures from a couple of piers inside the Hotel Zone, which cost more and take longer but save you the drive.

The single most common mistake is the return. Ferries stop in the evening, and the last one is earlier than most people assume, particularly outside high season. If your plan involves sunset on the island, check the final departure before you commit to it, and build in a margin. Missing it means finding a room on an island that fills up.

Take a photograph of the timetable at the dock when you arrive. Schedules change seasonally, and the version you found online in March may not be the one running in September.

Golf carts are the real risk

Almost nobody drives a car here. Visitors rent golf carts, and this is where injuries actually happen.

The roads are narrow, there are few pavements outside the town, and the perimeter road along the eastern side has real drops beside it. Carts have no doors, no belts in most cases, and they tip more easily than people expect, particularly turning fast or with four adults aboard and someone half standing.

None of that is a reason not to rent one, and it is genuinely the best way to see the island. It is a reason to go slowly, keep everyone seated, and not drive one after a long lunch. If you are travelling with small children, the taxis on the island are inexpensive and plentiful.

Which beaches are calm and which are not

The island has two very different coasts, and confusing them is the other way visitors get into trouble.

Playa Norte, at the northern tip, is the postcard beach: shallow, protected, almost no waves, and you can walk out a long way. It is also the reason so many sessions happen here rather than in Cancun, because it is one of the few beaches nearby that faces west. The sun sets into the water in front of you, which the Hotel Zone cannot give you at all.

The eastern side is the opposite: open Caribbean, rock, real surf and current. It is spectacular to look at from the cliffs at Punta Sur, and it is not a swimming beach. Take the photographs, stay out of the water.

That contrast is the whole island in one sentence. Calm on the sheltered side, wild on the exposed one, and about a fifteen minute cart ride between them.

Practical notes nobody tells you

Cash. Many small places on the island prefer pesos, and a few do not take cards at all. Bring some, and expect a poor rate if you pay in dollars.

Sun. There is very little shade on Playa Norte and the breeze hides how strong the sun is. This is the most common thing that ruins a day here, by a wide margin.

Sargassum. The seaweed that affects the east-facing coast from spring into early autumn hits Playa Norte far less, because it sits on the sheltered side. In a bad sargassum week on the mainland, the island is often the better beach.

Crowds. The island belongs to day trippers between roughly eleven and four. Before and after that it empties out. If you want the version of Isla Mujeres people fall in love with, be there early or stay the night.

Punta Sur. The southern point has cliffs, a small Mayan structure and a sculpture path. There is an entrance fee, it is modest, and the views are the best on the island.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Isla Mujeres safe to walk around at night?

In the northern town, yes. The centre is small, well lit on the main streets and busy with restaurants into the evening. As on any island, the outer roads have no pavements and very little lighting, so walking the perimeter after dark is worth avoiding in favour of a taxi, which is inexpensive.

Is it safe to rent a golf cart on Isla Mujeres?

Yes, and it is the best way to see the island, but golf carts are where most visitor injuries here come from. The roads are narrow, the eastern perimeter has drops beside it, and carts tip more easily than people expect. Go slowly, keep everyone seated, and do not drive after drinking.

Can you swim at all the beaches on Isla Mujeres?

No. Playa Norte on the northern tip is shallow, calm and very safe for swimming. The eastern side of the island faces the open Caribbean and has rock, surf and current, and is not a swimming coast. Enjoy the view from the cliffs at Punta Sur instead.

How long does the ferry from Cancun take?

About twenty minutes from the mainland piers north of downtown Cancun. There are also slower, more expensive departures from a couple of Hotel Zone piers. Check the last return of the day before you plan a sunset, because the final ferry is earlier than most visitors expect.

Is Isla Mujeres better than Cancun for a beach day?

For calm, shallow water and a west-facing sunset, yes. Playa Norte is protected and faces west, so the sun sets into the sea in front of you, which the east-facing Cancun Hotel Zone cannot offer. Cancun has the wider beaches and the bigger surf.

Does sargassum reach Isla Mujeres?

Far less on Playa Norte, because it sits on the sheltered northwestern side of the island. During a heavy sargassum week on the mainland coast, the island is often the cleaner beach.

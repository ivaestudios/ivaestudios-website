Sneak Peek in 24 Hours: Post Before You Fly Home

In short

A curated sneak peek arrives within 24 hours of the session so you can post before you fly home, and the full private 4K gallery lands within 1 to 3 business days. Every session includes a deliberate vertical set composed for a 9:16 phone screen, plus short clips, rather than crops made after the fact.

Ten years ago nobody asked a vacation photographer how quickly the photos would arrive. Today it is one of the first three questions in almost every message we get, and it should be. A family session on a seven day trip is not the same product as a family session at home: the photographs have a job to do while you are still there. This is an honest account of what we deliver, when, and why the timing is built the way it is.

Why turnaround matters more on a seven-day trip

At home, a two week edit is fine. Nothing depends on it. On a trip, the clock is different in three specific ways.

First, the trip has an emotional shape. The session usually happens on day three or four, and the photographs are the thing everyone wants to look at over dinner that night and on the flight home. Waiting three weeks means the images arrive into a completely different mood, when the tan has faded and the group chat has moved on.

Second, families genuinely want to share while they are away. Grandparents who did not travel are waiting. Nearly seven in ten younger clients now tell us the social or vertical version matters as much as the framed print, and a photographer who cannot produce anything shareable for a fortnight has quietly failed at part of the job.

Third, and least discussed: if something is wrong, a fast turnaround is the only thing that lets us fix it. If a child had their eyes closed in every group frame, or a family member arrived late and missed the wide shots, we would rather know on day four when you are still in the country than three weeks later when you are not. Speed is a safety net, not just a convenience.

What we actually commit to

The full private 4K gallery lands within one to three business days of your session. A small hand-picked preview set is what we aim to send within about twenty four hours. The first number is the promise; the second is what we plan for and hit in the great majority of sessions. We would rather write that plainly than advertise an hour and disappoint you.

What arrives within 24 hours and what follows

A golden hour family session in Cancún finishes at dusk. The cards come back to the studio that night, the files are copied to two drives before anyone sleeps, and the selection and colour work begin the next morning. That morning pass produces the preview: a small set, usually five to ten frames, chosen because they carry the session rather than because they were easiest to finish. There is always at least one full group frame in it, because that is the picture the family actually wants to send.

The preview arrives as a link, already colour graded, sized for phones and social use. It is not a proof sheet and it is not straight out of camera. Sending unfinished files would be faster and it would also be worse, because the grade is a large part of what distinguishes these images from the ones your phone already took.

The full gallery follows within one to three business days. That is where the whole session lives: every keeper from the evening, full resolution, individually corrected, organised so you can find the group shots, the children, the couple and the candid frames without scrolling through four hundred thumbnails. Weekends and Mexican public holidays are the usual reason a delivery lands at the far end of that window rather than the near one, and we will tell you which day to expect it before you leave the beach.

The vertical set: clips and 9:16 frames

A horizontal photograph cropped to a phone screen loses most of what made it good. The sky goes, the negative space goes, and a family of six becomes a family of four with two elbows. So we do not crop afterwards. We shoot a deliberate vertical set during the session, composed for a 9:16 frame, with the group placed so that the composition works upright.

Practically, that means a few passes through the evening where we turn the camera and reframe the same moment: the walk along the waterline, the lift, the whole group standing in the shallow water, the parents alone at the end. It costs a couple of minutes and it doubles what you can actually use.

Alongside those we capture short clips. Not a film, and not a substitute for one. Ten to twenty seconds each of a family walking toward the camera, children running, a grandmother laughing at something off frame. They are stabilised, graded to match the stills and delivered in the same gallery. Families use them as reels, as story backgrounds, and increasingly as the moving photograph on a phone lock screen.

All of it is included. There is no vertical package and no video add-on, because a set of images that only works on a laptop is an incomplete delivery in 2026.

"A vertical frame is not a crop. It is a different photograph, taken on purpose, in the same two seconds."

How to post them without ruining the album later

This is the advice families thank us for most, and it costs nothing.

Post one image while you are still on the trip. One. The strongest frame from the preview, ideally the group. It carries all the excitement, it answers everyone who has been asking, and it leaves the rest of the session intact. What we watch families regret is dumping thirty images into a single post on the flight home, because the gallery then feels spent before the prints are even ordered.

Hold the vertical clips for later. They are the best material for a slower post two or three weeks after the trip, when the holiday feels far away and a fifteen second clip of your children running on a beach is worth more than it was on day four.

Keep two or three frames entirely offline. Every family we know has one photograph from a trip that never went anywhere public, and it is almost always the one that ends up printed and framed. Decide which those are before you start sharing, not after.

And a small technical point that matters: post from the full resolution file, not from a screenshot of the preview link. Social platforms compress hard enough on their own without you handing them a compressed file to start with.

What a 4K private gallery includes

The gallery is private, it is yours, and it is not a public link on somebody else's marketplace. Inside it you get every selected image from the session at full resolution, downloadable individually or as a complete set, with the vertical frames and clips in their own section.

You can favourite images, which is how most families narrow down a print order, and you can share the gallery with relatives without giving them your login. Downloads are unrestricted. The point of handing over full resolution files is that the photographs end up on your drives and your backups, not held hostage on a platform that might change its terms next year.

Printing is unrestricted too. The files are large enough for wall prints and albums, and if you want a specific crop for a frame size you already own, write to us and we will prepare it properly rather than letting a print lab take the top of somebody's head off.

Marketplace turnaround versus booking a studio directly

Vacation photography marketplaces are a real and reasonable option, and we would rather explain the difference than pretend they do not exist. The large international platforms typically publish a four to five day delivery benchmark, which is a fair standard for a network operating in dozens of countries at once.

The structural difference is who takes the picture. On a marketplace you book a slot and a photographer is assigned; you often meet them for the first time on the beach, and the editing may pass through a central pipeline rather than the hands that held the camera. Booking a studio directly means you know who is arriving, you have spoken to them about your family and your dates beforehand, and the person who framed the picture is the person who grades it.

That is also why our turnaround is shorter rather than longer despite every image being individually corrected: there is no queue between the session and the edit. The studio caps itself deliberately, at twelve weddings a year and a limited number of sessions per week, precisely so that the delivery promise stays real in high season. A promise that only holds in September is not a promise.

Neither model is dishonest. If price is the deciding factor, a marketplace will often win. If you want a named photographer, a scouted location and the gallery in your hands before you fly home, book directly.

Reprinting and resharing after you get home

The photographs have a long second life, and most of it happens months later. Holiday cards in November. A grandmother's birthday. The anniversary of the trip. A frame that finally replaces the one in the hallway.

Two habits make that easy. Download the full set to your own storage within the first week, while the link is fresh in your inbox and nobody has changed their email address. Then keep a copy somewhere other than one laptop, because the single most common message we get years later is from a family whose only copy was on a phone that died.

We keep our own archive of the sessions we photograph as a courtesy, and families do write to us years afterwards asking for a file again. We help whenever we can. But the reliable copy is the one you hold, and that is the whole reason we deliver full resolution files rather than a viewing licence.

If you are planning a trip and want to know exactly when your gallery would land, tell us your session date. Questions families ask

How fast do we get our family photos?

The full private 4K gallery is delivered within one to three business days of the session. Alongside that, we aim to send a small hand-picked preview set within about twenty four hours, so most families have something to share before they fly home. The one to three business days is the commitment; the preview is what we plan for and hit in the great majority of cases.

Why does a preview set take a day rather than an hour?

A golden hour session finishes at dusk. The files are transferred and backed up that night, and the culling and colour work happen the next morning with fresh eyes. Sending frames straight out of the camera would mean sending unfinished work, and the colour grade is a large part of what you are paying for. A day is the shortest honest turnaround for images we are willing to put our name on.

Do you shoot vertical photos and video for social media?

Yes. We capture a deliberate vertical set alongside the horizontal frames, composed for a 9:16 phone screen rather than cropped from a wide image afterwards, plus short clips of the family walking, laughing and moving. They are included, not an upsell, and they arrive in the same gallery in a separate section.

How is this different from a vacation photography marketplace?

Marketplace platforms typically publish a four to five day delivery benchmark, and the photographer who shows up is assigned to you rather than chosen by you. Booking a studio directly means you know who is coming, you speak to them before the session, and the editing is done by the same person who took the picture. That is why our turnaround is shorter, not longer, despite the images being individually graded.

Can we post the preview before the full gallery arrives?

Yes, that is exactly what it is for. The only thing worth planning is which frames you hold back. We suggest posting one strong preview image while you are still on the trip and keeping the full set for when you are home, because posting sixty images in a single week is the fastest way to make a beautiful session feel used up.

How long can we download the photos, and can we print them?

The gallery holds full resolution files that you download and keep, so the images live on your own drives rather than only on a link. Printing is unrestricted, and the 4K files are large enough for wall prints and albums. If you need a specific crop or a print size that does not match the frame, write to us and we will prepare the file properly rather than letting a lab crop somebody's head.

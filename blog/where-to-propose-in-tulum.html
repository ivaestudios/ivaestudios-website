Tulum makes the question easy to picture: the Caribbean, pale sand, the green of the jungle. The hard part is the exact spot and the exact minute, and keeping it secret until you are on one knee. Here is the guide we share with couples: where to ask, when the light helps, what the ruins really allow, and how our photographer stays hidden until the yes.

Start With the Sun: Tulum Faces East

Tulum's coast looks east over the Caribbean; even INAH notes that the eastern side of the old walled city faces the sea. At sunrise the sun comes up out of the water, low and warm. In the evening it sets behind the land, so the sand falls into soft, even light while the sky over the sea turns pastel.

For an evening proposal we plan the question for about 25 minutes before sunset, which leaves the best light for the photos after the yes. Times on the 15th of each month in 2027 (they barely change from year to year):

The Beach at Sunrise

Light: the sun rises straight out of the sea, and the warm, low light lasts about half an hour. People: first light is usually the quietest the sand gets. Where we hide: down the beach with a long lens, one more early riser photographing the sunrise.

The simplest stage is the sand in front of your hotel: no car, no tickets, nothing to explain, and watching the sunrise is the most natural excuse for an early alarm. Our online morning times are 6:30, 7:00 and 7:30 am; any other time is arranged on WhatsApp.

Your Hotel or a Beach Club, at the Last Light

Which hotel in Tulum is best for a romantic proposal? Usually the one you are already in: no transfer to explain, your partner suspects nothing, and you can leave your room at exactly the right minute.

Light: with the sun going down behind the land, the sand gets soft, flattering light and the sky over the water often turns pink. People: the beach is busier than at sunrise, which helps, because a long lens disappears among guests photographing the sea. Where we hide: our photographer arrives early, checks in as your guest and waits at a distance from the spot with a long lens. Our online evening times are 5:00 and 5:30 pm; any later start, timed to your sunset, is arranged on WhatsApp.

Some hotels charge a fee for outside photographers. If yours does, the hotel sets it and you pay it to the hotel, not to us; we confirm your hotel's current rule before the day. If a beach club is part of the plan, we handle that conversation in Spanish ahead of time. See also coordinating a surprise proposal with your resort concierge.

We have also mapped the spot and the minute at Azulik Tulum, Conrad Tulum and Secrets Tulum.

The Ruins: Visit Together, Ask Elsewhere

The walled city facing the Caribbean is the postcard of Tulum. INAH's page for the Tulum archaeological zone (updated August 10, 2026, and checked by us on November 13, 2026) lists hours of 8:00 am to 5:00 pm, last entry 3:30 pm, and a unified ticket of 265 pesos for foreign visitors and 80 pesos for Mexican nationals, which INAH says includes the CONANP and/or Grupo Mundo Maya charges. An INAH bulletin from February 2025 places the zone inside the Parque del Jaguar.

Set those hours beside the table: the zone opens after sunrise even in January and closes more than an hour before sunset every day of the year. INAH also says personal photos with phones and non-professional cameras are allowed, within each site's rules, while photographing with special or professional equipment requires an INAH authorization (procedure INAH-01-001). A hired photographer with a long lens is exactly that.

So we do not plan the question inside the walls. Visit together earlier in the day, take your own photos, and keep the question for the beach. Our guide to INAH photo permits at Tulum and Chichen Itza goes deeper.

A Cenote, for a Different Kind of Yes

Light: where a cenote opens to the sky, light falls from above, so midday, the worst hour on the beach, can be the best one underground. People: a cenote is shared, and the hour decides how busy it is. Where we hide: among the other visitors, one more person with a camera.

Mind the wet ground, your partner's shoes, and keep the ring zipped away until the moment. Before we plan a cenote proposal, we confirm that cenote's current rules for entry, cameras and hours. Our cenote photography in Tulum page shows the light.

Jungle Paths and Rooftops at Sunset

Under the trees the light goes earlier than on open sand, because the canopy stands where a horizon would be, so when a plan mixes jungle and beach we start in the trees and finish on the sand. A jungle path is green and private, and easy to hide in.

A rooftop works the other way: on the beach the sun sets behind you, but from a terrace above the treetops you can watch it go down over the jungle. Its rules are the venue's, so we ask ahead; on the night our photographer is one more guest with a camera.

Getting There From Cancun

INAH places the entrance to the archaeological zone 128 kilometers south of Cancun, on federal highway 307. From a Cancun hotel, Tulum is a day trip, and the last light is kinder: a sunrise question would mean setting off well before first light. Either way, we build the drive into the timeline so nobody arrives as the light goes.

How We Photograph Your Proposal

We have photographed more than 100 surprise proposals, always with the same quiet plan:

  1. In private. We settle the date, the spot and the minute in private messages, never where your partner might see.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best place to propose in Tulum?

For most couples, the beach, at sunrise or in the last light, and often the sand in front of your own hotel. A cenote, a jungle path or a rooftop at sunset are lovely alternatives. The archaeological zone works better as a visit together.

Can you propose at the Tulum ruins?

We do not recommend it for a photographed proposal. INAH's page, checked on November 13, 2026, lists hours of 8:00 am to 5:00 pm, so the zone opens after sunrise and closes more than an hour before sunset all year, and photographing with professional equipment at INAH sites requires an INAH authorization.

What time should I propose in Tulum?

For an evening proposal, about 25 minutes before sunset. Through the year, sunset in Tulum moves between 6:08 and 7:34 pm and sunrise between 6:09 and 7:28 am, and Quintana Roo keeps the same clock all year.

How does the photographer stay hidden?

Our photographer waits at a distance with a long lens, looking like any other guest. We agree three signals: a message when you leave your room, a marker where you stop, and the moment you turn your partner around. We walk over only after the yes.

How much does a proposal photographer in Tulum cost?

We suggest The Keepsake, 45 minutes and 25 edited photos for 375 USD, or The Chapter, 60 minutes and 35 edited photos for 475 USD. There is no deposit: you pay at the end of the session. A preview arrives within 24 hours and the full gallery in 1 to 3 business days.

Do Tulum hotels charge a fee for an outside photographer?

Some do. If yours charges one, the hotel sets it and you pay it to the hotel, not to us. We confirm your hotel's current rule before the session.

In short

Kimpton Tres Rios, in IHG's words the first Kimpton worldwide to be all-inclusive, sits in a protected 340-acre nature preserve between Cancun and Playa del Carmen. Its rivers, cenotes and mangroves are open only on staff-guided excursions, so we plan family photos on the beach and the resort's green grounds, near sunrise or in the last light, and confirm the resort's rules for outside photographers first.

A resort inside a nature preserve is a gift for family photos: turquoise water and white sand on one side, deep tropical green on the other. Kimpton Tres Rios has both, plus rules for a photo session that most listings skip. Here is where a session can happen, the light by month, what we confirm with the resort, seaweed and what to wear. Every resort detail comes from IHG's own pages or the owner's press room, checked on November 14, 2026.

Kimpton Tres Rios in Brief

In short, from IHG's official pages unless noted:

  • Where: Carretera Cancun-Tulum, Km 54.5, Playa del Carmen, on the Riviera Maya coast between Cancun and Playa del Carmen.
  • Setting: a protected 340-acre nature preserve of rivers, cenotes, mangroves and eco-trails, with direct access to a sandy beach.
  • Rooms: 355 suites on three floors, each with a balcony or terrace and ocean or mangrove views, including two- and three-bedroom suites and connecting rooms.
  • Kids: Club Aventuras for ages 4 to 12 (registration fee required), a family pool with a water slide, a children's pool and a shallow splash zone.
  • Status: IHG's news release of March 18, 2026 still called it forthcoming. On November 14, 2026, IHG's page marked it New to IHG, with reservations open and verified guest reviews posted.
  • Formerly: Hacienda Tres Rios. In a press release dated October 25, 2024, the owner, Sunset World Group, said its flagship hotel was in the final stage of a total renovation and would be renamed Kimpton Tres Rios.

The Nature Park Is Guided, So We Plan Around It

This detail shapes the plan. According to IHG, kayaking, snorkeling, cenote visits and eco-tours in the on-property Tres Rios Natural Park run only as guided excursions that need a reservation and are led by trained resort staff, and independent exploration of the ecological areas, cenotes included, is not permitted. So we will not promise you a portrait by a cenote or deep in the mangroves. If you would love a photographer along on an excursion, we ask the resort first and plan on it only if they say yes.

That still leaves plenty to work with. The beach gives you the classic frames: kids running the waterline, a family walk with the sea behind you. Away from the sand, the lush grounds around the pools and suites bring in the green of the preserve and softer light. Bright Caribbean blue and deep tropical green in one session: that contrast is the point.

It is also why we suggest The Chapter or The Story here. The Chapter, the collection we recommend for families, is 60 minutes and 35 edited photos with one look, enough for the beach and a green corner. The Story is 75 minutes and 50 photos with two looks, so you can change outfits between the two settings.

Do the excursions anyway

IHG says kayaking, snorkeling, cycling and hiking are included in the all-inclusive rate, so enjoy them as a family and keep your phone for snapshots. If a cenote portrait is the dream, our guide to a cenote family photoshoot with kids explains how those sessions work, and our family photoshoot at Mayakoba shows a lagoon and mangrove session.

Sunrise and Sunset by Month

On this coast the sun rises out of the Caribbean and sets inland, over the preserve. Mornings bring soft light from the sea side; evenings bring warm color in the sky. Getting the Photographer In at a Newly Rebranded Resort

A resort that has just changed its name and brand may still be settling its procedures for outside photographers. When we checked IHG's pages on November 14, 2026, we found no published policy for outside photographers. So before anything is final, we ask the resort directly, in English or Spanish, and tell you exactly what they say. Some resorts charge a fee for an outside photographer; if this one does, you pay it to the resort and know the amount before your session. Then we coordinate the visit with the resort, so your vacation time goes to the beach, not to logistics.

Booking early while we confirm costs nothing: sessions need no deposit, you pay at the end by card or cash, and cancellation is free until a day before.

Sargassum on This Stretch of Coast

Seaweed does not respect resort lines. The whole Quintana Roo coast can see sargassum in season, and it changes from week to week, so we never promise a clean beach months ahead. Our month-by-month seaweed guide covers the usual pattern. For your dates, we check the official sources. As we wrote this, the sargassum bulletin of Mexico's Navy (SEMAR) for November 13, 2026 rated the coast escaso, scarce, and the University of South Florida's October 31 outlook expected Caribbean sargassum to keep decreasing until possibly the end of November or December.

If the sand is heavy on the morning of your session, we change the frame rather than the plan: tighter shots at the clean edge of the water and more time on the green grounds. Our page on beach conditions today shows how to read the Navy bulletin, the state map and the webcams yourself.

What to Wear for Jungle Shade and White Sand

The two settings pull in different directions. White sand throws bright light back up at you, while green shade cools every color down. Soft, mid-toned palettes work in both: sand, cream, sage, dusty blue, muted terracotta. White works too, if the rest of the family stays soft. Skip neon, big logos and busy prints, and coordinate by palette rather than identical outfits.

Choose sandals you can slip off for the sand, and light, breathable fabrics for the humidity. Put on insect repellent for the green areas before the session starts. With The Story's two looks, try lighter colors on the beach and richer tones in the green. Our guide on what to wear for a family photoshoot in Mexico has palettes for every age.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Kimpton Tres Rios open yet?

Yes. When we checked IHG's official page on November 14, 2026, the resort was marked New to IHG, was taking reservations and already showed verified guest reviews. IHG's news release of March 18, 2026 had still called it forthcoming.

Is Kimpton Tres Rios the former Hacienda Tres Rios?

Yes. Its owner, Sunset World Group, said in a press release dated October 25, 2024 that its flagship hotel, Hacienda Tres Rios, was in the final stage of a total renovation and would be renamed Kimpton Tres Rios, the first all-inclusive hotel of the Kimpton brand.

Can we take family photos in the cenotes or mangroves at Kimpton Tres Rios?

Not on your own. IHG says kayaking, snorkeling, cenote visits and eco-tours in the nature park are guided excursions, by reservation and led by resort staff, and independent exploration is not permitted. We plan on the beach and the green grounds, and ask the resort before promising anything inside the park.

Can we bring an outside photographer to Kimpton Tres Rios?

We found no published rule on IHG's pages when we checked on November 14, 2026, so we ask the resort directly before your session is final. If the resort charges a fee for an outside photographer, you pay it to the resort, and we tell you the amount in advance.

What ages does the Kimpton Tres Rios kids club take?

IHG lists Club Aventuras for children 4 to 12 years old, with a registration fee required, and the resort has a family pool with a water slide and a shallow splash zone.

What time is sunset at Kimpton Tres Rios?

On the 15th of each month in 2027, sunset at the resort runs from 6:07 pm in November to 7:32 pm in July, and sunrise from 6:07 am in June to 7:27 am in January, local time (UTC-5 all year), according to the U.S. Naval Observatory.

How much is a family photo session at Kimpton Tres Rios?

The Chapter, our recommended collection for families, is 60 minutes and 35 edited photos for $475. The Story is 75 minutes, 50 photos and two looks for $545. There is no deposit, you pay at the end of the session, and cancellation is free until a day before. Any outside-photographer fee is paid by you to the resort.

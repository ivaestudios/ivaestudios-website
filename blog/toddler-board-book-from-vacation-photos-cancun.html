Of all the vacation photo books to make after a Cancun trip, this one will probably get the most use. The grown-up album sits on the coffee table. The board book lives in the diaper bag and next to the bed, read by someone who is two, cannot read yet and will ask for it again tomorrow. Here is how we would turn a family gallery into a book your toddler can hold.

Why the Smallest Reader Gets a Book First

The best vacation photo books are the ones somebody opens, and nobody opens a book like a toddler. The American Academy of Pediatrics' guide to choosing books for children lists what appeals to toddlers 1 to 2: pages where they can find and name real things, simple words that repeat and topics from their own day. It also notes that sturdy board books are still good at this age, and that by around 30 months children can often turn paper pages and enjoy picture books as well. KidsHealth, from the children's health system Nemours, adds that toddlers love homemade books, scrapbooks and photo albums full of people they know, and suggests adding simple captions.

A board book with family photos from your own trip checks every box. Months from now, when your toddler turns to the page with Grandpa in the water, they may say his name before you do. That is the point: not to remember Cancun for them, but to help them remember who was there.

Tell the Trip in Order: Plane, Hotel, Beach, Family

A toddler's book needs a beginning, a middle and an end, and your trip has all three. Your phone holds the travel days; our gallery holds the faces. Put them in the order they happened:

  1. Getting ready. The open suitcase, or a sun hat tried on at home.
  2. The plane. The window, the wing, a sleepy head on a shoulder.
  3. The hotel. The room, the pool, the first look at the sea.
  4. The beach. Feet in the sand, a shell in a fist.
  5. The people. From your session, your toddler with each person who came, one page each.
  6. The family photo. Everyone together, the page for pointing at every face.
  7. Going home. A quiet last page turns the book into a bedtime story.

Keep the order the same every time. By the third night, your toddler will know the sea comes after the plane, and will turn the page to prove it.

Choosing 10 to 14 Photos

Ten to 14 photos tells the whole trip and still ends before a two-year-old wanders off. From The Chapter, the collection we recommend for families (60 minutes, 35 edited photos and one look for 475 USD), that is less than half the gallery, so you can be choosy. A page the size of a small hand asks for different photos than a wall does:

  • Faces big enough to point at. On a page 5 to 6 inches across, a wide beach frame turns people into dots. Choose close and medium frames, and save the big sunset for the grown-up album.
  • One person, one page. Your toddler with Grandma teaches the word Grandma better than a crowd does, especially for the relatives they do not see every week.
  • Big feelings over perfect poses. A laugh, a splash, a hug that tipped over.
  • Your toddler doing something. Digging, running, holding up a shell: the pages they will narrate to you.
  • Simple backgrounds and room to crop. Sea and sky read better than a busy pool deck, and the books below are square or nearly square, so leave space around the heads.

The grown-up album works differently; our guide to which 20 photos to print from 300 walks through it, and how many photos you get from a family session lists each collection.

Write It the Way Your Toddler Talks

One short line per page is plenty, in your child's own words: Nana, not Grandmother; the big plane, not our flight. A line that repeats gives your toddler a part to say, and the AAP's guide notes that repetitive phrases can help toddlers predict what comes next.

Lines that work

Who came to the beach? Grandpa came to the beach!
Who came to the beach? Auntie came to the beach!
Splash! Here comes a wave.
Everybody together. Can you find you?

Save the last page for the grown-ups: the place, the month and the year, and everyone's names. Bilingual family? Write each line in both languages.

  • Finishes for small hands. Artifact Uprising lists a matte-laminated cover, semi-gloss pages 1/16 inch thick and rounded corners. Pinhole Press lists thick, rigid pages with a glossy finish that lets spills wipe away, and a spiral plastic binding.   • Pages come in steps. Artifact Uprising says single pages cannot be added or removed. Pinhole Press lets you add pages, but not once the book is bound, so count everyone who came first.
  • Age and timing. Pinhole Press recommends this book for children 1 and up and lists 5 to 7 business days of production.
  • Shipping to Canada. Shutterfly's help page says orders shipped to Canada include customs duties and fees in the checkout price, and it estimates 10 to 12 business days from shipping for international addresses. Artifact Uprising's shipping guide says it ships internationally, with no expedited international option, and the customer pays duties, taxes and fees. Pinhole Press currently ships only to the US and Canada, and its help center says shipping costs to Canada include customs fees.

Any of them will do the job, and so will a printer we did not list: your gallery comes with print rights.

Downloads are unlimited and print rights are included: print with any printer you like, in the US, in Canada or anywhere else.

  1. Download the full files from your gallery, not a screenshot of the preview or a photo forwarded by text.
  2. Lay out the trip in order: your phone photos of the plane and the hotel, then our frames of the people.
  3. Check every crop. Keep faces away from the edges, the rounded corners and the binding; a 4K file leaves plenty of room to crop.
  4. Read the preview out loud. If a line is hard to say, it will be hard at bedtime.
  5. Order a spare. Board books get chewed and dropped in the sand, and a second copy at the grandparents' house means the story is waiting there too.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many photos should go in a toddler board book?

About 10 to 14: enough to tell the trip from the plane to the family photo, and short enough for a two-year-old to finish. Choose close frames with big faces, one person per page.

What size is a photo board book?

Small and square. As listed on November 14, 2026: Artifact Uprising's Baby Board Book, 5.5 x 5.75 inches; Pinhole Press board books, 5.25 x 5.25 inches; Shutterfly's Custom Children's Board Book, 6 x 6 inches. All three start at 20 pages.

Can we print our IVAE photos with any printer?

Yes. Every session comes with a private 4K gallery, unlimited downloads and print rights, so you can print a board book, an album or wall prints with any printer, in the US, in Canada or anywhere else.

How soon after the session can we make the book?

A preview arrives within 24 hours of the session and the full private 4K gallery in 1 to 3 business days, so you can build the book the same week.

Is a board book better than a regular photo book for a toddler?

For a young toddler, usually. The American Academy of Pediatrics' guide to choosing books says sturdy board books are still good for toddlers, and that by around 30 months children can often turn paper pages too. Make the grown-up album separately.

Is Cancun Safe for Families With Kids? A 2027 Guide

In short

Quintana Roo, the state that includes Cancun and the Riviera Maya, is Level 2 of 4 in the US travel advisory issued May 29, 2026: exercise increased caution, due to terrorism and crime. Canada advises a high degree of caution for all of Mexico, with no extra warning for the state. Both were current on November 14, 2026. Plan hardest for what touches children every day: the sea, the pool, the sun and the road.

This question probably came before the one about outfits. We are a Cancun studio, and we would rather give you the dated, sourced answer than a cheerful one: what the US and Canadian governments say, and the everyday risks that deserve as much attention as the headlines.

What the US and Canada Say, With Dates

The US State Department rates Mexico state by state, from Level 1, exercise normal precautions, to Level 4, do not travel. In its advisory issued May 29, 2026, still the latest on November 14, 2026, Quintana Roo is Level 2, exercise increased caution, due to terrorism and crime: the same level as Mexico overall. The state section warns of a risk of violence from terrorist groups, cartels, gangs and criminal organizations, says shootings between rival gangs have injured and killed innocent bystanders, and says US citizens have been victims of non-violent and violent crime in tourist and non-tourist areas. Its advice: pay attention to your surroundings after dark, and stay in well-lit pedestrian streets and tourist zones.

It also lists rules for US government employees anywhere in Mexico, and strongly advises US citizens to follow them, including no travel between cities after dark and no taxis waved down on the street.

Canada's travel advice for Mexico, last updated October 19, 2026, asks for a high degree of caution across the country because of high levels of criminal activity and kidnapping, and does not list Quintana Roo among the states where it advises against non-essential travel. It credits Mexican authorities with lowering gang-related violence in major tourist destinations, but says violent crime still occurs in popular tourist areas and resorts, that fighting between criminal gangs has taken place in hotels, nightclubs and restaurants, and that innocent bystanders have been injured or killed.

On February 22, 2026, amid Mexican security operations, road blockades and criminal activity in several states, the US Embassy told US citizens in Quintana Roo, Cancun included, to shelter in place; the next day it said the situation there had returned to normal. To get alerts like these, enroll in the State Department's STEP program; Canada's Registration of Canadians Abroad lets the Government of Canada notify you in an emergency.

Hotel Zone, Downtown and Tulum After Dark

There is no separate rating for the Hotel Zone: the same Level 2 covers the beach in front of your resort, downtown Cancun, Playa del Carmen and Tulum. The US Embassy's spring break message of March 2, 2026 asks for caution in popular spring break spots, especially after dark. Its messages of February 26, 2024 and February 19, 2025 were more specific, naming the downtown areas of Cancun, Playa del Carmen and Tulum, especially after dark.

See Playa del Carmen or Tulum by day and head back before dark. After sunset, stay on busy, well-lit streets, keep bags and phones close in crowds, and let the restaurant or hotel call your ride. For nights out, see our safety guide for luxury travellers; for a day trip by ferry, our Isla Mujeres safety guide.

The Sea, the Flags and the Pool

The advisories are as direct about water as about crime, and water is the part a parent controls minute by minute. The State Department says Mexico's beaches can be dangerous because of strong currents, rip tides and rogue waves: do not swim alone, stay out of the water when black or red flags are up, and remember that not all dangerous beaches are marked. Canada adds that riptides are common, several drownings occur each year, and lifeguard services do not always meet Canadian standards.

On Cancun's public beaches, the municipal Civil Protection office lists three flags in a release of October 26, 2024 (in Spanish): green, safe to swim; yellow, caution; red, entering the sea is prohibited. It says municipal lifeguards watch public beaches from 9:00 am to 5:00 pm, and that if a current pulls you, you should stay calm, not fight it, swim parallel to the shore, call for help and, if you tire, float until it weakens; teach that to any child who swims. See calm bay or open sea and which Cancun beach suits small children for the gentler side of the Hotel Zone, and how to check beach conditions for your own day.

Pools need the same attention. In the United States, the CDC says, on a page dated September 29, 2025, that more children ages 1 to 4 die from drowning than from any other cause. Its prevention advice: designate a responsible adult to supervise closely and constantly when children are in or near water, and use life jackets for children in and around natural water. For boat and snorkel trips, the US Embassy's 2026 spring break message says to make sure the tour operator carries life jackets, radios and accident insurance.

Sun, Balconies and a Lost Child

Canada notes that severe heat waves occur across Mexico from April to September, and that dehydration and heatstroke can both be fatal. Plan shade at midday, offer water before children ask, and pack hats and light long sleeves.

Canada also says Mexico's standards for balcony railing height are considerably lower than Canada's, and that people have been injured or killed in falls. Keep chairs away from the railing, and the balcony door locked when small children are in the room.

Then the moment every parent dreads: a small child gone among the umbrellas. Take a photo of each child every morning, so today's clothes are on your phone. Dress little ones in bright colors and write your mobile number, with the +1 code, on a wristband. Pick one meeting point, such as the lifeguard tower, and tell children that if they lose you, they should stay where they are and ask a lifeguard or anyone in a hotel uniform. If a child is missing, alert the lifeguard or hotel security at once, and call 911.

Getting Around: Taxis, Car Seats and Daylight

The State Department says motor vehicle accidents are a leading cause of death for US citizens in Mexico, and recommends driving between cities only during the day. Book your airport transfer before you fly, ask in writing for car seats or boosters that fit each child, and check them when the van arrives.

For rides, the US advisory points to regulated taxi stands and apps such as Uber or Cabify. Canada says to use only officially marked taxis and avoid hailing them on the street; with a ride-share app, confirm the driver's identity and license plate before getting in. Numbers to Save Before You Land

Checked November 14, 2026
  • 911: emergencies. The State Department suggests asking a Spanish speaker to help place the call, since not every operator speaks English.
  • 089: free, anonymous crime reports, according to Quintana Roo's citizen security ministry.
  • 078: tourist information and roadside help from the Green Angels, Mexico's highway assistance service.
  • Guest Assist, the Quintana Roo government's tourist assistance program: its call center lists 1 855 267 3363, toll-free from the US and Canada, and +52 998 868 5180 from the rest of the world. The State Department and the US Embassy suggest downloading its app.
  • US Embassy: (55) 2579 2000 from Mexico. There is also a US Consular Agency in the Hotel Zone.
  • Canada: Emergency Watch and Response Centre, toll-free 001-800-514-0129; Consular Agency of Canada in Cancun, +52 55-5724-9795.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Cancun safe for families with kids right now?

When we checked on November 14, 2026, the US advisory of May 29, 2026 rated Quintana Roo, where Cancun is, Level 2 of 4: exercise increased caution, due to terrorism and crime. Canada advised a high degree of caution for all of Mexico, with no regional warning for Quintana Roo.

Is the Cancun Hotel Zone safe?

It has no separate rating: the Hotel Zone is part of Quintana Roo, Level 2 in the US advisory, which says to pay attention to your surroundings after dark and stay in well-lit pedestrian streets and tourist zones. US Embassy spring break messages in 2024 and 2025 singled out the downtown areas of Cancun, Playa del Carmen and Tulum, especially after dark.

Is Playa del Carmen safe for families?

It is in Quintana Roo, so it shares the same Level 2 in the US advisory of May 29, 2026. The same habits apply: well-lit streets after dark, regulated taxis or apps, and driving between cities only by day.

What do the beach flags mean in Cancun?

In a release of October 26, 2024, Cancun's municipal Civil Protection office lists green as safe to swim, yellow as caution and red as entering the sea prohibited. The US State Department says to stay out of the water when black or red flags are up, and warns that not all dangerous beaches are marked.

What number do I call in an emergency in Cancun?

Dial 911, ideally with help from a Spanish speaker, since not every operator speaks English. For tourist help, Quintana Roo's Guest Assist program has an app and a call center, toll-free from the US and Canada at 1 855 267 3363, and Mexico's 078 line gives tourist information and roadside help.

Can we have our family photos at our resort instead of a public beach?

Yes: your own resort beach, with no transfer and your room close by, or a public beach at sunrise, when it is quiet. Some hotels charge a fee for outside photographers, paid by the guest to the hotel; we confirm your hotel's rule beforehand.

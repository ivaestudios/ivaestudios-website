Mayan Ruins Near Cancun With Kids: Which One to Pick

In short

The closest Mayan ruins to Cancun are El Rey (km 18, Hotel Zone) and San Miguelito, through the Maya Museum (km 16.5), with El Meco just north of the city. Tulum, Coba, Ek Balam and Chichen Itza are 1.5 to 2 hours away by car; the last two run on Yucatán time, an hour behind Cancun. Only Coba's INAH page lists climbing hours. Mexico's federal fee law exempts children under 13 from the federal entry fee. Take phone photos at the ruins and book your family session for the next sunrise.

A ruins day near Cancun can mean under an hour in the Hotel Zone or a full day inland. We compared seven sites families can reach, with hours, fees and climbing rules checked on official pages on November 14, 2026, and what each one is like for a four-year-old and for a teenager.

The Sites at a Glance

Fees are for a foreign adult, in Mexican pesos, as listed by INAH, the federal institute that runs the sites, or by CULTUR, Yucatán's state culture and tourism agency. Mexico's Federal Law of Fees (article 288) exempts children under 13 and adults over 60 from the federal entry fee; Tulum's park tariff and Yucatán's state fee are separate. Some INAH visitor pages word these exemptions for Mexican visitors, so ask at the ticket office. Drive times come from the federal SICT route planner, for a car, before stops. Times are local: Ek Balam and Chichen Itza run one hour behind Cancun.

Everywhere, food stays outside the gate, so feed everyone first. INAH's pages give no stroller rules; they list accessibility services at El Rey, the Maya Museum, Tulum, Coba and Chichen Itza, but a soft carrier is the safer bet for under-threes. Every site opens at 8:00 or 9:00 am, so use the first hours; our heat plan for family sessions explains how we work around the sun.

El Rey and San Miguelito, in the Hotel Zone

El Rey sits at km 18 of Kukulcan Boulevard. INAH describes two small plazas and structures along a main avenue, some with traces of murals of gods, and an INAH bulletin puts a visit at 30 to 50 minutes. It opens daily at 8:00 am, with parking and toilets. With a four-year-old, go at opening and you are done before the heat. With a teenager, add the Maya Museum for the story behind the stones.

San Miguelito is reached by a path from the lower corridor of the Maya Museum (Museo Maya de Cancun), at km 16.5, which INAH says holds one of the country's most important Maya collections. One 210-peso ticket covers both, and the same bulletin suggests about two hours for the museum and one to two for the site. Both close on Mondays and open at 9:00 am the rest of the week, with last entry at 4:30 pm for the museum and 4:00 pm for San Miguelito. There is a cafeteria. With a four-year-old, a few rooms and the path out are plenty. With a teenager, it is the best context stop before Tulum or Chichen Itza.

El Meco, North of the City

El Meco is at km 2.7 of the road from Puerto Juárez to Punta Sam. The INAH bulletin says its largest building, El Castillo, has a staircase ending in two large serpent heads that recall Chichen Itza, and that the site lies a few meters from Puerto Juárez, the crossing point to Isla Mujeres, so you can pair it with a beach day. It opens daily at 8:00 am, and INAH's zone page lists parking, toilets and a rest area. With a four-year-old, make it a hunt for the serpent heads. With a teenager, add the Isla Mujeres ferry for the afternoon.

Tulum: Ruins by the Sea

INAH places Tulum about 128 km (80 miles) south of Cancun, and its visitor page describes a walled coastal city with some of the best-preserved murals in the Maya area. INAH's zone page, updated August 10, 2026, lists 8:00 am to 5:00 pm, last entry 3:30 pm, and one 265-peso ticket for foreign visitors. That matches a federal decree in force since July 20, 2026: a 100-peso Parque del Jaguar tariff for foreign visitors plus 165 pesos of federal fees, and the federal fee law's exemptions, such as children under 13 at the ruins, still apply to the federal part.

Quintana Roo's government says entering the park and its beaches is free, with the ruins ticket as the only charge, and that you can walk, cycle or push a stroller through it; the optional electric shuttle costs 20 pesos per adult and 10 per child (July 23, 2026). With a four-year-old, that makes Tulum the easiest of the big sites, at opening. With a teenager, the walls, the murals and the Caribbean beside them are worth the drive. Coba: Climbing Hours on Nohoch Mul

Coba is the pick if your children want to climb. INAH's Coba page, updated February 4, 2026, lists access to the Nohoch Mul pyramid from 8:00 am to 3:30 pm, in groups of up to 15 people, with up to 15 minutes at the top. INAH describes a city of about 70 square kilometers around five lakes, linked by more than 50 stone roads called sacbé, so expect real walking between groups of buildings. With a four-year-old, bring a carrier and decide about the climb on the day; INAH sets group size and time at the top, not an age. With a teenager, choose Coba if the climb is the point.

Ek Balam and Chichen Itza, on Yucatán Time

Both sites are in Yucatán, which keeps Central time all year under Mexico's time zone law, one hour behind Cancun. Their 8:00 am opening is 9:00 am Cancun time, so the drive west gives you an hour back.

Ek Balam lies about 30 km north of Valladolid, says CULTUR, which lists a 709-peso entry for foreign visitors. INAH's visitor page adds 100 pesos for parking and includes the site museum in the ticket. Its showpiece is the Acropolis, whose entrance, INAH says, looks like the mouth of a monster with enormous fangs. With a four-year-old, the monster is a great hook, but the drive is long. With a teenager, pair it with Valladolid; our Valladolid guide shows how a day inland runs.

Chichen Itza, on UNESCO's World Heritage List since 1988, is the big one. Foreign visitors pay 105 pesos to INAH plus a 592-peso Yucatán state fee at the ticket office, 697 in all, and CULTUR lists 124 pesos to park a car. INAH's zone page, updated October 6, 2026, lists last entry at 4:00 pm, its Lugares page says 3:00 pm, and tour agencies get preferential attention from 11:00 am to 2:00 pm, so arrive at the 8:00 am opening. With a four-year-old, we would choose El Rey or Tulum instead. With a teenager, it is the one they will remember: at each equinox, INAH says, triangles of light and shadow seem to slide down El Castillo's staircase to the serpent's head.

Cameras at the Ruins

Phones are welcome: in a June 2024 bulletin, INAH confirmed that personal photos with phones, tablets and non-professional cameras are allowed, within each site's rules. Special or professional equipment needs an INAH authorization (procedure INAH 01-001), and the Maya Museum, Tulum and Chichen Itza list a fee for professional cameras. The gates also open after sunrise and close before sunset, the hours we photograph families in.

So the ruins are for your own phone pictures. Our guide to INAH photo permits at Tulum and Chichen Itza covers professional and bridal shoots.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the closest Mayan ruins to Cancun?
El Rey, at km 18 of Kukulcan Boulevard in the Hotel Zone, and San Miguelito, reached through the Maya Museum at km 16.5. El Meco sits just north of the city, at km 2.7 of the road from Puerto Juárez to Punta Sam. INAH runs all three (checked November 14, 2026).
How much are the El Rey ruins in Cancun?
INAH lists 145 pesos for foreign visitors and 80 for Mexican nationals, with the site open daily from 8:00 am and last entry at 4:30 pm (checked November 14, 2026). Under Mexico's Federal Law of Fees, children under 13 and adults over 60 do not pay this fee.
Can you still climb the Coba ruins?
Yes. INAH's Coba page, updated February 4, 2026, lists access to the Nohoch Mul pyramid from 8:00 am to 3:30 pm, in groups of up to 15 people, with up to 15 minutes at the top. Of the seven sites we compare, it is the only one whose INAH page lists climbing hours.
Are the Tulum ruins worth seeing with kids?
For most families, yes, at the 8:00 am opening and paired with the beach. INAH describes a walled coastal city with some of the best-preserved murals in the Maya area. Foreign visitors pay 265 pesos, last entry is 3:30 pm, and Quintana Roo's government says strollers are welcome in the park around the ruins.
Is Chichen Itza a good day trip with young kids?
It is a long one. SICT's route planner puts it 196 km and 1 hour 50 minutes from Cancun by car, before stops, and foreign visitors pay 697 pesos in INAH and Yucatán state fees. The site keeps Yucatán time, one hour behind Cancun. With children under about eight, we would start with El Rey, El Meco or Tulum.
Can we book a professional family photo session at the ruins?
Not easily. INAH allows personal photos with phones and non-professional cameras, but photography with special or professional equipment needs an INAH authorization (procedure INAH 01-001), and the sites close before the evening light. We suggest phone photos at the ruins and a session on the beach, at a cenote or in the jungle, ideally the next sunrise.

In short

We plan each session around the people in it: naps for babies, play for toddlers, a say for teenagers, shade for grandparents and a running order for big groups.

IVAE Studios photographs families at resorts and beaches across Cancun and the Riviera Maya. Led by Vianey Díaz, who has more than ten years behind the camera, in a team that brings more than 20 years of photography experience and thousands of families and couples photographed, the studio has delivered more than 500 sessions and holds a 5.0 rating across 73 Google reviews, where family photos is the topic mentioned most. Sessions start at 295 USD with no group limit and no per-person charge; a preview arrives within 24 hours, the gallery in 1 to 3 business days, and there is no deposit. Here is how we work with each kind of family, with links to our family guides.

Babies, Newborns and a First Trip

With a baby, we plan around the nap and the feed, not the sunset: a rested, fed baby gives about twenty good minutes, which is all a session needs. We stay close to your room and never ask an infant to perform. Newborns are photographed in your suite with window light, no flash, a warmed room, a feeding pause every 20 to 30 minutes and young siblings first.

Toddlers: Play, Not Poses

A toddler session is a sprint: the best frames come in the first twenty minutes, so the group portrait goes early and the youngest goes first. We do not direct toddlers; we set up a game (bubbles, a shell, chasing the foam line), keep snacks out of sight and photograph the reaction. A meltdown means a pause in a parent's arms, often the most tender frame. Under about five, sunrise usually wins: empty beach, cool sand, rested kids.

Kids and Tweens: Give Them a Verb

Six to eleven is the golden window: they can swim, follow direction and are not yet self-conscious. We ask what they love (water, boats, climbing, animals) and pick a place that fits their answer and the light, because a child who helped choose arrives invested. Then we give them a verb (dig, swim, float, chase), permission to get wet and a small reward for the end. A cenote works only when it matches the child's age and comfort in water.

Teenagers: A Say, a Job, a Finish Line

With teenagers, the eye-roll is not the obstacle; being managed is. We give them a real choice beforehand (the location, their own outfit within the family palette, the song on the speaker), keep everyone moving and never ask them to smile. The family portrait comes first, then they can go off with a sibling, and we promise about an hour. At a quinceañera, the family and padrinos are photographed early, while everyone is fresh.

Grandparents and Three Generations

We design these sessions around the oldest guest: firm ground, short walks, a chair in the shade with water nearby, and grandparent portraits made early so they can rest. We keep a quiet checklist so no pairing is skipped, and we direct in English and Spanish, so an abuela and her grandchildren are equally at ease. On skip-gen trips, a parent at home signs the session agreement and the gallery opens with each child's face.

Big Families, Reunions and Villa Stays

There is no group limit and no per-person charge; big groups need structure instead. Before the trip we agree a running order and a list of must-have combinations, ask one relative to call each group forward and release young families in waves. For twenty or more we build the portrait in tiers so every face sees the lens, and we suggest the first or second evening, leaving days to reschedule. In a villa, we photograph the trip as it happens: kitchen, pool, long table.

Blended Families: Circles, Not Rankings

We build blended family sessions from overlapping small groups, never one ranked lineup, so a new partner or step-sibling is connected to several people before everyone gathers. Beforehand we ask how you want each relationship shown and whether any grouping or pose is better skipped, so nobody has to say no on the beach. A reluctant child starts with something they enjoy.

One Parent, One Child

Two people need no arranging, so we give you something to do (walking into the water, shoulder rides, a conversation we start and step away from) and photograph the contact. The side-by-side portrait comes at the end, with a frame of each of you alone, something mothers rarely have. You can add one to the end of a family session; tell us at booking.

Expecting: Maternity, Babymoon and Gender Reveal

Comfort leads: sunrise or the last light of the day, short flat walks, shade and water nearby, and often your suite as a base. Our babymoon guide points to the second trimester and our maternity guide to weeks 28 to 34; check both with your airline and doctor. At a gender reveal with family, we catch the couple's reaction up close, then widen to everyone else.

Planning: Light, Heat, Outfits and Cost

Tell us at booking

Everyone's ages, nap times, anyone who struggles with heat, sand or stairs, and family dynamics to plan around quietly.

When to shoot

Outdoors, we shoot at sunrise or late afternoon: sunrise for children under about five, the last hour before sunset for older kids and teens. Online booking offers 6:30, 7:00 and 7:30 am and 5:00 and 5:30 pm, with other times by WhatsApp. From Europe, jet lag helps: on London time, an early session feels like late morning.

Heat

Nobody is posed outdoors between roughly 11 am and 3 pm. We plan a route that alternates shade and sand, keep water and a seat close, and cap large groups at 90 minutes with a shade break.

Outfits by age

Choose a shared palette of three to five soft, warm tones instead of matching. Toddlers need comfort and a backup outfit, teens pick their own piece, grandparents often prefer light long sleeves. Linen breathes; pale grey shows sweat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you photograph babies and newborns during a Cancun vacation?

Yes. We plan around the nap and the feed, keep it short and stay near your room, because a rested, fed baby gives about twenty good minutes. Newborns are photographed in your suite with window light, no flash, a warm room and feeding pauses every 20 to 30 minutes.

Is there a limit on how many people can be in a family session?

No. There is no group limit and no per-person charge; collections are priced by time. For large families we recommend 90 minutes, a running order agreed before the trip and one relative who calls each group forward.

How do you get teenagers to enjoy a family photo session?

Give them a say first: the location, their outfit within the family palette or the song on the speaker. We keep everyone moving, never ask them to smile, take the family portrait first, let them go off with a sibling and promise about an hour.

How much does a family session cost for a family of four or five?

Price depends on time, not headcount: from 295 USD for 35 minutes and 15 edited photos to 895 USD for 90 minutes and the complete edited gallery. Most families choose The Chapter, 60 minutes and 35 edited photos for 475 USD. No deposit: you pay at the end, by card or cash, and can cancel free up to one day before.

How far in advance should we book a family session in Cancun?

Around 90 days ahead for most trips, and earlier for the December holidays, March spring break and June to August, because the best golden-hour slots go first. Book us before you lock in excursions.

How soon do we receive our family photos?

A preview arrives within 24 hours, so you can share before you fly home. The full edited gallery follows in 1 to 3 business days, in a private 4K gallery with unlimited downloads and print rights.

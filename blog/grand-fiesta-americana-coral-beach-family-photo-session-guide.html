In short

IVAE Studios can photograph families staying at Grand Fiesta Americana Coral Beach in Cancun: on the beach in front of the resort when the hotel allows an outside photographer on your dates, or, if not, on public Playa Caracol by the Ultramar ferry terminal, a short walk from the hotel. We photographed guests of this resort in October 2026, on the beach in front of the hotel. The best hour is the last one before sunset. On the 15th of each month in 2027, sunset falls between 6:05 pm (November) and 7:32 pm (July). Sessions start at 295 USD, and for families we recommend The Chapter: 60 minutes and 35 edited photos for 475 USD. No per-person charge, no deposit, a preview within 24 hours and your gallery in 1 to 3 business days.

Grand Fiesta Americana Coral Beach is an all-inclusive, all-suite resort on the bay side of Punta Cancun, at Blvd. Kukulcan km 9.5, and its beach faces north toward Isla Mujeres. The resort does not publish a rule for outside photographers on its official websites (we checked on November 16, 2026), so ask the hotel in writing for your dates, and we confirm the answer with you before the session. If outside photographers are not allowed, we meet on Playa Caracol instead, and the price of your collection stays the same. You pay when the session ends, by card or cash, in USD or MXN, and cancellation is free up to one day before.

The resort and its beach

The resort's full name is Grand Fiesta Americana Coral Beach Cancun All Inclusive Spa Resort. It has 602 suites, all with private balconies or terraces, and sits about 25 minutes from Cancun International Airport, according to the resort. It stands on the bay side of Punta Cancun; the very tip belongs to Hyatt Ziva Cancun, next door to the east.

The beach faces roughly north across the bay, with Isla Mujeres straight across the water about 8 km away. Forbes Travel Guide calls it a small, secluded stretch of white sand and says the island shelters it, so the water is calm and clear. OpenStreetMap maps this sand as part of Playa Caracol and marks it as public access. Under Mexico's Ley General de Bienes Nacionales, articles 7 and 8, beaches and the federal zone beside them are common-use property. The pools, gardens, loungers and lobby are the resort's.

Two things shape the light. The resort tower stands right behind the beach, and buildings stand wherever the sun sets during the year, so the sun never sets over open water here and the sand can fall into shade before sunset. In the morning, from about September to March, the sunrise is hidden from the resort's beach by buildings to the east, including the resort's own east end and the Hyatt Ziva. Neither spoils a family session; they decide the order we shoot in.

Best spots by hour

Every plan below works on public sand. Pools, gardens and terraces need the resort's permission, so we never count on them.

The last hour before sunset, on the bay. Our first choice for most families: the beach in front of the resort if the hotel allows us, or Playa Caracol by the ferry terminal if not. Both face the same water. On the resort's beach the sun goes down behind buildings, so we start about an hour before sunset rather than at the last minute. When the shade reaches the sand, the light turns soft and even, which flatters faces and means no squinting for toddlers or grandparents.

Sunrise, on the Caribbean side. For a sunrise over the sea, walk about seven minutes to Playa Gaviota Azul, also called Playa Forum. Its shore faces southeast with no mapped building toward the sunrise, which comes at 6:05 am in June and 7:27 am in January. From about April to August the sun also comes up just north of the Hyatt Ziva building as seen from the resort's own beach.

Midday. Between about 11 am and 3 pm the sun is harsh and children get hot and tired, so we avoid it.

On this beach the buildings, not only the clock, decide the light, so we plan the order of the session around them.

Kids, naps and timing

We plan the session around the youngest child: before outfits, we ask when the little ones nap and eat, and choose the hour around the answers.

The resort's suite pages include complimentary access to the Coral KidZ Club, a 20,000 sq ft club for ages 3 to 12. Its microsite, which may be older than the main site, describes city, park and jungle play paths for ages 3 to 5, a stage with a light-up dance floor and a costume room for ages 6 to 12, and an outdoor area with a Mayan temple to climb, slides, a spray-ground with a wading pool and a big sandbox. Club hours are not published online, so check them when you arrive.

For children old enough for the club, a good rhythm is the club or the pool in the morning, lunch and a nap, then the beach an hour before sunset. With toddlers and babies, sunrise often goes better: they are awake anyway, it is cool and the session is done before breakfast. See why sunrise beats sunset with young kids and our tips on family photos with toddlers. Bring a towel and dry clothes; we save the splashing photos for last.

Getting the photographer in

The resort does not publish a policy for outside photographers. On November 16, 2026 we read its official websites, fiestamericanatravelty.com and coralbeachcancunresort.com, including the weddings, events, amenities, FAQ and terms pages, and none mentions outside photographers, vendor fees, registration or day passes.

Ask the resort in writing

Some resorts charge outside photographers a fee or ask them to register. Email the hotel or your booking agent with your dates and session time, ask whether an outside photographer can work with you on the beach, and forward us the reply. We confirm the rule with you before the session.

IVAE Studios is an independent studio, not the resort's in-house photographer. If the resort says yes, we photograph you on its beach. If not, the shoreline is public, so we meet on Playa Caracol by the ferry terminal without crossing the resort, at the same price. Our guide on whether resorts allow outside photographers explains how these rules usually work.

The public beach option nearby

Walking distances come from OpenStreetMap routes; kilometre markers on this stretch are only approximate.

  • Playa Caracol, on the bay. OpenStreetMap maps the sand in front of the resort as part of this beach. The Ultramar ferry terminal at Playa Caracol is about 515 m away on foot, around 7 minutes, and Forbes Travel Guide calls it a five-minute walk. Same north-facing water, and our free alternative for the evening.
  • Playa Gaviota Azul, or Playa Forum, on the Caribbean side. About 510 m on foot, around 7 minutes, by a paved public access lane. It faces southeast, so this is where we go for a sunrise over the sea. It sits beside the Punta Cancun nightclubs.
  • Playa Chac Mool, further south on the Caribbean side. About 1.05 km on foot, around 14 minutes, with a paved public access lane. It is a longer walk with small children, so we keep it as a backup.

Ultramar ferries to Isla Mujeres also leave from Playa Caracol; for a day on the island, see our Isla Mujeres family photo session guide.

What to wear

Keep it soft and simple. Against white sand and blue water, two or three light neutrals plus one muted accent work well, in fabrics that move in the breeze, such as linen and cotton. Skip big logos and neon, plan on bare feet on the sand, and dress little ones in clothes they already like wearing. With a two-look collection, the second outfit can be dressier. Our guide on what to wear for a family photoshoot in Mexico has full palettes, and there is a separate one on what grandparents should wear for beach photos.

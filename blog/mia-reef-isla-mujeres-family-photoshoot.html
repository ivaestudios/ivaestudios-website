On the long, east-facing beaches of Cancun's Hotel Zone, the sun rises over the sea and sets behind the hotels. At the north tip of Isla Mujeres you get both, sunrise over open water and sunset over the bay, and Mia Reef sits right on that tip. Here is what the resort publishes, where a family session fits, how to get there and the light by month, with every resort fact read on its official site on November 14, 2026.

The Resort at a Glance

Mia Reef Isla Mujeres describes itself as a five-star all-inclusive on Islote El Yunque, a private islet at Playa Norte, about 20 minutes from Cancun. It has 164 rooms, suites and villas, 100 in a tower and 64 in villas, plus a private white-sand beach, a pool with a swim-up bar and four restaurants. Meals, drinks, taxes and tips are included; airport and ferry transfers are not.

Children under 3 stay free, ages 3 to 11 pay a child rate, and from 12 they pay as adults. Kayaking, paddleboarding and snorkeling are included, and the resort says reef rings the islet, so guests snorkel from its beach at no extra cost. Its website describes no kids club, so ask before you book if you need one.

Where Photos Happen on the Property

The resort's wedding brochure, linked from its website, lists ten event spaces. For a family we would start with the beach and the dock, a wooden pier with a palm-thatched palapa at its end, over turquoise water. The brochure also shows two white, red-roofed gazebos on lawns by the sea, an esplanade at the water's edge and a central garden below the tower. Which spaces an outside photographer may use is the hotel's decision, and we confirm its rule with you before the session.

Small children are the reason to start on the sand. The resort's snorkeling guide calls Playa Norte a safe natural pool where you can walk about a hundred yards out and the water will hardly pass your waist, so small children can splash at the water's edge with you right beside them.

At the tip of the beach, the King's Pool is a natural rock pool filled by the sea, where the resort says colorful fish come to feed. A 2022 resort article reserves it for hotel guests, and the FAQ says it may close without notice, so treat it as a bonus.

Two species of tern also nest on the rocks beside the hotel, where the resort's foundation runs a conservation project for them. The colony overlaps some common areas and the resort invites guests to observe and care for the birds, so plan photos away from the nests.

A Sunset Over the Water

This is the photograph that sets the north end apart. From the resort, the sun goes down across the bay toward the mainland all year, west-southwest in December and west-northwest in June, and the resort's Sarimar restaurant faces those sunsets over Playa Norte. The low sun can sit over the water behind your family while the sea catches the light.

Times below are for the resort's map pin on the 15th of each month in 2027 (other years differ by a minute or less), on the UTC-5 clock Quintana Roo keeps all year. Golden light means the sun is under 6 degrees high.

Getting There, for You and the Photographer

The resort's directions send you to the ferry station in Puerto Juárez, in Cancun, and on the island to the taxis right next to the ferry station. Its FAQ names three boat companies there; we checked Ultramar, which on November 14, 2026 listed 290 pesos one way or 580 round trip per adult, and 220 or 440 per child from age 1 up to 1.20 meters (3 ft 11 in); babies under 1 ride free with a boarding pass. Ultramar also sails from Playa Tortugas and Playa Caracol in the Hotel Zone, with a dock fee on top at Playa Caracol, and Xcaret Xailing leaves El Embarcadero about once an hour from 9:00 am to 7:00 pm, last return 8:00 pm, crossing in 20 to 30 minutes. Departures can change, so check close to the day.

Your photographer crosses too

Whoever photographs you at Mia Reef comes over by boat, so the first crossing sets the earliest start and the last ride back sets the latest finish. For the island, message us your date on WhatsApp first, so we can fit the session around the boats.

Visiting on a Day Pass

Staying in Cancun? Mia Reef's FAQ lists a day pass at 98 USD per adult and 49 USD per minor, from 9:00 am to 6:00 pm, no reservation needed, with a towel, beach chairs, the beach and pool area, open bar, breakfast and lunch. The King's Pool is not mentioned.

Settle two things first. Your passes cover your family only, and Mia Reef publishes no rule for outside photographers on its website or in its wedding brochure, so ask in writing whether one may work there, whether they need a pass and whether a fee applies. Some hotels charge a fee; if Mia Reef does, you pay it to the hotel, and we confirm the hotel's rule with you before the session. Our guide to resort day passes for family photos has a message you can send.

Then the hours. The pass opens at 9:00 am, long after sunrise, and closes at 6:00 pm. In November and December, golden light starts between 5:32 and 5:43 pm, so a 5:00 pm start ends at 6:00 with its last 17 to 28 minutes in warm, low light. From January 23 to October 7 it starts at 6:00 pm or later. If the timing or the answer does not work, our Isla Mujeres family photo session guide covers the rest of the island.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can we bring an outside photographer to Mia Reef Isla Mujeres?

The resort publishes no rule for outside photographers on its website or in its wedding brochure, so ask it in writing. Some hotels charge a fee; if Mia Reef does, you pay it to the hotel, and we confirm the hotel's rule with you before the session.

How much is the Mia Reef Isla Mujeres day pass?

Its FAQ lists 98 USD per adult and 49 USD per minor, from 9:00 am to 6:00 pm, with no reservation needed, including a towel, beach chairs, the beach and pool area, open bar, breakfast and lunch. Ask whether a photographer needs a pass too.

What time is sunset at Mia Reef Isla Mujeres?

By our calculation, between 6:04 pm in late November and 7:33 pm in early July, on Quintana Roo's year-round UTC-5 clock. Golden light starts about half an hour before, and from the resort the sun sets over the bay.

Is the water at Mia Reef calm enough for small children?

The resort calls Playa Norte a safe natural pool where you can walk about a hundred yards out and the water hardly passes your waist. Its King's Pool, a natural rock pool, may close without notice.

How do we get to Mia Reef from Cancun?

By ferry from Puerto Juárez, as the resort's directions say, then by taxi from right next to the ferry station on the island. On November 14, 2026, Ultramar's round trip was 580 pesos per adult and 440 per child.

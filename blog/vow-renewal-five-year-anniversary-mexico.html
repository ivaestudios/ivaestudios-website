Married in 2020? Your Vow Renewal Year Is Now

In short

Couples married between 2020 and early 2022 hit their five-year milestone in 2025 and 2026, and that cohort is driving the renewal wave. Most bring 6 to 20 guests (ten is the most common), write vows about the five years rather than forever, and hold the ceremony about 45 minutes before sunset.

There is a very specific group of couples booking us this year. They got married in 2020, or 2021, or early 2022. Ten people in a garden, or a courthouse, or a living room with a laptop propped on a stack of books so someone's mother could watch from another country. They are married, they are happy, and they never got the day. Five years later they are looking at an anniversary and thinking, quietly, that they would like to do it properly. If that is you, this is what a destination renewal on the Mexican Caribbean actually involves.

Why 2026 Is the Year This Cohort Books

The arithmetic is doing the work. A wedding in spring 2020 hits five years in 2025, and a wedding in late 2021 hits five in 2026. Five years is the first anniversary that feels like a real milestone rather than a nice dinner, and for this particular group it lands on a wedding that most of them still describe with an apology in the sentence.

There are practical reasons too. By year five, most couples have stable finances, passports that have been used again, and often a small child who is now old enough to walk down a beach without being carried. Parents who were shielding in 2020 can travel. The friends who watched on a screen are willing to fly. And the couples themselves have five years of proof, which changes the tone of the vows in a way that photographs differently. There is less nervousness in the frame and more amusement.

One scheduling note, because it is the most common mistake. If you want a specific week in the dry, calm season, which on this coast runs roughly December through April, book six to nine months out. That window is also peak destination wedding season, so you are competing for the same beaches and the same golden hours as full weddings. May, June, September and October are far more available and, in exchange, wetter and warmer.

Renewal Versus a Second Wedding: What Actually Changes

Almost everything gets easier and one thing gets harder.

Easier: there is no legal machinery. No licence, no filings, no waiting period, no fear that a document will not arrive in time. There is no wedding party to manage, no processional order that anyone will argue about, and no expectation that you feed a hundred people. Nobody is watching for tradition to be observed correctly, because the tradition already happened.

Harder: the day has no script. A wedding gives you a structure whether you want one or not. A renewal gives you an empty two hours on a beach, and couples who do not decide in advance what happens in them tend to drift. The fix is small. Choose three moments and build around them: the walk down, the words, and a toast. Everything else can be loose.

What we tell couples

Write the vows yourselves, and write them about the five years, not about forever. Forever was covered in 2020. The material that makes people cry in a renewal is the specific stuff: the flat you hated, the year one of you was ill, the night the baby would not sleep. Two minutes each is plenty.

Legal? Symbolic? What You Need in Mexico, Honestly

A vow renewal is symbolic by definition. You are already married, so nothing is being created and nothing is filed. That means none of the requirements attached to a legal marriage in Quintana Roo apply to you, and the officiant can be a resort celebrant, a Mayan officiant, a friend or one of your parents.

Where couples get confused is when someone suggests also making it legal in Mexico. That is a genuinely separate process handled through the local civil registry, with its own documentation, translation, witness and timing requirements that change from time to time and are administered locally. It is doable, and your planner or resort wedding coordinator is the right person to run it, but do not assume it can be bolted onto a renewal a fortnight beforehand. For the overwhelming majority of five-year couples the answer is simple: keep it symbolic, spend the effort on the ceremony itself.

Guest List: The Ten People Who Missed the First One

Six to twenty guests is the range we see, and ten is the mode. It is almost always the same list: both sets of parents, the siblings, and the two or three friends who were on a video call in 2020 instead of in the room.

Ten people changes the economics completely. You do not need a ballroom, a room block, a shuttle fleet or a rain plan for two hundred. A restaurant reservation covers dinner. A beach section covers the ceremony. Most resorts on this coast will run a small symbolic ceremony as a package, and if yours does not, a private beach club will.

Two things worth deciding early. First, whether guests pay for themselves, which for a five-year renewal is normal and should be stated plainly in the first message you send them. Second, whether children are in the ceremony. If you have a three or four year old who was not born or not walking at the wedding, putting them in the middle of the renewal is the single strongest photographic idea in this entire article. The pictures where a child is holding both parents' hands are the ones that end up printed.

Adding a Mayan Blessing or a Cenote Portrait

Two additions are specific to this coast and worth considering.

A Mayan blessing is a symbolic ceremony led by a local officiant using copal incense, a conch shell, flowers and water, calling the four cardinal directions. It runs 20 to 30 minutes and is usually spoken in Spanish and Maya, so if your guests need it, ask for a printed translation of the order. Photographically it is a gift. Copal smoke lit from behind at low sun is one of the very few effects on this coast that cannot be manufactured in post-production. Schedule it in the last hour before sunset, never at midday, and ask the officiant to work with the sun behind them.

A cenote portrait is the other one. The freshwater sinkholes inland from Playa del Carmen and Tulum give you a palette that has nothing to do with the Caribbean: dark stone, green water, and in the open cenotes a shaft of light between roughly 11 am and 1 pm. It is a separate half day, it involves an entrance fee and a drive, and sunscreen and insect repellent are usually banned in the water. If you want images that do not look like every other beach anniversary, this is how you get them. We wrote up the practicalities in what to expect from a cenote couples photoshoot.

"Five years in, the vows get funnier and the photographs get calmer. Nobody is performing a wedding. They are just very glad to be there."

Wearing the Original Dress Five Years Later

Roughly half of the couples we photograph wear the original, and it carries a weight nothing bought for the occasion can match. Plan for three specific problems.

Fit, first. Five years is five years, and pregnancy, fitness and time all move a dress. Try it on at home three months out, not the night before. A seamstress in your own city is cheaper and calmer than an emergency in a resort lobby.

Travel, second. A preservation box will not survive checked luggage, and a checked bag containing a wedding dress is a bag you cannot replace. Carry it on in a garment bag. Airlines on these routes will usually let you hang it, and if not, it sits in an overhead bin fine.

Humidity, third. Caribbean air is generous with soft fabrics, relaxing creases on its own, and unkind to anything stiff or heavily structured. Ask the hotel to steam it the day before rather than an hour before, and accept that the hem will meet sand and salt water. That is the correct outcome. A dress that has been in a box for five years should get wet. Our guide to wedding dresses in Mexican beach humidity covers fabrics in more detail.

The alternative is legitimate too. Plenty of couples buy something lighter and shorter for the beach and keep the original for the photographs indoors, or skip it entirely. There is no wrong version of a day that is already, technically, optional.

A Two-Day Plan That Fits Inside a Resort Stay

This is the shape we recommend, and it needs no more than a normal week's holiday.

  • Day one, late afternoon. Guests arrive and settle. A welcome dinner, informal, no speeches. We shoot 30 to 40 minutes of the two of you at golden hour beforehand while everyone else is at the pool. These are the relaxed frames, and they take the pressure off the ceremony day entirely.
  • Day two, morning. Optional. Either a cenote half day, or a slow morning while somebody does hair and makeup, or nothing at all. On this coast the morning is the honest choice for anything with children in it.
  • Day two, ninety minutes before sunset. Everyone gathers. Ceremony at roughly 45 minutes before sunset, which puts warm light on your faces rather than a silhouette. Twenty minutes of words, a blessing if you have chosen one, then a toast on the sand.
  • Day two, sunset to blue hour. Family groups first while people are still assembled, then the two of you alone as the light drops, then dinner. Blue hour on this coast is worth ten more minutes than most people give it.

That is two evenings of coverage and one optional morning, and it fits inside a stay that everyone was going to enjoy anyway. Full destination weddings are welcome to be bigger. Renewals rarely need to be.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a vow renewal in Mexico legally binding?
No, and that is the point. You are already married, so a renewal is symbolic by definition. Nothing is filed, no civil judge is required, and none of the paperwork that a legal marriage in Quintana Roo demands applies to you. If you want a legal Mexican marriage certificate as well, that is a separate civil process with its own requirements and lead time, and your resort or planner should walk you through it with the local civil registry.
How far ahead should we book a five-year renewal for 2026 or 2027?
For the dry, calm months of December through April, six to nine months is realistic, because that window is also peak wedding season on this coast. For May, June, September and October you can often book inside three months. The studio accepts a maximum of twelve weddings a year, and renewals compete for the same golden hours, so the date matters more than the notice.
Can we wear the original wedding dress five years later?
Many couples do, and it photographs with a weight nothing new can match. Plan for three things: fit, because five years is five years and a seamstress at home is cheaper than a panic at the resort; travel, because a preservation box will not survive a suitcase and a garment bag as carry-on is safer; and humidity, because Caribbean air relaxes creases but also flattens stiff fabrics. Ask your hotel to steam it the day before, not an hour before.
What is a Mayan blessing and how long does it take?
It is a symbolic ceremony led by a local officiant using copal incense, a conch shell, flowers and an invocation of the four cardinal directions. It usually runs 20 to 30 minutes and is spoken in Spanish and Maya, so ask for a translated printed order if your guests need it. Photographically it is a gift, because backlit copal smoke at low sun is one of the few effects on this coast you cannot manufacture.
How many guests do most five-year renewals have?
The pattern we see is between six and twenty people, and most often around ten. It is usually the parents, the siblings and the two or three friends who were on a video call in 2020 instead of in the room. That size fits inside an ordinary resort stay, needs no ballroom, and keeps the day built around light rather than logistics.

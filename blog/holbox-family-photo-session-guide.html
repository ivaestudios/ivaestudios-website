Holbox Family Photo Session Guide

In short

Worth it for the right family with one night booked: every trip runs through Chiquilá, two to two and a half hours from Cancún by road, then a ferry. November through April is the dependable window, September and October are the months we talk families out of, and Gulf-side Holbox largely escapes sargassum.

Isla Holbox has spent the last two years on every slow travel and babymoon list written about Mexico, and families keep asking us whether it is worth the trip for photographs. The honest answer is that it is worth it for the right family, in the right month, with one night booked. Holbox is not a shorter version of Cancún. It is a car-free sandbar on the far north west edge of Quintana Roo where the Gulf meets the Caribbean, the water turns pale silver instead of turquoise, and getting there costs you the better part of a morning. This is what a family session here actually involves.

Getting there: Chiquilá, the ferry and the day cost

There is no bridge and no airport worth the name. Every trip to Holbox goes through the village of Chiquilá, which sits about two to two and a half hours north west of Cancún by road, depending on where you start and how the highway is behaving. From the Riviera Maya resorts further south, add the time it takes you to get back up to Cancún first. Families driving themselves leave the car in one of the guarded lots in Chiquilá, which charge by the day and are easy to find because they line the road into the village.

From the pier, passenger ferries cross to Holbox in roughly twenty five to thirty minutes. Boats run frequently through the day, starting early and finishing mid evening, and there is more than one operator, so you rarely wait long. What we tell every family is this: confirm the last return departure on the day you travel, at the ticket window, in person. Seasonal schedules move, weather cancels crossings, and no travel article on the internet, including this one, should be your source for the boat that gets your children back to the mainland.

Budget the day honestly. From a Cancún hotel you are looking at roughly three hours door to door each way once you add the drive, the parking, the ticket line, the crossing and the cart ride to your hotel. That is the number families underestimate, and it is the single reason most Holbox sessions we photograph belong to guests who are already staying on the island.

The one thing to check before you commit

Ferries are passenger only. Whatever you cannot carry across the pier and lift into a golf cart, you are not bringing. Families with a large stroller, a travel cot and four suitcases should plan for a porter with a luggage cart at the Holbox pier, which is a normal service and worth the tip.

No cars: golf carts, sand streets and a family

Holbox has no cars. Transport is golf carts, bicycles and your own feet, and the streets in the village are sand rather than pavement. Golf cart taxis wait at the pier and can be flagged down along the main streets, and most hotels will send one for you if you ask ahead.

For photographs this is the island's best feature. Nothing on Holbox moves quickly, so a family session here has a completely different rhythm from a resort beach in the Hotel Zone. Nobody is dodging a jet ski vendor, no music is competing from a beach club two hundred metres away, and children who normally hold a parent's hand near a road simply run. Some of the strongest frames we make on this island are children walking ahead down a sand street with washed pastel walls on both sides, which is a picture you cannot take anywhere else in the state.

Be realistic about the flip side. Sand streets and wheels do not get along. A stroller is genuinely difficult, and after heavy rain the low streets hold water for a day or two. If a grandparent has a cane, a knee or limited stamina, plan the session inside a small radius, use a cart between every location, and tell us in advance so we choose spots with a firm approach from the road. We have run sessions here with three generations and it works, but only when the walking is designed rather than assumed.

Sandbars, Punta Cocos and the shallow-water look

The reason Holbox photographs the way it does is the water depth. The island sits on an enormous shallow shelf, and in places you can walk out a very long way and still be knee deep. That geography gives us two signature locations.

Punta Cocos is the south western tip, about fifteen to twenty minutes from the village by cart. It is where the island faces west, which means it is the only part of Holbox where the sun sets into open water, and the shallow flats there hold a mirror surface on a still evening. For a family portrait this is the frame people book the trip for: everyone standing in a few inches of water with the reflection of the sky underneath them and no horizon line to speak of.

Punta Mosquito is the opposite end, east of the village along the beach, and at low tide a long sandbar opens up into the lagoon. It is the wilder side, it is where the flamingos and shorebirds feed, and it is a protected part of the Yum Balam reserve, so we photograph from the sand and keep a wide distance from the birds. It is also a real walk. With small children we go by cart as far as the carts are allowed and treat the rest as part of the session rather than a march to a location.

Between the two, the beach directly in front of the village gives us the swimming pool colours and the palapas, with the shallowest water and the shortest walk. For families with toddlers, that stretch does most of the work.

"Holbox is the only place on this coast where a family can stand in the sea, in a reflection, with the sun going down into the water behind them. That combination does not exist in Cancún."

Best months, and the months we would talk you out of

November through April is the dependable window. It is the drier half of the year, the heat is manageable, the water is calmer and the mosquitoes are far less of an event. If you have flexibility and you are choosing dates purely for photographs, that is where we point you.

Mid May to mid September is whale shark season, which is the island's high season and the reason many families come at all. The wildlife is extraordinary and the trips are worth doing, but understand what you are buying: heat, humidity, the busiest streets of the year, and boats leaving early enough that a tired family is not at its best for a session that evening. If you are here for the whale sharks, we schedule the session for a day with no boat, not the same afternoon.

September and October are the two months we will talk a family out of. That is the wettest stretch of the year on this coast, it sits inside the peak of hurricane season, the sand streets flood after a heavy night and a noticeable number of restaurants and small hotels close for low season. Holbox in October can be beautiful and empty, and it can also be three grey days in a row with a cancelled ferry. If those are your only dates, we would rather photograph you in Cancún and say so.

One more honest note that applies year round: sargassum is far less of a problem here than on the Caribbean facing coast, because Holbox sits on the Gulf side of the peninsula. What you get instead is sea grass and a naturally soft, silty bottom in places. It is a different look, not a dirty one, but it is not the swept white powder of a resort beach that a groundskeeper rakes at six in the morning. Our sargassum season guide explains how the currents work if you are comparing coasts.

Where the light is different from Cancún

This is the part that matters most to us, and it is the reason we recommend Holbox to families who already have a Cancún gallery at home.

Cancún and the Riviera Maya face east. The water is deep close to shore, the colour is saturated turquoise, and the sun sets behind the buildings. Holbox faces north and west across a shallow, plankton rich shelf where the Gulf and the Caribbean mix. The result is a paler, cooler palette: silver, pale green, milky blue, and a sky that reflects into the water instead of contrasting against it. Sunsets are long, low and directly over water, and the last twenty minutes go pink and lilac in a way the east coast simply does not do.

For a family, that translates into softer skin tones, no squinting, and a picture that reads as a painting rather than a postcard. It is also why we shoot Holbox almost entirely in the evening. Sunrise here is over the lagoon and mangrove side and it is lovely, but the classic Holbox photograph is the last hour of the day at Punta Cocos, and everything else in the session builds toward it. The studio works golden hour only in every location we cover, and on this island that discipline is not a preference, it is the entire reason to come.

Staying overnight versus a long day trip

Run the arithmetic on a day trip with a sunset session and the problem is obvious. To be at Punta Cocos for the last hour of light you finish somewhere around dusk, ride back to the village, wait for a ferry, cross for half an hour, collect the car and drive two hours or more in the dark. With children, you are walking into your hotel close to midnight, and the next morning is written off.

Staying one night changes everything. The session becomes a relaxed evening walk that ends with dinner in the village, nobody watches the clock, and if the sky is flat on your first evening we have a second chance the following day. It also gives us a sunrise option on the lagoon side at no travel cost, which is useful for families with very small children who fall apart after 6 PM.

Two nights is better still and is what we suggest to families who are travelling specifically for these photographs. It buys you one weather buffer and it turns Holbox into part of the holiday rather than an errand with a camera at the end.

Who Holbox suits and who it does not

Holbox suits families who want a different picture. If you have three galleries of turquoise water and white sand from previous trips, this island gives you a visual palette that will not sit alongside them. It suits couples travelling with one or two children who are old enough to walk, families who like slow places, and anyone who values quiet over amenities. It also suits multigenerational groups who are already staying on the island and have time to spare.

It does not suit everyone. If your trip is five days and already full of excursions, the travel time will cost you a day you cannot spare. If someone in the group has limited mobility, the sand streets and the distances are a genuine obstacle. If your dates fall in September or October, the odds are against you. And if what you actually want is the classic bright turquoise family photograph, Holbox is the wrong island; that picture lives in Cancún, Playa Mujeres and the Riviera Maya, where we photograph families every week.

When Holbox is right, it is one of the most rewarding places we work. Tell us your dates, where you are staying and how old the children are, and we will tell you straight whether the trip earns its travel time. Our approach to luxury family photography is the same here as everywhere: golden hour only, a plan built around the youngest person in the group, and a private 4K gallery in your hands within one to three business days. Questions families ask

How do you get to Holbox from Cancún?

You drive or ride roughly two to two and a half hours north west to the village of Chiquilá, leave the vehicle in one of the guarded lots there, and take a passenger ferry across to the island. The crossing takes about twenty five to thirty minutes and boats run frequently through the day from early morning until mid evening. Schedules and the last departure change with the season, so confirm the return time on the day rather than trusting an old article.

Are there cars on Isla Holbox?

No. The island runs on golf carts, bicycles and walking, and the streets are sand rather than pavement. Golf cart taxis wait at the pier and can be flagged in the village. For a family session this is mostly good news, because it slows everything down, but it does mean strollers are hard work and you should plan a cart for any location outside the village.

When is the best time of year for family photos on Holbox?

November through April is the most reliable stretch: drier, cooler, fewer mosquitoes and calmer water. Mid May to mid September brings the whale shark season and the biggest crowds, along with heat and humidity. September and October are the months we would talk a family out of, because that is the wettest part of the year, the sand streets flood after heavy rain and a number of businesses close for low season.

Is the water on Holbox as turquoise as Cancún?

No, and that is the point of going. Holbox sits where the Gulf of Mexico meets the Caribbean, so the water is shallow, plankton rich and reads pale green, silver and milky blue rather than deep turquoise. It photographs as a soft pastel palette instead of the saturated postcard blue, which is exactly why it looks different from every other gallery from this coast.

Should we do Holbox as a day trip or stay overnight?

For a golden hour family session we strongly recommend staying at least one night. A day trip that ends with a sunset session means the last ferry, a two hour drive in the dark and small children arriving back near midnight. Staying over turns the same session into a relaxed evening, and it gives us a sunrise option the next morning at no extra travel.

Who is Holbox not right for?

Families with limited mobility, families travelling with a stroller and a lot of gear, and families on a tight four or five day trip who are already committed to excursions. The sand streets, the ferry and the drive add up. If the trip is short, a session in Cancún, Playa Mujeres or the Riviera Maya gives you the same quality of photograph with a fraction of the logistics.

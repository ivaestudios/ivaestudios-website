In short

Plan the session in the order your family grew: the two of you, the original family before partners join, each couple, then the grandchildren, with everyone together at the end. If a baby or toddler is coming, their frames go first. Partners and grandchildren add no cost, there is no deposit, and a preview arrives within 24 hours.

For years, family photos meant managing the kids: the nap, the snacks, the sandal that vanished ten minutes before the start. Then the kids grew up. Now they fly in from different cities, one with a new spouse, another with a baby of her own, and nobody needs to be bribed to stand still. The job changes with them. Now it is about giving every grown household its own portraits while keeping the two of you, the parents who started it all, at the center of the frame.

Since 2023 the studio has delivered more than 500 sessions, and it holds 5.0 across 75 Google reviews. This is how we would plan family beach photos with adult children, whether your kids are 20 or 40.

When Everyone in the Frame Is an Adult

For empty nesters, the trip that brings everyone back may be the only week of the year with the whole family in one place.

The calendar is the hard part

Grown siblings land on different flights, leave on different days and sometimes stay at different resorts. Book the session early in the days when everyone overlaps, not on the last evening, when someone is already packing. Online you can choose a 6:30, 7:00 or 7:30 am start, or 5:00 or 5:30 pm; for any other time, message us on WhatsApp. If no little ones are coming, pick the window your earliest riser and latest sleeper can both live with. If plans change, cancellation is free until one day before.

Where everyone is staying

Tell us where each household is staying. Some hotels charge their own fee for outside photographers, which you pay to the hotel, and we confirm your resort's rule before the session. For groups of up to 16 people, we can also arrange transport in full vans to the session location; ask about it in your first message.

Adults are harder to relax than toddlers

A three-year-old forgets the camera within minutes. A 34-year-old who dislikes photos of himself does not. So we would rather give grown-ups something to do than ask them to smile: walk together at the water's edge, tell the story of the worst family road trip, whisper something to the sibling next to you. As one client wrote on Google:

“I'm usually not someone who loves taking pictures, but the team made the whole process easy, relaxed, and actually enjoyable.”

Adam H., Google review

The partner question

Should a son-in-law or daughter-in-law be in the family photo? Yes. We also suggest a few frames without them: the original family, the parents with the children they raised. Neither frame ranks anybody.

One message before the trip

Send one line to the family chat before anyone packs, something like a few with just the original five, then everyone. Partners know the plan, and nobody has to decide on the sand whether to step in or out.

Shoot It in the Order Your Family Grew

Family photos with adult kids and grandkids work best in layers. When everyone is an adult, we suggest building the session the way your family was built:

  1. The two of you. The parents alone, while your grown kids stand behind us and make you laugh.
  2. The original family. The parents and the children they raised, no partners, no grandchildren: the four or five people who once fit in one car. Then the siblings alone, and each grown child with the two of you.
  3. Partners join. The adult family together, then each couple on its own.
  4. Grandchildren join. Each household with its children, the two of you with all the grandchildren, and finally everyone, with the two of you at the center and each household gathered around you.

Each step adds people, so the session tells your family's story in the order it happened.

If a baby or toddler is coming, turn it around: every frame with the little one goes first, the everyone frame too, while they are fresh. Then they can play at the water's edge with a parent while we photograph the original family and the two of you. When young grandchildren are the heart of the trip, our multigenerational planning guide goes deeper on settings, heat and naps, and our photo ideas for three generations include a full shot list.

Ideas for frames with grown kids

Worth adding to your list:

  • The siblings in birth order, the way they lined up as kids.
  • Each parent with each child.
  • The two of you walking ahead, your grown children a few steps behind.
  • The partners together, the people who joined the family later.

Whatever the order, the two of you stay at the center. Twenty years ago the photos were about the kids. This time they are about all of you.

Recreate the Childhood Beach Photo

Many families have one: three kids in a row on the sand, oldest to youngest, someone squinting, a parent's shadow in the corner. Recreating it twenty or thirty years later gets a laugh on the day and a long look afterward. A few things make it work:

  • Send us the original before the trip. A phone picture of the print, sent on WhatsApp, is enough. It shows us where the camera stood: how high, how close, how wide.
  • Copy the positions, not the outfits. Who stood where, whose hand was on whose shoulder. Echo the colors instead of hunting for the exact clothes, and if someone still owns the original T-shirt, bring it.
  • Adjust what no longer fits. The toddler on dad's shoulders is now a grown man. Turn the lift into an arm around the shoulders: anything that could hurt a back stays a joke.
  • Then take the honest version. Same people, same spot, everyone as they are now. Then and now, side by side, tell the whole story.

If the original was taken on this coast, tell us where; if it was a lake in Ontario or a beach back home, sand and water still carry the resemblance. Frequently Asked Questions

Should partners be in family photos with adult children?

Yes, and the original family deserves a few frames of its own. We suggest both: the parents with the children they raised, then everyone with partners and grandchildren. Share the plan in the family chat before the trip.

Do partners and grandchildren cost extra?

No. There is no limit on the number of people and no per-person charge, so partners and grandchildren add no cost. You pay only for the collection, with no deposit: payment is at the end of the session, by card or in cash.

How do we recreate a childhood beach photo with grown kids?

Send us the original before the trip; a phone picture of the print is enough. Repeat who stood where, echo the colors instead of the exact outfits, and turn any old lift into an arm around the shoulders. Then take one more, as you are now.

Which collection fits parents, grown kids and their partners?

The Chapter, 60 minutes and 35 edited photos for 475 USD, is our recommended family collection. If each couple wants a second look, choose The Story, 75 minutes and 50 photos for 545 USD, or The Editorial, 90 minutes and 70 photos for 645 USD, both with 2 looks.

How do siblings in different cities get the photos?

A preview arrives within 24 hours of the session, and the private 4K gallery in 1 to 3 business days, with unlimited downloads and print rights, so you can share the full-resolution files with every household, and each one can print its own copies wherever it lives.

In short

IVAE Studios is a Cancun family photographer offering sessions to families staying at The Royal Sands (Royal Resorts). Sessions start at 295 USD, with no per-person charge and no deposit; you get a preview within 24 hours and your private gallery in 1 to 3 business days. The best light is at sunrise or in the last hour before sunset (sunset is 6:06 pm in mid-November and 7:32 pm in mid-July). Royal Resorts does not allow outside vendors inside its resorts, so the session happens on the public beach: you walk out to the sand and we meet you at the water's edge in front of the resort, or at the Playa Marlin public access, about 0.7 km north. For families we recommend The Chapter, 60 minutes and 35 edited photos for 475 USD.

The Royal Sands calls itself a family resort, and it is built like one: 340 two-bedroom villas at km 13.5 of the Hotel Zone, a Kids Club, an Aqua Park and a beach on the open Caribbean. It is part of Royal Resorts, a vacation ownership club of more than 60,000 member families from 51 countries. This guide covers where a session can happen under the resort's rules, which hour suits this beach, naps and the Kids Club, and what it costs.

The Royal Sands and its beach

The official name is The Royal Sands by Holiday Inn Club Vacations. Holiday Inn Club Vacations bought it in 2023 with three other Royal Resorts properties, and the only other one of the four in Cancun is The Royal Cancun, at km 4.5 on the north shore. The Royal Sands opened in 2000 and has the most villas of the four: 340 two-bedroom villas on 17 acres, with three pools and a children's pool, four restaurants, a spa and a supervised Kids Club, 20 minutes from the airport according to the resort.

For photographs, the beach is what matters. It runs along the east side of the property and faces the open Caribbean, roughly east-southeast. The sun rises out of the sea all year and sets behind the resort, on the Nichupte Lagoon side, so there is no sunset over the sea from this beach: mornings here have the sun itself, evenings have softer, gentler light.

The outside vendor policy, and the public beach

Royal Resorts publishes an External Vendors Policy, in force since November 15, 2025. It reminds members and guests that inviting outside vendors to provide services inside the resorts is strictly prohibited, and that the resort may refuse entry to, or remove, anyone who tries to provide services there without authorization. Friends and relatives who are not staying may visit for the day, registering at the front desk under the Day Pass policy. A photographer working for you is a vendor, not a visiting relative, so we do not come in as your guest and we will not ask you to register us.

In practice that rules out the villas, terraces, lobby, gardens, pools and Aqua Park. It does not rule out the beach. Mexican beaches are federal and public, so the shoreline next to the resort is the free alternative: you walk out to the sand, we reach it from a public access, and the whole session happens at the water's edge.

Ask the resort in writing

The policy does not name photographers or publish a fee, and rules change. Some resorts charge outside photographers a fee or ask them to register; Royal Resorts keeps outside vendors out instead. Ask the resort in writing what applies to your dates; we confirm the rule with you before the session.

Weddings have their own rule: Royal Resorts' 2026 wedding packages, which include symbolic ceremonies and vow renewals, state that all services for the ceremony and reception are supplied by Royal Resorts and that external suppliers are not permitted. A family session with us is a separate hour on the public sand.

Best spots by hour

Sunrise, 6:05 am in June to 7:27 am in January. The sun comes up out of the sea in front of you, low and warm, and Hotel Zone beaches are usually at their quietest. The youngest walk straight from the villa to the sand, and we finish before breakfast.

Midday, roughly 11 am to 3 pm, is the one window we avoid: the light is hard and children squint.

The last hour before sunset. Evening golden light starts between 5:34 pm in November and 7:00 pm in July, and the sun sets about half an hour later, behind the resort. We start about an hour before sunset with the whole-family portraits and finish with walking and play at the waterline.

The default spot is the waterline in front of the resort, because nobody has to travel; to meet away from your building, we use the Playa Marlin public access next to Plaza Kukulcan, about 0.7 km north. Have an older family photo from a past stay? Bring it and we will recreate it on the public sand.

A local tip: the resort's official page carries two live webcams looking across the pools to the beach. Check them the day before and you will see the sand, the waves and any seaweed.

On this beach the sun rises out of the sea and sets behind the resort. Plan the hour around that, and around the nap, and the rest is easy.

Kids, naps and the resort's schedule

The photography schedule and the toddler schedule are the same schedule. Before we talk about outfits, we ask when the youngest ones nap and eat, and what the family has already booked that day.

For children under four, sunrise usually wins: they are awake early anyway, it is cooler, and the session is over before the day starts. La Veranda, which the resort describes as family friendly, opens at 7 am for breakfast afterwards. Our guide to sunrise sessions with young kids explains why it works.

For older children the evening is easier. The Kids Club, for ages 4 to 12, is open 9 am to 6 pm on weekdays and 9 am to 5 pm at weekends, so collect the children from the Kids Club or the Aqua Park early enough for a snack, a shower and fresh clothes: a child who comes straight off the slides is a tired child. Hacienda Sisal opens at 5 pm and needs a reservation, so book dinner for after sunset. More ideas in our guide to family photos with toddlers.

Public beaches near the resort

Public accesses near the resort, with straight-line distances. Like the resort's beach, all face the open Caribbean, so expect waves; the session does not need calm water.

  • Playa Marlin, km 13, about 0.7 km north. Public access next to Plaza Kukulcan, with restrooms, showers, lifeguards and limited free parking by day. The surf can be strong, so follow the flags. Photographer guide
  • Playa Ballenas, km 14.5, about 1 km south. The public entrance, between Hard Rock Hotel Cancun and Secrets The Vine, looks like a hotel driveway. Restrooms, showers, palapas and picnic tables, no restaurants; strong waves and frequent red flags. Photographer guide
  • Playa Delfines, km 18, about 4 km south, so a drive rather than a walk. Free palapas, restrooms, showers and a playground. Photographer guide

Prefer the bay side of the north shore? The Royal Resorts shuttle runs to the sister resort, The Royal Cancun, during the day (times and reservations at the motor lobby). Royal Resorts calls that beach sheltered and kid-friendly, and Playa Langosta, a public beach about 260 m away, is where we would meet you; check the return times for an evening session.

Sargassum reaches this coast too: it usually builds from April and is heaviest from June to August, with November to March normally the clearest stretch, but it changes week to week. If the sand in front of the resort is covered the day before, we compare Marlin and Ballenas or move to another day. Details in our Cancun sargassum season guide.

What to wear on this beach

Keep it simple: two or three soft neutrals such as white, sand and cream, one muted colour, light fabrics that move in the breeze and bare feet on the sand. Skip big logos and neon. Our guide on what to wear for a family photoshoot in Mexico has full palettes, and grandparents have their own beach outfit guide.

Questions families ask

Can you photograph our family at The Royal Sands (Royal Resorts)?

Yes, on the public beach. Royal Resorts' External Vendors Policy, in force since November 15, 2025, does not allow outside vendors to provide services inside its resorts, so we never work in the villas, gardens, pools or lobby. Mexican beaches are federal and public, so you walk out to the sand and we meet you at the water's edge in front of the resort, or at the Playa Marlin public access, about 0.7 km north.

What time is best for family photos at The Royal Sands?

Sunrise or the last hour before sunset. The sun rises out of the sea, at 6:05 am in mid-June and 7:27 am in mid-January, and sets behind the resort, at 6:06 pm in mid-November and 7:32 pm in mid-July. With children under four we usually choose sunrise; we avoid 11 am to 3 pm.

How much does a family photo session at The Royal Sands cost?

Our collections run from 295 USD for 35 minutes and 15 edited photos to 895 USD for 90 minutes and the full edited gallery. For families we recommend The Chapter: 60 minutes and 35 edited photos for 475 USD. There is no deposit and no per-person charge: you pay when the session ends, see a preview within 24 hours and receive the gallery in 1 to 3 business days.

Does The Royal Sands charge outside photographers?

No fee is published. Some resorts charge outside photographers a fee or ask them to register; Royal Resorts instead prohibits inviting outside vendors to provide services inside its resorts. Mexican beaches are federal and public, so the shoreline next to the resort is the free alternative. Ask the resort in writing for your dates, and we confirm the rule with you before the session.

Can grandparents and cousins join?

Yes. There is no limit on the number of people and no per-person charge. For three generations we suggest The Story (75 minutes, 50 photos, 545 USD) or The Editorial (90 minutes, 70 photos, 645 USD), both with two looks, so there is time for the whole group, each family and the grandparents with the grandchildren.

Where do we meet the photographer?

At the water's edge in front of the resort, at the time we agree for your date, or at the Playa Marlin public access next to Plaza Kukulcan if you prefer. We do not meet in the lobby or ask the front desk to let us in, because the resort does not allow outside vendors inside.

What happens if it rains or the beach has seaweed?

Rescheduling for weather is free, and cancellation is free up to one day before. The resort's official page has two live webcams of the beach, so you can check the sand the day before. If seaweed covers the sand in front of the resort, we look at Playa Marlin and Playa Ballenas or move to another day of your stay.

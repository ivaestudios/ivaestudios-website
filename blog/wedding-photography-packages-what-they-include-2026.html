What Do Wedding Photography Packages Include? The Types, Explained

By Vianey Díaz · September 22, 2026 · 7 min read

In short

Wedding photography packages are built from six parts: hours of coverage, the size of the crew, an engagement or welcome session, what is delivered and how fast, travel, and whether film is included. Read a package by those six lines and the price makes sense. Ours: half day 1,800 to 2,800 USD, full day 3,500 to 5,500, full day with a highlight film 5,500 to 8,000, always with the complete edited gallery in one to three business days.

Package names are marketing. Silver, Gold, Signature, Editorial: they tell you nothing about what you get. What you get is always the same six ingredients in different amounts, and once you can read them, comparing studios becomes easy.

The six parts of every package

Hours of coverage is the first and biggest: it decides whether getting ready, the first look, the ceremony, portraits and the party are all covered, or only some of them. Crew is the second: a second shooter means two angles at the ceremony and the reception covered while the couple is in portraits. A pre-wedding session, engagement or welcome, is the third, and it is more useful than it sounds because it is where we learn how you two are together before it counts. Deliverables are the fourth: how many images, edited how, in what resolution, and whether prints or an album are included. Travel is the fifth, and on a destination wedding it is never optional. Film is the sixth.

The four types of package, by shape

By shape, there are four. The elopement or micro package: two to four hours, one photographer, built around the ceremony and the golden hour, for couples marrying with a handful of witnesses. The half day: up to four or five hours, usually the ceremony, portraits and the start of the reception. The full day: eight to ten hours, a second shooter, from getting ready to the dance floor. And the multi-day: a welcome dinner or a session earlier in the trip, the wedding day, and sometimes a day-after session. Most destination couples land on the full day, because the flight makes a half day feel like a missed chance.

Delivery: the line most couples skip

Delivery is where packages differ most and where couples look least. Ask three questions. How many images, and are they all edited or only a selection? What resolution, and is the full gallery downloadable or only viewable? And when: the industry standard on this coast is a sneak peek in days and the full gallery in six to eight weeks, sometimes longer in high season. We deliver the complete edited gallery in one to three business days through a private 4K gallery, with a sneak peek within 24 to 48 hours, and that single line changes what the trip feels like, because you fly home with the wedding rather than waiting for it.

What our collections include

Our published ranges: a half day, up to four hours built around the ceremony and golden hour, runs 1,800 to 2,800 USD. A full day, eight to ten hours with a second shooter and venue scouting, runs 3,500 to 5,500. The full day with a cinematic highlight film runs 5,500 to 8,000. Every collection includes the complete edited gallery in one to three business days through a private 4K gallery, bilingual coverage on the day, and travel across Cancún and the Riviera Maya arranged as part of the coverage. Sessions, for families, couples and portraits, typically run 650 to 3,500 USD and most start around 850.

How to compare two quotes fairly

Line them up on the six parts and the comparison writes itself. Same hours? Same crew? Is a pre-wedding session included or an add-on? How many edited images, at what resolution, delivered when? Is travel in the number or on top? Is film in, and is it a highlight or a full edit? A cheaper package with fewer hours is not cheaper; it is smaller. And the one line that rarely appears on a quote but decides how the day feels is the studio's discipline about light: whether they plan your timeline against sunset, or against the venue's convenience.

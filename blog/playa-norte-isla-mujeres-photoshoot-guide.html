In short

Playa Norte is the northern tip of Isla Mujeres: wide sand, water that stays shallow a long way out and, unusually for this coast, a sunset over the sea. It is nearly empty at sunrise, full from late morning and beautiful again in the last hour of the day. It is a ten-minute walk from the ferry terminal, and the ferry from Puerto Juárez in Cancun takes about 20 minutes.

If you have seen one photo of Isla Mujeres, it was probably taken at Playa Norte. The water is pale turquoise and knee-deep for a long way, the sand is wide and the beach looks toward the sunset. It is also the busiest beach on the island for most of the day. This guide is about the two hours when it is not, and how to be standing there at the right minute.

The light times are calculated for Playa Norte. Quintana Roo keeps the same clock all year, with no daylight saving.

Why It Photographs So Well

Shallow water. The seabed slopes so gently that you can walk thirty or forty metres from the sand and still be waist deep. In a photo that means people standing in flat, pale turquoise with nothing behind them but water and sky. With small children, it means they can play in the frame safely beside you.

The direction it faces. Most beaches around Cancun face east. Playa Norte faces north and west, so at sunrise the light arrives soft and from the side, and at the end of the day the sun sets into the sea. It is one of the few places in the region where both ends of the day work on the same beach.

Shelter. Isla Mujeres is a narrow island about seven kilometres long. Its eastern side takes the wind and the waves, and Playa Norte sits in the shelter. In the months when sargassum reaches the mainland beaches, the water here is usually cleaner. It is a strong tendency, not a guarantee; our sargassum season guide explains the year.

The Beach, Hour by Hour

First light to 8 am. The best hour. Day visitors have not crossed yet, the sand is clean and the water is flat. This is when we photograph couples who want the beach to themselves and families with early risers.

Late morning to late afternoon. Full. The beach clubs are open, the loungers are out and tour boats anchor offshore. The sun is high and hard. We do not schedule sessions in these hours.

The last hour before sunset. The day visitors start leaving for the ferry and the light turns warm. There are still people on the beach, so we work at the quieter ends and frame toward open water. For color, this is the hour.

After sunset. The sky keeps its glow for about 25 minutes. It is a good moment for a last few frames, and for checking the time of your boat.

Getting There from Cancun

From Puerto Juárez. The main terminal, just north of downtown Cancun. Passenger ferries run frequently from early morning until late at night and cross in about 20 minutes. This is the only terminal with departures early enough for a sunrise session. Ultramar asks passengers to arrive 30 minutes before departure, and its published fare in August 2026 was 290 pesos one way per adult.

From the Hotel Zone. The piers at Playa Tortugas, Playa Caracol and El Embarcadero are more convenient for Hotel Zone hotels, but they start later, run fewer departures and take 25 to 35 minutes.

On the island. From the ferry terminal, Playa Norte is a ten-minute walk through town. No taxi or golf cart is needed for the session itself.

For sunrise. From a Hotel Zone hotel, allow about 90 minutes from your door to being in position on the sand. Or stay one night on the island and walk. Confirm the first departure at the terminal the day before, because timetables change with the season. Coming from the north side of Cancun, see our day plan from Costa Mujeres.

Families. The shallow water is the reason to bring children here. A session at the end of the day lets them play in the sea while we photograph, and the ten-minute walk from the boat is easy with grandparents. What to Wear and Bring

The water at Playa Norte is very pale, so soft colors look best against it: white, sand, light blue and sage. A flowing dress or a linen shirt moves well in the breeze. If you plan to walk into the shallows, choose fabric that looks good wet at the hem, and bring a towel and a dry change for the boat back.

Leave shoes at the edge of the sand; you will be barefoot. Our guides to family outfits and couples outfits have full palettes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best time for photos at Playa Norte?
First light until about 8 am, when the beach is nearly empty, or the last hour before sunset, when the light turns warm and the sun sets over the water. From late morning to late afternoon the beach is full and the sun is hard.
Can you see the sunset at Playa Norte?
Yes. Playa Norte faces north and west, so the sun sets into the sea. Sunset falls between 6:04 pm in late November and 7:33 pm in early July.
What time is sunrise on Isla Mujeres?
Between 6:05 am in June and 7:27 am in mid-January. Quintana Roo does not use daylight saving, so the clock never shifts.
How do you get to Playa Norte from Cancun?
Take a passenger ferry to Isla Mujeres, about 20 minutes from Puerto Juárez or 25 to 35 minutes from the Hotel Zone piers, then walk ten minutes from the ferry terminal to the beach.
Is Playa Norte good for family photos?
Yes. The water stays shallow a long way from the sand, so children can play in the frame safely, and the beach is a short, flat walk from the ferry.
Is there seaweed at Playa Norte?
Usually less than on the mainland. The beach sits on the sheltered side of the island, so in sargassum months the water is normally cleaner, although a week with the wrong wind can bring some around the north tip.

A baby and a toddler want opposite things from the same hour. The baby wants milk, shade and a familiar shoulder. The toddler wants to run straight at the waves. Our guides to family photos with toddlers and family photos with a baby each plan around one small child. This one is for families traveling with two under three, or a baby and a preschooler, who need one plan that works for both.

Two Naps, One Slot

With one small child, we plan backward from the nap. With two, the start time has to land where both schedules overlap: after both children have slept and eaten, and before either one needs the next nap or bedtime. Write both rhythms down on Cancun time; if the kids are still on home time, tell us when they actually nap here.

The booking page lists five start times, three in the morning and two in the late afternoon, all in Cancun time. If the gap between the two naps falls somewhere else, write to us on WhatsApp and we will look for a time that fits it.

Morning: 6:30, 7:00 or 7:30 am

Mornings suit little ones who wake early anyway. Both children start the day rested, the beach is usually at its quietest and the sand is still cool under crawling knees. If the baby's first nap comes early, 6:30 finishes soonest; if everyone needs a feed or breakfast first, 7:30 gives you room. Sunrise comes later in the winter months, so for those dates ask us which morning start makes the most of the light. For the longer argument in favor of early light with small children, see sunrise versus sunset for families with young kids.

Late afternoon: 5:00 or 5:30 pm

The 5:00 and 5:30 pm starts work when both children take a long nap after lunch. The catch is the day itself: a toddler who spent it at the pool can arrive running on empty, so keep the hours before the session quiet and protect both naps.

Send us both schedules

When you book, tell us each child's age, when each one usually naps and eats, and which of the two is harder to wake up happy. We build the start time around the answers.

Keep It to 45 or 60 Minutes

With two small children, a session should be long enough for one feed and one reset, and short enough to end before anyone fades. For most families with a baby and a toddler, that points to one of two collections:

  • The Keepsake: 45 minutes, 25 edited photos and one look for 375 USD. A good fit when both children run on short fuses, or when the slot sits tight between naps.
  • The Chapter: 60 minutes, 35 edited photos and one look for 475 USD. It is the collection we recommend for families, and with two little ones the extra 15 minutes are what make room for a feed, a diaper change or a meltdown without anyone watching the clock.

We rarely suggest The Postcard here, since that 35-minute collection was designed for couples, and the longer ones, up to 90 minutes, usually ask more of a baby and a toddler than they want to give. Moving up a collection later costs only the difference.

If grandparents are on the trip, bring them: we have no cap on the number of people and charge nothing per person, and with two under three, an extra pair of hands is worth a lot.

Which Frames Come First

A baby and a toddler are rarely calm at the same moment for long, so the frames that need both of them happy go first, while everyone is fresh.

  1. The whole family together. The portrait you will frame comes right after hello, while the baby is fed and the toddler is still curious about us.
  2. The two children together. The sibling frame is the hardest to get, so it comes next: the toddler beside the baby, a kiss on the head or a hand on a tiny foot, with a parent close enough to steady the baby.
  3. Each parent with each child. One parent with the baby in the shade, the other with the toddler, and then you swap.
  4. The toddler's free play. Running, digging and racing the edge of the water come last, because they work even if the baby is feeding or asleep on a shoulder by then.

Neither child has to look at the camera or hold a pose: you play with them, and we photograph what happens. Short and calm beats long and heroic. Shade for One, Room to Run for the Other

The baby needs shade close by, a calm spot to feed and a short walk back to the room; the toddler needs open sand. For beach photos with a toddler and a baby, your own resort beach usually gives you both, and it spares you a transfer with two car seats. If your toddler will want to wade, our guide to which Cancun beaches are safest for kids compares the calmest water, and our heat plan for family sessions covers fabrics and shade breaks. If the baby is under 6 months, read the shade section of our sun safety guide for kids before the trip.

Between frames, it is one parent per child: while one of you feeds or settles the baby in the shade, the other keeps the toddler busy at the water's edge, and we keep photographing both. A feed is not lost time; it is the toddler's turn. If someone melts down, we pause the plan, let a parent hold them, and keep photographing quietly from a little distance. The cuddle that comes after a cry, a small face pressed into a parent's neck, is often a frame parents end up keeping.

One resort note: some hotels charge a fee for an outside photographer. You pay it directly to the hotel, and we check your resort's rule before the session.

Pack for two

Time a feed for the baby and a proper snack for the toddler just before we meet, and hide one surprise treat in the bag for the moment the toddler needs a reason to come back. Bring a spare outfit for each child, the baby's muslin wrap and pacifier, water and sun hats for between frames. Dress both children in soft clothes they have worn before, and leave the shoes in the bag: both can go barefoot on the sand.

Two Toddlers, Twins or a Newborn

Two toddlers, or twins, tend to want the same thing at the same moment, usually in opposite directions. The plan barely changes: the frames that need both children still go first and stay short, and between them each parent takes one child while we follow. For two under two, treat the younger one as the baby in everything above.

A toddler with a newborn is often the gentler version of the juggle: a newborn may well sleep through much of the session in a parent's arms, out of the sun, and then the clock belongs to the toddler. If this is the baby's first trip abroad, our first-trip guide explains how a session changes between newborn, infant and toddler.

After a Bad Night

With two small children on vacation, a bad night is always possible, so the booking terms matter. Nothing is paid up front: the session is paid at the end, by card or in cash. Cancelling is free up to one day before, so if a run of short nights, teething or a cold tells you by the day before that the kids need rest, you can cancel and book another day if one is still open. Booking early in the trip leaves spare days for exactly that, and an afternoon slot leaves room for a recovery nap first.

A preview arrives within 24 hours, so the grandparents can see the first frames right away, and the full private 4K gallery, with unlimited downloads and print rights, comes 1 to 3 business days after the session.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best time for family photos with a toddler and a baby in Cancun?

Whichever slot falls after both children have napped and eaten. You can book a 6:30, 7:00 or 7:30 am start or a 5:00 or 5:30 pm start online, or ask on WhatsApp for a different time. Mornings suit early risers and the beach is usually quieter; the late-afternoon starts suit children who nap after lunch.

How long should a family session with a toddler and a baby be?

For most families, 45 to 60 minutes. The Keepsake is 45 minutes and 25 edited photos for 375 USD. The Chapter, our recommendation for families, is 60 minutes and 35 edited photos for 475 USD, which leaves time for a feed and a reset. Moving up a collection later costs only the difference.

Which photos do you take first with a baby and a toddler?

The frames that need both children calm: the whole family together, then the two children together with a parent close by. Then each parent with each child, and last the toddler's free play, which works even if the baby is feeding or asleep by then.

What if the baby needs a feed or the toddler melts down?

We pause the plan, not the session. One parent feeds or settles the baby in the shade while the other stays with the toddler, and we keep photographing quietly. The cuddle after a cry is often a frame parents end up keeping.

Can we cancel if the kids are sick or overtired?

Yes, free of charge up to one day before the session. Nothing is paid up front: you pay at the end of the session, by card or in cash.

When do we get the photos?

You get a preview within 24 hours and the full private 4K gallery in 1 to 3 business days, with unlimited downloads and print rights.

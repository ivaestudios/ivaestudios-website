In short

Insure every child by name for hospital care and evacuation: abroad, OHIP pays at most 400 CAD a day in hospital, Alberta 100, B.C. 75, and Medicare usually nothing. Add cancellation cover, check your card's gaps, and read a resort hurricane guarantee as credit, not insurance. Your photo session needs none of it: there is no deposit.

We are photographers, not insurance brokers, and this page sells nothing. It gathers what we would want to know if we were flying to Cancun with children, from official pages and the providers' own documents, read on November 14, 2026.

Do You Need Travel Insurance for Mexico?

Both governments say yes. Canada advises buying trip interruption and travel health insurance before you leave: a provincial plan may pay nothing or only a small part of care abroad, never pays up front, and the Government of Canada will not pay your medical bills. The U.S. Embassy in Mexico says hospitals here usually want payment up front and hold a credit card at admission, the U.S. government does not pay for care overseas, and insurers that cover you abroad usually reimburse later.

Canadians, check one more line: many policies exclude regions under an advisory to avoid non-essential travel, and Canada's Mexico page, updated October 19, 2026, does not list Quintana Roo, Cancun's state. Passports come first: see our entry guides for U.S. travelers and Canadians.

Medical Cover for Every Child

From the U.S., ask your health plan whether it covers emergencies in Mexico, pays the hospital or reimburses you, and pays for an air ambulance home; the embassy notes very few insurers pay for evacuation. With grandparents along, Medicare usually covers nothing outside the U.S., while Medigap Plans C, D, F, G, M and N pay 80% of foreign travel emergency care, up to plan limits.

From Canada, three provinces pay this per person for an emergency abroad:

  • Ontario: up to 400 CAD a day in an operating room or intensive, coronary, neonatal or pediatric special care, 200 CAD a day for lower levels of care, up to 50 CAD a day as an outpatient, doctors at Ontario rates; acute, unexpected conditions only, and no transfer home.
  • Alberta: 100 CAD a day in hospital, 50 CAD for one outpatient visit a day, doctors at Alberta rates; nothing for hospital services in a private facility or for ambulances.
  • British Columbia: 75 CAD a day in hospital and doctors at B.C. rates; no ambulances or prescriptions.

Other provinces publish their own limits. Whatever you buy, name every child on it: Travel Guard's Preferred plan, for example, includes one child 17 or under in each paying adult's rate, though not for optional upgrades. Carry each child's insurance card and a claim form, as the U.S. Embassy advises, and keep every original receipt: Ontario asks for them, with each health card number, before OHIP repays part of a bill. Keep our guide to what to do if your child gets sick in Cancun handy too.

Evacuation and the Way Home

Evacuation is its own line, so read it separately. The U.S. Embassy puts a medical evacuation at $10,000 and up. Canada says every travel health policy should cover evacuation to Canada or the nearest place with medical care, with a medical escort, plus pre-existing conditions confirmed in writing and repatriation in case of death. Canada also suggests asking whether the insurer pays bills or advances cash up front, which matters when a hospital wants a card at admission.

Cancellation, Interruption and Cancel for Any Reason

Cancellation pays when a covered reason stops you before departure; interruption repays the unused part when you must come home early. Both pay only for the covered reasons in your policy; the table below shows one plan's limits.

Cancel for any reason, for families who want to decide later, is an option in Travel Guard's Preferred plan: it repays up to 50% of the insured trip cost, is available only if you buy the plan within 15 days of your first trip payment, and requires cancelling at least 48 hours before departure. The pre-existing condition waiver has the same 15-day window.

What Your Credit Card Leaves Out

A premium card can protect much of the trip and little of a hospital stay. Take Chase Sapphire Reserve's guide, in effect from June 11, 2026. When you charge the trip, even in part, to the card, its cancellation and interruption cover, up to $10,000 per person and $20,000 per trip, reaches family members such as the children's grandparents and counts a named storm warning within 50 miles of your airport or hotel as a covered reason. Emergency medical care and evacuation cover only you, a spouse or domestic partner and dependent children under 26. Medical care stops at $2,500, after a $50 deductible and only after any other insurance; evacuation, up to $100,000, covers trips of 5 to 60 days, is arranged by the card's provider and is not an insurance benefit; and a change of plans is not covered. Other cards differ, so read your own guide.

Resort Hurricane Guarantees: Credit, Not Cash

Hurricane season in the Atlantic and Caribbean runs from early June to the end of November, says Canada's Mexico page. A resort's hurricane policy protects room nights, not flights or health. Three we read:

  • Palace Resorts (including Moon Palace), 2025 to 2026: no penalty to reschedule if a hurricane warning is issued and you travel within 72 hours; if a hurricane directly hits your resort, a certificate for the unused nights, valid one calendar year, airfare and transfers excluded; extra nights at special rates if flights are canceled.
  • The Excellence Collection (its all-ages Finest resorts are at Playa Mujeres and Riviera Cancun): applies only once the U.S. National Hurricane Center confirms a hurricane, not for a tropical storm or a hurricane warning.
  • Club Med: a future travel certificate for the land portion of each night hit by hurricane-force winds from a named storm, Category 1 or higher; a guarantee, not insurance, with no cash compensation.

None of the three promises to pay for flights or medical care, and a 2021 U.S. Embassy hurricane alert notes that, under U.S. law, evacuation costs are the traveler's responsibility. Card cover does not simply stack on top: Chase's guide pays nothing for the part of a trip refunded as a supplier's credit voucher until that voucher expires. For timing, see our guide to Cancun's hurricane season.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need travel insurance for a family trip to Cancun?

Both governments advise it. Canada says to buy it before you leave; the U.S. Embassy in Mexico says hospitals there usually want payment up front and the U.S. government does not pay for care overseas.

Does OHIP cover me in Mexico?

Only in part: up to 400 CAD a day in an operating room or intensive care, 200 CAD a day for lower levels of hospital care, up to 50 CAD a day as an outpatient and doctors at Ontario rates, with no transfer home.

What medical insurance do kids need for Mexico?

Emergency medical and evacuation cover in each child's name. Travel Guard's Preferred plan includes one child 17 or under per paying adult; Chase Sapphire Reserve covers dependent children under 26 but caps medical care at $2,500.

Can I get cancel for any reason insurance for Mexico?

Yes, as an upgrade. Travel Guard's Preferred plan repays up to 50% of the insured trip cost if you buy within 15 days of your first trip payment and cancel at least 48 hours before departure.

Is a resort hurricane guarantee the same as insurance?

No. Palace Resorts offers penalty-free rescheduling and certificates for unused nights, Club Med says its program is not insurance, and none of the three we read promises to pay for flights or medical care.

Do we need insurance for our family photo session?

No. There is no deposit, you pay at the end of the session by card or in cash, and cancellation is free up to one day before.

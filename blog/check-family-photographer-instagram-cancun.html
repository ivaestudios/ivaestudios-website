Found a Cancun Photographer on Instagram? Five Checks Before You Pay

In short

Before you pay a photographer you found on Instagram, TikTok or Reddit, check five things: Google reviews that mention children, one complete recent family gallery, a real booking page, who will show up, and how much is due up front. Reverse image search a few of their photos and open About this account. The less you pay up front, the less you can lose.

You saw a reel of a family laughing at the water's edge in Cancun, tapped through to the profile, and now you are wondering whether to send a message. Families find us that way too. Before any money changes hands, take a few minutes for five checks. They work just as well for a name from TikTok or Reddit, and they make the two stories parents dread before a trip with children less likely: a photographer who never shows up, and a portfolio that turns out to be someone else's work.

1. Google Reviews That Mention the Kids

Comments under a post and testimonials in a story highlight live on an account the photographer controls. A Google review is posted by the reviewer, on the business's public Google listing. So search the photographer's business name on Google Maps, open the reviews and read the words, not just the stars.

  • Children, by age or situation. A baby, a shy three-year-old, two teenagers, a group of fourteen. If you are bringing a toddler, look for reviews about toddlers.
  • A hard moment. Wind, heat, a child who would not cooperate. How the photographer handled it is what you are paying for.
  • Logistics. Whether they arrived on time, came to the resort and delivered when they said they would.
  • Dates spread over months. A steady stream reads differently from a burst of praise in one week.

Here is our own listing as an example. As of November 2026, we hold a 5.0 rating across 78 Google reviews, and 29 of them mention the children. Google also sorts the reviews into topics, among them family photos, kid-friendly photographer and patience with kids. One specific line often tells a parent more than the average does:

“She knew exactly how to keep them engaged and smiling naturally, which is no small task on a sandy beach in the sun!”

Danielle P., Google review

We gathered excerpts from our family reviews, grouped by situation, in what parents wrote about their sessions with us.

2. A Full, Recent Family Gallery

A grid shows the best frames from many sessions. Your family gets one session, start to finish: the first minutes, when your youngest is still hiding behind your leg, and the last ones, when everyone is warm and tired. Ask for one complete gallery from a recent family session, ideally with children close to your kids' ages. Look for color and skin tones that hold from the first image to the last, and for moments between parents and children, not only rows of posed smiles.

Know what complete means, too: a full gallery from The Chapter, the collection we recommend for families, holds all 35 edited photos from 60 minutes.

Then make sure the photos are theirs. Take screenshots of two or three images and run a reverse image search:

  • Google Lens. In the Google app, tap the Lens icon in the search bar and choose the screenshot. Results can include websites with the image or a similar image (Google Search Help, checked November 14, 2026).
  • TinEye. Upload the image at tineye.com and sort the matches by Oldest, which shows the earliest copies TinEye found first (TinEye tutorial, checked November 14, 2026).

If a photo shows up under another photographer's name, or online long before the account existed, ask about it. A match is not proof of theft on its own, since a hotel or a magazine may share a photographer's work with credit, and no match does not prove the work is theirs. If faces look too smooth or hands look strange, see how to tell AI family portraits from real ones and our explainer on Content Credentials.

3. A Real Booking Page, Not Only DMs

Booking entirely through direct messages leaves the terms scattered across a chat. Look for a website that lists the collections, the price in a currency you understand, how many photos you receive, when they arrive and what happens if you cancel. Check that the business name matches on Instagram, the website and the Google listing.

Then look at the account itself. On their profile, tap the three-dot menu and choose About this account. When you are logged in, Instagram shows the date the account was created and how many times its username has changed, and for certain accounts the country it is based in, judged from the account's activity (Instagram Help Center on where to find it and what it shows, checked November 14, 2026). A new account or a string of name changes proves nothing by itself, but it is a reason to ask more before you pay.

4. Who Exactly Will Show Up

An Instagram account can belong to one photographer or to a whole team. Ask for the name of the person who will photograph your family, whether an assistant comes along, and whether the gallery you were shown is that person's work. On a booking platform, ask whether you choose the photographer or one is assigned; we compare both routes in marketplace versus booking a studio directly. With small children, a second pair of hands for a toy, a snack or a runaway hat makes a real difference.

“Both the photographer and assistant were exceptionally kind and helpful”

Susie H., Google review

Ask about your hotel, too. Some hotels charge a fee for an outside photographer, so find out who checks the rule and who pays. We confirm your hotel's rule before the session, and if your hotel charges a fee, you pay it to the hotel directly. For more questions worth asking, from editing style to bad weather, see how to choose a photographer in Mexico.

5. What You Pay Before the Session

A deposit is not a red flag by itself; our own weddings are booked with 10% to hold the date. What matters is how much leaves your account before anyone shows up, and how. If the photographer cancels or never confirms, whatever you paid up front comes back only on their terms.

The US Federal Trade Commission is blunt about the how: anyone who says you can only pay by bank transfer, wire transfer, cryptocurrency, a payment app or a gift card is probably a scammer. It recommends paying by credit card whenever possible, since cards give you the option to dispute charges, and searching the name of the company or person with words like review, scam or complaint (FTC consumer alert, July 22, 2026, checked November 14, 2026).

Our sessions: nothing up front

There is no deposit for our photo sessions. You pay at the end of the session, by card or in cash, and cancellation is free up to one day before.

Planning photos for a family reunion? Ask whether the price changes with the number of people. Ours does not: there is no limit on group size and no per-person fee.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if a photographer I found on Instagram is legit?

Check five things before you pay: Google reviews that mention children, one complete recent family gallery, a real booking page, the name of the person who will show up, and how much is due up front. Then reverse image search a few of their photos.

Is it a red flag if a photographer asks for a deposit?

Not by itself. Look at how much, and how you are asked to pay. The FTC says anyone who tells you that you can only pay by bank or wire transfer, cryptocurrency, a payment app or a gift card is probably a scammer. Our sessions have no deposit: you pay at the end, by card or in cash.

How can I tell if a photographer's portfolio is stolen?

Search two or three of their photos with Google Lens or TinEye. If they appear under another photographer's name, or online before the account existed, ask about it. A match is not always theft and no match is not proof, so also ask for one complete gallery from a recent family session.

What does About this account show on Instagram?

When you are logged in, it shows the date the account was created and how many times its username has changed. For certain accounts, it may also show the country the account is based in, accounts with shared followers, active ads and whether the account is verified.

What happens if the photographer cancels?

If you paid a deposit, getting it back depends on their terms and on how you paid; a credit card gives you the option to dispute a charge. With us there is nothing to refund: you pay at the end of the session, and cancellation is free up to one day before.

Can I trust a photographer recommended on Reddit or TikTok?

A recommendation is a good place to start, not the final word. Run the same five checks, and search the photographer's name with words like review, scam or complaint, as the FTC suggests.

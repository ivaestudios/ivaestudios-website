In short

For most families, the easiest Tulum session is on your hotel's beach, at sunrise or in the last hour before sunset. Tulum photographs nothing like Cancun: the jungle comes down to the sand, the hotels are low and wooden, and everything feels greener. Its most famous corner is a protected archaeological zone reached through a national park, so there are more rules. Here is what we tell families, with every third-party fee, schedule and rule checked on official sources on November 13, 2026.

Start With Your Hotel Beach, Then Look Further

For most families, the best Tulum location is the one closest to your room. Tulum's beach hotels share a look we love: wood, rope, palm and soft light. Photographing where you stay means no transfer and no car seat, with shade, a bathroom and snacks a few minutes away. Some hotels charge a fee for an outside photographer, which you pay to the hotel.

For a family visit, the beaches of the Parque del Jaguar, the park around the archaeological zone, are free for national and international visitors, according to Mexico's tourism ministry (July 17, 2026) and the Quintana Roo government, which adds that you can explore on foot, by bike or with a stroller without an entry fee, and that the optional electric shuttle costs 20 pesos per adult and 10 per child (July 23, 2026).

A professional session is a different question. Santa Fe, Pescadores and Maya beaches lie inside the Parque Nacional Tulum, whose management program, published by CONANP, requires an authorization from SEMARNAT, through CONANP, for photography with commercial purposes (rule 20). Some places to stay sit inside the protected area, too.

Send us your hotel first

Where you stay decides which stretch of sand is yours and whether a permit applies. We confirm your hotel's rule before the session, and if a permit is needed, we say so before you commit.

The Ruins With Kids: Visit Early, Photograph Elsewhere

INAH's official page lists the zone's hours as 8:00 am to 5:00 pm, with last entry at 3:30 pm, and tickets at 265 pesos for foreign visitors and 80 pesos for Mexican nationals, fees that include the CONANP and/or Grupo Mundo Maya charges (page updated August 10, 2026).

Three things shape what a camera can do there. Photography and filming with special or professional equipment in INAH sites go through an authorization procedure, INAH 01-001. The hours miss the best light, since in Tulum the sun rises before 7:30 am and sets after 6:00 pm all year. And in the national park you cross to reach the ruins, rule 10 of its management program allows drones only for scientific and monitoring work.

So we suggest visiting the ruins on your own, early, with water, hats and snacks, and keeping the session for your hotel beach at the right hour. Our guide to INAH photo permits at Tulum and Chichen Itza goes deeper, and if a site cannot legally give you the frame you want, we say so and suggest a look-alike that can.

Cenotes: Match the Water to the Child

The cenotes inland from Tulum add clear fresh water, pale stone and light from above, but they are not for every child. As our guide to a cenote family photoshoot with kids explains, open-air cenotes with shallow ledges suit young children and cave cenotes suit confident swimmers of about eight and up. We schedule cenote sessions for mid-morning, when kids are fresh, and if your children are shy of water, we will say honestly that a beach is the better choice. Before we drive, we check the cenote's current rules; our cenote photographer in Tulum page shows how those sessions run.

The Best Hours: Sunrise and Sunset by Month

Sixteenth-century sources called the ancient site of Tulum Zamá, Maya for morning or dawn, which makes sense, notes the national park's management program, since it stands on the highest stretch of the east coast, where sunrise is especially remarkable. It is still our favorite hour: the sun comes up over the Caribbean, and little ones who wake early on vacation are often at their best. Sunset is softer, with a warm sky and long shadows. Under the trees the light fades first, so we photograph in the jungle first and finish on the beach.

Getting There From Cancun or Tulum Airport

INAH places the archaeological zone about 128 km south of Cancun, on federal highway 307. Our rule of thumb: if a location is more than about 45 minutes from your room, book it only if your children travel well and you really love the backdrop. Staying in Cancun with a toddler? Read Tulum vs Cancun for your family photoshoot. Still choosing flights? Our CUN vs TQO airport guide compares both transfers. Either way, we build the drive into the timeline so nobody arrives as the light leaves.

Seaweed, Rain and a Real Plan B

Sargassum seaweed can reach Tulum's beaches. In January 2026, Mexico's Navy said its sargassum strategy with the Quintana Roo government would stay in place permanently through 2026, and CONABIO's SATsum system publishes a 7-day forecast of sargassum drift and landings for the Quintana Roo coast. We check conditions the week of your session and move to a cleaner stretch, or inland to the jungle or a cenote, if needed. Rain does not have to end a session either:

What to Wear for Green Light and White Sand

Part of a Tulum gallery may be lit by green rather than blue, so dress for the brightest backdrop, usually the open beach: two soft neutrals such as sand and cream, plus one muted accent like sage or terracotta. Linen and cotton gauze move in the breeze, bare feet suit the sand, and little ones are happiest in soft fabrics they already know. Our guide on what to wear for a family photoshoot in Mexico has the full palette, and you are welcome to send us yours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can we take family photos inside the Tulum ruins?

Photography and filming with special or professional equipment in INAH sites go through an authorization procedure, INAH 01-001. The zone is also open only from 8:00 am to 5:00 pm (last entry 3:30 pm, INAH, checked November 13, 2026), missing sunrise and sunset, so we suggest visiting on your own and photographing on your hotel beach.

How much does it cost to visit the Tulum ruins and beaches?

INAH lists 265 pesos for foreign visitors and 80 pesos for Mexican nationals, fees that include the CONANP and/or Grupo Mundo Maya charges (checked November 13, 2026). The Parque del Jaguar beaches are free for national and international visitors (Sectur and the Quintana Roo government, July 2026), and the optional electric shuttle costs 20 pesos per adult and 10 per child.

What time of day is best for family photos in Tulum?

Usually sunrise, when the sun comes up over the Caribbean. In Tulum, on the 15th of each month in 2027, sunrise ranges from 6:10 am in June to 7:28 am in January, and sunset from 6:10 pm in November to 7:33 pm in July (U.S. Naval Observatory). With babies and toddlers, the nap comes first.

Is there seaweed in Tulum?
